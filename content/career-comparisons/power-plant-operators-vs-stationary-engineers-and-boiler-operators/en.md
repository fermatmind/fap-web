# Power Plant Operators vs Stationary engineers and boiler operators

## Track electric output or keep facility utilities running

A power plant operator follows generation equipment and changes in electric output. A stationary engineer or boiler operator runs equipment that provides services such as steam or heat. They can work at the same site, and some duties overlap. Ask what equipment you own on shift and what you hand over to the next crew.

## Compare the two operating records

| Compare | Power plant operators | Stationary engineers and boiler operators |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focus | Generation units, output and auxiliaries | Boilers and other stationary utility equipment |
| Setting | Plant control room and equipment areas | Boiler room, central utility plant or industrial facility |
| Handoff | Unit status and generation exceptions | Utility service status and equipment exceptions |

## A campus needs an electricity log and a steam log

A Department of Energy case study describes the University of Missouri’s combined heat and power system, which supplies electric and thermal energy. For an illustrative handoff at a similar campus, the generation operator reports an output record and the stationary equipment operator flags a gap in the corresponding steam record. Each checks their own record and logs what remains open. The authorized shift lead coordinates any operating decision. [DOE campus case study](https://betterbuildingssolutioncenter.energy.gov/sites/default/files/attachments/University%20of%20Missouri%20District%20Energy%20Microgrid%20Case%20Study.pdf)

## Ask what training and licensing apply to the assignment

BLS lists a high school diploma or equivalent and long-term on-the-job training for both occupations. In generation, look for structured training on assigned units and supervised shift work. For boiler or stationary equipment work, check the state and local licensing rules where the facility is located. If a power plant assignment also includes regulated boiler or stationary equipment work, ask the employer and the relevant state or local authority which licensing category applies. Confirm the specific equipment and duties before choosing a training route. [BLS preparation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) · [BLS boiler licensing](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/production/stationary-engineers-and-boiler-operators.htm) · [BLS power plant training](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/production/power-plant-operators-distributors-and-dispatchers.htm)

## Compare shift coverage alongside national pay

The 2025 national median annual wages in BLS OEWS were $102,040 for power plant operators and $78,620 for stationary engineers and boiler operators. Ask about rotating shifts, work outside the control room and holiday coverage. Compare offers by similar facility, seniority and shift hours. [BLS wage table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Use shift documentation to bridge the roles

Generation experience can transfer to a utility plant through equipment records and shift handoffs. Add the relevant boiler and facilities training. Boiler operators can use their steam system experience to move toward generation support while learning unit terminology, control room communication and site authorization.

## Try a thirty-minute shift handoff

Use fictional records for unit A from 8 to 10 a.m. The generation log is complete. The steam summary says complete as well, but its source record is missing from 9 to 9:30 a.m. Spend 12 minutes writing a generation note and 13 minutes writing the boiler-side handoff. Take five minutes to identify what each record shows and who verifies the missing interval.
