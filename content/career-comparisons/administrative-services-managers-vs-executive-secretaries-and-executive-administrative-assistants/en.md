# Administrative Services Managers vs. Executive Secretaries and Executive Administrative Assistants

## Where the work differs

Administrative services managers coordinate support that several departments use. Executive secretaries and executive administrative assistants organize activity around an executive, including schedules, travel and meeting materials. A visit can require both kinds of work.

## Compare the handoffs

| Work product | Administrative Services Managers | Executive Secretaries and Executive Administrative Assistants |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Office coverage and a room allocation | Confirmed executive itinerary and briefing materials |

## A shared situation

An executive arrives an hour earlier than planned while the booked room is still in use for training. The assistant verifies arrival and departure times and identifies meetings that could move. The administrative manager checks alternative rooms, equipment and visitor support. Once the executive confirms an option, the assistant sends the revised schedule.

## How to prepare

To explore administration, take on a small project scheduling shared resources. To explore executive support, practice combining travel updates, meeting materials and calendar changes into a brief. Ask employers whether they want team coordination, direct executive support or both. BLS reports that administrative services managers typically bring both a bachelor’s degree and related experience. Executive secretaries and executive administrative assistants usually bring several years of related experience. Compare the experience each employer counts before choosing a training program. [BLS administrative services](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/administrative-services-managers.htm#tab-4) · [BLS executive support](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/secretaries-and-administrative-assistants.htm#tab-4)

## What to ask an employer

The office coordination load depends on shared resources and competing department needs. Executive support can be more sensitive to travel changes and decisions made on short notice. Ask administrators who may move another department's booking. Ask assistants what they can confirm on behalf of an executive and what to do if a change arrives outside local work hours.

## Moving between roles

To move into administration, turn a one-time scheduling fix into a procedure several teams can use. To move into executive support, condense a complex office plan into an itinerary and a list of items that need a decision.

## Try it in 30 minutes

An executive now arrives at 9 instead of 10 and must leave at 10:30. Training occupies the main room until 10. A six-seat room opens at 9:15, but only the main room has the presentation equipment. The meeting has five people. Spend ten minutes checking options, fifteen drafting two schedules and five marking choices that need approval.
