# Audio and Video Technicians vs. Sound Engineering Technicians at a Recorded Show

## Run the media systems or shape what people hear

Audio and video technicians help cameras, displays, sound sources, and other equipment work together for an event. Sound engineering technicians record and adjust sound to meet the production's goals. At a recorded show, the two may exchange notes about the same microphone, with different work to deliver.

## What does the producer receive?

| Work | Audio and Video Technicians | Sound Engineering Technicians |
|---|---|---|
| Primary concern | Working inputs, displays, and connections | Sound levels, recording quality, and mix choices |
| Example output | Media setup and a record of what the next crew receives | Organized audio tracks or a mix with review notes |
| Main counterpart | Venue and production crew | Performers, producers, and recording staff |

## Applause obscures the host on the recording

In a fictional show, the screens and room sound work, but applause hides part of the host's introduction on playback. The A/V tech checks which feeds and recording are involved and gives the audio team the relevant timestamps. The sound engineering tech compares the available tracks, then tells the producer whether an alternate mix or a pickup is feasible.

## Start with work you can show

BLS says requirements for both audio and video technicians and sound engineering technicians can range from a high school diploma to a college degree, depending on the position. Compare programs by the kind of equipment and production work you will practice. To explore A/V, help with a supervised setup and document equipment and handoffs. To explore sound, practice labeling, editing, and delivering your own recordings, then show what you changed and why. [BLS occupational preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/broadcast-and-sound-engineering-technicians.htm#tab-4)

## Compare setup time with revision time

Venue A/V work can follow the setup, rehearsal, and teardown schedule. Sound work can include live capture followed by concentrated listening and revision. For venue A/V, ask how much time you get to check equipment before a show and who handles breakdown. For sound work, ask how many listening and revision rounds are typical and who gives mix feedback.

## Build a sample for the other role

To move into sound, take audio you have permission to use and prepare two versions with different priorities, such as speech clarity and room ambience. To move into A/V, expand an audio project into a plan that covers screens, cameras, and changeovers.

## Try a 30-minute recording handoff

Use fictional track notes. At 00:40 the audience mix obscures the host. The isolated host track is clear, the crowd track carries loud applause, and the music track is silent. Spend ten minutes making a feed and timestamp handoff, fifteen outlining two mix options, and five identifying what the producer should decide.
