# Vessel Captain vs. Sailor or Marine Oiler: An Unresolved Report Before Departure

## What each role delivers

Captains, mates and pilots contribute to command, watchkeeping or pilotage according to their assignments. Sailors and marine oilers form a combined U.S. statistical group that includes separate deck and engine-room duties.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Captains, Mates, and Pilots of Water Vessels | Sailors and Marine Oilers |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Navigation, vessel status and coordination | Assigned deck work or engine-room support |
| Record | Voyage plan, status and unresolved items | What was observed or done, when and who received the report |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $92,460 | $51,520 |

## How the work meets

A fictional cargo vessel is preparing to leave port. A deck sailor reports that the assigned preparations are complete. A marine oiler reports an unusual reading and its time and equipment ID to the engine watch. The engine watch tells the bridge that the issue is still under review. The mate records the two statuses and the captain waits for the designated update under the vessel's procedure. If a pilot is assigned, the bridge shares the change in status.

## How to enter

BLS lists a postsecondary nondegree award as typical entry education for the combined bridge occupation. It lists no formal educational credential and moderate-term on-the-job training for sailors and marine oilers. [BLS Table 1.2](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) For a U.S. national master or mate endorsement, check the Coast Guard qualification checklist for the intended route and vessel tonnage. Federal first-class pilotage has a separate, route-specific First Class Pilot endorsement. Maritime pilots may also need a state license. ## Work conditions

Bridge work draws on reports from several stations and outside sources. Deck and engine-room assignments follow their own watches, tasks and physical conditions. Ask how an open exception carries into the next shift.

## Moving between roles

Deck or engine-room experience can build familiarity with how a vessel reports and responds to issues. Moving toward a bridge assignment requires checking the specific watch, training and endorsement requirements. Moving toward a rating assignment also means learning the duties and requirements of the specific billet.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use three mock entries: the deck tasks are confirmed, an engine-room reading is still under review and the original pilot plan says 3 p.m. Spend ten minutes writing the station records, ten creating a bridge handoff and ten marking what is still open and who supplies the next update. Use the records alone.
