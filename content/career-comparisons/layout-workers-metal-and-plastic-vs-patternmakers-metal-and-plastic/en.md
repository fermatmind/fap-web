# Layout workers, metal and plastic vs. patternmakers, metal and plastic

## Mark the part or make the pattern

Layout workers transfer locations and reference marks from drawings or templates to workpieces. Patternmakers make, repair and check patterns or templates that another process can use. Layout workers may also make their own positioning templates. Patternmakers may lay out and scribe features on pattern stock. Ask whether the workpiece is the main deliverable or whether the work produces a pattern, such as a casting pattern.

## Compare the roles

| Item | Layout worker | Patternmaker |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deliverable | A marked workpiece with references for the next operation | A dimensionally verified production pattern, such as a casting pattern |
| Setting to ask about | Fabrication bench or shop floor near the next operation | Pattern bench or prototype and tooling shop |
| Typical U.S. entry education | High school diploma or equivalent | High school diploma or equivalent |
| Typical on-the-job training | Moderate-term | Moderate-term |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $63,870 | $58,000 |

## The work order changes revision

At a fictional shop, a work order calls for revision B, but the template at the station is revision A. The layout worker puts the workpiece on hold and records the mismatch. The patternmaker makes and checks the template against the approved revision. A shop inspector verifies the revision and dimensions before releasing it. The layout worker can then mark and hand off the workpiece with the revision recorded.

## Getting started in the U.S.

BLS lists a high school diploma or equivalent as typical entry education and moderate-term on-the-job training to develop competency for both occupations. For layout work, practice reading a drawing and marking references on a paper mock-up. For patternmaking, prepare a mock dimensional check and revision record. Ask which materials and tools the shop uses, who trains new workers and how initial work is reviewed. [BLS preparation](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Ask who checks the first piece

For layout, ask how often jobs change and who checks marks before they go to the next station. For patternmaking, ask whether the work is mainly new patterns or repairs, what determines acceptance and who approves rework.

## Moving between the jobs

A layout worker can bring a redacted reference checklist and revision discrepancy log, then build experience making and checking reusable patterns. A patternmaker can bring a redacted dimensional report and revision log, then practice locating references on workpieces and handing off marked stock.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use mock records only. The order requires revision B. An old A template has holes 100 mm apart. The B drawing specifies 104 ± 1 mm, but a candidate B template measures 102 mm. Spend ten minutes checking revision and tolerance, ten writing the correction and inspection request, and ten drafting the layout worker's release checklist. Decide whether either template can be used and identify who releases a corrected one.
