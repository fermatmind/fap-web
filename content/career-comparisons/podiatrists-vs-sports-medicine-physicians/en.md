# Podiatrists vs Sports Medicine Physicians

## Choose whether to specialize in the foot and ankle or the demands of sports

Podiatrists evaluate and treat foot and ankle problems within their licensed scope. Sports medicine physicians address sports and exercise-related injuries and health questions across body regions. Their work overlaps when an athlete's foot or ankle concern affects participation.

## Compare the questions and work products

| Work | Podiatrist | Sports medicine physician |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main question | What explains the foot or ankle problem? | How does the condition affect safe participation and recovery? |
| Common work product | Focused examination and foot or ankle care plan | Sports-related assessment and follow-up plan |
| Common partners | Rehabilitation and other clinical specialists | Rehabilitation, athletic trainers and medical teams |

## Follow one referral in a California clinic

In a fictional California clinic, a sports medicine physician sees a runner whose activity goals and training history are documented but whose foot concern needs focused evaluation. The physician sends the question to the podiatrist. The podiatrist assesses the foot within their scope and returns an assessment and follow-up recommendations. The sports medicine physician integrates that report into the clinic's activity follow-up. Actual duties depend on each clinician's scope and facility privileges. [California podiatry scope](https://www.pmbc.ca.gov/forms_pubs/app_summary.pdf) · [Sports medicine tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-1229.06)

## Compare training routes before applying

BLS describes the podiatry route as a DPM degree, a three-year residency and state licensure. California requires a DPM license and distinguishes resident and permanent practice. Sports medicine physicians qualify through physician education and postgraduate training and hold the applicable state physician license. One additional qualification route is the ABFM Sports Medicine CAQ, which requires current family medicine certification and at least 12 months of full-time ACGME-accredited sports medicine fellowship training and a passing certification exam. [BLS podiatry training](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/podiatrists.htm) · [California DPM licensing](https://www.pmbc.ca.gov/forms_pubs/app_summary.pdf) · [California physician licensing](https://www.mbc.ca.gov/Licensing/Physicians-and-Surgeons/Apply/Physicians-and-Surgeons-License/) · [ABFM CAQ](https://www.theabfm.org/added-qualifications/sports-medicine/)

## Compare the clinic schedule and opportunities to collaborate

Ask a podiatry practice about routine visits, procedures and call. Ask a sports medicine group about clinic time, team coverage and return-to-activity follow-up. A DPM interested in sports-focused work can start by collaborating on foot and ankle referrals within their license, then build experience communicating functional findings and activity goals. A sports medicine physician can build a foot and ankle focus through joint consultations and additional specialty training within their physician scope. For a move between the licensed professions, check the separate education and licensure routes. [BLS podiatry work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/podiatrists.htm) · [Sports medicine tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-1229.06)
