# Emergency Medicine Physicians vs Family Medicine Physicians

## An acute encounter and care across visits

Emergency medicine physicians assess and treat acute illness and injury, then document decisions and follow-up for that encounter. Family medicine physicians diagnose, treat, and provide preventive care across age groups and coordinate continuing care. Both may see people with acute symptoms.

## The emergency visit is complete and a result is pending

At a fictional hospital, the emergency physician completes an acute evaluation and records the disposition. The handoff to the family practice names the emergency team as the owner of an outstanding result. The family physician keeps the existing preventive follow-up and records that the result is pending. Under this hospital's example rules, the emergency team tracks and communicates the result, and the family team updates its continuing care record. [SAEM emergency department documentation](https://www.saem.org/about-saem/academies-interest-groups-affiliates2/cdem/for-students/online-education/m3-curriculum/documentation/documentation-of-em-encounters)

## Training and licensing

Both U.S. paths ordinarily involve medical school (MD or DO), residency in the applicable specialty, and state physician licensure. Compare the residency tracks and check the relevant state medical board. Ask employers about specialty certification and privileges for the job. [BLS physician entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/physicians-and-surgeons.htm#tab-4)

## How a workweek is scheduled

Physicians may work irregular, overnight or on-call hours. Ask emergency departments how overnight shifts and simultaneous cases are assigned. Ask family practices how scheduled appointments, same-day visits, follow-up time and after-hours coverage are handled. [BLS physician work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/physicians-and-surgeons.htm#tab-3)

## Where to start when switching specialties

If moving toward family medicine, ask a residency program to review prior training for possible advanced placement under ABFM policy. If moving toward emergency medicine, ask an emergency medicine residency program what training you must complete and verify ABEM’s qualifying training for board eligibility. Verify your state license separately and ask the hospital to confirm which duties it can privilege you to perform. [ABFM advanced placement](https://www.theabfm.org/become-certified-advanced-placement-credit-policy/) · [ABEM board eligibility](https://www.abem.org/for-residents/board-eligibility/)

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use a different fictional case. E-24 lists a pending result owned by the emergency team. F-24 shows a family medicine visit already booked. U-24 records a family member's call but includes no formal result. Spend ten minutes assigning each item's status, ten minutes writing separate result-tracking and continuity notes, and ten minutes naming the next owner and the missing evidence for each. Use the records only.
