# Energy Engineers, Except Wind and Solar vs. Wind Energy Development Managers: Two Different Project Reviews

## What each role delivers

Energy engineers in this occupation measure and analyze facility energy use and recommend savings measures. Wind energy development managers coordinate assessments, project schedules and agreements.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Energy Engineers, Except Wind and Solar | Wind Energy Development Managers |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Energy baseline and savings verification proposal | Wind project milestones and open agreements |
| Basis | Metering, use periods and comparison conditions | Site, assessment, budget and agreements |

## How the work meets

A fictional company reviews a factory efficiency project and a candidate wind site in the same week. The engineer marks the metering boundary and missing shift data. The development manager submits the site assessment and an unsigned interconnection agreement for a separate decision.

## How to enter

A bachelor’s degree was the most frequent O*NET education response for both occupations. For efficiency engineering, show measurement and savings verification. For wind development, show project coordination and milestone planning. ## Work conditions

Energy work can span site measurements and reviews. Development can follow project approvals, agreements and external meetings. Ask what travel each role involves.

## Moving between roles

Moving to wind development, bring technical review skills and add site and agreement coordination. Moving into energy efficiency, bring project management and learn metering and savings verification.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use mock records: the factory baseline lacks night-shift measurements, and the proposed wind site passes assessment but lacks a signed interconnection agreement. Spend ten minutes each on the baseline gap, the milestone record and what can proceed or must wait.
