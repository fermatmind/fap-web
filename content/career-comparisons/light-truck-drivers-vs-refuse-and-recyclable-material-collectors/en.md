# Light truck drivers vs. refuse and recyclable material collectors

## A freight handoff and a collection route

Light truck drivers verify a load and document delivery or pickup. Refuse and recyclable material collectors pick up designated materials on routes and document missed stops or exceptions. Both jobs can use trucks, with different records and reasons to escalate a stop.

## Compare the roles

| Item | Light truck driver | Refuse and recyclable material collector |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deliverable | A reconciled load and signed or otherwise documented delivery | A completed pickup or exception record and transfer |
| Work setting to ask about | Roads, loading areas and receiving docks | Outdoor route stops and designated transfer or disposal sites |
| Typical U.S. entry education | High school diploma or equivalent | No formal educational credential in the BLS classification |
| Typical on-the-job training | Short-term | Short-term |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $44,860 | $49,690 |

Wages cover nonfarm wage and salary workers and exclude self-employment. ## Two stops at one office park

At a fictional site, a light truck driver delivers three cartons to an authorized receiver, confirms the count and closes the delivery record. A collection worker finds wet trash mixed with the cardboard at a scheduled recycling stop. Under the site's example agreement, only separated cardboard is accepted. The collector records the missed pickup and reports why. The crew lead contacts the property and decides when to schedule another pickup after separation. Freight dispatch handles any delivery issue.

## Preparing for U.S. work

Ask the light truck employer for the vehicle and assignment, then check the license class that applies. For collection work, ask whether the position includes driving, riding with a crew or operating collection equipment, and what training the employer requires. Where driving is involved, check the vehicle or combination and applicable state licensing rules. FMCSA's CDL classes depend on vehicle and use, including certain passenger or hazardous cargo assignments. [BLS preparation](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) · [FMCSA CDL classes](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/registration/commercial-drivers-license/drivers)

## Ask how stops are handled

For delivery, ask who loads the truck, how long receiving can take and who reschedules a stop. For collection, ask how many stops are assigned, who handles exceptions and what training takes place before working on the route.

## Moving between the jobs

A light truck driver can bring a redacted route log and delivery exception report, then learn material categories, site rules and crew handoffs. A collector can bring a redacted route and skipped-stop log, then learn shipping paperwork, receiver confirmation and the driving requirements of the new job.

## A 30-minute tabletop exercise

Use a mock freight order for three cartons that an authorized receiver has counted and a collection sheet with one cart of clean cardboard and a second mixed cart. The example policy permits pickup of the first cart. The second stays for separation and crew-lead follow-up. Take ten minutes to log delivery, ten to record the collection decision, and ten to write the two handoffs. Mark who signs for the cartons, the status of each cart and who schedules follow-up.
