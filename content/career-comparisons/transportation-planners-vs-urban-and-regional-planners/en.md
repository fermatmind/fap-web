# Transportation Planners vs. Urban and Regional Planners: Adding Homes Near a Station

## What each role delivers

Transportation planners study travel and compare service options. Urban and regional planners evaluate land use and area development. Some urban planning jobs specialize in transportation.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Transportation Planners | Urban and Regional Planners |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Travel needs and service options | Land-use policy and district recommendations |
| Basis | Ridership, transfers, networks and travel time | Land-use rules, housing needs and community facilities |

## How the work meets

A fictional city considers more housing near a station. The transportation planner compares added trips, bus connections and crowded platform periods. The urban planner compares development options and considers public facilities, walking connections and community feedback.

## How to enter

For transportation planners, 55% of U.S. O*NET education responses cited a bachelor’s degree and 45% cited a master’s degree. BLS Table 1.2 lists a master’s degree as typical for urban and regional planners. Check the work described in each posting. ## Work conditions

Transportation assignments may combine network analysis and field surveys. Urban planning may combine district research and public engagement. Ask how teams keep land-use and ridership assumptions in sync.

## Moving between roles

Transportation planners can bring accessibility analysis to land-use work and add policy and facilities evaluation. Urban planners can bring development scenarios and community needs and build transport network and operations skills.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock plans: A adds more homes near the station, with school capacity unchecked. B adds fewer homes and a longer walk to transit. Spend ten minutes each on transport measures, land-use and facility measures, and missing evidence.
