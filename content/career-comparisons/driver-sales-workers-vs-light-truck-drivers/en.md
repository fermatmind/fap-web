# Driver/sales workers vs. light truck drivers

## One delivery, two responsibilities

Driver/sales workers deliver goods and also handle customer orders or sales along a route. Light truck drivers focus on delivering or picking up loads and documenting the handoff. Ask what you own when a customer wants something that is not on the shipping papers.

## Compare the jobs

| Item | Driver/sales worker | Light truck driver |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deliverable | Delivery record and customer order or follow-up | Verified load, delivery confirmation and exception record |
| Setting to ask about | Regular customer stops and loading areas | Distribution sites, road routes and receiving docks |
| Typical U.S. entry education | High school diploma or equivalent | High school diploma or equivalent |
| Typical on-the-job training | Short-term | Short-term |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $38,770 | $44,860 |

Wages cover nonfarm wage and salary workers and exclude self-employment. [O*NET sales route duties](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-3031.00#Tasks) · [O*NET light truck duties](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-3033.00#Tasks) · [BLS Table 1.2](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## The store asks for two more cases

A fictional store receives four cases and asks for two more. The remaining cases on the vehicle are reserved for the next stop. The driver/sales worker records the request and asks the order desk to confirm when it can be filled. The light truck driver obtains confirmation for the four cases, retains the reserved stock and logs the exception. The order desk confirms stock and dispatch controls any route change.

## Getting started in the U.S.

BLS identifies a driver's license and clean driving record for both occupations. Ask the employer about the actual vehicle and load, then confirm which license and endorsements apply with the state licensing agency. Federal CDL classes account for vehicle or combination weight and certain passenger or hazardous-material uses. A job title alone does not determine the required license. Sales-route applicants can also practice taking an order and communicating when stock is unavailable. [BLS entry guidance](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/transportation-and-material-moving/delivery-truck-drivers-and-driver-sales-workers.htm#tab-4) · [FMCSA CDL classes](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/registration/commercial-drivers-license/drivers)

## Compare what drives the schedule

Ask how much loading time is built into each route and who handles delayed receiving. For driver/sales openings, ask whether you are measured on orders, collections or account follow-up. For light truck openings, ask how dispatch handles rejected deliveries or route changes.

## Moving between the jobs

A driver/sales worker can bring a redacted route and exception log, then practice reconciling shipping papers and coordinating with dispatch. A light truck driver can bring a redacted delivery log and list of recurring customer issues, then learn the product catalog, order system and limits on availability promises.

## A 30-minute tabletop exercise

Use four cases delivered and two additional cases requested. The only two on the truck are reserved for the next stop. The warehouse cannot replenish before 2 p.m. and this store stops receiving at 1:30. The order desk confirms restock timing and dispatch authorizes reroutes. Take ten minutes to reconcile the cases, ten to draft a customer update, and ten to write the delivery and dispatch handoff. Explain what prevents filling the request in this window and who confirms the next one.
