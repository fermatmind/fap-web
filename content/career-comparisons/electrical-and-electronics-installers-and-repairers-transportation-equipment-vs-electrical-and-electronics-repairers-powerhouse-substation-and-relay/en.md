# Transportation Equipment Electrical Repairer vs. Substation and Relay Repairer

## Vehicle equipment and fixed utility equipment

Transportation equipment electrical and electronics installers and repairers inspect vehicle electrical equipment, verify repairs and keep service records. Powerhouse, substation and relay repairers check fixed electrical equipment and document findings and maintenance.

## Compare the asset records

| Item | Transportation equipment electrical repairer | Powerhouse, substation and relay repairer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verify | Vehicle identity, installed equipment and service history | Fixed equipment identity, inspection and maintenance history |
| Document | Findings for the vehicle and what needs review | Findings for the fixed asset and outstanding questions |
| Hand off | To the fleet maintenance lead | To the designated electrical equipment lead |

## A vehicle ticket has an attachment for a fixed asset

A fictional fleet depot receives a ticket with a vehicle number but an attachment referring to fixed electrical equipment. The vehicle repairer checks the service history and marks what belongs to the vehicle. The fixed equipment repairer checks the asset register and explains what the attachment covers. The coordinator assigns separate follow-up items after reviewing both notes.

## U.S. education and training

BLS assigns both occupations a postsecondary nondegree award and moderate-term on-the-job training. The transportation equipment row assigns no related experience, while the powerhouse, substation and relay repair row assigns less than five years of related experience. Check the employer's equipment training and any job-specific credential or authorization. [BLS detailed education and training table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/education-and-training-by-occupation.htm)

## Ask what shapes the shift

For vehicle work, ask how maintenance is scheduled around fleet availability and releases. For fixed utility equipment, ask how maintenance windows and work across sites are allocated and who reviews the records.

## Moving between roles

Vehicle repairers can bring drawing literacy and service records into fixed equipment training, then learn the assigned equipment and its documentation. Fixed equipment repairers can bring record verification and maintenance reporting to transportation work, then learn the fleet's equipment and service procedures.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a fictional vehicle list, a service ticket and a fixed equipment register. The ticket cites a fixed asset number. Spend ten minutes writing the fleet concern, ten drafting the fixed-asset verification, and ten identifying the two recipients and the records that would confirm where each item belongs.
