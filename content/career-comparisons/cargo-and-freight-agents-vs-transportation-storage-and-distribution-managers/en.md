# Cargo and Freight Agents vs. Transportation, Storage and Distribution Managers When Docks Fill Up

## Start with the responsibility

Cargo and freight agents focus on how a particular shipment will move and what the carrier has agreed to. Transportation, storage and distribution managers allocate site resources across several jobs. A single delay can affect the rest of the dock schedule.

## Compare the handoffs

| Handoff | Cargo and Freight Agents | Transportation, Storage and Distribution Managers |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Shipment appointment, documents, and carrier response | Dock priorities, staffing plan, and responsibility for changes |

## One working example

At a fictional warehouse, two trucks arrive and only one dock remains. The freight agent confirms the carrier's waiting limit and the customer's receiving commitment. The distribution manager plans dock and staffing coverage and identifies which commitment has to be reconsidered. After an authorized decision, the freight agent updates the carrier.

## How to prepare

O*NET places freight agents in Job Zone 1–2 and distribution managers in Job Zone 4, broad levels of preparation. For freight, build experience in transport tracking or booking support. For distribution management, look for receiving, team coordination or site planning where you can learn capacity and priority decisions. Check the employer's expectations for supervision and site training. ## How the workday differs

Freight agents work around pickup and delivery windows. Distribution managers may spend part of the day checking floor capacity and handling competing shipments. Ask who can change a dock priority and when shifts need backup.

## Moving between the roles

Move toward distribution management by connecting shipment records to staffing, dock and capacity decisions. Move toward freight by learning the documents and communications that make one shipment possible.

## Try it in 30 minutes

A fictional warehouse has one dock. Truck A arrives at 2, takes 25 minutes, and must leave by 2:40. Truck B arrives at 2:10, takes 20 minutes, and has a 2:30 handoff deadline. Spend ten minutes identifying limits, fifteen testing both sequences, and five identifying the commitment that needs a new decision.
