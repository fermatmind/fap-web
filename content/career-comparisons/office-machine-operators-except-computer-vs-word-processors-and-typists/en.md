# Office Machine Operators vs. Word Processors and Typists: A Missing Line on the Printed Notice

## What each role delivers

Office machine operators fill and inspect office document orders. Word processors and typists enter, format and check the documents that can become the source for those orders.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Office Machine Operators, Except Computer | Word Processors and Typists |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Complete physical document set | Proofread and formatted document |
| Basis | Sample output, quantities and sequence | Revision instructions, layout and text accuracy |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $40,960 | $49,280 |

## How the work meets

A fictional office updates a training notice. The word processor verifies dates and paragraphs and saves the approved file. The machine operator checks a duplex sample, notices the page order differs from the source and records the problem before checking the next copy.

## How to enter

BLS lists a high school diploma or equivalent and short-term on-the-job training for both. Ask whether the job’s trial assignment checks document formatting, finished output or both. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Machine operation may follow production and pickup deadlines. Word processing may follow revision and approval cycles. Ask how the approved version is identified.

## Moving between roles

Moving to word processing, bring attention to pagination and add proofreading and document formatting. Moving to machine operation, bring version control and learn setup and batch inspection.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Approved file V2 says June 12 and includes the location on page 2. Draft V1 still says June 10. The duplex sample omits the location and shows pages 1, 3, 2. Spend ten minutes each checking dates and formatting, checking the physical page order and identifying the approved version and conditions for reprinting.
