# Art Therapist vs. Mental Health Counselor

## What happens during a session

An art therapist uses art-making as part of the therapeutic process, choosing media around a client's goals and observing how the client works with them. A mental health counselor may use a structured conversation to understand the client's concerns and develop coping strategies. Both can plan treatment and document progress.

## Compare what goes in the record

| Step | Art therapist | Mental health counselor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prepare | Pick media and adapt the task to the client | Identify a recent experience to explore together |
| Observe | Note how the client engages and describes the work | Ask about circumstances, reactions and effects on daily life |
| Follow up | Record responses and decide what to revisit through art-making | Record agreed goals and strategies for the next session |

## One goal, two sessions

Suppose a client wants to reconnect with family but repeatedly puts down the phone before calling. An art therapist might offer movable collage pieces and ask the client to show where the sequence tends to stop. The note can record the client's own explanation of the arrangement. A mental health counselor might ask about the last attempt, what went through the client's mind and what the client wants to track before the next session. If the professionals are authorized to coordinate, they can share the agreed goal and relevant observations within that authorization.

## Graduate education and licensure

For art therapy, start by checking whether the state regulates the title or practice and which graduate programs count. In New York, practice and title use in Creative Arts Therapy generally require licensure unless a legal exemption applies. The state reviews qualifying graduate education, supervised experience and examination. For mental health counseling, BLS describes a typical master's and internship route and reports that all states require a license for mental health counselors. Check the state board for approved education, supervision and exams before committing to training.

## How the week is organized

Ask an art therapy employer how it schedules setup and cleanup, budgets for materials, and handles artwork storage. Ask a counseling employer about recurring appointments, gaps for documentation, and arrangements for case consultation. In either setting, those answers help you compare the workload behind the posted client hours.

## Moving into the other role

An art therapist can bring experience agreeing on treatment goals and documenting sessions. For a counseling role, check what coursework and clinical supervision the licensing board will count. A mental health counselor can bring interviewing and progress documentation skills, then develop competence in selecting media, adapting studio activities and documenting how the client responds. Check the target state's training requirements before applying.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a fictional client who picked up the phone three times last week without calling, is willing to try picture cards and declines photographs of the result. Spend ten minutes drafting an art activity with three cards and one observable response to record. Spend ten minutes writing two counseling questions and one point to revisit. In the last ten minutes, draft a shared goal and record the client's preference about retaining images.
