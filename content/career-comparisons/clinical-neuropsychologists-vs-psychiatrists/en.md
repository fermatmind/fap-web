# Clinical Neuropsychologists vs Psychiatrists

## A cognitive report and a medical decision

Clinical neuropsychologists assess cognitive and behavioral function and integrate the findings in reports and recommendations. Psychiatrists evaluate mental health symptoms and history and are responsible for medical diagnosis and treatment within their scope. Both may interview the same patient and coordinate follow-up.

## Outputs, work settings, and U.S. entry

| Item | Clinical neuropsychologists | Psychiatrists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | Cognitive assessment report, limitations, and recommendations | Medical evaluation, treatment plan, and follow-up record |
| Setting examples | Assessment, rehabilitation, or multidisciplinary clinics | Outpatient psychiatry, hospitals, or specialty services |
| U.S. preparation | Doctoral psychology training and specialty preparation in clinical neuropsychology | Medical school (MD or DO) and residency in psychiatry |

Check the relevant state licensing boards for independent practice. [O*NET clinical neuropsychologist tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-3039.03#Tasks) · [O*NET psychiatrist tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-1223.00#Tasks) · [ABPP specialty criteria](https://abpp.org/application-information/learn-about-specialty-boards/clinical-neuropsychology/specialty-specific/) · [BLS physician entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/physicians-and-surgeons.htm)

## What is the referral asking?

At a fictional outpatient clinic, a patient reports losing track of multistep handoffs at work. After an interview, the psychiatrist sends the clinical neuropsychologist a focused referral about those difficulties, with a note that the sleep history is pending. The neuropsychologist returns a report that describes performance within the assessment setting, the limits of its interpretation, and the missing information. The psychiatrist reviews the report with the interview and updated history and decides what to clarify at follow-up. Each specialist signs their own record.

## Training, licensure, and specialty certification

For clinical neuropsychology, check doctoral programs and supervised specialty training. ABPP's requirements describe specialty board certification, including postdoctoral preparation. Verify psychology licensure with the state board and check the employer's credential requirements. For psychiatry, confirm medical school, psychiatry residency, and physician licensure with the program and the state medical board.

## Ask where the time goes

Ask the assessment team how many evaluations and reports fit into a week and who reviews complex cases. Ask the psychiatry team how the schedule splits across new visits, follow-up, and inpatient work and who covers urgent referrals.

## What carries over between paths

Interview summaries, careful reports, and coordinated referrals can demonstrate transferable skills. A move into clinical neuropsychology calls for supervised assessment training. A move into psychiatry calls for medical training, residency, and physician licensure.

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use fictional referral J-04 (the patient reports losing track of multistep handoffs at work, and the sleep history is pending) and summary N-04 (in a simulated handoff, the patient filled in one omitted item after a step-by-step prompt). Spend ten minutes separating self-report, observed behavior, and missing information. Spend ten minutes writing an assessment handoff that states what the observation covers. Spend ten minutes drafting questions for the psychiatry follow-up. Mark who signs each output and which output documents performance versus the next follow-up decision.
