# Recycling Coordinators vs Refuse and Recyclable Material Collectors

## Manage recycling service or complete collection stops

Recycling coordinators plan service, track issues, and work with crews and customers. Refuse and recyclable material collectors complete assigned stops and record conditions that affect collection. The coordinator needs reliable route information to decide what to follow up on.

## Compare the assignments

| Item | Recycling coordinators | Refuse and recyclable material collectors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Example setting | City or company recycling program | Collection yard and assigned routes |
| Main work product | Schedule and follow-up on service problems | Collected material and route exception record |
| Key question | What intervention gets service back on track? | What was collected and what prevented a stop? |

The wage series on the O*NET coordinator page covers a broader supervisory occupation. [O*NET coordinators](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-1042.01) · [O*NET collectors](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-7081.00)

## A service complaint and an unfinished stop

New York City’s sanitation department publishes missed-collection guidance and provides a 311 reporting route. In a work example using that setting, a collector reports that a stop could not be serviced and records the reason. A coordinator checks the published schedule and the route record before asking dispatch to confirm a return window. The authorized team assigns the follow-up, and collection staff record the outcome. [DSNY collection guidance](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dsny/collection/residents.page)

## Check the U.S. assignment before choosing training

For coordination roles, build experience with route records, crew schedules and service requests. Ask whether the posting calls for program management or supervisory experience. For collection roles, ask about ride-along training, lifting and equipment duties, and whether you drive. For covered commercial driving, match the CDL class to the assigned vehicle and verify state licensing requirements. A qualifying heavy single vehicle generally falls under Class B. [O*NET coordinator tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-1042.01) · [O*NET collector tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-7081.00) · [FMCSA license classes](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/registration/commercial-drivers-license/drivers)

## Ask how a typical shift works

Collectors spend time on routes handling materials and working around vehicles. Ask about start times, lifting and adverse-weather procedures. Coordination involves schedules and follow-up with crews. Ask how often the work includes field visits, how much time goes to service calls, and who takes over open cases after a shift. [O*NET collector work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-7081.00#WorkContext) · [O*NET coordinator work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-1042.01#WorkContext)

## Build a sample for the other side of the handoff

A collector moving toward coordination can turn a fictional route exception into a service plan with a named owner, then learn crew and customer coordination. A coordinator moving toward collection can show how to read a route plan and record exceptions, then obtain hands-on training and verify any driving requirements.

## Try a 30-minute missed-stop exercise

Use fictional records. Route R shows A collected and B blocked. Site note P says B opens at 2 p.m. Dispatch card D has a crew available at 3 p.m. Spend eight minutes classifying the records, twelve drafting a collector report or a coordinator’s follow-up, and ten checking it. B stays open, 3 p.m. is a proposed return, and the note identifies who confirms and records completion.
