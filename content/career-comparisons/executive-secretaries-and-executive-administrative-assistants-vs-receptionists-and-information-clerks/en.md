# Executive Administrative Assistant vs. Receptionist: An Early Arrival Before a Review Meeting

## What each role delivers

An executive administrative assistant keeps a meeting ready for an executive and its participants. A receptionist or information clerk checks why a visitor has arrived and gets the visitor to the right person or place. Both may field calls and maintain appointments.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Executive Secretaries and Executive Administrative Assistants | Receptionists and Information Clerks |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Coordinated calendar, agenda and briefing materials | Visitor intake, host notification and directions |
| Decision inputs | Attendee availability, priorities and delegated authority | Appointment record, site procedure and host confirmation |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $76,590 | $38,010 |

## How the work meets

At a fictional company, a guest arrives 40 minutes before a review and requests time with the director. Reception verifies the booking, offers a waiting location and forwards the request. The assistant sees that the director is occupied and a required attendee is available only at the scheduled time. The assistant keeps the meeting in place and checks whether the project contact can greet the guest. Reception relays the confirmed plan.

## How to enter

BLS lists high school completion or equivalent as typical entry education for both occupations. It lists less than five years of related experience for executive support, and no related experience plus short-term on-the-job training for reception. For executive support, bring a calendar conflict you resolved and a sample briefing. For reception, walk through handling an early guest while the phone rings. [BLS Table 1.2](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Reception's attention can shift between visitors, deliveries and calls in a single minute. Executive support may involve long stretches of coordination interrupted by priority changes. Ask how front-desk coverage works and who can approve an executive calendar change.

## Moving between roles

Receptionists moving into executive support can build on intake and follow-up, then practice managing dependencies and controlled documents. Executive assistants moving into reception can use host coordination skills and learn visitor-volume routines and site procedures.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Make three mock notes: a guest arrives early, the director is still in a meeting, and finance can attend only at the original time. Spend ten minutes drafting the visitor response, ten drafting the assistant's coordination note and ten checking who confirms the plan and tells the guest.
