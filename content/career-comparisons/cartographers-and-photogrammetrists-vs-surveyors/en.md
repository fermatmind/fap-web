# Cartographers and Photogrammetrists vs. Surveyors: When a Mapped Line and a Survey Differ

## What each role delivers

Cartographers and photogrammetrists compile sources and produce maps. Surveyors conduct and verify measurements and prepare boundary records. Both use spatial data, and assignments can overlap.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Cartographers and Photogrammetrists | Surveyors |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Map and source record | Boundary survey and report |
| Basis | Base map, features and source version | Boundary evidence, deeds and measurements |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $81,390 | $75,440 |

## How the work meets

A fictional park map shows a line that differs from survey records. The cartographer documents the map source and flags the conflict. The surveyor compares property and field records before the team decides how to represent the line.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for both and internship or residency training for surveyors. Surveyors need a license to certify legal documents or provide surveying services to the public. Some mapping assignments may also require licensure; check the state board for the actual scope. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) [BLS surveyor licensing](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/surveyors.htm) [BLS cartography licensing](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/cartographers-and-photogrammetrists.htm)

## Work conditions

Map work can follow feature and version reviews. Survey work may combine field measurements, calculations and property records. Ask who signs formal deliverables.

## Moving between roles

Moving to surveying, add field practice, boundary evidence and the relevant licensing path to spatial data skills. Moving to mapping, bring accuracy and documentation and learn map representation and inspection.
