# Payroll and Timekeeping Clerks vs Tax Preparers

## Pay employees accurately or prepare a client's return

Payroll and timekeeping clerks verify hours, pay adjustments, and deductions for a pay cycle. Tax preparers gather and check a taxpayer's materials and prepare a return under the relevant filing rules. A wage statement can be used in both jobs. Payroll owns the employer's source records, while the preparer works from the client's filing evidence.

## How a pay record correction reaches the return

In this fictional U.S. case, a September payroll report and a draft year-end wage reconciliation both have a field for the employee's September gross pay before deductions. The payroll report shows $5,000, including an approved $200 adjustment. The draft still has the prior $4,800 value. The payroll clerk locates the adjustment and routes the discrepancy to the employer's designated approver to check whether the year-end statement reflects the change. The employee's tax preparer logs the discrepancy, requests a confirmed statement and records which document supports the return.

## U.S. entry requirements and who holds the obligation

BLS identifies a high school diploma or equivalent and moderate-term on-the-job training as typical for both occupations. In payroll, expect to learn the system, approvals, and which records support hours and wages. FLSA recordkeeping duties apply to covered employers for covered nonexempt workers. For paid preparation of all or substantially all of a federal return, IRS rules generally require a PTIN, with listed exceptions. State rules can add requirements. For example, Oregon’s standard license pathway for preparing personal income tax returns includes an approved 80-hour course and a state exam. A licensed tax preparer must work under qualified supervision. Check the state board for the route that applies. ## Ask about payroll cutoffs and filing deadlines

Ask a payroll manager how many pay groups you would cover, which adjustments arrive after cutoff, and who checks them. Ask a preparer supervisor how hours and caseload change during filing season, who contacts clients about missing records, and when a draft must reach review.

## Use reconciliation experience when you switch

Moving from payroll to return preparation, bring a fictional reconciliation that traces a wage correction to its source. Learn how to gather client materials and document filing positions, then verify any PTIN or state licensing requirements for the work. Moving into payroll, bring a fictional missing-document log and learn timekeeping controls, pay codes, and the employer's approval procedures. [IRS PTIN guidance](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/frequently-asked-questions-do-i-need-a-ptin) · [Oregon exam requirements](https://www.oregon.gov/obtp/pages/examrequirements.aspx)

## Try a 30-minute records exercise

Use the fictional September records. The $5,000 gross pay in payroll includes the approved $200 adjustment. The draft year-end reconciliation still uses $4,800 for September gross pay. There is no confirmation that the wage statement reflects the adjustment. Take eight minutes to label the records and their versions, twelve to draft either an internal payroll reconciliation or a document request to the client, and ten to check the result. The reconciliation should trace the difference and identify the employer approver. The request should identify the missing confirmation and leave the return item open.
