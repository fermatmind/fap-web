# Occupational Therapists vs. Occupational Therapy Aides: Who Sets the Treatment Goal?

## Who makes the decision

An occupational therapist evaluates what makes daily activities difficult, sets goals, and reviews progress. An occupational therapy aide supports the service through tasks such as preparing equipment, keeping supplies stocked, and scheduling.

## Compare the work

| Compare | Occupational therapist | Occupational therapy aide |
|---|---|---|
| Main deliverable | Evaluation and treatment plan | Prepared workspace and equipment, with issues reported |

## In the same outpatient visit

Imagine a client wants to put on a jacket without help. The therapist reviews which part of the task is difficult and decides what to observe this visit. An aide prepares a jacket and worksheet, spots a loose button, and reports the problem. The aide sets out the backup the therapist has approved. The therapist decides what the observation means for progress.

## Getting started in the U.S.

BLS lists a master's degree in occupational therapy as typical for therapists and says every state requires therapist licensure. Aides typically enter with a high school diploma or equivalent and on-the-job training. Check state and employer rules on delegated work. In California, for example, equipment setup is a non-client-related task. The board says aides must demonstrate competence for assigned tasks and that the occupational therapist is responsible for their overall supervision. ## Working conditions

Therapists balance client visits, team discussions, and documentation. Aides may reset equipment between visits and flag missing supplies. Ask how much time staff have between visits and who handles equipment problems.

## Moving between roles

Aides can bring familiarity with clinic schedules, equipment, and handoffs into professional study and fieldwork. They still need to meet the applicable education and licensure requirements before working as therapists. A therapist considering a support role can use their service experience and check which tasks the employer assigns to that role.

## Try both in 30 minutes

Use three fictional notes: the client wants to put on a jacket independently, the last evaluation noted a pause at the second sleeve, and the usual jacket has a loose button with a spare in the cabinet. Spend 10 minutes assigning review of the evaluation, 10 drafting an aide's setup checklist, and 10 writing a handoff that flags the equipment issue for the therapist.
