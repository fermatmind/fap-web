# Postsecondary Education Teachers vs. English and Literature Teachers: Two Uses of a Reading

## What each role delivers

Postsecondary education teachers teach instructional methods and assess work such as lesson plans. Postsecondary English language and literature teachers teach literature, language or writing and respond to textual analysis and papers.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Education Teachers, Postsecondary | English Language and Literature Teachers, Postsecondary |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Methods course and teaching feedback | Literature coursework and paper feedback |
| Basis | Learning goals and instruction evidence | Textual and writing evidence |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $75,350 | $78,760 |

## How the work meets

Two fictional courses use the same short reading. The education instructor grades a discussion plan and checks what would reveal student understanding. The literature instructor assesses how a paper uses passages to explain narrative point of view.

## How to enter

BLS lists a doctoral or professional degree as typical for both occupations; its education teacher row also lists less than five years of related experience. Review each department’s teaching and research expectations. [BLS occupation rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Both positions include coursework, grading and sometimes research. Education work may include field placements; English teaching may involve several writing revisions. Ask how each kind of supervision counts.

## Moving between roles

To move into English teaching, build on course design and deepen disciplinary research, close reading and writing feedback. To move into education, bring discussion and writing examples and add curriculum evaluation.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use a fictional class note: students summarize events but cite no passage when explaining point of view. Spend ten minutes on an education observation task, ten writing a literary analysis task, and ten distinguishing their rubrics.
