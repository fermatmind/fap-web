# Floor Layers vs. Terrazzo Workers and Finishers: Matching a Sheet Seam to a Divider

## What each role delivers

In this example, floor layers cut and join sheet coverings. Terrazzo workers and finishers place material in panels and check dividers and the finished surface.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Floor Layers, Except Carpet, Wood, and Hard Tiles | Terrazzo Workers and Finishers |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Sheet covering and joint record | Panel, divider and finish checks |
| Basis | Substrate, sheet joints and edges | Divider strips, panel drawing and finish |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $56,460 | $76,170 |

National medians; local and starting pay vary. [BLS Table 1.2, rows 47-2042 and 47-2053](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## How the work meets

Sheet flooring in a fictional reading room meets a terrazzo entrance. The floor layer reviews the sheet seam. The terrazzo worker checks divider locations. Their layouts differ at the transition and they send the two records to the site lead.

## How to enter

BLS lists no formal educational credential and moderate-term on-the-job training for this floor-laying occupation. It lists high school or equivalent and apprenticeship for terrazzo workers and finishers. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Floor layers follow substrate readiness and access. Terrazzo work may be staged around panel placement and surface finishing. Ask who accepts the transition.

## Moving between roles

Moving to terrazzo, bring substrate and measurement skills and learn panel layouts and surface finishing. Moving to sheet floors, use layout checks and learn cutting and joints.
