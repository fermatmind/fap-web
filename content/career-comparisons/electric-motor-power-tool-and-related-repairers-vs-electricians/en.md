# Electric Motor, Power Tool, and Related Repairers vs Electricians

## Equipment work and system work

Electric motor and power tool repairers service motors, tools, and related components. Electricians install, maintain, and repair electrical systems at buildings or facilities. Both may check wiring or devices. The job assignment determines what each person documents and who reviews the result.

## Outputs, U.S. preparation, and pay

| Item | Electric motor and power tool repairers | Electricians |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main output | Device repair and verification record | Site electrical work and acceptance record |
| Typical setting | Repair bench or equipment service site | Buildings, construction sites or facilities |
| Typical entry education | High school diploma or equivalent | High school diploma or equivalent |
| Typical training | Moderate-term on-the-job training | Apprenticeship |
| Typical related work experience in BLS data | Less than five years | None |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $56,210 | $63,190 |

National medians are for wage and salary workers and exclude self-employment. ## The shop report arrives first

At a fictional facility, work order M-63 says motor A-63 was repaired and its shop verification record was signed. Site order E-63 says the field check for the same unit is pending. The repairer sends the equipment ID, work order and check scope. The electrician links the site order to the unit and sends the field check and any open items to the designated reviewer. Under this facility's rules, the reviewer records whether the unit can be accepted after both records are complete.

## Training and authorization

Ask motor repair employers which motors, tools, or switches they assign and what related experience and supervised training they expect. For electrician work, compare apprenticeship programs and check the state or local licensing authority for the intended work. Ask the employer what tasks require supervision and who signs the site records. [BLS electrician entry guide](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/electricians.htm#tab-4)

## What to ask about the workweek

Ask a repair shop how it prioritizes devices waiting for parts or verification. Ask an electrical team about jobsite travel, assignment handoffs, and who is responsible for acceptance.

## What carries across

A fictional traceable work order can show skills that both roles use. Moving toward site electrical work calls for system training and the applicable license or authorization. Moving toward motor repair calls for training on the assigned equipment and its verification and recordkeeping.

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use M-63 and E-63, plus fictional update U-63: the repair record is filed and the field check remains unsigned. Spend ten minutes separating confirmed and open items, ten minutes writing the shop handoff and site follow-up, and ten minutes writing the reviewer’s checklist. Stay with the example records and decisions.
