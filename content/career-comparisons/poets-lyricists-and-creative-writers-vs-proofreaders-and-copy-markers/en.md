# Poets, Lyricists and Creative Writers vs. Proofreaders and Copy Markers: A Line Break Changes in Proof

## What each role delivers

Poets, lyricists and creative writers create and revise text. Proofreaders and copy markers compare proofs with source copy, flag errors and recheck corrections.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Poets, Lyricists and Creative Writers | Proofreaders and Copy Markers |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Author copy and revision decisions | Marked proof and recheck log |
| Basis | Meaning, voice and deliberate usage | Approved text, style rules and layout errors |

## How the work meets

A fictional poetry collection has a proof that wraps a long line and drops an intentional blank line. The proofreader logs both differences and asks how to handle the long line in a narrow layout. The writer confirms how the space and break should appear, and the proofreader checks the next proof.

## Work conditions

Writing may follow commissions and revision deadlines. Proofreading may follow typesetting and release schedules. Ask how approved copy is identified and author queries are resolved.

## Moving between roles

Moving into proofreading, bring language judgment and learn style comparison and proof tracking. Moving into writing, bring close reading and develop original structure, voice and revision skills.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock source: a blank line follows line two, and line three repeats a word deliberately. The proof removes both. Spend ten minutes each marking the differences, drafting an author confirmation and listing what to recheck.
