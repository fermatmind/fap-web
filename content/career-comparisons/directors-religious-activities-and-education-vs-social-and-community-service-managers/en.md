# Religious education director vs. social and community service manager

## Two kinds of programs to keep running

A religious education director plans what a congregation teaches and organizes the people who teach it. A social and community service manager runs services and coordinates the staff who deliver them. Both may manage schedules and volunteers. Look at what each role is accountable for when a schedule breaks.

## Compare the work products

| Work | Religious education director | Social and community service manager |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decide what to schedule | Class sequence, instructor coverage, and volunteer support | Staff coverage, appointment capacity, and service follow-up |
| Deliver | A teaching schedule and assigned instructors | A staffed service schedule and a record of what was delivered |

[O*NET education tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/21-2021.00#Tasks) · [BLS service management duties](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/social-and-community-service-managers.htm#tab-2)

## One room closes before Saturday

At a fictional organization, a congregation class and a community program use the same room at different times. Repairs close it on Saturday. The education director decides to move the advanced class to Sunday after confirming the instructor, then updates the class roster and notifies learners. The service manager moves appointments to an intake room with a separate waiting area and sets the number of slots based on staffing. Each signs off on their own schedule before the front desk updates directions.

## U.S. preparation and first roles

O*NET classifies religious education directors in Job Zone Four, indicating considerable preparation. Ask the hiring congregation about teaching experience, curriculum expectations, and who approves instructors. Coordinating classes or volunteers is one way to build relevant experience. BLS describes at least a bachelor's degree and related work experience as typical for community service managers. A program support role can give you experience scheduling staff and recording service outcomes. [O*NET Job Zone](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/21-2021.00#JobZone) · [BLS preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/social-and-community-service-managers.htm#tab-4)

## Ask about hours and work settings

For education work, ask which evenings or weekends have classes and who covers instructor absences. BLS lists offices, clinics, hospitals, and shelters among the settings for community service managers. Ask which service hours need a manager on site and who takes the call when capacity falls. [O*NET education tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/21-2021.00#Tasks) · [BLS work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/social-and-community-service-managers.htm#tab-3)

## Moving between the roles

Bring a class calendar and a volunteer coverage log from religious education to show how you can allocate people. For community service management, add experience planning service capacity, budgeting, and reviewing outcomes. In the other direction, bring a staffing roster and a notice template. Build experience sequencing lessons, selecting materials, and working within the congregation's approval process for instruction.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a fictional closure. The instructor is available Sunday. The Saturday intake room has four staffed slots and six existing appointments. Spend ten minutes drafting a revised class schedule, ten allocating the four intake slots and marking two appointments for follow-up, and ten writing a handoff for the front desk. Mark who confirms each schedule and which messages still need to go out.
