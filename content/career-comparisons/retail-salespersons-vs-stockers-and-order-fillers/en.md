# Retail Salespersons vs. Stockers and Order Fillers When Inventory Is Reserved

## Start with what you deliver

Retail salespersons help shoppers make a choice. Stockers and order fillers handle physical counts, replenishment, and picking. A reservation can make a product visible on the shelf but unavailable for another order.

## Compare the handoffs

| Work product | Retail Salespersons | Stockers and Order Fillers |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Customer options and a purchase or follow-up record | Count, reservation status, and replenishment note |

## Follow one order

A fictional shopper wants two lamps in the same color. The salesperson confirms the needed color and use. The stocker finds one reserved for pickup and one available. The salesperson can offer one now, check a future delivery, or discuss a different product without assigning reserved inventory.

## How to prepare

O*NET places both roles in Job Zone 1–2. For sales, practice identifying customer needs and explaining alternatives. For stock work, look for receiving and picking assignments and learn how the site tracks reservations. Ask which tasks a new hire performs first. ## Work conditions

Sales can be paced by customer traffic. Stock work can involve walking, handling merchandise, and meeting order deadlines. Ask which teams cover both activities during busy periods.

## Move between the roles

To move toward sales, practice using verified stock information to explain suitable options. To move toward picking, build experience with locations, reservations and count checks.

## Try it in 30 minutes

A fictional customer needs two white lamps now. Two are on the shelf, but one is reserved. Three gray lamps are available. More white ones might come tomorrow, but delivery is unconfirmed. Spend ten minutes on the inventory handoff, ten writing a customer response, and ten noting what you cannot promise.
