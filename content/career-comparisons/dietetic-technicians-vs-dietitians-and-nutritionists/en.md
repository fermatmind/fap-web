# Dietetic Technicians vs Dietitians and Nutritionists

## Implementing a plan and deciding on a change

Dietetic technicians support food service and nutrition programs under dietitian supervision. They may check meals against established instructions and document intake or exceptions. Dietitians and nutritionists assess needs, plan nutrition services, and evaluate outcomes. Both can work with menus and records.

## Work, U.S. entry, and pay

| Item | Dietetic technicians | Dietitians and nutritionists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | Meal checks, intake notes, and exception records | Assessment, nutrition plan, and review decision |
| Setting examples | Institutional food service or nutrition services | Health care, community services, or food service management |
| Typical entry education in BLS occupation data | Associate degree | Bachelor's degree |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $37,640 | $76,400 |

The BLS figures are national medians for wage and salary workers, excluding self-employment. ## A replacement is available and still needs review

At a fictional U.S. facility, approved plan P-09 is active and lists menu item A. Kitchen list K-09 shows A is unavailable and substitute B awaits approval. The technician records the shortage, sends the plan reference and B's product details to the supervising dietitian, and leaves the change request pending. The dietitian reviews the material against the plan and sends a documented decision back. Under this facility's rules, the technician updates the meal order after receiving approval.

## Education, credentials, and state rules

Ask the technician employer whether it requires the Nutrition and Dietetics Technician, Registered (NDTR) credential. One CDR route to exam eligibility combines an associate degree with an ACEND-accredited technician program and at least 450 hours of supervised practice. CDR also lists a bachelor's route with an accredited didactic program. For RD/RDN exam eligibility, CDR requires a graduate degree plus ACEND-accredited coursework and supervised practice. The BLS bachelor's classification covers the broader occupation. Check the state's rules for the work you intend to do. [CDR technician exam eligibility](https://www.cdrnet.org/NDTReligibility) · [CDR dietitian exam eligibility](https://www.cdrnet.org/RDNeligibility)

## What to ask about workload

Ask a technician team how many orders it checks before service and what happens when items run out. Ask a dietitian team how quickly it reviews exceptions, who supplies the product information, and how it documents decisions.

## Moving between the roles

Technicians can bring a fictional or approved example of a meal-service exception and build assessment and planning skills. Dietitians and nutritionists moving into a technical service role can bring menu and communication experience and learn the order system and production checks.

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use P-09 and K-09, plus fictional update M-09: B has arrived, and the dietitian's review is still pending. Spend ten minutes recording the three states, ten minutes drafting the technician's request, and ten minutes writing the next record for both possible outcomes: approval or review still pending. Check that the record distinguishes stock, approval, and service, and assign each action to an owner.
