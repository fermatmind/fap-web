# School and Career Counselor vs. Mental Health Counselor

## What each counselor works on

Educational, Guidance, and Career Counselors and Advisors work with students and other clients on choices such as registration and career planning. In a school setting, that can include identifying problems that interfere with participation. Mental Health Counselors develop treatment plans and keep confidential records of progress.

## Compare the deliverables

| Decision | Educational, guidance, and career counselor | Mental health counselor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First conversation | Identify course constraints and deadlines | Explore patterns of distress and their effect on daily functioning |
| Record to revisit | Options, referrals and an advising follow-up | Treatment goals and a progress note |
| Typical coordination | Student services, registrars and academic staff | Clinical supervisors and other authorized care staff |

## A withdrawal deadline and a referral

Imagine a student who has missed two required classes because of anxiety about speaking and has until Friday to withdraw. An advisor can check section openings and see how withdrawal would affect prerequisites. A mental health counselor can explore when the avoidance occurs and agree on what to review in the next session. For any coordination, the teams would first follow their respective policies on permission to share student or clinical information.

## U.S. education and credential pathways

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reports that school counselors typically need a master's degree and a state-issued credential. For a school job, check the state education agency and the district's posting. Some states license career counselors, so verify requirements for the particular setting. Mental health counselors typically need a master's degree and an internship. BLS reports that all states require mental health counselor licensure. Look up the state's requirements for clinical supervision, examination and work permitted while earning hours before enrolling.

## What changes with the setting

A campus role may put registration deadlines and appointments on the same calendar. A clinic may schedule recurring sessions, documentation and case supervision across the week. Ask the school how it covers registration peaks and ask the clinic how it allocates time for notes and consultation.

## Moving between the two jobs

An advisor can bring interviewing and referral experience into mental health training and should check which graduate courses and clinical placements satisfy the state's licensing rules. A mental health counselor can bring interviewing and documentation skills to advising, then learn credit rules and registration software. Check credential rules again if moving into a K–12 school counseling position.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a fictional student with two absences, a Friday withdrawal deadline and an open section that conflicts with an internship. In ten minutes, draft an advising note listing two options, who can verify each one and when to follow up. Spend ten minutes drafting two open questions for a first counseling conversation about where the avoidance happens and how it affects the student's week. Spend the final ten minutes marking which details belong on an advising referral and identifying whose permission would be needed to share more.
