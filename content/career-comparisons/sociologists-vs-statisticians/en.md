# Sociologist vs. Statistician: Explaining Differences in Community Participation

## What each role delivers

Sociologists study groups, institutions and social behavior and may design and analyze research. Statisticians evaluate evidence with sampling and models and explain the limits of estimates.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Sociologists | Statisticians |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Social explanation, supporting evidence and alternatives | Estimates, model checks and limitations |
| Basis | Local institutions, access, interviews and observations | Sampling frame, variable definitions and group sizes |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $106,030 | $105,650 |

## How the work meets

A fictional city studies participation in community events. Interviews suggest that tenants have less access to registration notices, so the sociologist checks communication channels. The statistician estimates participation by group checks differences in response rates and age composition, and reports how the estimate changes after adjustment. The report separates the plausible explanation from the statistical conclusions.

## How to enter

BLS lists a master’s degree as typical for both. Show a theory-based study or a reproducible analysis, according to the job. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Sociology projects may involve multiple rounds of interviews or observation. Statistical projects also follow data freezes and reviews. Ask how new evidence can be added.

## Moving between roles

Sociologists can bring question design and field context to statistics and add estimation and validation. Statisticians can bring methods to sociology and add social theory and research ethics.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock study: tenants in area A participate less. Areas differ in age composition, and interview records exist only for A. Spend ten minutes each on a hypothesis, limitations of the statistical comparison and what the evidence can support.
