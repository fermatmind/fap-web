# Office Machine Repairers vs. Office Machine Operators: Who Owns the Print Job?

## What each role delivers

Office machine operators in this example produce and check copies and assemblies. Repairers assess the equipment, perform assigned service and report the result.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Computer, Automated Teller, and Office Machine Repairers | Office Machine Operators, Except Computer |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Service record and machine status | Completed production job |
| Basis | Equipment condition and recheck | Quantity, version and assembly |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $47,810 | $40,960 |

## How the work meets

A fictional print room has two versions due. The operator finds offset output in the second batch and submits a sample with the work order. The repairer services the machine and reports the recheck. After handback, the operator checks the replacement output against version, quantity and finishing requirements.

## How to enter

BLS lists some college without a degree for the repair occupation and high school or equivalent for office machine operators. Both rows list short-term on-the-job training. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Operators work around batches and deadlines. Repair work follows service requests and parts availability. Ask how the team tracks rework and equipment return.

## Moving between roles

Operators can bring clear symptom and sample records into repair and then learn device servicing. Repairers moving to production need practice with batch control and output inspection.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use a fictional print order: version A has 40 accepted copies, and 6 of version B’s 20 copies are offset. Spend ten minutes on a service request, ten on return conditions, and ten on an output checklist. Use paper only.
