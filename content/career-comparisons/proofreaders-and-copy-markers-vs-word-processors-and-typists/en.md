# Proofreaders and Copy Markers vs Word Processors and Typists

## Checking the proof and producing the document

Proofreaders and copy markers compare a proof with the source and flag errors or unresolved questions. Word processors and typists turn drafts and instructions into formatted files, check their work, and route or store the result. The jobs overlap in accuracy checks. Look at whether the position mainly owns the error list or the editable file.

## One missing caption and a date nobody has approved

In a fictional publisher's workflow, a proofreader finds a missing caption and a date that differs between the contents page and the body. The proofreader marks the caption against approved copy and asks the editor to resolve the conflicting dates. The word processor restores the authorized caption using the correct style and logs the date as an open question. The editor confirms the date in writing. The word processor applies it and returns the revised file for the proofreader to verify before the production lead approves release.

## Prepare a work sample for the U.S. role

BLS lists a bachelor's degree as typical entry education for proofreaders and copy markers. For word processors and typists it lists a high school diploma or equivalent and short-term on-the-job training as typical. For a proofreader opening, ask which style guide and markup tools are used, and bring a proof that separates corrections from queries. For document production, bring a before-and-after file built with named styles and ask which templates and document checks you'll learn on the job. [BLS entry and training data](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Ask what the deadline controls

In proofreading, ask how close to release new corrections are accepted and who answers questions when source copies disagree. In document production, ask how a queue is prioritized, who supplies the current template, and who verifies the file sent for approval. Ask for one ordinary shift and one deadline-day example.

## Use the work you already do to switch roles

A proofreader moving into document production can show clear decisions recorded on a markup, then demonstrate building a styled document and saving retrievable revisions. A word processor moving into proofreading can show a document comparison, then practice checking against the approved source and using the team's style guide to distinguish direct corrections from questions for the editor.

## Try a 30-minute proof handoff

Make a one-page fictional proof with a missing approved caption and two dates that disagree between the contents page and the body. Spend eight minutes matching the proof against the approved caption and identifying the date conflict. Spend twelve preparing either a proof mark with a question for the editor or a revised editable file with the approved caption and a new version number. Spend ten checking that the caption has a source and the date is still flagged for decision. Write down which handoff you would like to repeat.
