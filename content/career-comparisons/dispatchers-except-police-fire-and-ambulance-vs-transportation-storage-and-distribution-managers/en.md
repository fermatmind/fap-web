# Dispatchers (Except Police, Fire and Ambulance) vs. Transportation, Storage and Distribution Managers When a Vehicle Drops Out

## Two levels of planning

Dispatchers match individual jobs with available crews and vehicles. Distribution managers look across receiving, staff and outbound work to set priorities when resources run short. A change to one vehicle can affect both levels of planning.

## Compare the handoffs

| Handoff | Dispatchers (Except Police, Fire and Ambulance) | Transportation, Storage and Distribution Managers |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Updated run, contact and acknowledgment | Staffing and capacity options across the shift |

## A truck is unavailable

In a fictional fleet, one truck becomes unavailable while two customers expect deliveries. The dispatcher checks feasible arrivals and records who has received the change. The manager looks at goods waiting to load, occupied space and the effect of missing each window, then decides or escalates priorities under the employer's rules. The dispatcher updates the run after that decision.

## How to prepare

O*NET places dispatchers in Job Zone 1–2 and distribution managers in Job Zone 4. Explore dispatch through scheduling or fleet support. Explore management through receiving, inventory flow and team coordination. Ask what supervisory experience and authority the employer expects. [Dispatch preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-5032.00#JobZone) · [Distribution preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-3071.00#JobZone)

## Work conditions

Dispatch is paced by calls, arrivals and changed assignments. A distribution manager may coordinate several shifts and keep an eye on warehouse space and deadlines. Ask what the dispatcher can resolve alone.

## Moving between roles

Move toward management by adding workload and capacity decisions to your run history. Move toward dispatch by turning priorities into assignments and checking that the people involved receive the same update.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use two fictional orders that each need one vehicle. A has a 2:30 deadline and takes 35 minutes to reach. B has a 3:00 deadline and takes 25 minutes. Only one vehicle is free at 2, and no return trip can serve the other order in time. Spend ten minutes checking arrival, fifteen outlining a priority and backup question, and five listing who can approve a reschedule.
