# Atmospheric and Space Scientists vs. Remote Sensing Scientists and Technologists: Reading the Same Cloud Image

## What each role delivers

Atmospheric and space scientists use observations and models to study weather and atmospheric processes. Remote sensing scientists and technologists analyze sensor data, review quality and coverage and integrate other geospatial sources.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Atmospheric and Space Scientists | Remote Sensing Scientists and Technologists |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Atmospheric analysis and forecast | Remote sensing result with quality notes |
| Basis | Models, observations and assumptions | Acquisition time, quality and coverage |

## How the work meets

A fictional research team gets two images an hour apart. The remote sensing specialist flags the missing area in the second image and supplies the comparable coverage. The atmospheric scientist uses surface observations and models to distinguish movement from an apparent change caused by missing coverage.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for atmospheric and space scientists. A bachelor’s degree was also the most frequent O*NET education response for remote sensing scientists and technologists. Ask whether the assignment emphasizes research, forecasting or geospatial analysis and prepare a small public-data project in that area. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) [O*NET education](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-2099.01#Education)

## Work conditions

Atmospheric work may follow observation and forecast cycles. Remote sensing analysis may follow image arrivals, coverage and quality checks. Ask about shift, research and production duties.

## Moving between roles

Moving into remote sensing, bring physical and observation knowledge and add imagery and quality review. Moving into atmospheric work, bring geospatial analysis and develop meteorology, modeling and uncertainty interpretation.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use mock records: both A and B are visible at 9 a.m. At 10 a.m. only A is visible and its cloud band moved east. B has one surface observation at 9:30. Spend ten minutes each on image coverage, atmospheric interpretation and what evidence is still needed for B.
