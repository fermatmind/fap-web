# Kindergarten Special Education Teachers vs. Special Education Teaching Assistants

## What each role delivers

Kindergarten special education teachers design and deliver adapted instruction and monitor progress. Special education teaching assistants support participation and classroom routines under assigned direction.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Special Education Teachers, Kindergarten | Teaching Assistants, Special Education |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Adapted instruction and progress reports | Assigned support and observations |
| Basis | Learning goals and team decisions | Teacher direction and classroom routines |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $65,120 | $36,780 |

## How the work meets

A fictional class uses an existing picture prompt during transitions. The assistant records that the learner moved on after two prompts and shares the observation with the teacher. The teacher reviews patterns and uses the team process for any plan-level change.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree for its combined kindergarten and elementary special education teacher row. Its broader non-postsecondary teaching assistant row lists some college without a degree. Under IDEA, appropriately trained and supervised assistants may help provide services in line with applicable state rules or written policy. Check the school role and state requirements. [IDEA 34 CFR 300.156](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/b/300.156) [BLS occupation rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Teachers track instructional goals and team decisions alongside class routines. Assistants focus on assigned activities and observations. Ask who supervises support and where records go.

## Moving between roles

Moving from assistant to teacher, add special education preparation, assessment and the applicable credential. Moving to an assistant role, use your classroom experience while working within the assigned support and reporting scope.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use two fictional observations from an existing picture-prompt routine. Spend ten minutes on an assistant note, ten on a teacher review question, and ten identifying who can see the notes and who decides changes.
