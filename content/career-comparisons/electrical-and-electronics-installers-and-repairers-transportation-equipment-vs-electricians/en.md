# Electrical and Electronics Installers and Repairers, Transportation Equipment vs Electricians

## Mobile electronics and facility electrical systems

Transportation equipment electrical and electronics workers install and maintain communications, navigation and other mobile electronics on platforms such as trains and vessels. Electricians install, maintain and repair wiring, lighting and power systems in facilities and other settings. The two roles can work at the same location on different systems.

## Work, preparation, and U.S. pay

## Two closeouts at one ferry operator

A fictional ferry operator schedules work on its onboard communications terminal and dock workshop lighting. The fleet team needs a record that the terminal can complete a call with the shore station before departure. The facilities team needs a record that the installed lights match approved fixture locations. The transportation electronics worker coordinates the call check with the shore team and documents the result. The electrician records any differences between the installed locations and the drawing for the facilities lead. Dispatch updates each ticket after approval from its lead.

## Check training and assignment scope

For transportation electronics, compare relevant postsecondary certificate programs and ask which platforms and systems the employer services. For electrician jobs, check apprenticeship options and verify licensing requirements with the authority responsible for the intended state or locality. Ask the employer what work is supervised. [BLS electrician entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/electricians.htm#tab-4)

## Ask how work fits the schedule

Vessel work can depend on a short berth window and access to the shore counterpart for a check. Ask how those slots are coordinated. Facility work can share space with other trades. Ask how drawing changes and return visits are scheduled.

## Where to start when switching

Moving into facility electrical work, use a fictional verification report to show documentation skills. Under supervision, start with comparing installed fixture locations with a drawing, and learn facility drawings, code requirements and licensing. Moving into vessel electronics, use a fictional site discrepancy report to show traceability. Start with supervised terminal functional checks and learn vessel interfaces, shore communications and berth coordination.

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use fictional F-74 (terminal F9 passed its local check; no shore call confirmation), E-74 (approved drawing C identifies fixture position L2; installation log shows L3), and U-74 (fleet requires a shore call record; facilities requires matching locations or an approved written change). Spend ten minutes classifying the evidence, ten minutes drafting the shore coordination request and fixture discrepancy note, and ten minutes identifying what each lead still needs before acceptance. Use only the records.
