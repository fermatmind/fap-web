# Postsecondary Family and Consumer Sciences Teachers vs. Instructional Coordinators: Revising a Budget Case

## What each role delivers

Postsecondary family and consumer sciences teachers teach and assess disciplinary work. Instructional coordinators compare implementation, review resources and support teachers.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Family and Consumer Sciences Teachers, Postsecondary | Instructional Coordinators |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Course and project assessment | Materials review and staff training |
| Basis | Disciplinary evidence | Implementation across classes |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $75,870 | $77,440 |

## How the work meets

Three fictional classes use a budget case. One omits recurring costs in many submissions. The instructor gives students feedback. The coordinator finds that one worksheet lacks a field and proposes a revision to the shared version.

## How to enter

BLS lists a doctoral or professional degree as typical for the postsecondary teacher occupation and a master’s degree and five or more years of related experience for instructional coordinators. Verify institutional and, for school assignments, applicable state requirements. [BLS occupation rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Faculty work follows the term. Coordinators alternate among review, observation and staff sessions. Ask who approves revisions to a shared resource.

## Moving between roles

Faculty can bring classroom evidence into coordination and add implementation analysis and facilitation. Coordinators moving to teaching need discipline preparation and practice with coursework and assessment.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use three fictional worksheets. Only the form for Class B lacks a recurring-cost field. Spend ten minutes drafting student feedback, ten writing a revision proposal, and ten identifying how to verify both outcomes.
