# Bioinformatics Scientist vs. Biostatistician

## Reproducible inputs and a defensible comparison

A bioinformatics scientist may build computational methods and preserve the history of the data they process. A biostatistician may write the statistical analysis plan, define what the study compares, and explain uncertainty. Both can contribute code and analysis.

## Compare the work products

| Work | Bioinformatics scientist | Biostatistician |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First check | Sample IDs, input versions, and processing records | Units of comparison, repeated observations, and missing data |
| Deliverable | A reproducible dataset, image measurement method, and source notes | A statistical plan and limits on interpretation |

## One plant appears twice in the table

A fictional project compares leaf area across two groups of potted plants. The bioinformatics scientist checks the scale markers in the leaf images, chooses a method to put the measurements in a common unit, and flags images without a usable scale. The resulting table retains two measurements from plant P01 on different days. The biostatistician treats the plant as the unit of comparison, distinguishes repeated observations from separate plants, and checks which days have observations in both groups.

## U.S. education and early projects

O*NET places both occupations in Job Zone Five, which commonly involves graduate preparation. For bioinformatics, try a supervised project that preserves the history of published teaching data and lets someone reproduce the result. For biostatistics, try a research or coursework project that specifies the comparison, handles repeated observations, and explains uncertainty. Check degree and subject requirements for the specific opening. [Bioinformatics preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-1029.01#JobZone) · [Biostatistics preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-2041.01#JobZone)

## Compare how the work is scheduled

Both O*NET profiles include indoor work. On a project like this one, bioinformatics work can include reviewing scale markers with the imaging team and checking a workflow before releasing a table. Biostatistics work can include planning meetings where the team agrees on the comparison and later reviews the limits of the results. Ask who reviews data and code before release, when the statistician joins study planning, and who explains the results to the investigators. [Bioinformatics work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-1029.01#WorkContext) · [Biostatistics work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-2041.01#WorkContext)

## Moving between the roles

A bioinformatics scientist can bring coding and data review into biostatistics, then build skills in study design, inference, and explaining uncertainty. A biostatistician can bring modeling and reproducibility into bioinformatics, then learn the structure, metadata, and versioning of biological data.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use three fictional rows: P01, Group A, Day 1; P01, Group A, Day 8; P02, Group B, Day 1. Spend ten minutes distinguishing row count from plant count, ten explaining why the two P01 rows should remain, and ten listing information you need before comparing groups on Day 8.
