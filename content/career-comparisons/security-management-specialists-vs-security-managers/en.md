# Security Management Specialists vs. Security Managers: Who Acts on a Drill Finding?

## What each role delivers

A security management specialist assesses physical security risks, recommends improvements, and may verify measures. A security manager coordinates operations, procedures, staff, and budgets. The employer assigns approval authority.

## Compare the work

| Compare | Security management specialist | Security manager |
|---|---|---|
| Focus here | Whether shifts pass along the same information | How to schedule a change, training, and follow-up |
| Output | Findings, recommendation, and verification criteria | Owner, resources, deadline, and review plan |

## The same drill review

Imagine a building runs a tabletop drill. The specialist sees one shift records when a notice was sent and the next records whether it was acknowledged, with neither entry showing both and proposes common handoff fields. The manager checks the time needed with shift and training leads and assigns one shift to try the form. Using the same fictional case, the specialist checks whether the new record communicates progress consistently. The manager reviews the result before deciding whether to expand the change.

## Getting started in the U.S.

O*NET places both occupations in Job Zone Four, which indicates considerable preparation. For a specialist opening, show how you tied a finding to evidence and defined a verification check. For a manager opening, show how you staffed and tracked a change. Ask the employer what authority and qualifications apply. [Specialist Job Zone](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/13-1199.07#JobZone) · [Manager Job Zone](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-3013.01#JobZone)

## Working conditions

Specialists may need time to review records and write findings. Managers also coordinate shifts, training, and budgets. Ask who can schedule a pilot and how the team makes time to review the results.

## Moving between roles

Specialists can build on risk assessment and verification by learning staffing and budgeting. Managers can bring operations knowledge into specialist work and practice gathering evidence, comparing options, and documenting verification criteria.

## Try both in 30 minutes

Use these fictional notes. Day shift writes “notice sent,” night shift writes “receipt unconfirmed,” a form can separate those two statuses, and only one shift is available for a pilot. Spend 10 minutes describing the finding and how to verify a fix, 10 assigning a pilot owner and resources, and 10 drafting a progress update.
