# Electrical and Electronic Equipment Assemblers vs. Timing Device Assemblers and Adjusters: The Timing Module Check

## What each role delivers

Electrical and electronic equipment assemblers install parts and check wiring or circuits. Timing device assemblers and adjusters assemble movements and check fit and accuracy.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Electrical and Electronic Equipment Assemblers | Timing Device Assemblers and Adjusters |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Electrical assembly and inspection record | Timepiece assembly and rate check |
| Basis | Components, wiring and contacts | Movement fit and timing accuracy |

## How the work meets

A fictional instrument’s timing module powers on but runs outside its tolerance. The electrical assembler returns the connection check. The timing adjuster records the movement ID, rate check and item to retest.

## How to enter

For electrical assembly, show a wiring and inspection record. For timing device work, show precision assembly and timing checks. Ask which mechanisms, training and inspections the assignment covers.

## Work conditions

Electrical assembly follows parts and connection checks. Timing device work uses close observation of small components and repeat checks. Ask what the employer expects at the bench.

## Moving between roles

Moving to timing devices, add movement fit and rate checks to drawing and inspection skills. Moving to electrical assembly, add wiring diagrams and electrical testing to precision assembly skills.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use a mock record: wiring and power pass, the limit is 5 seconds per day and the movement loses 12 seconds over 24 hours. Spend ten minutes each on the electrical record, timing exception and the retest needed before release.
