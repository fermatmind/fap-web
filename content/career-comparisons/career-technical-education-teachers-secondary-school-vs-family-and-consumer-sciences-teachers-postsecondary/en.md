# High-School CTE Teachers vs. Postsecondary Family and Consumer Sciences Teachers

## What each role delivers

High-school CTE teachers teach occupational skills and assess how students carry out projects. Postsecondary family and consumer sciences teachers build disciplinary courses and grade analyses or projects.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Career/Technical Education Teachers, Secondary School | Family and Consumer Sciences Teachers, Postsecondary |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Vocational project and process assessment | Case analysis and disciplinary feedback |
| Focus | Roles and communication | Comparable costs and household context |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $66,270 | $75,870 |

## How the work meets

A fictional high-school group compares lunch purchasing options. The teacher assesses team roles, price explanation and communication. The college instructor uses the same topic to ask students to account for household constraints, explain comparable costs and state the limits of their advice.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree and less than five years of related experience as typical for secondary school CTE teachers. For family and consumer sciences teachers, postsecondary, its occupation row lists a doctoral or professional degree as typical. California’s Designated Subjects CTE credential is one example of authorization for named vocational subjects through grade 12. Check the state and school assignment. [California CTC CTE authorization](https://www.ctc.ca.gov/credentials/leaflets/cl-888/) [BLS occupation rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

High-school teaching follows class periods and project milestones. College work may include longer grading, project supervision or laboratory instruction. Ask which subjects and assignments the position covers.

## Moving between roles

Moving into college, bring a documented teaching project and build disciplinary inquiry and written assessment. Moving to high school, adapt the case into a practical task and develop classroom instruction and authorization.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use two fictional households. One shops weekly and one can shop twice. Package sizes differ. Take ten minutes to draft a high-school team rubric, ten to write a college analysis assignment and ten to identify what evidence each would collect.
