# Customer Service Representative vs. Executive Administrative Assistant: Escalating a Meeting Request

## What each role delivers

A customer service representative receives requests, follows cases and updates customers. Executive secretaries and administrative assistants maintain executive schedules, prepare documents and coordinate meetings.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Customer Service Representatives | Executive Secretaries and Executive Administrative Assistants |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Service case, progress notes and follow-up | Confirmed schedule, attendee details and meeting materials |
| Basis | Order or service record, authority and deadlines | Executive calendar, priorities and material access |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $44,770 | $76,590 |

## How the work meets

A fictional equipment supplier receives a request to meet a business lead about a delayed delivery. The representative confirms the current service status and records the customer’s meeting topics. The executive assistant checks the calendar and attendees, then sends the confirmed time and agenda items to the representative for the reply.

## How to enter

BLS lists high school completion or equivalent as typical for both. It lists short-term on-the-job training for customer service representatives and less than five years of related experience for executive secretaries and executive administrative assistants. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Customer service can follow queues and response deadlines. Executive support follows calendars, materials and urgent meetings. Ask how a schedule change reaches the service case.

## Moving between roles

Representatives can bring communication into executive support and add calendar coordination, meeting materials and discretion. Assistants can bring coordination into service and add order systems and queue handling.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock file: the customer requests Wednesday morning. The executive has a slot at 10, but the technical colleague is free starting at 10:30. Spend ten minutes each on an escalation note, availability and approval checks, and an update the customer can receive now.
