# Brokerage Clerks vs. Credit Analysts: A Posting Discrepancy in a Credit File

## What each role delivers

Brokerage clerks document securities transactions and investigate account questions. Credit analysts evaluate borrower information and prepare a risk assessment for the designated decision-maker.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Brokerage Clerks | Credit Analysts |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Transaction records and discrepancy notes | Credit analysis for decision review |
| Basis | Execution, posting and source records | Statements, cash flow and credit policy |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $65,750 | $83,510 |

## How the work meets

A fictional lender receives an authorized securities activity statement from a business borrower. The redemption confirmation and day-end posting differ. The clerk checks the transaction and posting times through authorized channels and sends the outstanding difference and checked sources to the authorized credit analyst. The analyst flags that additional evidence is needed before treating the proceeds as available cash and routes the issue for review.

## How to enter

BLS lists high school completion or equivalent and moderate-term on-the-job training for brokerage clerks and a bachelor’s degree as typical for credit analysts. Ask whether duties are account operations or credit analysis, and verify any registration requirements for the assigned work. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Brokerage operations may follow transaction confirmation and posting schedules. Credit work may follow application documentation and reviews. Ask how unresolved differences reach the analyst.

## Moving between roles

Moving into credit, use reconciliation and source tracking skills and learn cash-flow and financial statement analysis. Moving into brokerage operations, add transaction cycles and exception handling to data verification experience.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock records show a $100,000 redemption confirmation, an $80,000 day-end posting and no settlement date. Spend ten minutes each on an exception note, missing credit evidence and a list of questions and recipients.
