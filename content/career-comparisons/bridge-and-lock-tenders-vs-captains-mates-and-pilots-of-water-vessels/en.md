# Bridge and Lock Tender vs. Vessel Captain: Who Confirms Readiness to Enter a Lock?

## What each role delivers

A bridge or lock tender operates a fixed facility, monitors passage and reports its status. Captains, mates and pilots contribute to command, watchkeeping or pilotage according to their assignments. The bridge team checks its own vessel's readiness and the information it receives from the facility.

## How the work meets

At a fictional lock, the previous vessel has departed and the next one requests passage. The tender still needs to check the facility, so sends a wait message and records when to update the vessel. The mate logs it and reports the pending status to the captain. When the tender sends the confirmed update under the lock's procedure, the bridge checks its own readiness. An assigned pilot can contribute local navigation information.

## How to enter

BLS lists high school completion or equivalent and short-term on-the-job training for bridge and lock tenders. It lists a postsecondary nondegree award as typical entry education for the combined vessel captains, mates and pilots group. ## Work conditions

Tender shifts revolve around a facility, traffic and maintenance interruptions. Bridge schedules follow the vessel, voyage and watches. Ask which incomplete checks must carry over between shifts.

## Moving between roles

Radio communication and accurate handoff logs transfer. Moving toward locks, build facility-specific knowledge and learn how the operator records passage. Moving toward vessel work, verify the role and credential and build experience in bridge watches and route information.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use three mock entries: the last vessel has left, the lock check is incomplete and the next vessel asks for an estimate. Spend ten minutes writing the tender's update, ten writing the bridge handoff and ten marking who owns each open item. Work from the entries only.
