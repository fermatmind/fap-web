# Surveying and Mapping Technicians vs. Traffic Technicians: Measuring a Redesigned Intersection

## What each role delivers

Surveying and mapping technicians locate physical features and maintain map records. Traffic technicians measure traffic and delays and compile study records for review.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Surveying and Mapping Technicians | Traffic Technicians |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Measured curbs, island ends and markings | Traffic counts and queue records by direction |
| Basis | Locations, coordinates and survey date | Count locations, intervals and direction |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $54,240 | $59,090 |

## How the work meets

A fictional intersection has just gained a longer turn lane. The survey and mapping technician records the new markings and curb locations. The traffic technician finds that the previous morning count used the old layout, retains it as a baseline and schedules a comparable turning count.

## How to enter

BLS lists a high school diploma or equivalent and moderate-term on-the-job training for both occupations. Ask employers how staff learn instrument and mapping work or how they conduct and review traffic counts. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Survey work may follow construction access and site schedules. Traffic studies may use specific peak and off-peak windows. Ask how the team dates records when a layout changes.

## Moving between roles

To move into traffic studies, add vehicle classification and timed sampling to field and location skills. To move into surveying, use roadway layout knowledge and add instruments, coordinate records and map review.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock records say markings changed Monday, the count is from last Friday and a Wednesday photo shows one queue. Spend ten minutes each on the map update, a new observation sheet and what the available records can support.
