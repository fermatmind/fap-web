# Captains, Mates, and Pilots of Water Vessels vs Commercial Pilot

## Three vessel roles and one aviation occupation

Captains command a vessel, mates supervise deck work and stand watch, and vessel pilots guide ships in designated waters. Commercial pilots fly aircraft on assignments such as charter or aerial work. Each crew has its own navigation and handoff records.

## One island delivery, two operating decisions

A fictional organization sends equipment by vessel and technicians by charter flight. A port notice changes the pilot meeting location. The vessel pilot confirms the notice and relays it to the captain, and the mate updates the onboard contact summary. The commercial pilot finds that the charter order and confirmed airport record name different destinations and requests clarification from operations. Each operator confirms its own readiness under its rules. The customer coordinator records arrival plans.

## Training and credentials

Choose the vessel role first. Verify with the U.S. Coast Guard which Merchant Mariner Credential endorsements, training, and sea service apply. Ask the relevant authority about route-specific pilotage requirements. For commercial flying, verify the FAA certificate, flight experience, medical eligibility, and knowledge and practical tests, then check aircraft ratings and operator requirements. [BLS maritime entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/transportation-and-material-moving/water-transportation-occupations.htm#tab-4) · [BLS pilot entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/transportation-and-material-moving/airline-and-commercial-pilots.htm#tab-4) [U.S. Coast Guard credential checklists](https://www.dco.uscg.mil/nmc/checklist/) · [14 CFR § 61.123 commercial pilot eligibility](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-61/subpart-F/section-61.123)

## Ask what time away looks like

Ask maritime employers how long voyages and watch rotations last, or how port pilot standby is assigned. Ask charter employers about base assignments, schedule changes, and overnights.

## What transfers and where to start

A maritime worker can bring fictional voyage and handoff records to show information review and start with approved flight training for the intended certificate. A pilot can bring a fictional flight preparation summary and start with supervised deck training and sea service for a target maritime role.

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use a different fictional batch. M-31 has an updated pilot meeting notice and an older version in the vessel summary. A-31 names the confirmed airport but has no confirmation from the air operator. Spend ten minutes identifying the two gaps, ten minutes drafting separate requests for the captain and the aviation coordinator, and ten minutes writing a customer update naming both owners and unresolved items. Work from the records only.
