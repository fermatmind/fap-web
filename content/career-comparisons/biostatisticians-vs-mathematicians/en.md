# Biostatistician vs. Mathematician: When an Estimator Meets Missing Follow-Up

## What each role delivers

Biostatisticians define comparisons, prepare analysis plans and interpret results with study teams. Mathematicians derive relationships from definitions and assumptions and check boundary cases. Both can study methods.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Biostatisticians | Mathematician |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Study comparison, analysis plan and limitations | Derivation, assumptions and counterexamples |
| Basis | Groups, outcome, follow-up and missing records | Definitions, assumptions and boundary cases |

## How the work meets

A fictional rehabilitation class study compares weeks attended in two groups. The mathematician tests a property of an estimator with complete data and shows why the property might fail with incomplete records. The biostatistician checks follow-up gaps and canceled sessions against the study question and documents which comparisons need extra assumptions.

## How to enter

BLS lists a master’s degree as typical for mathematicians. A biostatistics candidate can show study design, analysis plans and checks; a mathematics candidate can show a derivation or counterexample. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Biostatistics may follow protocols, data freezes and reporting. Mathematics projects may follow multiple rounds of derivation and review. Ask how a revised assumption reaches collaborators.

## Moving between roles

Biostatisticians can bring applied methods into mathematics and add proof and theory. Mathematicians can bring models to biostatistics and add study design, data provenance and interpretation.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock file: one participant in group A is missing the final week and two in group B are missing middle weeks. The method is stated only for complete records. Spend ten minutes each on the mathematical conditions, study evidence to check and what comparisons can be reported.
