# Craft Artist vs. Fine Artist: Approving the Image and the Finished Object

## What each role delivers

Craft artists make functional or decorative objects. Fine artists use visual elements to express ideas and may also make physical objects. A commission may call for separate approvals of the image and the finished handmade piece.

## How the work meets

A fictional bookstore commissions a set of textile wall hangings. The illustrator develops a seasonal motif and palette. The craft artist makes a full-size sample and sees that a seam crosses the motif. They move it off the seam and check the revised artwork and sample separately. The store uses the revised sample to confirm the quantity and delivery date.

## How to enter

BLS lists no formal educational credential as typical for craft artists and a bachelor’s degree for fine artists. It lists long-term on-the-job training for both. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Both roles may revise commissioned work. Craft work needs time for physical samples and fabrication; fine art projects may follow sketches, approval and work in the selected medium. Ask when the image and physical product will each be approved.

## Moving between roles

Craft artists moving into fine art can use material knowledge and develop composition, expression and a body of work. Fine artists bring sketching and visual judgment to craft and can add fabrication tolerances, joins and production records.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock brief: a 40-centimeter wall hanging needs a center seam, the current motif straddles it, and the first round includes one sample. Spend ten minutes each marking where the motif can move, listing material and seam constraints, and writing separate approval steps.
