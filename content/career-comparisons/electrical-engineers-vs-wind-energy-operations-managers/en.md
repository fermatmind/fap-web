# Electrical Engineers vs. Wind Energy Operations Managers: Two Turbines Are Down

## What each role delivers

Electrical engineers review electrical systems and equipment interfaces. Wind energy operations managers oversee maintenance, staff or contractors, and downtime records.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Electrical Engineers | Wind Energy Operations Managers |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Electrical scope and drawing review | Downtime priorities and site assignments |
| Basis | Equipment interfaces and electrical status | Site downtime, staffing and service windows |

[O*NET 17-2071.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/17-2071.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 11-9199.09](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-9199.09#Tasks)

O*NET uses the Managers, All Other wage series for 11-9199.09. A separate figure for this specialty cannot be derived from the broader series. [O*NET wage mapping](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/11-9199.09)

## How the work meets

Two turbines stop at a fictional site. The electrical engineer confirms they are on different collector branches and notes that log times have not been reconciled. The manager assigns separate follow-ups and makes log reconciliation a shared task.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for electrical engineers. In the O*NET wind operations survey, two-year degrees and bachelor’s degrees were the two most frequent responses. Ask how the employer weighs operating experience and management responsibility. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) [O*NET education](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-9199.09#Education)

## Work conditions

Electrical work may follow equipment events and design reviews. Site operations may coordinate dispatch across shifts, contractors and weather windows.

## Moving between roles

Moving into operations, build on systems knowledge and learn prioritization and staffing. Moving into engineering, build on asset records and develop design and verification skills.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use a mock card: W2 and W3 stop in one shift on branches A and B. W2 has a confirmed recheck window, W3 has no owner, and log clocks have not been checked. Spend ten minutes each on electrical follow-up, operations assignments and what prevents a shared cause finding.
