# Statistician vs. Survey Researcher: Reporting Results With Uneven Response

## What each role delivers

Statisticians design sampling or analysis methods and evaluate estimation error. Survey researchers develop questionnaires, coordinate recruitment and monitor responses, and may also analyze survey results. Both can contribute to sampling design.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Statisticians | Survey researchers |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Estimates, uncertainty and sensitivity checks | Questionnaire, response records and fieldwork deviations |
| Basis | Sampling frame, weights, missingness and target measure | Instrument, contact modes, recruitment and response patterns |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $105,650 | $69,460 |

## How the work meets

A fictional transit agency invites a random sample of riders to a survey. The survey researcher finds different response rates at the two stations and identifies extra responses from a public QR code. The statistician evaluates station coverage and nonresponse in the invited sample, summarizes the public responses separately and explains which conclusions depend on how the groups are handled.

## How to enter

BLS lists a master’s degree as typical for both. Statistics candidates can show reproducible estimation and checks. Survey candidates can show instrument design, sample documentation and fieldwork controls. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Survey research follows pretesting, collection and response monitoring. Statistics work also follows data freezes and reporting reviews. Ask who updates method records and labels when contact procedures change.

## Moving between roles

Statisticians can bring sampling and error analysis to survey work and add instrument design and field coordination. Survey researchers can bring measurement and nonresponse experience into statistics and add inference, weighting and sensitivity analysis.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock records: 50 randomly sampled riders were invited at each station. Verified invitees submitted ten responses from station A and 30 from station B. Keep the 20 public-code responses with unverified invitation status separate. Spend ten minutes each on a response-status table and station response rates, limits and weighting checks, and a statement suitable for publication.
