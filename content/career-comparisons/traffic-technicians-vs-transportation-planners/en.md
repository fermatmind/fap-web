# Traffic Technicians vs. Transportation Planners: Setting Priorities on a Bus Corridor

## What each role delivers

Traffic technicians collect and summarize observations under a study protocol. Transportation planners use operations data, travel needs and public input to compare options and recommend priorities.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Traffic Technicians | Transportation Planners |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Segment counts and timed delay observations | Corridor alternatives and priorities |
| Basis | Locations, intervals and count protocol | Travel needs, service coverage, policy and resources |

## How the work meets

A fictional city compares two sections of a bus corridor. The technician records bus passage times and roadway volumes during a school break and marks the dates. The planner reviews the results alongside passenger volumes, transfers and budget, then requests data from a school week before ranking the segments.

## How to enter

BLS lists high school or equivalent and moderate-term on-the-job training for traffic technicians. For transportation planners, 55% of U.S. O*NET education responses cited a bachelor’s degree and 45% cited a master’s degree. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) [O*NET education data 19-3099.01](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-3099.01#Education)

## Work conditions

Field studies may follow observation windows and reporting deadlines. Planning may include analysis, option reviews and feedback sessions. Ask which evidence needs to be ready for review.

## Moving between roles

Moving to planning, bring field observation and data quality skills and add needs analysis and option appraisal. Moving to traffic studies, use survey design knowledge and practice sampling, timing and record checks.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock records show higher car volume on segment A and more bus transfers on B. All observations were during a school break. Spend ten minutes each on the survey scope, measures for comparing options and the data you would collect during a school week.
