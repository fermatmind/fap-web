# Credit Analysts vs. Credit Authorizers, Checkers, and Clerks: Two Versions of an Income Record

## What each role delivers

Credit analysts evaluate financial information and prepare a risk assessment. Credit authorizers, checkers, and clerks may collect, verify or record information, with any approval duties defined by their employer.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Credit Analysts | Credit Authorizers, Checkers, and Clerks |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Risk assessment for decision review | Verified application records and discrepancies |
| Basis | Cash flow, financial statements and credit policy | Application fields, source and delegated authority |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $83,510 | $50,080 |

## How the work meets

A fictional borrower submits two records with different income amounts. The checker records their sources, dates and missing page and requests documentation through approved channels. The analyst compares recurring and one-time income, records assumptions and gaps and sends the assessment to the designated decision-maker.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for credit analysts and high school or equivalent with moderate-term on-the-job training for credit authorizers, checkers, and clerks. Ask who can verify records and who can approve credit. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Checking may follow a case queue and requests for missing pages. Analysis may follow financial review and approval meetings. Ask who closes discrepancies and how incomplete case files are routed.

## Moving between roles

Moving into analysis, bring source checks and develop financial statement, cash-flow and report writing skills. Moving into checking, bring risk awareness and add process, field validation and authorization records.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock records show $80,000 and $100,000 from records dated a month apart. The higher record is missing a page. Spend ten minutes each logging the source issue, outlining analysis implications and writing what must be obtained before review.
