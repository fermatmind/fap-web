# Cartographers and Photogrammetrists vs. Database Architects on a Map of Two Parks

## Different questions on the same data

Cartographers and photogrammetrists decide how to represent places and boundaries from spatial evidence. Database architects decide how information about those places is structured so applications can query it. Two parks can share a display name and still need separate records.

## Compare outputs

| Handoff | Cartographers and Photogrammetrists | Database Architects |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Park layer with source, boundary and labeling notes | Place model, boundary versions and query assumptions |

## One project, two handoffs

A fictional city has two parks with the same name. One expanded last year. The map team checks which boundaries the sources support and labels the two locations so readers can distinguish them. The architect separates the park identifier from the label and describes how boundary versions attach to each place. The mapping team supplies example questions about past boundaries, and the architect checks that the model can answer them.

## How to prepare

BLS describes a bachelor’s degree in cartography, geography, geomatics, surveying or a related field as typical for map work. Look for supervised work reviewing source imagery and preparing layers. For regulated work, verify the specific duties with your state board. Oregon’s board, for example, provides a professional photogrammetrist registration route. BLS also describes a related bachelor’s degree as typical for database architects. Look for database development or data modeling work where you can practice designing relationships and reviewing changes. [BLS mapping](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/cartographers-and-photogrammetrists.htm#tab-4) · [Oregon registration guidance](https://www.oregon.gov/osbeels/obtaining/pages/default.aspx) · [BLS database architecture](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/database-administrators.htm#tab-4)

Use the two parks to show how a map communicates duplicate names and how the model stores boundary history.

## What to ask before applying

Mapping can involve desk-based imagery review and some field verification. Architecture work can be organized around model reviews and the schedules of applications that depend on the data. Ask map teams who reviews published labels and boundaries. Ask architecture teams who sets retention and versioning rules and how they coordinate changes across applications.

## Skills that transfer

Move from mapping toward architecture by formalizing features and their history in a model. Move toward cartography by learning what scale and spatial evidence mean for a map reader.

## A 30-minute exercise

Two fictional parks share a name. East Park is P1. Its 2024 boundary reached the bridge and its 2025 boundary reaches the station. West Park is P2 and its boundary did not change. Spend fifteen minutes sketching and labeling a 2024 map with source dates, then fifteen designing how to retrieve the boundary of P1 as of December 2024.
