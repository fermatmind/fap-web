# Acupuncturists vs. Physical Therapists: What Does Each Record at Follow-Up?

## What each role evaluates

An acupuncturist takes a history, develops an acupuncture treatment plan, and reviews the person's response. A physical therapist evaluates movement and physical function and develops and reviews a physical therapy plan.

## Compare the work

| Compare | Acupuncturist | Physical therapist |
|---|---|---|
| Focus here | Symptoms and response discussed at follow-up | Standing from a chair under documented conditions |
| Output | Treatment record and follow-up questions | Functional assessment and reassessment plan |

[O*NET 29-1291.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-1291.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 29-1123.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-1123.00#Tasks)

## The same daily goal

Imagine a client wants to stand up more easily after prolonged sitting. The acupuncturist documents when the client reports discomfort and how it has changed. With permission, the acupuncturist sends a brief summary to the physical therapist. The therapist records the chair, assistance, and any pauses during an observed sit-to-stand task and shares the authorized findings for follow-up. Both records preserve the conditions under which the information was obtained.

## Getting started in the U.S.

Check the state where you plan to work. California requires a state acupuncture license and its licensing exam. To qualify for the exam, applicants can document Board-approved education and clinical training, a Board-approved tutorial, or qualifying education and clinical experience outside the United States. Physical therapists entering the occupation need a Doctor of Physical Therapy degree, and all states require licensure. [California Acupuncture Board exam requirements](https://www.acupuncture.ca.gov/students/exam_require.shtml) · [BLS physical therapy entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/physical-therapists.htm#tab-4)

## Working conditions

Acupuncture work includes appointments, documentation, and follow-up conversations. Physical therapy may require space to observe movement and time for reassessment. Ask how the teams exchange authorized findings.

## Moving between roles

Patient interviewing and clinical handoffs carry over. Acupuncture and physical therapy have separate education, clinical training, and licensing paths. Verify requirements for the destination role.

## Try both in 30 minutes

Use fictional notes. The client reports shorter periods of discomfort after sitting. They stand independently from a chair with armrests, and a chair without armrests has yet to be assessed. The client agrees to share a symptom and mobility summary. Spend 10 minutes on the acupuncture follow-up note, 10 on the functional findings and reassessment question, and 10 drafting the handoff.
