# Subway and Streetcar Operators vs. Traffic Technicians: Investigating Delays at a Crossing

## What each role delivers

Subway and streetcar operators run rail transit vehicles and report operating issues. Traffic technicians collect and summarize data on volume, speed and delay. This example concerns a streetcar at a surface crossing.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Subway and Streetcar Operators | Traffic Technicians |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Trip and incident log | Timed roadway study records |
| Basis | Vehicle, trip and dispatch logs | Location, direction, volume and delay |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $86,380 | $59,090 |

## How the work meets

A fictional streetcar arrives late. The operator records the affected trip, intersection and time and reports it to dispatch. The technician observes the same period on several days and separately records streetcar passage times and turning-vehicle queues for review.

## How to enter

BLS lists a high school diploma or equivalent and moderate-term on-the-job training for both occupations. For operator work, check the actual agency’s training and qualification requirements. For technician work, look for supervised counting and data review. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Operators work to route and shift assignments. Study work may include peak-hour field observation and time at a desk. Ask when the fieldwork takes place and who reviews the logs.

## Moving between roles

Operators can bring route and incident experience to traffic studies and add counting and sampling skills. Technicians can bring traffic observation skills to transit and complete the operator’s required training.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock records show a three-minute delay at 8:10 a.m. Tuesday, no delay Wednesday and no Tuesday turning counts. Spend ten minutes each on a trip log, survey fields and assumptions still to test.
