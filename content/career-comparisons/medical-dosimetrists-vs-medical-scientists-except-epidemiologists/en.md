# Medical Dosimetrists vs Medical Scientists, Except Epidemiologists

## Compare a clinical plan with a research conclusion

Medical dosimetrists prepare patient-specific radiation treatment plans based on the radiation oncologist's prescribed course and coordinate review with the clinical team. Medical scientists design studies, interpret evidence and explain what the data support. The work can overlap in a cancer center, with separate clinical and research deliverables.

## Follow a version discrepancy in radiation oncology

A ClinicalTrials.gov study illustrates how researchers can relate patient-reported outcomes to dosimetric factors. At a fictional center, the dosimetrist records that plan B is pending review and hands it to the medical physicist for technical review. The radiation oncologist gives clinical approval before the center uses the revised plan for treatment. The scientist sees that a research table points to plan A, flags the discrepancy, and asks the authorized data steward to confirm the approved research record. The investigator applies the study protocol to the verified record. The patient's plan stays in the clinical review workflow. ## Check training and credential requirements

For the U.S. CMD exam route, MDCB lists a bachelor's degree and graduation from a JRCERT-accredited medical dosimetry program lasting at least 12 months. BLS says employers usually require certification. For medical scientists, BLS lists a doctoral or professional degree as typical. Ask about the research methods, publications or clinical qualifications expected for the specific assignment. [MDCB eligibility](https://www.mdcb.org/certification-exam-information/eligibility) · [BLS dosimetry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/medical-dosimetrists.htm) · [BLS medical scientists](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/life-physical-and-social-science/medical-scientists.htm)

## Read national pay beside the work rhythm

May 2025 U.S. median annual wages were $147,470 for medical dosimetrists and $103,410 for medical scientists, except epidemiologists. Ask a clinic how plan review deadlines affect the week, and ask a research group how study milestones and funding shape the job. Both roles value traceable records. A move into research adds study design and analysis, while a move into clinical planning requires checking the program and credentials. [BLS national wage table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Try a 30-minute record handoff

Take eight minutes to review two fictional cards: clinical plan B is pending review, and study row 14 points to plan A without a verified source. Spend 12 minutes writing separate follow-up notes for the clinical reviewer and the research data steward. Use the last ten minutes to check whether a colleague can identify what is uncertain and who owns each next step. Work from the mock cards only.

## Ask which deliverable you would want to own

Ask a dosimetrist what sends a plan back for review. Ask a scientist what makes a finding ready to share. Compare the examples and identify the kind of review and collaboration you would rather do regularly.
