# Artist Agent vs. Producer or Director: What Changes When a Performer Has Half a Day?

## What each role delivers

Agents and business managers represent clients in engagement discussions and scheduling. Producers coordinate a project's resources and schedule. Directors shape the work on set and guide its creative execution. Production duties can overlap.

## How the work meets

A fictional documentary has one day for an interview and pickup shots. The featured performer is now free only in the morning. The agent confirms that window and flags an additional recording request as open. The producer checks crew and location availability, and the director identifies the scenes that must be shot with the performer. The agent confirms the revised scope with the performer, and production updates the schedule.

## Work conditions

Agency work can follow several client schedules and rounds of negotiation. Production follows crew, location and shoot constraints. Ask who confirms a change and how the team records its effects.

## Moving between roles

Agents can bring scheduling and client communication to production and learn crew planning, budgets and production workflow. Producers or directors moving into agency work can bring project experience and learn client mandates and contract discussions.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use mock records: the performer is free only in the morning, the location is booked for the afternoon and an extra recording is unconfirmed. Spend ten minutes writing the agent's reply, ten outlining production questions and ten assigning owners to each open decision.
