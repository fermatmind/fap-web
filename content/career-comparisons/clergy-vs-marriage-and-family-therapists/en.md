# Clergy vs. Marriage and Family Therapist

## Spiritual care and treatment focused on relationships

Clergy may lead religious services and offer spiritual guidance. Marriage and family therapists provide therapy to individuals, couples, or families with attention to relationships. A family can seek both kinds of support for different reasons.

## Compare the work products

| Work | Clergy | Marriage and family therapist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First check | The spiritual support a family is seeking | The relationship concerns the family wants to address |
| Deliverable | A plan for spiritual support or referrals | Treatment goals and clinical follow-up |

U.S. median annual wages in May 2025 were $60,810 for clergy and $66,940 for marriage and family therapists. These national OEWS medians cover wage and salary jobs and exclude self-employment. ## A family asks for two kinds of support after moving

A fictional family wants to continue a religious observance after moving. They also report arguments whenever they discuss packing responsibilities. A clergy member asks which parts of the observance the family wants to keep and how they want to participate. A therapist works with the family to identify a relationship goal and how to review it. If the family requests a referral, the clergy member can offer contact information and let the family decide whether to use it.

## U.S. entry routes

O*NET places clergy in Job Zone Five, a broad category for extensive preparation. Ask the religious body about its training, supervised service, and appointment or ordination pathway for the position. BLS describes a master’s degree as typical and licensure as required in every state for marriage and family therapists. Check the applicable state board for education, supervised experience, and exams. New York’s requirements are one example. [O*NET clergy preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/21-2011.00#JobZone) · [BLS family therapy preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/community-and-social-service/marriage-and-family-therapists.htm#tab-4) · [New York licensure requirements](https://www.op.nysed.gov/professions/marriage-and-family-therapists/license-requirements)

## Compare settings and schedules

Clergy work can involve public events and face-to-face conversations. Marriage and family therapists may work by appointment in mental health centers or private practice. Ask how religious services and community visits shape the schedule, or how a therapy position allocates time for sessions, supervision, and records. [O*NET clergy work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/21-2011.00#WorkContext) · [BLS family therapy work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/community-and-social-service/marriage-and-family-therapists.htm#tab-3)

## Moving between the roles

Spiritual care experience can bring listening and community support skills to therapy training, where supervised clinical work and state requirements still apply. A therapist can bring communication and service coordination skills into religious work, then prepare under the relevant religious body’s pathway.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use two fictional notes from a family. One says they want to keep an observance they practiced before moving. The other says arguments start when they divide packing tasks. Spend ten minutes identifying the two requests, ten writing a clarifying question for each, and ten drafting a sentence offering a referral the family can choose whether to accept.
