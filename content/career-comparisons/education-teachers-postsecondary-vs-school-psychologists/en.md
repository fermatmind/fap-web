# Postsecondary Education Teachers vs. School Psychologists: How Each Uses Classroom Records

## What each role delivers

Postsecondary education teachers teach methods and assess coursework. School psychologists conduct school-based evaluations, interpret findings and work with school teams on student support.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Education Teachers, Postsecondary | School Psychologists |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Course design and assessment | School evaluation and support recommendations |
| Basis | Course goals and student work | Protected records and team process |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $75,350 | $95,990 |

## How the work meets

In a fictional school–college project on classroom participation, a school psychologist routes a class-level summary without individual records through the school’s approval process. The education instructor uses the approved summary with teacher candidates to review instructional strategies. Candidate work stays in the course. The authorized school team handles individual records and support requests separately.

## How to enter

BLS lists a doctoral or professional degree and less than five years of related experience for postsecondary education teachers, and a master’s degree and internship or residency as typical for school psychologists. Check state credentials and approved preparation. California lists School Psychology as a separate PPS specialization. [California CTC](https://www.ctc.ca.gov/credentials/leaflets/cl-606c/) [BLS occupation rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Faculty work follows terms and grading. School psychologists coordinate referrals, evaluation timelines and meetings. Ask who can access records and who leads each handoff.

## Moving between roles

To move toward school psychology, pursue supervised school psychology preparation and state credentials. To move to teaching, use approved de-identified case material and build course design and assessment skills.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Read two fictional cards: one has class totals; the other simply says an individual support request was received. Spend ten minutes on a teaching prompt, ten on a records and authorization checklist, and ten on sharing limits. Do not conduct or interpret a psychological assessment.
