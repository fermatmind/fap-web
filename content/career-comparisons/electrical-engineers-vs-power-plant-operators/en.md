# Electrical Engineer vs. Power Plant Operator

## Design requirements and plant operating records

An electrical engineer may design plant electrical systems, write specifications, and define how to verify a change. A power plant operator monitors equipment and indicators and records what happens during a shift. Those observations can help an engineer check whether the conditions assumed in the design apply. Depending on the assigned duties, plant operators also control or adjust generating and auxiliary equipment.

## Compare the work products

| Work | Electrical engineer | Power plant operator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First check | Drawings, specifications, and test conditions | Equipment status, readings, and shift records |
| Deliverable | Technical findings and evidence needed for review | Confirmed observations and items for the next shift |
| Handoff | Requests evidence from operations or maintenance | Shares the shift record through plant procedures |

## Two shifts record different readings

At a fictional plant, readings for an auxiliary unit differ between two shifts. The operator assembles the readings, times, and recorded conditions and flags what remains unknown. The engineer checks whether the two readings describe comparable conditions and lists which measurement points and document versions need clarification. The review team has a record of what happened and what it still needs to know. The operator also gives the next shift a list of equipment states to confirm, while engineering reviews the technical question.

## U.S. education and workplace training

A related engineering bachelor’s degree is typical for electrical engineering, according to BLS. An internship or co-op can help you practice reading specifications and documenting verification. For the grouped occupations of power plant operators, distributors, and dispatchers, BLS describes high school or equivalent as the typical minimum and extensive on-the-job training after hire. Ask a plant about trainee openings, supervised work, and qualification for independent shifts. The BLS nuclear reactor operator licensing guidance applies to that separate occupation. [BLS engineering preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/electrical-and-electronics-engineers.htm#tab-4) · [BLS power plant group preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/production/power-plant-operators-distributors-and-dispatchers.htm#tab-4) Check state requirements and any employer qualifications for the specific plant, equipment, and duties.

## Ask about site work and shifts

Plant operators may split their time between control rooms and rounds in the plant and often work rotating shifts. Electrical engineers generally work in offices and may visit sites as projects require. Ask an engineering employer how often people go on site and who approves design changes. Ask a plant about shift rotation, training shifts, and who reviews the handoff when you are new. [BLS engineering work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/electrical-and-electronics-engineers.htm#tab-3) · [BLS comparison work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/production/power-plant-operators-distributors-and-dispatchers.htm#tab-3)

## Moving between the roles

An engineer can bring drawing and technical documentation skills into plant operations, then learn plant-specific procedures and complete its training. An operator can bring knowledge of actual plant conditions and careful shift records into engineering, then build experience with calculations, specifications, and design verification.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use two fictional shift entries. At 7 a.m., an auxiliary unit display shows 42 and the unit is recorded as running steadily. At 3 p.m., a display with the same label shows 49 and the status field is blank. A fictional specification says comparisons require the same measurement point revision and equipment state. Spend ten minutes identifying what is known, ten writing a handoff about the unit, and ten listing two items engineering would need to compare the readings.
