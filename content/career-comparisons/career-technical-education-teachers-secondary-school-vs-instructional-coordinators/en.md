# High-School CTE Teachers vs. Instructional Coordinators: Calibrating a Project Rubric

## What each role delivers

High-school CTE teachers teach technical projects and grade their students’ work. Instructional coordinators compare implementation, lead staff development and help align shared materials and expectations.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Career/Technical Education Teachers, Secondary School | Instructional Coordinators |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Class scores and project feedback | Cross-class calibration and version recommendation |
| Basis | Student work and course goals | Staff feedback and sample scoring |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $66,270 | $77,440 |

## How the work meets

Two fictional high-school CTE classes submit a cost report. The teachers interpret the evidence criterion differently. A teacher marks observable evidence in sample work; the coordinator schedules a blind scoring comparison and submits the findings and proposed change to the curriculum lead.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree and less than five years of related experience as typical for secondary school CTE teachers. For instructional coordinators, its occupation row lists a master’s degree and five years or more of related experience as typical. California’s Designated Subjects CTE credential is one example of authorization for named vocational subjects through grade 12. Check the state and school assignment. [California CTC CTE authorization](https://www.ctc.ca.gov/credentials/leaflets/cl-888/) [BLS occupation rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Teaching follows class and grading cycles. Coordination follows meetings, review and implementation. Ask which cohort uses a revised rubric and how the transition is documented.

## Moving between roles

Teachers moving to coordination can bring scoring examples and build facilitation across classes. Coordinators moving to teaching can use curriculum design experience and add technical lessons, classroom practice and credentials.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use three fictional submission notes: one lists sources and quantities, one gives a quote only, and one gives neither. Spend ten minutes scoring your class, ten designing a staff calibration, and ten naming the approval and start-date decisions.
