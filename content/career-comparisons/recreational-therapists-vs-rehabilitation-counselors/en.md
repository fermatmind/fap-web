# Recreational Therapists vs. Rehabilitation Counselors: Who Helps a Client Rejoin a Game Group?

## What each role delivers

A recreational therapist uses leisure activities in a treatment plan and documents participation and progress. A rehabilitation counselor works with clients on employment or independent living goals and coordinates access to services.

## The same community goal

Imagine a client wants to rejoin a community board game group. The recreational therapist observes the client taking turns after seeing a visual turn cue during a treatment session. The counselor checks registration, transportation, and the group's point of contact with the client. With the client's permission, the team shares the client's preference for seeing the turn rules in advance and schedules a check-in after the first visit.

## Getting started in the U.S.

Recreational therapists typically need a bachelor's degree. Many employers require certification, and a small number of states require licensure or certification. Rehabilitation counselors typically need a master's degree in rehabilitation counseling or a related field. Some jobs accept a bachelor's degree and some require a license or certification. [BLS recreational therapy entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/recreational-therapists.htm#tab-4) · [BLS rehabilitation counselor entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/community-and-social-service/rehabilitation-counselors.htm#tab-4)

## Working conditions

Therapists may set up and lead groups and then write session notes. Counselors coordinate with clients and community providers on different schedules. Ask who follows up after a first visit and how participation issues are reported.

## Moving between roles

Activity observation and communication carry over. Moving into recreational therapy takes training in recreation-based treatment and documentation. Moving into rehabilitation counseling takes training in referrals, coordination, and follow-up. Verify credentials for the destination role.

## Try both in 30 minutes

Use three fictional notes. The client takes two turns without a verbal prompt when a visual cue is available. The group meets at 2 p.m. Saturday and requires advance registration. The trip home is unconfirmed. Spend 10 minutes writing a treatment observation and a follow-up check, 10 listing referral questions, and 10 drafting information the client may agree to share with the group contact.
