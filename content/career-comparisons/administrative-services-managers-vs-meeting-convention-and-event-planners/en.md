# Administrative Services Managers vs. Meeting, Convention, and Event Planners During a Venue Change

## A continuing service and a fixed event

Administrative services managers coordinate the office space, people, and support services that staff share. Meeting, convention, and event planners turn an event goal into a schedule guests and suppliers can follow. Both work with calendars, but their deadlines answer different questions.

## Compare the commitments

| Work | Administrative Services Managers | Meeting, Convention, and Event Planners |
|---|---|---|
| First question | What office resources can the event use? | What do guests and presenters need, and when? |
| Example output | Space allocation and staff service notice | Run of show, check-in plan, and vendor handoffs |
| Afterward | Restore ordinary room availability and coverage | Close out supplier work and review event delivery |

U.S. occupational task references · [O*NET 11-3012.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-3012.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 13-1121.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/13-1121.00#Tasks)

## The main room changes the day before rehearsal

The administrative manager checks which everyday meetings would lose their rooms and arranges temporary space. The planner tests whether the new room can handle check-in and transitions, updates the event schedule, and tells vendors where to deliver. If the room cannot seat everyone, the organizer decides what to change using both sets of constraints.

## Build entry experience on the right side of the event

BLS describes a bachelor’s degree and related experience as typical for administrative services managers. [BLS occupational preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/administrative-services-managers.htm#tab-4) O*NET classifies meeting, convention, and event planners as work that generally requires considerable preparation. [O*NET Job Zone](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/13-1121.00#JobZone) Job Zones describe a broad level of preparation. Ask the employer which education, training and experience count for the specific job.

For office services, take on room or equipment scheduling and document how you resolved conflicting requests. For planning, help deliver a small event and keep the actual schedule, vendor list, and one change you handled. Ask the employer whether the event job covers planning, on-site delivery, or both.

## Ask when the work spills beyond the schedule

Office services continue during the event. Event planning can concentrate work around fixed guest and vendor times, especially when the venue changes. Ask the administrative team how quickly workspaces have to return to normal. Ask event planners about rehearsals, evening or weekend coverage, and who can approve a schedule change on the day. The answers will differ between in-house event teams and agencies.

## Turn one artifact into evidence for the other role

To move into planning, expand a room allocation into a guest and vendor timeline. To move into office services, turn your event checklist into a repeatable process that settles room conflicts and restores resources afterward.

## Try a 30-minute room change

Seventy guests are expected, the replacement hall seats sixty, and check-in stays at 9. A twelve-seat room could take overflow but has a department meeting at 9:30. Spend ten minutes listing office options, fifteen revising the event run sheet, and five identifying the decisions the organizer must approve.
