# Quarry Rock Splitters vs. Stonemasons: From Quarry Stock to a Garden Wall

## What each role delivers

Quarry rock splitters read grain and separate and mark rough stone. Stonemasons lay out, set and align stone pieces and finish the joints.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Rock Splitters, Quarry | Stonemasons |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Rough stone and quarry record | Set stone and inspected joints |
| Basis | Grain, blank ID and dimensions | Wall pattern, exposed face and joints |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $48,740 | $57,390 |

## How the work meets

A fictional garden wall receives two blanks in lot Q8. The quarry crew documents size and grain. The stonemason matches one blank to the front pattern and reserves the other for the side, subject to sample approval.

## How to enter

BLS lists no formal educational credential and short-term on-the-job training for quarry rock splitters. It lists high school or equivalent and apprenticeship for stonemasons. Ask how training covers stock selection or on-site setting. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Quarry work can follow extraction and material handoffs by lot. Masonry work follows wall sections, stone delivery and sample review. Ask who confirms stock labels and layout changes.

## Moving between roles

Moving to masonry, bring knowledge of grain and defects and learn wall layout and joint work. Moving to quarry work, bring measurement and stock selection skills and add site training and quarry records.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock card: the wall front requires horizontal grain, the side has no direction rule, Q8-A has horizontal grain and Q8-B has diagonal grain. Both sizes pass. Spend ten minutes each on the stock handoff, a stone layout and the sample approval owner.
