# Biologist vs. Soil and Plant Scientist

## Biological processes and soil or crop performance

Biologists can study how living systems work and develop ways to investigate them. Soil and plant scientists study soils, plant growth, and the effects of management practices. They may work on the same trial with different questions about what the results support.

## Compare the work products

| Work | Biologist | Soil and plant scientist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Check | Whether observations support an explanation of growth | Whether soil, crop, and management records can be matched by plot |
| Deliverable | Research explanation and evidence still needed | Plot-level findings and questions about management practices |

## Two plots have different emergence counts

A fictional trial site records emergence counts for two plots on different days. The biologist checks seed lots, days since planting, and the number of seeds observed. The soil and plant scientist identifies a missing week of irrigation records. They give the project team the conditions needed for a fair comparison and a list of missing field records.

## U.S. preparation and first projects

O*NET places biologists in Job Zone Five, its extensive-preparation category, which commonly includes graduate study. Start building research experience through a supervised project where you help record observations and compare results. BLS describes a bachelor’s degree as typical for the broader agricultural and food scientists group, with graduate study required for some jobs. For soil and plant work, look for field or lab projects that connect soil observations to plant growth. [O*NET biologist preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-1029.04#JobZone) · [BLS agricultural science preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/life-physical-and-social-science/agricultural-and-food-scientists.htm#tab-4) Some soil or plant science duties may involve state licensing. Check the services you would perform with the relevant state licensing authority. North Carolina, for example, defines regulated soil science practice and specifies exceptions. [North Carolina Soil Scientist Licensing Act](https://www.ncleg.gov/enactedlegislation/statutes/html/bychapter/chapter_89f.html)

## Compare the workplaces

Biology work can include indoor analysis and outdoor observation, depending on the project. BLS describes lab, office, and field work for agricultural and food scientists. Ask whether the position follows a field season, how much travel it involves, and who is responsible for analyzing and reporting data. [O*NET biologist work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-1029.04#WorkContext) · [BLS agricultural science work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/life-physical-and-social-science/agricultural-and-food-scientists.htm#tab-3)

## Moving between the roles

A biologist can bring study design and data interpretation into soil and plant science, then build knowledge of soil properties, crop management, and field schedules. A soil and plant scientist can bring experience comparing plots into broader biology research, then practice testing hypotheses that extend beyond a particular crop or site.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use fictional records from two plots planted from the same seed lot. The east plot has 12 emerged seedlings counted on Tuesday and no irrigation entry. The west plot has 20 counted on Thursday. Spend ten minutes listing confirmed facts, ten writing an explanation involving count timing and a question about management records, and ten drafting a note on comparability and a request for missing field information. Specify that comparing emergence proportions would require counts at the same number of days after planting and the number of seeds planted in each plot.
