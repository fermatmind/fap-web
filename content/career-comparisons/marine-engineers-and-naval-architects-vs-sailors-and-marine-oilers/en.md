# Marine Engineer vs. Sailor or Marine Oiler: How a Vibration Report Reaches the Design Team

## What each role delivers

Marine engineers and naval architects use specifications and trial data to design vessels or systems and document engineering findings. Sailors can handle deck and watch duties. Marine oilers may lubricate equipment, assist engineers aboard and record machinery observations.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Marine Engineers and Naval Architects | Sailors and Marine Oilers |
|---|---|---|
| Main output | Design or engineering analysis and trial report | Deck or engine-room support and a watch handoff |
| Evidence after an exception | Trial conditions, measurements and engineering basis | Time, location, observation and who was notified |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $112,230 | $51,520 |

## How the work meets

A fictional vessel vibrates intermittently on a sea trial. An oiler records when and where it was noticed and notifies the engine watch. A deck sailor records whether it was also observed on deck. The trial lead passes those records and the test conditions to the design engineer. The engineer compares their time stamps with measurements and reports what can be checked and what needs more evidence.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor's degree as typical entry education for marine engineers and naval architects. The combined sailors and marine oilers group lists no formal educational credential and moderate-term on-the-job training. Ask about the actual deck or engine-room assignment and any credentials or endorsements it requires. [BLS Table 1.2](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) Most mariners need a USCG-issued Merchant Mariner Credential (MMC). Check the Deck Ratings or Engine Ratings section of the Coast Guard qualification list for the endorsement and requirements that apply to the actual billet. [USCG qualification checklists](https://www.dco.uscg.mil/nmc/checklist/) · [BLS water transportation occupations](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/transportation-and-material-moving/water-transportation-occupations.htm)

## Work conditions

Engineering work can move between offices, shipyards and vessel trials. Deck and engine-room duties follow assigned watches and onboard conditions. Ask who preserves original observations and measurements after a trial.

## Moving between roles

Onboard work builds familiarity with how systems behave in service. Moving into design, add engineering analysis, drawings and test interpretation. Moving aboard from design, bring your ability to read plans and verify the credential, watch training and practical experience needed for the assignment.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use mock records: the engine room observes vibration at 2:15 p.m., the deck has a note at 2:17, and the trial has no measurement between 2:10 and 2:20. Spend ten minutes writing the handoff, ten comparing the times and ten stating what the engineer can support and what data are missing. Use the reports alone.
