# Occupational Therapists vs. Recreational Therapists: Who Does What in a Gardening Group?

## What each role is assessing

Occupational therapists assess the demands of daily activities and the conditions that affect performance. Recreational therapists plan treatment around leisure interests and therapeutic goals, then record participation and progress. Both can use gardening when it fits the client's goals.

## The same goal in the community

Imagine a client wants to return to a community gardening group. The occupational therapist sees that tools stored on a deep shelf need to be passed to the client and documents the shelf and work surface. The recreational therapist uses that information to plan a session around taking turns planting seeds and records whether the client joins voluntarily or after an invitation. They share the notes and review independent tool use and group participation separately next time.

## Getting started in the U.S.

Occupational therapists typically need a master's degree in occupational therapy and must be licensed in every state. Recreational therapists typically need a bachelor's degree. Many employers require certification, and a small number of states require a license or certification. Check the requirements in the state where you plan to work. [BLS occupational therapy entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/occupational-therapists.htm#tab-4) · [BLS recreational therapy entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/recreational-therapists.htm#tab-4)

## Working conditions

Occupational therapists may assess an individual in a clinic or simulated setting. Recreational therapists may lead groups at a facility or in the community. Ask who checks access when an activity changes venues and who arranges a follow-up if a session is canceled.

## Moving between roles

Activity planning and observation carry over. A move into occupational therapy calls for further training in activity analysis and environmental adaptations. A move into recreational therapy calls for training in leisure assessment and group intervention design. Check the destination role's education and credential requirements.

## Try both in 30 minutes

Use three fictional notes: a peer passes tools from a deep shelf, a work surface beside the client is clear, and the client joins the first planting round after an invitation and the second without one. Spend 10 minutes writing a task note, 10 documenting group participation, and 10 writing what each therapist would check in the next session.
