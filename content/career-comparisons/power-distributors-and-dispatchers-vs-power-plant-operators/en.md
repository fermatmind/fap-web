# Power Distributors and Dispatchers vs Power Plant Operators

## Coordinate the system or run the generating unit

Dispatchers coordinate power flows and schedules within their assigned system. Power plant operators operate and monitor generating equipment and report what the unit is doing. If you prefer tracking conditions across an operating area, look at dispatch. If you prefer tracking equipment and unit status, look at plant operations.

## Compare the two shift records

| Compare | Power distributors and dispatchers | Power plant operators |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inputs | System status, schedules and open events | Unit readings, equipment status and shift records |
| Setting | Dispatch desk or control center | Plant control room and equipment areas |
| Handoff | Schedule updates and unresolved coordination | Unit status, work in progress and exceptions |

[O*NET dispatch tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/51-8012.00) · [O*NET plant tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/51-8013.00)

## When the desk and plant see different versions

The NYISO billing guide defines a real-time schedule field communicated to support generation dispatch. In a fictional handoff at another facility, the dispatch desk logs a revised schedule version, and the plant operator reports that the shift record still shows the previous version. Each team checks its own source record and escalates the discrepancy to the designated desk and plant leads. Those leads confirm the assignment under their site procedures. This is an illustration of records and responsibilities. [NYISO billing guide](https://www.nyiso.com/documents/20142/1392088/smd2_billing_guide.pdf/22fb1861-1ba0-d881-ee84-79d790482571)

## Check the job’s operating authority before choosing training

BLS lists high school or equivalent and long-term on-the-job training as typical for both jobs. For a covered dispatch position under NERC PER-003-2, certification applies to specified real-time reliability work. An uncertified person performing those tasks needs direct supervision by a certified operator stationed at that position, who retains ultimate responsibility. Plant applicants should ask about unit training and any state or local nonnuclear credential required for the duties. [BLS preparation](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) · [NERC PER-003-2](https://www.nerc.com/globalassets/standards/reliability-standards/per/per-003-2.pdf) · [BLS job profiles](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/production/power-plant-operators-distributors-and-dispatchers.htm)

## Compare shift conditions with wages

BLS reports 2025 national median annual OEWS wages of $106,730 for power distributors and dispatchers and $102,040 for power plant operators. Ask dispatch employers about night coverage and desk assignments. Ask plant employers how much time goes to equipment rounds and how they handle outages or holidays. Compare actual offers with similar responsibility and hours. [BLS wage table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Bring documented shifts to the next role

A plant operator can use well-documented unit records to move toward dispatch training and learn system-level monitoring and handoff. A dispatcher can bring event records and communications experience to plant operations support and learn the facility’s equipment and delegated duties.

The desk log shows version V2 sent and the acknowledgment field blank. The plant log still shows V1. Spend 12 minutes writing the desk note and 13 minutes writing the plant handoff. Spend five minutes naming the people who check each record and who can confirm the next assignment. Use paper only.
