# Clinical Neuropsychologist vs. Marriage and Family Therapist

## Cognitive evaluation and relationship-focused therapy

A clinical neuropsychologist evaluates cognitive functions such as memory and attention and explains the findings. A marriage and family therapist works with clients on relationship concerns and treatment goals. Both can contribute to care for the same people.

## Compare the deliverables

| Work | Clinical neuropsychologist | Marriage and family therapist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First check | Evaluation findings, sources, and interpretation limits | What each person reports and wants to change |
| Deliverable | Evaluation summary and its scope | A therapy goal and plan to review it |

O*NET links neuropsychologist wages to Psychologists, All Other. That series cannot isolate clinical neuropsychologist pay. [O*NET wage coverage](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-3039.02#WagesEmployment)

## A cognitive report informs a family session

At a fictional clinic, one partner reports forgetting plans, and both describe arguments afterward. The clinical neuropsychologist reviews interview and assessment information and records the conditions under which observations were made. With consent and under clinic confidentiality procedures, the family therapist receives the relevant summary and asks both partners what happens when one reminds the other.

## U.S. preparation and authorization

For clinical neuropsychology, look for doctoral psychology training and supervised specialty assessment experience. New York, for example, lists doctoral education, experience, and an exam for psychology licensure. ABPP separately describes specialty board certification and its postdoctoral requirements. For family therapy, BLS describes a master’s degree as typical and state licensure as required. Verify the two pathways with the applicable state boards. [New York psychology requirements](https://www.op.nysed.gov/professions/psychology/license-requirements) · [ABPP specialty certification](https://abpp.org/application-information/learn-about-specialty-boards/clinical-neuropsychology/specialty-specific/) · [BLS family therapy preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/community-and-social-service/marriage-and-family-therapists.htm#tab-4) · [New York family therapy requirements](https://www.op.nysed.gov/professions/marriage-and-family-therapists/license-requirements)

## Compare the workday

Neuropsychology can involve client contact and time for scoring, reviewing records, and writing reports. Family therapy can involve appointments in mental health centers and private offices. Ask how the employer schedules evaluation, report writing, therapy sessions, and supervision. [O*NET neuropsychology work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-3039.02#WorkContext) · [BLS family therapy work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/community-and-social-service/marriage-and-family-therapists.htm#tab-3)

## Moving between the roles

A neuropsychologist can bring interviewing and documentation into family therapy training, then develop supervised experience in family systems work. A family therapist can bring interviewing and team coordination into psychology training, then build assessment, interpretation, and reporting skills and meet licensure requirements.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use fictional notes. One partner reports forgetting an appointment on Tuesday. The other reports an argument after a reminder. The evaluation summary says only that assessment took place in a quiet room. Spend ten minutes separating observations, ten drafting a question for the evaluator, and ten drafting a question about how the partners responded.
