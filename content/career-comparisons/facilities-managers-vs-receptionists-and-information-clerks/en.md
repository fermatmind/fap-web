# Facilities Manager vs. Receptionist: Rerouting Visitors During a Lobby Repair

## What each role delivers

A facilities manager coordinates repair work and confirms which parts of a building can stay open. A receptionist or information clerk uses the approved plan to guide visitors and alerts the facilities team when directions stop working.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Facilities Managers | Receptionists and Information Clerks |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Repair schedule and confirmed access plan | Visitor check-in, directions and exception reports |
| Decision inputs | Work area, contractor status and reopening criteria | Appointments, visitor needs and current site instructions |

## How the work meets

At a fictional office, workers close the west lobby to replace flooring. The facilities manager checks the work zone, the alternate entrance and the hours of the closure with the site team, then updates reception. Reception checks the day's appointments and alerts people whose invitations name the west entrance. When a visitor reports standing water outside the alternate door, reception passes on the location and time. The facilities team checks the report and updates the route if needed.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor's degree and less than five years of related work experience as typical for facilities managers. It lists high school completion or equivalent and short-term on-the-job training for reception. Ask which maintenance tasks are overseen by the manager and which are performed by qualified tradespeople. ## Work conditions

Facility work may follow outage windows or contractor schedules. Reception has to apply updates in the middle of a queue. Ask who can change directions and how the next shift gets the current version.

## Moving between roles

From reception, bring clear incident notes and learn to track work orders, vendors and signoffs. From facilities, bring knowledge of the building and practice explaining directions in the terms visitors can use.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Sketch two entrances on a fictional site plan. Close one for the morning and mark the other open, with a possible delay to the reopening. Spend ten minutes writing the facilities update, ten writing the front-desk directions and ten checking consistent times, routes and reporting contacts.
