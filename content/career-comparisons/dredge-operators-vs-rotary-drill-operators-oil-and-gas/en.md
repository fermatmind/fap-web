# Dredge Operator vs. Oil and Gas Rotary Drill Operator

## What each operator is trying to verify

Dredge operators run excavation equipment and verify depths in assigned areas. Rotary drill operators run drilling equipment, log footage and other activities, and direct assigned crew work.

## Compare what a depth entry refers to

| Record | Dredge operator | Rotary drill operator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work location | Assigned dredging area | Assigned well and interval |
| What to record | Excavation observations and areas needing another check | Footage, equipment and shift activity |
| Handoff item | Area identifier and who will verify the check | Well and interval identifiers and who verifies the shift entry |

[O*NET 53-7031.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-7031.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 47-5012.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/47-5012.00#Tasks)

## The drilling equipment barge is waiting for a berth

A fictional oil and gas project uses a barge to bring drilling equipment to its worksite. One section of the departure berth still needs a dredging depth check. The dredge operator can report the areas already worked and identify the outstanding check for the port coordinator. The rotary drill operator can identify which crew assignments depend on that delivery. The port coordinator verifies berth status through the designated process, and the drilling lead updates the receiving schedule.

## U.S. education and on-the-job training

BLS assigns a high school diploma or equivalent to dredge operators and no formal educational credential to rotary drill operators. Both have a moderate-term on-the-job training assignment. Ask the employer about experience, equipment training and any position-specific credentials before accepting independent work. [BLS education and training table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/education-and-training-by-occupation.htm) BLS assigns less than five years of related experience to rotary drill operators. For the comparison occupation, the table assigns no prior related experience.

## Ask what can change the schedule

Dredging can depend on waterway access, weather and verification of completed areas. Ask how the crew communicates those changes. On a drill crew, ask about rotations, time with the incoming shift and which person maintains the drilling log.

## Skills that transfer

A dredge operator can bring equipment observation and accurate area records to drill training, then learn well identifiers, crew roles and drilling documentation. A drill operator can bring shift handoffs and equipment reporting to dredging, then learn the dredge controls, area maps and worksite qualifications.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a fictional berth map, an outstanding depth check and a rig equipment delivery schedule. Spend ten minutes identifying the dredging area and depth reference, ten minutes identifying the rig assignment that depends on delivery, and ten minutes writing two handoffs naming who verifies berth status and who updates the receiving schedule.
