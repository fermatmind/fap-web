# Transportation inspectors vs. transportation, storage, and distribution managers

## Check the shipment or run the operation

Transportation inspectors examine transportation conditions and document findings. Transportation, storage, and distribution managers coordinate people, facilities and the movement of goods. Both may inspect conditions. In a shipping operation, the inspection record and the decision about how to schedule work serve different purposes.

## Compare the work

| Item | Transportation inspector | Transportation, storage, and distribution manager |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deliverable | A finding, exception record and recheck | A staffing, warehouse or dispatch plan |
| Work setting to ask about | Freight site, yard or location relevant to the inspection | Warehouse, transportation office or distribution network |
| Typical U.S. entry education | High school diploma or equivalent | High school diploma or equivalent |
| BLS preparation category | Moderate-term on-the-job training | Five years or more of related work experience |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $92,100 | $107,230 |

Wages cover nonfarm wage and salary workers and exclude self-employment. ## One load needs a recheck

At a fictional carrier, the inspector records that shipment A passed and that the securing condition on shipment B needs correction and reinspection. The operations manager changes the dispatch sequence and coordinates the crew and receiving site. Under the example company's policy, the inspector performs the recheck and signs the finding, then a designated dispatch lead decides whether to release the shipment. Dispatch staff carry out the decision.

## Getting started in the U.S.

BLS lists high school or equivalent for typical entry education in both roles. For inspection, it lists moderate-term on-the-job training. Ask which mode or inspection program the position covers and verify role-specific qualifications with the employer or relevant authority. For management, BLS lists five or more years of related experience. Receiving, warehouse or transport coordination can build relevant experience. Ask which operating responsibilities the opening includes. [BLS preparation categories](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Ask what causes the day to change

An inspector can ask how rechecks affect the route or shift and who follows up on unresolved findings. A manager can ask how many sites or teams are covered and who handles late arrivals, staffing changes and downstream notices.

## Moving between the roles

An inspector can bring a redacted finding and recheck log, then build experience with scheduling, staffing and receiving coordination. A manager can bring a redacted dispatch decision and exception debrief, then practice applying inspection criteria and documenting evidence.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use mock shipping records only. A has passed and must ship by 10. B has an open finding and another slot at 11. Example rules require a passing check before the dispatch lead can approve release. Spend ten minutes setting the statuses, ten writing a reinspection request and revised schedule, and ten preparing the handoff. Identify which load can be sent for approval and who checks, approves and executes.
