# Derrick Operators, Oil and Gas vs Wellhead Pumpers

## Drilling circulation and producing wells

Oil and gas derrick operators set up rig equipment, monitor drilling-fluid circulation, and prepare mud reports. Wellhead pumpers operate pumps and auxiliary equipment at producing wells and record operating conditions. Both work with pumps and shift records, with different purposes.

## Work, preparation, and U.S. pay

| Item | Oil and gas derrick operators | Wellhead pumpers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assignment | Rig and drilling-fluid equipment | Producing well pumps and auxiliary equipment |
| Output | Circulation and mud records | Production-well equipment and inspection records |
| Typical entry education | No formal educational credential | High school diploma or equivalent |
| Typical related experience | Less than five years | Less than five years |
| Typical on-the-job training | Short-term | Moderate-term |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $58,620 | $69,960 |

National wage and salary medians exclude self-employment. ## One field has two shift records

At a fictional oilfield, the drilling crew reports unusual vibration in circulation equipment. The derrick operator checks equipment IDs and the circulation log within the assigned scope and reports when vibration was first observed to the drilling lead. At a neighboring producing well, the latest reading differs from the previous inspection. The wellhead pumper checks the well ID and source record and reports the discrepancy to the production lead. The operators perform their assigned checks and report results. Under the site's example rules, each authorized lead decides on maintenance, isolation, or return to service. The coordinator tracks the two decisions separately.

## Training and assignment

For derrick jobs, check which duties the drill crew assigns first, what prior field experience counts, and how supervised work is assessed. For wellhead jobs, ask how you learn producing-well equipment and records, which wells you cover, and when you may work independently. Verify employer and site requirements for the work.

## A rig rotation or a route between wells

BLS reports that some remote or offshore oil and gas crews spend weeks away from home and work long shifts. Ask how the derrick role rotates with the drill crew. For a wellhead role, ask whether the assignment stays at one well or involves visits to several, and how travel and repeat visits fit into a shift. [BLS oil and gas work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/oil-and-gas-workers.htm#tab-3)

## A supervised starting point in either direction

Moving into wellhead work, bring a fictional equipment and exception record and start with supervised production-well inspection documentation. Learn the field equipment and production logs. Moving into derrick work, bring a fictional well equipment record and start with supervised circulation log checks. Learn the drill crew's roles and assigned equipment.

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use a different shift at the fictional field. D-72 is the circulation log for new well N2, with the final hour blank. P-72 is a signed equipment inspection at producing well P2. The coordinator is following up on P3. Spend ten minutes marking the scope of each record, ten minutes requesting the missing hour and P3 record separately, and ten minutes listing each open item and owner for the coordinator.
