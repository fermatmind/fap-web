# Pump Operators, Except Wellhead Pumpers vs Wellhead Pumpers

## Track a transfer or track an individual well

Pump operators in the non-wellhead occupation monitor equipment and document material transfers. Wellhead pumpers check well-site equipment and record production. If you like tracking what moved between facilities, look at transfer operations. If you prefer following individual wells and their daily production records, look at wellhead work.

## Look at the evidence each shift hands off

| Compare | Pump operators, except wellhead pumpers | Wellhead pumpers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work focus | Transfer equipment and material movement | Well-site equipment and production records |
| Setting | Station, terminal or another transfer facility | Well sites and the reporting point for a field crew |
| Handoff | Transfer interval, equipment status and open items | Well ID, production history and exceptions |

## A well log and a station log cover different things

Baker Hughes describes a Permian Basin project that converted three wells to electric submersible pumps and added production monitoring. Imagine a similar field with a downstream transfer station. The wellhead pumper marks a missing interval in the log for one well. The transfer operator provides the station total and specifies that it includes several wells. Each verifies the source record for their own facility. Unresolved differences go to the shift lead, and any operating changes are decided by authorized personnel under site procedures. The station and job assignments illustrate a handoff. [Baker Hughes field case](https://dam.bakerhughes.com/m/b3794f30cb86d14/original/Integrated-production-solution-doubled-production-Permian.pdf)

## Check how training works at the specific site

O*NET assigns both occupations to Job Zone 1–2, where a high school diploma or equivalent is usual and the amount of preparation varies. Ask transfer employers which equipment and records trainees work with. At oilfield employers, check the supported field training, assignments and credentials required for the actual tasks. OSHA’s drilling and servicing guidance illustrates general and task-specific training within that scope. [O*NET transfer preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-7072.00) · [O*NET wellhead preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/53-7073.00) · [OSHA oil and gas eTool](https://www.osha.gov/etools/oil-and-gas) BLS lists a high school diploma or equivalent and moderate-term on-the-job training for both. Its typical related-experience categories are “none” for non-wellhead pump operators and “less than five years” for wellhead pumpers. Ask employers how they apply those categories to a specific opening. [BLS Table 1.2](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Compare a station roster with a field route

Ask a transfer employer whether the shift stays at one station and who covers after-hours calls. For wellhead work, ask how many sites a shift covers, what travel looks like and how the employer handles severe weather. Compare base pay, travel and shift coverage for similar local openings. BLS reports 2025 national median annual wages of $61,770 for pump operators except wellhead pumpers and $69,960 for wellhead pumpers, using its Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics program. [BLS wage table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Bring a checked field log to the next job

A transfer operator moving toward wellhead work can show how they document time and equipment status, then learn production records and site-specific training. A wellhead pumper moving toward transfer can bring experience documenting exceptions and learn how station totals combine or separate multiple sources.

## Try a thirty-minute handoff on paper

Use fictional records. Well W3 shows 22 barrels from 7 to 9 a.m. and no record from 9 to 10 a.m. Station S2 shows 61 barrels from 7 to 10 a.m. across W3 and another well. Spend 12 minutes writing the well note, 13 minutes writing the station note, and five minutes naming who checks each source. State which volume is specific to W3 and which cannot establish its missing hour.
