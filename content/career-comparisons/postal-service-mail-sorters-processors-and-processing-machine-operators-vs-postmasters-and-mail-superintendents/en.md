# Postal Service Mail Sorters, Processors, and Processing Machine Operators vs Postmasters and Mail Superintendents

## Prepare the dispatch batch or manage the shift

Processing workers use sorting equipment, handle odd-sized pieces by hand, and route labeled mail according to the dispatch plan. Postmasters and mail superintendents coordinate staff, track operational performance, and resolve escalated problems. Consider whether you would rather maintain the accuracy of one workstream or decide how several workstreams share staff and time.

## One container is ready and the other is short

In a fictional facility, the processor confirms that container A is ready for dispatch but finds that B has one fewer recorded piece than expected. The processor keeps B in the exception record and gives the supervisor the count and its source. The supervisor assigns someone to reconcile B and keeps staff available for cleared work. If the review runs beyond cutoff, the supervisor assigns the exception to the next shift under the facility's procedure. The processor logs which containers actually left.

## Check entry data and the process for the posted job

BLS lists no formal educational credential and short-term on-the-job training as typical for processing workers. For postmasters and mail superintendents it lists a high school diploma or equivalent, less than five years of related experience, and moderate-term training as typical. USPS lists VEA 476 for specified Mail Processing Clerk roles and 475 for listed Mail Handler roles. For a posted nonbargaining postmaster position under EL-312, check the announcement's area of consideration, whether outside applicants can apply, and the stated qualifications. [BLS entry data](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) · [USPS exams](https://about.usps.com/careers/how-to-apply/exams.htm) · [USPS selection](https://about.usps.com/handbooks/el312/el312c7_020.htm)

## Ask when workloads peak and who takes over

For processing, ask about standing time, rotation, overnight shifts and the volume near dispatch cutoff. For management, ask who can move employees between stations, what gets escalated, and how the next shift acknowledges an open case.

## Build a work sample for the other side of the handoff

To move from processing into management, expand a fictional count discrepancy into a coverage plan with a reviewer and a follow-up time. Seek opportunities to practice shift coordination and check the manager vacancy's eligibility. To move into processing, turn a fictional shift plan into station-level checks, then learn the sorting rules and complete training for the assigned equipment. [USPS selection](https://about.usps.com/handbooks/el312/el312c7_020.htm) · [USPS exams](https://about.usps.com/careers/how-to-apply/exams.htm)

## Try a 30-minute paper dispatch review

Use one fictional record: dispatch is in fifteen minutes, A is verified, B should have twelve pieces but the record shows eleven, and one employee can help. Spend eight minutes tagging the evidence, twelve drafting either a processor's exception note or a supervisor's coverage plan, and ten checking it. Leave A ready and flag B for review. The manager's plan should assign a reviewer and identify a next-shift owner if the cutoff passes.
