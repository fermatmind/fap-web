# Plumber, Pipefitter, and Steamfitter vs. RV Service Technician

## Site piping and the systems onboard an RV

Plumbers, pipefitters, and steamfitters may install and service site piping and equipment connections. RV service technicians diagnose problems in the vehicle, including onboard plumbing where assigned. A complaint at an RV water connection can require information from both sides.

## Compare the work products

| Work | Plumbing or fitting trade | RV service technician |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting point | Site supply and connection records | Vehicle configuration and repair history |
| Handoff | Verified observations about the site supply | Vehicle findings and open diagnostic questions |

May 2025 U.S. median annual wages were $63,800 for plumbers, pipefitters, and steamfitters and $52,000 for RV service technicians. Both figures are from BLS OEWS and describe the whole occupation, including experienced workers. ## A campground guest reports low flow

A fictional customer reports low flow on the campground connection. Another RV used the same connection without a complaint, and nobody documented what happened in onboard tank mode. The site plumber reviews supply records and notes what the site can confirm. The RV technician checks vehicle configuration and service history and documents which observations are missing. The service lead uses both notes to assign follow-up.

## U.S. entry routes

BLS describes apprenticeship as the usual route for plumbers, pipefitters, and steamfitters. Most states and some localities license plumbers, so check your trade and location. O*NET puts RV service technicians in Job Zone 1–2. Look for an entry-level shop role with supervised training in service records and vehicle systems. Ask how trainees qualify for work on specific systems. [BLS piping trades preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/plumbers-pipefitters-and-steamfitters.htm#tab-4) · [O*NET RV preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/49-3092.00#JobZone)

## Compare worksites and schedules

Plumbing and fitting jobs can take you to homes, commercial sites, and factories. O*NET reports that RV service technicians often work indoors without environmental control. Ask whether work is in a service bay or on customer sites, whether travel is regular, and how urgent calls affect the schedule. [BLS piping trades work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/plumbers-pipefitters-and-steamfitters.htm#tab-3) · [O*NET RV work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/49-3092.00#WorkContext)

## Moving between the jobs

A piping trades worker can bring drawing literacy and fault documentation to RV service while learning vehicle layouts and onboard interfaces. An RV technician can bring service diagnosis and customer records to site piping while learning system drawings, jobsite handoffs, and local requirements.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use these fictional notes. At 10:10, a customer reports low flow on the campground connection. At 10:20, another RV uses the same connection without a complaint. Nobody has recorded operation in onboard tank mode. Spend ten minutes sorting facts and gaps, ten writing one question for site staff and one for the customer, and ten assigning each question to the appropriate service team. Use the paper records only.
