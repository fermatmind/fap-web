# Substation and Relay Repairer vs. Power Dispatcher

## Equipment work and system operations

Powerhouse, substation and relay repairers check equipment and document maintenance. Power distributors and dispatchers monitor and regulate the distribution of power within their authority and coordinate operating arrangements. Both need reliable equipment identifiers and records.

## Compare what each role documents

| Item | Powerhouse, substation and relay repairer | Power distributor or dispatcher |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verify | Equipment identity and maintenance findings | Approved arrangements and recorded system status |
| Record | Findings and unresolved equipment questions | Operating status, receipt time and pending confirmation |
| Hand off | Equipment facts to the designated owner | Authorized operating coordination and its status |

[O*NET 49-2095.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/49-2095.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 51-8012.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/51-8012.00#Tasks)

## A maintenance report is received but the status is still open

In a fictional utility, a repair report is marked received while the dispatch log still shows a status pending confirmation. The repairer checks the report identifier, date and scope. The dispatcher verifies which assignment the report covers checks whether it affects the approved operating arrangements, and identifies the outstanding confirmation. Authorized personnel handle any operating decision under the site's procedures.

## U.S. education and training

BLS assigns the repair occupation a postsecondary nondegree award, less than five years of related experience and moderate-term on-the-job training. For distributors and dispatchers, it assigns a high school diploma or equivalent, no related work experience and long-term on-the-job training. BLS notes that some grid-affecting positions may need certification. Ask the employer whether the assignment requires it and how staff qualify for the specific duties. [BLS detailed education and training table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/education-and-training-by-occupation.htm) · [BLS power operations entry guidance](https://www.bls.gov/OOH/production/power-plant-operators-distributors-and-dispatchers.htm#tab-4)

## Ask what drives the shift

Ask repair employers when equipment checks and record reviews are scheduled. Ask dispatch employers about rotations, handoff overlap and the process for tracking items across shifts.

## Moving between the roles

Repairers can bring equipment familiarity and maintenance documentation into dispatcher training, then learn operating records and coordination procedures. Dispatchers can bring equipment identification and teamwork to repair work, then develop equipment-specific diagnostic and maintenance skills under the employer's training process.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a fictional maintenance report, dispatch handoff and equipment index. Spend ten minutes writing what the maintenance report confirms, ten marking what remains open in the dispatch log, and ten identifying the owner and documentary evidence that would close each item. Use records only.
