# Adapted Physical Education Specialist vs. School Psychologist

## Accessible PE instruction and school-wide support

An adapted PE specialist provides individualized physical education for students with disabilities whose needs call for adapted instruction. The specialist evaluates movement and participation to plan instruction. A school psychologist evaluates learning, behavioral, or emotional needs and works with school staff and families on support. Both can contribute to the same student’s school team.

## Compare what each role delivers

| Work | Adapted PE specialist | School psychologist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First check | Movement demands, instructions, and opportunities to participate | Participation across settings and supports already in place |
| Deliverable | An instructional adjustment and observation plan | A school support recommendation and follow-up questions |

[O*NET adapted PE tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/25-2059.01#Tasks) · [O*NET school psychology tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-3034.00#Tasks)

## A student leaves when the relay starts

In this fictional case, the student already receives adapted physical education. At a fictional school, a student joins warmups and leaves when the relay begins. The adapted PE specialist documents the instructions and pace and proposes a visual cue. The school psychologist checks whether transitions present similar difficulties in other classes. The team agrees to record cue use and participation for a week before reviewing the support.

## U.S. preparation

For adapted PE, seek supervised teaching experience adapting activities and recording participation. Check state educator credentials and any adapted PE qualification that applies to the assignment. IDEA Part B requires states to establish relevant personnel qualifications. BLS describes at least a master’s degree as typical for school psychologists, and programs usually include an internship. Check the state school psychology credential for the job location. [IDEA personnel qualifications](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title34-vol2/pdf/CFR-2024-title34-vol2-sec300-156.pdf) · [BLS school psychology preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/life-physical-and-social-science/psychologists.htm#tab-4) For example, California lists an Adapted Physical Education Specialist Credential and a Pupil Personnel Services credential with a School Psychology specialization. [California adapted PE credential](https://www.ctc.ca.gov/credentials/leaflets/cl-623/) · [California school psychology credential](https://www.ctc.ca.gov/credentials/leaflets/cl-606c/)

## Compare the workday

An adapted PE specialist may work in gyms or outdoor spaces following class schedules. A school psychologist may observe classrooms, review records, or attend support meetings. Ask how much time goes to direct service and how many schools the role covers.

## Moving between the roles

A PE educator can bring classroom observation and instructional adjustment skills, then complete the assessment training and supervised preparation required for school psychology. A school psychologist can bring team consultation and observation skills, then build expertise in PE instruction and meet the teaching requirements for the assignment.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use three fictional entries. On Monday the student joins warmups and leaves at the start of the relay. On Wednesday the teacher uses a visual cue and the student completes one handoff. Another teacher has no record of classroom transitions. Spend ten minutes listing observations, ten proposing one PE adjustment, and ten writing a question for the other teacher.
