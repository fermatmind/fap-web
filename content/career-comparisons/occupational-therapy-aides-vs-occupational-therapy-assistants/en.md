# Occupational Therapy Aides vs Occupational Therapy Assistants

## Prepare the room or carry out the session

An occupational therapy aide helps the clinic get people, equipment and forms where they need to be. An occupational therapy assistant carries out assigned therapy activities under an occupational therapist’s supervision and records how the client participated. Choose aide work if you like keeping appointments and supplies in order. Explore OTA training if you want more time working on activities with clients and can commit to accredited education and licensure.

## Follow the two records

| Compare | Occupational therapy aide | Occupational therapy assistant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Usual starting point | Schedule, room checklist and materials | Assigned treatment activities and plan |
| Handoff | Room status, supply issues or appointment notes | Client participation and progress observations |
| Physical routine | Standing, preparing and resetting rooms | Standing while delivering activities, plus documentation |

Both may interact with clients. Check the assigned duties and supervision for each job. [BLS duties and work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/Healthcare/Occupational-therapy-assistants-and-aides.htm)

## A missing item before a Texas clinic visit

In Texas, supervision of an OT aide requires on-site contact and an occupational therapy practitioner who can respond immediately. The rules allow trained non-licensed personnel to carry out certain predetermined care tasks the client has shown some prior ability to perform. Consider an illustrative outpatient handoff. The aide finds that item R221 on the room checklist is missing, logs the discrepancy and tells the OTA. The OTA checks with the supervising OT about the scheduled activity. If the client later asks to stop, the OTA documents the request and what occurred, then brings the observation to the OT for any change to the plan. Texas restricts non-licensed personnel from evaluating clients or modifying treatment. [Texas occupational therapy rules](https://ptot.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/OT-Rules-June-2026.pdf)

## Check education and licensure for the job location

Aides typically enter with a high school diploma or equivalent and train on the job. OTAs typically earn an associate degree from an accredited OTA program and usually need a state license. In Texas, use the board’s application instructions to verify the education and examination requirements for your route. Ask an employer who supervises new hires and how much of a shift is reserved for setup or documentation. [BLS entry pathways](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/Healthcare/Occupational-therapy-assistants-and-aides.htm) · [Texas OT applications](https://ptot.texas.gov/ot-application/)

## Compare local offers against two wage figures

In May 2025, national annual median wages were $39,160 for OT aides and $72,300 for OTAs, according to BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics. The same BLS profile notes that some shifts include evenings or weekends. For two offers, compare base pay, guaranteed hours and weekend rotation in the same metro area and setting. Include training time and tuition if comparing an immediate aide offer with an OTA program. [BLS pay and work schedules](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/Healthcare/Occupational-therapy-assistants-and-aides.htm)

## Decide what to learn in an aide job

An aide role can build practice in accurate handoffs, supply tracking and communication with clients. For an OTA pathway, look for accredited training that develops clinical observation and documentation. Ask the program how it arranges fieldwork and check the state board’s requirements where you plan to work. In job postings, separate time spent preparing a room from time spent carrying out an activity and documenting progress. [BLS education and advancement](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/Healthcare/Occupational-therapy-assistants-and-aides.htm) · [Texas OT applications](https://ptot.texas.gov/ot-application/)

## Try a 30-minute paper handoff

Spend 12 minutes writing a room checklist with R221 marked missing and name the person who needs the update. Spend 13 minutes writing a sample observation that separates what a fictional client said from what happened in the activity. Route the question about a plan change to the supervising therapist. Spend the last five minutes checking that someone else can tell what happened, who owns the next step and what still needs confirmation.
