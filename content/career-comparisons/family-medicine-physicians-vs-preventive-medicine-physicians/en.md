# Family Medicine Physicians vs Preventive Medicine Physicians

## Continuing care and prevention at different scales

Family medicine physicians diagnose, treat and provide preventive care across age groups. Preventive medicine physicians address health risks through individual services or population-level work. The settings can overlap. Ask which patients, populations and decisions the role is responsible for.

## Outputs, setting, and U.S. preparation

| Item | Family medicine physicians | Preventive medicine physicians |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | Diagnosis, preventive care plan and follow-up | Population assessment, program recommendations or clinical prevention record |
| Settings | Primary care practices and community clinics | Public health, program settings or clinical prevention |
| U.S. specialty reference | ABFM family medicine training requirements | ABPM public health and general preventive medicine pathways |

## A patient's appointment and a program count

A fictional clinic and community program review follow-up. The family physician records one patient's preference for an evening appointment and asks the clinic team to reschedule. Under the project's rules, the clinic shares only aggregate counts. The preventive medicine physician finds that one site's report counts bookings and the other counts completed visits, requests matching measures and reports which comparisons can be made. The clinic owns the individual schedule. The program lead decides whether to change program hours.

## Compare the U.S. training pathways

For family medicine board certification, ABFM's standard U.S. path uses 36 months of training in an ACGME-accredited family medicine residency. For ABPM's public health and general preventive medicine residency pathway, check prior clinical training, qualifying preventive medicine residency, and the MPH or equivalent graduate degree requirement. These are specialty certification pathways. Check your state physician license and the employer's clinical privileges separately. [ABFM training requirements](https://www.theabfm.org/become-certified/initial-training-requirements/) · [ABPM residency pathway](https://www.theabpm.org/become-certified/specialties/public-health-general-preventive-medicine/)

## Ask how the work is scheduled

Ask family medicine practices how appointment slots, same-day visits and follow-up are scheduled. Ask preventive medicine employers how program reviews, field assignments and individual clinical work are divided and who sets reporting deadlines.

## Where to start when changing specialties

Moving into preventive medicine, bring a fictional quality summary and ask a qualifying training program about supervised program evaluation and any prior training it recognizes. Moving into family medicine, bring fictional outreach follow-up records and ask a family medicine residency program whether it can assess prior training for advanced placement. Confirm the applicable board and the site's privileges before taking clinical responsibilities. Ask the preventive medicine program whether the ABPM Complementary Pathway is relevant to your prior training and recent practice. [ABPM pathways](https://www.theabpm.org/become-certified/specialties/public-health-general-preventive-medicine/) · [ABFM training and placement](https://www.theabfm.org/become-certified/initial-training-requirements/)

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use fictional F-35 (the patient requested another time, and there is no new appointment yet) and P-35 (Site A reports 40 bookings, Site B reports 30 completed visits, and the project requires completed visits for comparison). Spend ten minutes identifying what each record establishes, ten minutes writing separate scheduling and data requests, and ten minutes naming the owners and which decisions are still open.
