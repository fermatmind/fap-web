# Clergy vs. Director of Religious Activities and Education

## Religious services and education programs

Clergy may lead worship and provide spiritual guidance. Directors of religious activities and education may design courses, coordinate activities, and supervise instructors. The responsibilities can overlap in a congregation.

## Compare the work products

| Work | Clergy | Director of religious activities and education |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First check | The service, its purpose, and participants’ needs | Class goals, staffing, facilities, and schedule |
| Deliverable | Service plan and guidance for participants | Program plan and assignments for instructors |

May 2025 U.S. median annual wages were $60,810 for clergy and $52,100 for directors of religious activities and education. These OEWS figures cover wage and salary jobs and exclude self-employment. ## A memorial service and an introductory class

A fictional congregation plans a memorial service and an introductory class for new participants. The clergy member confirms the service plan and hands its schedule and room needs to the director. The director checks instructor and room availability and presents options when the schedules conflict. In this congregation, the clergy member confirms the service requirements and any change to its timing. The director decides when and where to hold the class. If a class option affects the service, they resolve the timing together before the director publishes the class schedule.

## U.S. preparation and entry routes

O*NET places clergy in Job Zone Five, which indicates extensive preparation. Ask the religious body about its training, supervised service, and appointment or ordination process. The religious education director profile is Job Zone Four, where a bachelor’s degree and relevant experience are common. A supervised program role can build experience scheduling courses and supporting instructors. Check whether the employer also expects religious credentials. ## Compare day-to-day work

Both roles may involve face-to-face communication. In this example, clergy schedule services and spiritual care, while the education director coordinates instructors, rooms, and registration. Ask how observances, class schedules, and community events shape your hours. [Clergy work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/21-2011.00#WorkContext) · [Director work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/21-2021.00#WorkContext)

## Moving between the roles

Clergy can bring a de-identified class outline and a short review of what participants learned. For a director role, build experience scheduling courses and supervising instructors. A director can bring a de-identified instructor roster and a record explaining a course change. For a clergy role, follow the religious body’s training and appointment or ordination process.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use fictional notes. The service ends at 11:15 a.m., the instructor arrives at 11:30, and the room must be clear by noon. The options are a 20-minute introduction or rescheduling the full class. Spend ten minutes listing constraints, ten writing a question for each role, and ten drafting a scheduling note for the education director that flags any change requiring confirmation from the clergy member.
