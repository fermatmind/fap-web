# Cartographers and Photogrammetrists vs. Data Warehousing Specialists on a Road Map Update

## Different questions on the same data

Cartographers and photogrammetrists turn imagery and survey information into a usable map. Data warehousing specialists bring records from different systems into a repeatable load and verify the result. A map can be spatially sound and still show records from the wrong batch.

## Compare outputs

| Handoff | Cartographers and Photogrammetrists | Data Warehousing Specialists |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Road layer, imagery notes, and uncertain locations | Source mapping, load checks, and batch exceptions |

U.S. occupational task sources · [O*NET 17-1021.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/17-1021.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 15-1243.01](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-1243.01#Tasks)

## One project, two handoffs

A fictional team is placing inspection photos and repair orders on a road map. The mapping team checks which road segment each photo supports and flags uncertain locations. The warehouse specialist reconciles segment IDs across two order systems, checks for duplicates, and sends unmatched orders back to the data owners. The project checks the spatial matches and the completeness of the batch before releasing the update.

## How to prepare

BLS describes a related bachelor’s degree as typical for cartographers and photogrammetrists, with coursework such as GIS or remote sensing. Check the state board for the specific duties. Oregon’s board, for example, directs applicants for professional photogrammetrist registration to contact the board. Start by looking for supervised map production or imagery work. [BLS preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/cartographers-and-photogrammetrists.htm#tab-4) · [Oregon registration guidance](https://www.oregon.gov/osbeels/obtaining/pages/default.aspx)

O*NET puts data warehousing specialists in a category that generally requires considerable preparation. The category describes a broad level of preparation. [Data Warehousing Specialists Job Zone](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-1243.01#JobZone) Look for data integration or reporting roles that let you work on source mapping, transformation, and validation.

## What to ask before applying

Map work can involve editing layers and interpreting imagery at a desk, with field verification on some assignments. In a warehouse role, much of the coordination can happen around scheduled source loads and exceptions. Ask the map team how often it verifies features in the field and the warehouse team who is available when an evening load fails.

## Skills that transfer

Move into warehousing by making a layer update reproducible and checking each step against its inputs. Move into mapping by learning why an apparently good record match may still put an item in the wrong location.

## A 30-minute exercise

Sketch two adjacent road segments with a bridge between them, a station on the east segment, and a tower on the west. Two fictional photos show a station beside a bridge and a stretch of unmarked asphalt. Spend fifteen minutes marking a location or uncertainty for each. Then use the lists below to decide what to keep. In this exercise, both systems preserve the same work-order ID for the same event. Spend fifteen minutes checking IDs, dates and work types. Hand over an annotated sketch and a list with a reason for each keep or merge decision.
