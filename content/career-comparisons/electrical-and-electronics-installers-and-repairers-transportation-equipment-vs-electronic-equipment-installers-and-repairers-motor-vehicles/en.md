# Electrical and Electronics Installers and Repairers, Transportation Equipment vs Electronic Equipment Installers and Repairers, Motor Vehicles

## Similar systems on different platforms

Transportation equipment electrical and electronics workers may install or service communications or navigation equipment on trains, watercraft, and other mobile equipment. Motor vehicle electronics installers and repairers install and troubleshoot communications, audio, security, or navigation devices in motor vehicles. Both can receive a communications work order. The platform determines the assignment and the acceptance checks. The U.S. SOC classifies motor vehicle electronics installation and repair separately under 49-2096. [BLS SOC manual](https://www.bls.gov/soc/2018/soc_2018_manual.pdf)

## Work, preparation, and U.S. pay

| Item | Transportation equipment electronics | Motor vehicle electronics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setting examples | Rail shop, shipyard, or electronics bench | Automotive service or installation shop |
| Output | Platform-specific installation and system check record | Vehicle device installation and verification record |

National wage and salary medians exclude self-employment. ## One dispatch note covers two different terminals

A fictional ferry operator is replacing communications terminals on ferry F-62 and shore service vehicle V-62. Record S-62 shows the ferry check was signed. Record A-62 says the vehicle installation is complete and its check is pending. A shared dispatch note says only “communications complete.” The vessel repairer hands over the verified terminal and ferry IDs. The vehicle installer logs completed installation and the pending vehicle check. Under the example rules, vessel and vehicle leads accept their own orders and dispatch updates the two statuses separately.

## What to check before taking a role

For transportation electronics, compare postsecondary programs related to the platform and ask how the employer supervises site work. For motor vehicle electronics, ask how the shop teaches vehicle-specific installations, device compatibility and documentation. Verify credentials and authorization for the assigned work.

## What changes the workweek

Ask transportation shops how work fits around platform downtime and who signs checks. Ask motor vehicle shops how daily appointments, incomplete records and parts availability affect the work.

## Skills that carry across

Moving from vehicle electronics to transportation equipment, bring a fictional vehicle terminal diagnosis and installation check. Look for supervised assignments checking vessel terminal records and documenting verification, and learn vessel interfaces and site procedures. Moving in the other direction, use a fictional vessel check to show equipment traceability. Start with supervised vehicle-device fitment and post-installation checks, and learn vehicle configurations and manufacturer documentation.

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use fictional S-62 (ferry F-62, check signed), A-62 (vehicle V-62, installation complete, vehicle check pending), and U-62 (shared note says “communications complete”; neither lead has accepted an order). Spend ten minutes assigning records to their platforms, ten minutes listing confirmed and missing evidence, and ten minutes giving dispatch two updates. Say which order is ready to submit for acceptance, which still needs a check, and who decides.
