# Energy Engineers, Except Wind and Solar vs Petroleum engineers

## Follow the equipment or follow the well

An energy engineer studies where a system uses energy and how to verify an improvement. A petroleum engineer studies how wells and reservoirs behave and how to plan or improve production. A well-site project can put both at the same table. They bring different evidence to decide whether energy use improved while the well met its production needs.

## Compare the records each role brings

| Compare | Energy engineers, except wind and solar | Petroleum engineers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical inputs | Utility or meter records, equipment information, operating hours | Well histories, production records, subsurface or test data |
| Typical workplace | Site checks and desk analysis for energy projects | Office analysis and field work depending on the assignment |
| Typical output | Energy baseline, options and verification plan | Well performance interpretation or a production, drilling or reservoir recommendation |

[O*NET energy engineering](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/17-2199.03) · [BLS petroleum engineering duties](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/petroleum-engineers.htm)

## Review power and production after the same change

A Baker Hughes case study from the Permian Basin describes a three-well change from gas lift to electric submersible pumps, with a motor trial on one well and a production optimization system across the three wells. Imagine a review meeting for a similar project. The energy engineer checks which energy inputs were metered before and after conversion. For the gas-lift period, that includes verifying how compressor energy was supplied and allocated before comparing it with the pumps’ electricity use. The petroleum engineer brings each well’s production and downtime records and marks when the conversion, motor trial and automation changes occurred. The project lead uses both records to decide what can be attributed to the trial. [Baker Hughes field case](https://dam.bakerhughes.com/m/b3794f30cb86d14/original/Integrated-production-solution-doubled-production-Permian.pdf)

## Prepare for the work and check signing authority

O*NET puts energy engineering in Job Zone Four, where a bachelor’s degree is common and substantial preparation is expected. An energy project that includes a documented measurement baseline is one way to practice. BLS says petroleum engineers typically need a bachelor’s degree in petroleum engineering or a related field. Look for coursework and supervised projects in well performance, reservoir behavior or drilling. For Texas work, ask who signs regulated deliverables and which provisions of the Engineering Practice Act apply. [O*NET preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/17-2199.03) · [BLS entry requirements](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/petroleum-engineers.htm) · [Texas Engineering Practice Act](https://pels.texas.gov/downloads/lawrules.pdf)

## Ask how often office work becomes field work

For energy engineering, ask how many days typically go to site surveys and who arranges access to equipment and meter records. Petroleum engineering can involve travel to drilling or well sites. Ask whether the role is mainly office-based analysis, field support or both, and how field coverage affects work hours. Compare base compensation, travel and rotation across comparable offers. [O*NET energy work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/17-2199.03) · [BLS petroleum work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/petroleum-engineers.htm)

## Use a joint project to test a move

A petroleum engineer can use experience with downtime and production records to move toward equipment efficiency work. Add measurement planning, energy accounting and verification. An energy engineer can start on the facilities side of a supervised well project and learn to interpret production trends. Moving toward reservoir or drilling work takes more domain training and mentorship.

## Write two notes in thirty minutes

Use these fictional weekly records. The same meter covers the same well’s surface lift equipment in both weeks.

| Week | Electricity (kWh) | Produced fluid (bbl) | Logged run hours | Log status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1,200 | 300 | 140 | Complete |
| 2 | 960 | 200 | 100 | Wednesday shutdown cause and restart time missing |

Spend 12 minutes comparing total electricity and kWh per barrel of produced fluid. Spend 13 minutes writing the production note and naming the records you need to confirm comparable operation. Take five minutes to write a handoff that distinguishes the measured changes from any efficiency claim that needs more evidence.
