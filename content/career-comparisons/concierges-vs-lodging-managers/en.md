# Concierge vs. Lodging Manager: When a Hotel Shuttle Is Canceled

## What each role delivers

A concierge captures guests’ travel needs and arranges personal services. A lodging manager coordinates shifts and departments and addresses requests beyond the front desk’s delegated authority.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Concierge | Lodging Manager |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Guest needs and confirmed alternatives | Shift assignments and service recovery plan |
| Basis | Departure deadlines, passenger counts, transport and accessibility needs | Queue, staffing, vendor capacity and delegated authority |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $38,950 | $69,250 |

## How the work meets

In a fictional hotel, the regular shuttle is canceled. The concierge records guests’ arrival deadlines and accessibility needs, then checks seats on alternate services. The manager reviews confirmed capacity and the queue, assigns one person to coordinate with vendors and another to update the front desk, and decides when to use the backup service under hotel policy.

## How to enter

BLS lists a high school diploma or equivalent for both. It lists moderate-term on-the-job training for concierges and less than five years of related work experience for lodging managers. Ask about delegated authority for unplanned expenses. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Concierges take guest requests directly; managers may coordinate front desk, housekeeping and other teams at once. Ask how the duty manager tracks unresolved issues.

## Moving between roles

A concierge moving into management can build on shift handoffs, coaching and service reviews by learning scheduling and exception handling. A manager moving into concierge work can build on coordination by learning local services and individual follow-up.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock records show eight travelers, six confirmed replacement seats and one accessibility request awaiting verification. Spend ten minutes each on a guest needs list, staff assignments and backup plans, and a notice distinguishing confirmed seats from pending arrangements.
