# Executive Secretaries and Executive Administrative Assistants vs Paralegals and Legal Assistants

## Two users for a document packet

Executive secretaries and executive administrative assistants support leaders through calendar management, briefing materials, and follow-up. Paralegals and legal assistants support lawyers through research, drafting, and organizing case or matter records. Both track documents, and each works toward a different review.

## Work, U.S. entry, and pay

| Item | Executive administrative support | Paralegals and legal assistants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | Meeting packet, schedule, and action list | Fact index, research notes, and drafts for attorney review |
| Setting | Leadership offices across industries | Law firms, in-house legal teams, or government |
| Typical U.S. entry education | High school diploma or equivalent | Associate degree |
| Typical related experience | Less than five years | None in the BLS classification |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $76,590 | $62,890 |

National wage and salary medians exclude self-employment. ## The agenda and matter file carry different versions

At a fictional company, agenda A-17 for a contract review meeting includes v2. The paralegal sees in the matter log L-17 that the other party sent v3 and the attorney has yet to choose a review version. The executive assistant marks the attachment pending and keeps the meeting slot. The paralegal sends the attorney the source and time of each version and the open question. After the attorney confirms the review version, the executive assistant updates the meeting packet and notice, and the paralegal records the decision and corresponding attachment in the matter file.

## How to prepare for U.S. hiring

Consider administrative support assignments that let you demonstrate calendar coordination, writing, and discretion. Check employer expectations for executive work experience. For paralegal work, compare associate degree and certificate programs and ask whether the employer expects a bachelor's degree or other preparation. Ask what research and drafting the attorney supervises. [BLS executive and administrative entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/secretaries-and-administrative-assistants.htm#tab-4) · [BLS paralegal entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/legal/paralegals-and-legal-assistants.htm#tab-4)

## What to ask about deadlines

Ask an executive office who approves schedule changes and how many leaders or teams share support. Ask a legal team how it assigns research requests, checks citations, and handles review deadlines across matters.

## Skills you can carry over

An invented version index or handoff can show accuracy in either role. Moving into legal support calls for legal research and supervised drafting practice. Moving into executive support calls for calendar coordination, meeting logistics, and follow-up across several teams.

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use three fictional records. A-17 is an executive office invite for Wednesday at 3 p.m. with v2 from the procurement team at 9 a.m. Tuesday. L-17 logs v3 from the counterparty at 11:30 a.m. Tuesday. U-17 says the attorney has not chosen the meeting version. Spend ten minutes listing each attachment by source and time, ten minutes writing the schedule update and a separate version list for the attorney, and ten minutes naming who updates the invitation and matter file after confirmation. Check that the time remains booked and the review version remains pending.
