# Photographer vs. Writer or Author: Checking Images and Text for a Market Story

## What each role delivers

A photographer captures, selects and processes images. Writers and authors research, draft, verify and revise text. For a shared assignment, both roles confirm what their work can establish.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Photographers | Writers and authors |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Selected photos, scene notes and capture times | Story draft, source notes and fact checks |
| Basis | Light, angle, site access and identifying details | Interviews, documentation, narrative and editorial requirements |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $44,660 | $76,910 |

## How the work meets

A fictional neighborhood magazine covers a market reopening. The photographer captures the entrance line and open stalls and logs the time. The writer interviews the market manager and vendors, verifies the time shown in the photos and asks for comparable attendance figures. The editor pairs a captioned image with what the reporting supports.

## How to enter

BLS lists a high school diploma or equivalent and moderate-term on-the-job training as typical for photographers. For writers and authors it lists a bachelor’s degree and long-term on-the-job training. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Photography follows site access, changing conditions and image deadlines. Writing also needs time for interviews, fact checks and editorial feedback. Ask how the team reconciles conflicting image and text details.

## Moving between roles

Photographers can use observation and visual sequencing in writing and add interviewing, documentation and drafting. Writers can bring story planning to photography and learn exposure, lighting and image editing.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock record: the photo shows an entrance line at 10 a.m. The operator claims record attendance but has no comparison figures, and a stallholder can report only personal observations. Spend ten minutes each on a caption, two publishable sentences and a list of the evidence still needed.
