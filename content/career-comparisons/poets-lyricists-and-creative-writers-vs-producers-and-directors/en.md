# Poets, Lyricists and Creative Writers vs. Producers and Directors: Shortening a Voice-Over

## What each role delivers

Poets, lyricists and creative writers choose forms, develop themes and revise their work. Producers and directors may manage resources or make creative decisions. This example concerns narration against picture.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Poets, Lyricists and Creative Writers | Producers and Directors |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Narration and revision notes | Scene plan, review notes and production decisions |
| Basis | Voice, meaning and rhythm | Visual information, performance and runtime |

## How the work meets

The opening narration of a fictional short film runs past the available picture. The director marks the narrative turn to preserve and the details already visible. The writer submits two shorter approaches. Production has time for one recording session, so the team decides what to compare and records the selected version.

## Work conditions

Writing can follow drafts and feedback. Production and direction can involve table reads, filming and reviews. Ask how narration changes after the picture is locked.

## Moving between roles

Writers can bring narrative pacing to directing and learn performance direction and visual storytelling, or build schedule and resource skills for producing. Producers and directors can bring production context to writing and add drafting and revision.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock edit: the read takes 28 seconds, the picture allows 20, the story turn must stay and the location is already visible. Spend ten minutes each on an edit, review criteria and the recording and approval plan.
