# Shampooers vs Spa Managers

## Serve one guest or organize the shift

A shampooer prepares the station, checks what the guest expects, and washes and rinses hair. A spa manager balances staff, facilities, and bookings so the whole shift runs. Ask whether you prefer hands-on service or deciding what to do when several appointments need the same resource.

## Compare the work products

| Work | Shampooer | Spa manager |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focus | Guest and station | Staff, facilities, bookings, and supplies |
| Handoff | Guest preferences, station readiness, and unresolved requests | Updated schedule, equipment follow-up, and shift reconciliation |
| Decision | When to pause or refer a request | How to use available capacity, and when to escalate a change |

## The shampoo station goes out of service

The Ritz-Carlton, Dallas describes an appointment-based hotel spa with a hair salon. In a fictional property with that setup, a shampooer notices a leaking station and reports that two booked guests have yet to start. The spa manager checks which station and staff member are available, asks the front desk to confirm revised times, and assigns a facilities follow-up before the station returns to service. At close, the manager checks that any rescheduled or canceled service is reflected in the day's records. ## Check Texas scope and build relevant experience

BLS lists short-term on-the-job training and no formal educational credential as typical for shampooers. Texas TDLR says shampooing and conditioning do not require a license in that state. Ask the employer whether duties extend to services with their own rules. For spa management, O*NET places the role in Job Zone Three, a medium-preparation category. Start with front desk or shift coordination and show that you can schedule staff, follow up on a facility issue, and reconcile bookings. Confirm the employer's education and experience requirements. ## Check standing time, peak coverage, and pay scope

O*NET respondents in shampooing report extended standing. Ask how long appointments run and who resets the station between guests. Managers oversee staff schedules and equipment checks. Ask who handles evenings, weekends, and failed equipment. BLS lists a U.S. May 2025 median annual wage of $32,600 for shampooers. O*NET reports a broader personal-service-manager wage series that does not isolate spa managers. Compare actual manager offers by hours, team size, and responsibilities. ## Bring one useful record and build the next

From shampooing, carry over clear station handoffs and practice a revised booking schedule with staffing and customer callbacks. From management, use your knowledge of service standards and ask a trainer to check your guest intake, shampoo routine, and station reset.

## Try a 30-minute rescheduling exercise

Assume station B goes out of service at 3:00 p.m. Guests A and B are both booked for 20 minutes. Station A is free at 3:00 and 3:25, and guest B must leave by 3:20. Spend 12 minutes writing a station handoff with the status of both guests. Spend 13 minutes scheduling guest B first and drafting a 3:25 offer for guest A. Use five minutes to check that neither guest was double-booked and record who can clear station B for use.
