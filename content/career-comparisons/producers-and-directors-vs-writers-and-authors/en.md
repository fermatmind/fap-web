# Producers and Directors vs. Writers and Authors: Rewriting After a Location Falls Through

## What each role delivers

Producers and directors may manage production or guide creative work. Writers and authors draft and revise text. This example concerns a writer revising a script.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Producers and Directors | Writers and Authors |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Approved scene split and shoot plan | Revised script and continuity note |
| Basis | Location, performance, schedule and shots | Motivation, clues and scene logic |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $90,360 | $76,910 |

National medians; local and starting pay vary. [BLS Table 1.2, rows 27-2012 and 27-3043](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## How the work meets

A fictional crew loses a train station location. The producer gives the team an available location and time. The director asks to preserve the tension of a first meeting. The writer relocates a necessary clue to a diner scene. The director runs a read-through, and production confirms the revised schedule and script version.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree for both occupations. It lists less than five years of related experience for producers and directors and long-term on-the-job training for writers and authors. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Production may follow locations, cast availability and shoot dates. Writing may follow notes and revision deadlines. Ask who identifies the locked script.

## Moving between roles

Moving into writing, use production constraints and build character, story and revision skills. Moving into production or directing, use story judgment and add scheduling or camera and performance direction.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock brief: the station is unavailable, the diner is available for half a day and the first meeting and a ticket must remain. Spend ten minutes each on the production constraints, moving the clue and what to confirm in a read and revised schedule.
