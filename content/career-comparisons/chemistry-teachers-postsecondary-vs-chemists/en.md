# Postsecondary Chemistry Teachers vs. Chemists: One Data Sheet, Two Deliverables

## What each role delivers

Postsecondary chemistry teachers teach chemistry, supervise student laboratory work and grade reports. Chemists analyze compounds, develop methods or perform quality tests for assigned projects.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Chemistry Teachers, Postsecondary | Chemists |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Coursework and lab-report assessment | Analytical or method report |
| Basis | Learning goals and student work | Samples, method and quality records |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $93,250 | $91,240 |

## How the work meets

A fictional sample table has one result with no unit. An instructor uses it in a report critique. A chemist logs the missing field for the designated reviewer and leaves the affected result unresolved until it can be verified.

## How to enter

BLS lists a doctoral or professional degree as typical for postsecondary chemistry teachers and a bachelor’s degree as typical for chemists. Check academic appointment, research specialty or analytical requirements with the employer. [BLS occupation rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Teaching follows terms, class labs and grading. Analytical work follows samples and project deadlines. Ask what raw records you own and who reviews findings.

## Moving between roles

To move from teaching into analytical work, bring data explanation and lab records and add method validation and quality documentation. To move into teaching, build course and assessment skills from your chemistry work.
