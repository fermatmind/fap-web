# Information Security Analyst vs Penetration Testers

## Different reasons to open a case

An information security analyst reviews signals from operating systems and documents whether an event needs follow-up. A penetration tester assesses an agreed scope under authorization and reports findings and limitations. The analyst’s work begins with an observed event or monitoring need. The tester’s deliverable answers a defined assessment assignment.

## Compare the work

| | Information security analyst | Penetration tester |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Event assessment and escalation record | Assessment findings and recommendations |
| Setting | Monitoring or incident response team | Internal assessment team or consultancy |
| Entry preparation | Related systems experience and analytical writing | Systems knowledge, scope discipline and evidence reporting |

[BLS analyst profile](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/information-security-analysts.htm) · [O*NET penetration testers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/15-1299.04)

## Two records go into a monthly review

In a fictional organization, an analyst has an account notice with an incomplete explanation. They send a query to the record owner and leave the classification open. Separately, the assessment team submits a report with one item outside its agreed scope, clearly identified as unassessed. The incident lead owns the notice’s disposition. The assessment sponsor decides whether a separate authorized assignment is needed.

## Preparing in the United States

BLS describes a bachelor’s degree in computing or a related field and related experience as typical for analysts. O*NET places penetration testers in Job Zone Four, where a four-year degree is common and substantial related preparation is typical across the occupational group. For analyst jobs, ask how junior staff learn triage and reporting. For testing jobs, ask how new staff learn to document scope and findings under supervision. [BLS analyst entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/information-security-analysts.htm) · [O*NET assessment entry](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/15-1299.04)

## Ask how the work arrives

Ask the analyst team who covers urgent referrals and how cases pass between shifts. Ask the assessment team how much work is booked in advance, how reports are reviewed and who confirms changes to the assignment.

## Moving between roles

An analyst can bring observation timelines and escalation notes to assessments, then learn scope documentation and writing findings for a project audience. A tester can bring concise reporting to monitoring work, then learn the organization’s normal operating patterns, case prioritization and shift handoffs.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a fictional register with four rows. N1 is an open notice missing owner confirmation. N2 has confirmation and reviewer disposition. R1 is an unassessed item outside the written scope. R2 is a reviewed finding within scope. Spend ten minutes routing and ten drafting an incident note and assessment referral. Check in the last ten minutes: N1 goes to the record owner, N2 is filed, R1 goes to the assessment sponsor for a scope decision, and R2 stays in the findings report.
