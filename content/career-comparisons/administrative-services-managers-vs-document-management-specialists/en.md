# Administrative Services Managers vs. Document Management Specialists: Who Makes Files Findable?

## Moving the files and making them useful

Administrative services managers can coordinate a migration so each department knows when to switch and how to keep working. Document management specialists make sure files can be classified and retrieved under the approved access rules. An on-time move can still leave people opening the wrong version.

## Compare the migration deliverables

| Check | Administrative Services Managers | Document Management Specialists |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare | Department contacts, cutover schedule, and notices | File types, metadata, and proposed access mapping |
| Verify | Teams know which workflow to use | Searches return the expected versions |
| Work with | Department users and service owners | Records owners and IT staff |

U.S. occupational task references · [O*NET 11-3012.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-3012.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 15-1299.03](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-1299.03#Tasks)

## Two folders both contain “Final”

An office is moving three departments off a shared drive. The administrative manager gets each department to confirm which files are active and when edits will pause. The document specialist proposes version fields and sample searches, then works with IT to test them. If a query returns last year’s version first, the specialist records the problem and the administrative manager decides whether affected teams need more time before switching.

## Choose preparation you can demonstrate

BLS describes a bachelor’s degree and related experience as typical for administrative services managers. [BLS occupational preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/administrative-services-managers.htm#tab-4) O*NET classifies document management specialists as work that generally requires considerable preparation. [O*NET Job Zone](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-1299.03#JobZone) Job Zones describe a broad level of preparation. Ask the employer which education, training and experience count for the specific job.

If you want office services work, build a cutover plan with owners, communications, and a fallback. If you want document work, assemble a small sample collection and show how a user can find the right version. Ask U.S. employers which document systems they use and whether they expect experience in records, content management, or system implementation.

## Find out where decisions sit

The administrative day can be shaped by departmental availability and cutover deadlines. Document work can involve longer stretches of classification review and testing failed searches with system staff. Ask the administrative team how many departments you would coordinate at once. Ask the document team who owns the classification scheme and who can approve access changes. Those answers shape whether you spend your day on meetings, review, or implementation.

## Switch by showing the missing skill

Add metadata rules and failed search examples to a migration schedule if you want to work in document management. Add user training, named owners, and a delayed cutover plan to a taxonomy project if you want to work in administration.

## Try a 30-minute file migration

Use three fictional department cards. Purchasing can pause edits Tuesday, Finance only after Wednesday close, and Administration can pause either day. Use three file cards: Purchasing Final 2025 retired, Purchasing Final 2026 approved, and Finance Final 2026 with unconfirmed access. Spend ten minutes scheduling the switch, fifteen writing two searches and their expected results, and five identifying who must confirm access.
