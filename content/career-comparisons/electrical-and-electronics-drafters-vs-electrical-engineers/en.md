# Electrical and Electronics Drafter vs. Electrical Engineer

## Documenting a design and deciding how it should change

Electrical and electronics drafters prepare usable drawings and check that identifiers and references agree across sheets. Electrical engineers evaluate changes against system requirements and define what needs to be verified. They can both work on drawings, especially when a revision exposes a design question.

## Compare the deliverables

| Work | Electrical and electronics drafter | Electrical engineer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starts with | Existing sheets and change markups | Functional requirements and design constraints |
| Produces | Revised drawings and a record of affected references | Design rationale and verification requirements |
| Handoff | Flags a conflict for review | Resolves the design question and identifies affected work |

BLS reported U.S. median annual wages of $120,630 for electrical engineers and $76,870 for electrical and electronics drafters in May 2025. These figures describe occupation-wide pay, including experienced workers. ## One part has two identifiers

A fictional team revises a control cabinet. The drafter notices a component labeled differently on the layout and the wiring drawing and flags both sheets. The engineer determines whether it is a labeling mistake or an incomplete design change, then identifies the affected drawings and verification documents. The drafter updates the set after clarification, and the project’s authorized reviewer handles approval.

## U.S. education and first experience

The Bureau of Labor Statistics describes community college or technical school as a typical route into drafting. An associate degree is common, and some people prepare through a certificate or diploma. For electrical engineering, a related engineering bachelor’s degree is typical. Internships and cooperative education can build practical design and testing experience. [BLS drafting preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/drafters.htm#tab-4) · [BLS engineering preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/electrical-and-electronics-engineers.htm#tab-4)

## Ask how revisions fit into the week

Drafters generally work at computers in offices and may visit jobsites. Electrical engineers may work in offices and visit sites to observe equipment or investigate a problem. Ask drafting teams who sends drawing changes and how they coordinate simultaneous markups. Ask engineering teams how time is split among design, testing, and field coordination, and who resolves conflicting requirements. [BLS engineering work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/electrical-and-electronics-engineers.htm#tab-3) · [BLS comparison work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/drafters.htm#tab-3)

## Moving between the roles

A drafter can show how they found a cross-reference problem and kept a drawing set consistent. To move toward engineering, build experience comparing design options and defining how to verify them. An engineer moving toward drafting can bring design interpretation skills and develop deeper proficiency in drawing conventions, revision controls, and release procedures.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Write two fictional sheet summaries that label the same part K1 and K2 and add a note about a change in function. Spend ten minutes listing the conflicts, ten writing questions for the designer, and ten outlining which documents need review and who needs to receive the resolved change. Use paper records only.
