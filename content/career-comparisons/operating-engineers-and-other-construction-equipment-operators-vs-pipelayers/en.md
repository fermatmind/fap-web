# Construction Equipment Operators vs. Pipelayers: Where the Work Area Ends and the Pipe Run Begins

## What each role delivers

Construction equipment operators perform assigned machine work such as grading or excavation. Pipelayers lay out and join pipe sections and record grade and connections against the plans.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Operating Engineers and Other Construction Equipment Operators | Pipelayers |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Machine work and shift note | Installed pipe run and grade record |
| Checks | Area and equipment status | Segment, grade and joint |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $59,850 | $49,000 |

## How the work meets

On a fictional drainage project, the operator’s log confirms machine work in Area A. The pipelayer sees that A3 has no grade check in the pipe record. Each reports the status of their own work, and the designated reviewer takes the open pipe item.

## How to enter

BLS lists high school or equivalent and moderate-term on-the-job training for the equipment occupation, and no formal educational credential and short-term on-the-job training for pipelayers as typical. Ask about the assigned machine or pipe setting and how supervision works. [BLS rows 47-2073 and 47-2151](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Equipment work follows machine and area assignments. Pipelayers rely on segment and measurement records. Ask which crew gets the first handoff and who receives unresolved grade questions.

## Moving between roles

Moving into pipelaying, bring your area records and learn pipe plans and grade documentation. Moving toward equipment, use your segment planning and train for the target machine and task.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use a fictional work log: Area A machine work is complete, A3 has no grade check, and B has not been turned over. Take ten minutes each on a machine handoff, a pipe review request and the conditions for the next crew. Keep it on paper.
