# Atmospheric and Space Scientist vs. Statistician: Comparing Records After a Weather Station Move

## What each role delivers

Atmospheric and space scientists study atmospheric processes using observations and models. Statisticians design comparisons, analyze data and report inference limits. Both roles may build models, depending on the research question.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Atmospheric and Space Scientists | Statisticians |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Observation interpretation, station history and atmospheric findings | Comparison plan, estimates and uncertainty |
| Basis | Terrain, observing conditions and weather context | Coverage, missing data, assumptions and validation |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $99,070 | $105,650 |

## How the work meets

A fictional research group reviews a temperature station that moved. The atmospheric scientist records changes in elevation, obstructions and instruments and identifies a possible reference station. The statistician checks the time series for breaks and missing records and compares analyses with and without the transition period. The report states where site effects and regional changes remain unresolved.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for atmospheric and space scientists and a master’s degree for statisticians. Ask which research problems and deliverables the position owns. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Atmospheric work may follow observation or forecasting schedules. Statistical work may follow data freezes, quality checks and reporting dates. Ask who reruns results when records are revised.

## Moving between roles

Atmospheric scientists can bring time series and physical context into statistics and add study design and sensitivity analysis. Statisticians can bring model checking into atmospheric work and add atmospheric processes and observation methods.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock records: a station moved midyear, one week is missing around the move, and a nearby reference has continuous data at a different elevation. Spend ten minutes each on comparable windows, statistical checks and a conclusion with evidence needs.
