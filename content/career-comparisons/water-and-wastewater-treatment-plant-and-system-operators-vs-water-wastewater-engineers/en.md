# Water and wastewater treatment plant and system operators vs Water/Wastewater Engineers

## Keep the plant running or design the change

Operators follow the plant’s status on shift and make sure the next crew can see what happened. Water/wastewater engineers turn performance needs and site conditions into design or review documents. Choose operations if you prefer live monitoring and handoffs. Choose engineering if you want to test design assumptions and document decisions.

## Compare two work products

| Compare | Plant and system operators | Water/wastewater engineers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inputs | Shift readings and equipment status | Operating history and project requirements |
| Setting | Plant rounds and control room | Office design, project coordination and site checks |
| Handoff | Operating log and open items | Design basis, review notes and evidence requirements |

[O*NET operators](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/51-8031.00) · [O*NET engineers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/17-2051.02)

## What a retrofit review needs from each person

EPA reports that Eloy, Arizona, replaced equipment including blowers and an aeration control panel. In an illustrative handoff for a similar project, the operator attaches the shift logs and flags a missing period in the equipment record. The engineer compares the usable data with the project’s design assumptions and identifies which acceptance checks still need evidence. The designated project team reviews acceptance under its procedures. [EPA Eloy case study](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2026-02/wastewater-treatment-plant-rehab-and-energy-savings-project.pdf)

## Check the facility and the engineering assignment

BLS describes high school or equivalent education and extensive on-the-job training for operators. In Arizona, check the appropriate operator certificate for the facility type and grade. For engineers, the BLS civil engineering profile gives a bachelor’s in civil engineering or a related field as typical. Ask who supervises junior design work and which documents require an Arizona registered professional to sign and seal. [BLS operators](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/production/water-and-wastewater-treatment-plant-and-system-operators.htm) · [Arizona operator certification](https://azdeq.gov/ensuring-your-operator-has-correct-certification-facility) · [BLS civil engineers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/civil-engineers.htm) · [Arizona registration board rules](https://apps.azsos.gov/public_services/Title_04/4-30.pdf)

## Compare coverage on nights and at startup

Operators may work rotating shifts that include nights or weekends. Ask how absences are covered and which tasks occupy a shift. For engineering, ask how often design work requires site visits or startup support. Compare base pay, shift differentials and travel at similar levels in the same market. [BLS operations](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/production/water-and-wastewater-treatment-plant-and-system-operators.htm) · [BLS engineering](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/civil-engineers.htm)

## Use a shared plant record to move between roles

An operator can bring facility trends and handoff experience to engineering support while learning fluid mechanics, drawings and design review. An engineer can bring design records to operations support while learning shift procedures and the applicable operator certification pathway.

## Try a thirty-minute paper review

Use fictional records for unit R1. Last Tuesday has readings from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. This Tuesday has the same window with readings missing from 11 a.m. to 1 p.m. Both summaries say complete. Spend 12 minutes drafting the operator handoff. Spend 13 minutes identifying the shared recorded hours in an engineering note and writing what still needs verification. Use five minutes to identify who can retrieve the records and who reviews the resulting comparison.
