# Light truck drivers vs. postal service clerks

## A load handoff and a retail transaction

Light truck drivers check the load and document delivery. Postal service clerks handle mailing transactions, assess postage and answer questions at the counter. The same facility can be a delivery stop for one worker and a retail workplace for the other.

## Compare the work

| Item | Light truck driver | Postal service clerk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deliverable | A verified load and delivery or discrepancy record | A mailing transaction and its acceptance or payment record |
| Work setting to ask about | Routes, receiving docks, warehouses and customer sites | A postal counter and supporting work area |
| Typical U.S. entry education | High school diploma or equivalent | No formal educational credential in the BLS occupation classification |
| Typical on-the-job training | Short-term | Short-term |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $44,860 | $62,130 |

Wages cover nonfarm wage and salary workers and exclude self-employment. ## A supply load arrives while a customer mails a parcel

At a fictional postal facility, the shipping paper lists five empty totes and four arrive. The driver checks the count with the authorized receiver, documents the shortage and sends it to dispatch. At the counter, the postal clerk weighs a customer's parcel, checks the destination, explains eligible options and records the service after the customer chooses. Dispatch controls the freight follow-up. The clerk handles the retail record under the facility's procedures.

## Getting started in the U.S.

BLS identifies a driver's license and clean driving record for delivery truck drivers. Verify what the specific vehicle and assignment require. For a postal clerk opening, the general USPS minimum age is 18 at appointment. Some positions allow applicants aged 16 or older who have graduated from high school or whose local school authority certifies that they ended formal education for adequate reasons. USPS also has limited authorized exceptions and restrictions for certain assignments. See [USPS EL-312 §513.2](https://about.usps.com/handbooks/el312/el312c5_003.htm) for the full rule. Applicants must be able to pass the criminal background check, drug screening, and medical assessment. Check the posting for any role-specific assessment or driving requirement. The education category in the table describes the occupation; it does not replace postal hiring eligibility. Practice explaining services and checking a mock transaction if the opening includes counter work. ## Ask about the pace

A driver can ask how dispatch handles delays at a receiving dock and when a shortage must be documented. A clerk can ask when customer volume peaks, how complex transactions are escalated and who checks records at the end of a shift.

## Moving between the roles

From driving, bring a redacted shortage report and receiving handoff, then learn mailing services, point-of-sale procedures and customer explanations. From the counter, bring a redacted discrepancy log and shift handoff, then develop load verification, dispatch coordination and the driving qualifications for the assignment.

## A 30-minute tabletop exercise

Use fictional records. The shipping paper lists five totes and only four arrive. A counter customer also has one eligible parcel and a $25 budget. The mock tariff offers two-day service at $12 and next-day service at $20; the customer needs next-day delivery. Spend ten minutes documenting the freight count and sending a dispatch note, ten selecting and preparing a quote for the counter service, and ten summarizing the two handoffs. Identify who confirms receipt, who accepts the quote and who follows up.
