# Cost Estimators vs. First-Line Construction Supervisors: The Change Request

## What each role delivers

Cost estimators quantify the resources and cost implied by the scope. In construction, first-line supervisors turn the approved scope into crew assignments and feed field constraints back into the estimate.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Cost Estimators | First-Line Supervisors of Construction Trades and Extraction Workers |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Quantities, rates and assumptions | Crew plan and verified field constraints |
| Change | Cost and time estimate | Feasibility and updated assignments |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $78,740 | $79,920 |

## How the work meets

A fictional corridor renovation adds night work. The supervisor checks who is available and which zones can still be used during the day. The estimator records labor quantities, the applicable rate and any assumptions. The authorized project lead decides whether to approve the changes before the supervisor updates the crew plan.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree and moderate-term on-the-job training as typical for cost estimators. For first-line construction and extraction supervisors it lists high school or equivalent and five or more years of related work experience. [BLS rows 13-1051 and 47-1011](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Estimators spend more time comparing plans and prices. Supervisors respond to crew and sequence changes at the site. Ask how actual labor records make it back to the estimator and who issues approved revisions.

## Moving between roles

Moving into estimating, pair your field labor records with takeoff and rate practice. Moving into crew supervision, bring scope planning and learn assignment, conflict resolution and safety responsibilities in the field.

## Try it in 30 minutes

On paper, assume three workers are available at night, the proposal calls for four, and the rate sheet has no night rate. Spend ten minutes on a field feasibility note, ten on an estimating assumptions sheet, and ten marking what needs approval or confirmation.
