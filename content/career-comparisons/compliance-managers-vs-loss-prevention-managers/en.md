# Compliance Manager vs. Loss Prevention Manager

## Inventory losses and compliance controls

A loss prevention manager investigates discrepancies and works with store teams to reduce losses. A compliance manager checks whether applicable procedures are being followed and tracks corrective action. Both may examine the same inventory adjustment for different reasons.

## Compare the work products

| Work | Compliance manager | Loss prevention manager |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting point | A procedure exception or an issue that needs review | An unexpected inventory variance |
| Deliverable | Control findings, corrective actions, and follow-up | Reconciliation notes and store-level actions |
| Collaboration | Process and approval owners | Store operations, inventory, and receiving teams |

## A store count comes up eight units short

At a fictional retailer, the loss prevention manager asks receiving and inventory staff to reconcile the count against source records and document which differences remain unexplained. The compliance manager spots two inventory adjustments without recorded approvals, tests a sample of similar adjustments, and asks the process owner to address any recurring gap. The original discrepancy remains open until the records support a conclusion.

## Preparing for U.S. openings

For compliance management, build experience checking processes and following up on findings in a compliance support or internal controls role. For loss prevention management, build experience in store operations, inventory, or loss prevention and take on responsibility for reconciling counts and coaching store teams. O*NET places both occupations in Job Zone Four, where a bachelor’s degree and relevant experience are common. Check the requirements for the specific opening. [O*NET Compliance Managers preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-9199.02#JobZone) · [O*NET Loss Prevention Managers preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-9199.08#JobZone)

## Ask where and when the work happens

For loss prevention, ask how many locations the job covers and when counts or store visits occur. For compliance, ask how reviews are scheduled, how often they involve store visits, and who assigns follow-up when an issue comes in between reviews.

## Switching between the roles

A loss prevention manager can use a documented variance review to show evidence handling, then build experience interpreting requirements and testing controls across locations. A compliance manager can bring an inventory-control review, then practice reconciling receiving, transfers, and count records and turning findings into changes store teams can sustain.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Make up three receiving slips, a count sheet, and two adjustment records. Spend ten minutes reconciling quantities and marking unexplained differences, ten minutes writing questions for the store team, and ten minutes documenting any missing approval and a corrective action you could verify.
