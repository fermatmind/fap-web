# Derrick Operators, Oil and Gas vs Roustabouts, Oil and Gas

## Operating equipment and supporting the crew

Derrick operators set up rig equipment and operate and monitor drilling-fluid pumps, check their condition, and prepare drilling mud reports. Roustabouts help assemble and repair equipment and carry out support tasks such as cleaning work areas. The crew may assign both roles work around the same equipment.

## A parts delivery needs to match the equipment

At a fictional drill site, a crew prepares for scheduled maintenance of circulation equipment. The roustabout checks incoming part labels against the crew list and reports what arrived and what is missing. The derrick operator records the equipment ID and circulation status, then checks whether the parts handoff matches that assignment. The drilling lead reviews both records before scheduling the work under the example procedure.

## What to check before taking a job

BLS says U.S. roustabout jobs typically accept entrants without related experience. New oil and gas workers may shadow experienced staff and begin with simpler supervised tasks, such as organizing tools or helping with supplies. That work can build the field experience used to move into derrick operations. Ask the employer what experience counts and how it assesses readiness for a derrick assignment. [BLS oil and gas entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/oil-and-gas-workers.htm#tab-4)

## What changes the workweek

BLS notes that some remote or offshore oil and gas crews work long shifts away from home. For derrick work, ask how the crew hands over monitoring across shifts. For roustabout work, ask whether the shift stays in one area or moves among equipment, cleanup, and supply assignments. [BLS oil and gas work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/oil-and-gas-workers.htm#tab-3)

## Supervised ways to change roles

If you are already a roustabout, ask whether the crew offers supervised circulation-record checks and progression to a derrick assignment. Use a fictional traceability record to demonstrate documentation skills, and learn the rig equipment and crew responsibilities. Moving toward support work, bring a fictional equipment status report and build experience with supervised label and parts-list checks.

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use a different fictional batch. D-43 records circulation status for pump P7. M-43 says a part arrived with a label for P8. U-43 requires both records to match the equipment before maintenance preparation can close. Spend ten minutes listing what each record covers, ten minutes writing the equipment handoff and parts discrepancy report, and ten minutes telling the lead what remains open and why. Work from the records only.
