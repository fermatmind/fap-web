# Derrick Operators, Oil and Gas vs Dredge Operators

## Circulation at a drill site and material removal in a waterway

Oil and gas derrick operators set up derrick equipment and operate and monitor drilling-fluid circulation pumps. Dredge operators remove material from waterways to excavate or maintain channels. Their shift records describe different work and results.

## Work, U.S. preparation, and pay

| Item | Oil and gas derrick operators | Dredge operators |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | Circulation and pump status, drilling mud reports | Dredging segment progress and equipment status |
| Setting | Drilling sites | Waterways and dredging crews |
| Typical U.S. entry education | No formal educational credential | High school diploma or equivalent |
| Typical related experience | Less than five years | None |
| Typical on-the-job training | Short-term | Moderate-term |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $58,620 | $49,640 |

National wage and salary medians exclude self-employment. ## Two crews report to a coastal project

A fictional project has a drilling crew and a separate channel dredging crew. The derrick operator gives the drilling lead the circulation and pump status and notes that a period of the original log is missing. The dredge operator reports that the crew finished work in the east segment and that the survey confirmation is still pending. The coordinator keeps the circulation gap and the unconfirmed dredging result separate. Each lead signs off on their own record.

## What to check before taking a job

Ask drilling employers which rig duties they assign to new staff, what prior work counts, and how equipment training is supervised. Ask dredging employers which equipment and waterways the crew covers and whether the role includes a separate vessel assignment. Check training and any applicable credentials for the actual job.

## What changes a shift

BLS describes long shifts and periods away from home for some remote or offshore oil and gas crews. Ask whether the derrick assignment involves a remote rotation and how long a tour lasts. Dredging takes place on waterways, and O*NET includes outdoor weather exposure in the work context. Ask what weather interrupts the assignment, whether the crew stays aboard, and where shifts change. [BLS oil and gas work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/oil-and-gas-workers.htm#tab-3) · [O*NET dredging work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-7031.00#WorkContext)

## A supervised starting point in either direction

Moving into dredging, use a fictional pump log to show careful recordkeeping and start with supervised segment and equipment documentation. Learn how crews hand off dredging status. Moving into derrick operations, use a fictional dredge equipment log and start with supervised circulation record review. Learn rig responsibilities and the assigned equipment.

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use a different fictional shift. D-46 reports normal pump status but the circulation log lacks the final hour. W-46 includes a survey receipt for the west segment, but the open order is for the east segment. Spend ten minutes marking what each record establishes, ten minutes requesting the missing log and matching segment record, and ten minutes giving the coordinator two open items with separate owners. Use the records only.
