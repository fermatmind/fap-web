# Substation and Relay Repairer vs. Electrician

## Utility equipment and electrical installations

Powerhouse, substation and relay repairers inspect equipment and interpret test or maintenance records. Electricians install or maintain wiring and electrical equipment from plans. The jobs can overlap in troubleshooting, so check which equipment and work the employer assigns.

## Compare the work records

| Item | Powerhouse, substation and relay repairer | Electrician |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verify | Utility equipment identifiers and maintenance history | Installation drawings, wiring and equipment locations |
| Document | Equipment condition, test records and repair findings | Installation or repair scope and deviations from drawings |
| Coordinate | The designated equipment owner | The designated construction or facility lead |

[O*NET 49-2095.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/49-2095.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 47-2111.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/47-2111.00#Tasks)

## A plant renovation has inconsistent drawing references

A fictional plant renovation finds that the substation register and building wiring plan cite different drawing revisions. The repairer checks the equipment identifiers and maintenance scope. The electrician checks the approved drawing for the building installation. Each records the discrepancy so the project lead can assign a drawing review and determine the next step under site procedures.

## U.S. education and training

The BLS detailed table assigns the repair occupation a postsecondary nondegree award, less than five years of related work experience and moderate-term on-the-job training. It assigns electricians a high school diploma or equivalent, no related work experience and apprenticeship. Those are typical categories. Check the applicable state or local electrician licensing rules and the employer's utility equipment training and authorization. BLS reports that most states require electricians to be licensed. Completing apprenticeship training does not remove the licensing requirement. Check the relevant state licensing agency or local electrical licensing office before independent work. [BLS detailed education and training table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/education-and-training-by-occupation.htm) · [BLS electrician entry and licensure](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/electricians.htm#tab-4)

## Ask what sets the schedule

Repair applicants can ask how outage windows, equipment records and shift handoffs are scheduled. Electrician applicants can ask how installation and maintenance work are allocated and who communicates drawing changes.

## Moving between the roles

Repairers can bring drawing literacy and equipment records to electrical installation, then learn installation codes and verify the local apprenticeship or licensing rules. Electricians can bring documentation and wiring knowledge to utility repair work, then pursue employer training in the assigned equipment and protection records.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use fictional drawing indexes, an equipment register and a work order. Spend ten minutes describing the equipment revision mismatch, ten marking the building installation scope that needs confirmation, and ten naming the recipients and documents needed to resolve each issue. Use records only.
