# School and Career Counselor vs. Social and Community Service Manager

## One role advises students, the other oversees services

Educational, guidance, and career counselors and advisors help students choose courses and career options and overcome barriers to participation. Social and community service managers coordinate programs, staff and resources and assess whether services are meeting their goals. A manager may also handle an individual referral.

## Compare the decisions and records

| Item | School or career counselor and advisor | Social and community service manager |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Check | What the student wants, can attend and qualifies for | What participation, staffing and budget allow |
| Produce | Advising options and a follow-up date | A staffing plan, service proposal or program evaluation |
| Coordinate | The student and the staff responsible for the referral | Program staff and people authorized to approve changes |

## Students cannot get to the only career workshop

A fictional school refers students to a nonprofit's job-readiness workshop. Several students have a class that ends too late to attend. The advisor can check alternative resources for each student and pass along an authorized summary of the scheduling barrier. The manager checks demand, facility hours and staff coverage, then proposes whether to add an evening session. The organization approves any change through its own process, and the advisor follows up with students.

## U.S. education and experience

BLS describes a master's degree and state-issued credential as typical for school counselors, with some states licensing career counselors. Academic and career advising requirements vary by employer. Social and community service managers typically need at least a bachelor's degree and related experience. BLS assigns this occupation the less-than-five-years experience category and no on-the-job training in its national table. Some management positions require a master's. The national BLS table assigns the combined education and career counselor category a master’s degree and no related work experience or on-the-job training. Compare that broad category with the requirements for the position you want. ## Ask how the workweek is divided

Ask an advising employer how it manages student appointments and follow-up at busy points in the academic calendar. Ask a program employer how it divides time among staff supervision, finance, service evaluation and work with partner organizations.

## Moving between advising and program management

Counselors can bring interviewing and referral knowledge to program management, then build staffing, budgeting and evaluation skills. Managers can bring knowledge of service eligibility and partner coordination to advising, then learn academic pathways, individual advising and applicable student-record procedures.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a fictional Wednesday workshop with ten seats. Four students are unable to attend until after six. A room is available in the evening, but staffing is unconfirmed. Spend ten minutes writing an advising option for one student, ten minutes listing what a manager needs to confirm, and ten marking the approval owner, what can be shared in aggregate and the follow-up date.
