# Audiologists vs. Occupational Therapists: Who Checks What After a Missed Queue Call?

## What each clinician checks

An audiologist evaluates hearing and related concerns and can fit and follow up on hearing devices. An occupational therapist assesses how a person completes an everyday task, including whether the environment makes it harder to proceed.

## The same trip to a service office

Imagine a client who misses their number in a busy public service office. The audiologist asks about the noise and device use at the time and checks hearing and device performance. In a practice setup, the occupational therapist sees whether the client can consult their paperwork and still see the number display, then compares a seat with a clear view. Both document their findings for the next review of the same routine.

## Getting started in the U.S.

Audiologists typically need a Doctor of Audiology degree. Occupational therapists typically need a master's degree in occupational therapy. Both occupations require licensure in every state. Verify the specific requirements with the relevant state board. [BLS audiologist entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/audiologists.htm#tab-4) · [BLS occupational therapy entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/occupational-therapists.htm#tab-4)

## Working conditions

Audiologists may use dedicated test equipment and schedule follow-up checks. Occupational therapists may observe tasks in clinical or community settings. Ask how the team handles referrals and who schedules follow-up.

## Moving between roles

Interviewing, documentation, and explaining findings carry over. Audiology requires training in hearing and device assessment. Occupational therapy requires training in activity analysis and environmental assessment. Verify training and licensure for the destination role.

## Try both in 30 minutes

Use three fictional notes. The client missed one announcement in a noisy room. They could see the display only after changing seats. Their hearing device was not checked that day. Spend 10 minutes writing a note for the audiologist, 10 documenting the task and setup for occupational therapy, and 10 listing what each clinician would check next.
