# Machine Assembler vs. Tool and Die Maker: Tracing a Misalignment to Its Fixture

## What each role delivers

Machine assemblers read specifications, position components and verify the completed build. Tool and die makers fabricate or repair tools, dies and fixtures that production uses, then verify their fit and dimensions.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Engine and Other Machine Assemblers | Tool and Die Makers |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Built machine or subassembly and function check | Tool, die or fixture and dimensional verification |
| Evidence after a fault | Part revision, sequence, measured position and fixture ID | Fixture drawing, locating surfaces, dimensions and fit |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $53,710 | $64,050 |

## How the work meets

In a fictional plant, several machine bases assembled on one fixture show an offset at the same inspection point. An assembler logs the component lot, sequence, measurement and fixture ID. The tool and die maker uses the report to check the locator against the fixture drawing, finds it out of tolerance and returns the corrected fixture with a dimensional report after authorized work. Assembly uses it on a trial build and records a separate machine check.

## How to enter

BLS lists a high school diploma or equivalent and moderate-term on-the-job training as typical for machine assemblers. For tool and die makers, it lists a postsecondary nondegree award and long-term on-the-job training. Ask which drawing and inspection skills the employer checks in training. [BLS Table 1.2](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Assembly may follow a schedule driven by parts and line capacity. Tool and die work may involve one fixture at a time, with repeated measurements. Ask how a fixture hold affects the line and who can return it to service.

## Moving between roles

Moving into tool and die work, bring evidence of how a fixture affected a build and learn fabrication drawings and dimensional measurement. Moving into assembly, bring an eye for fit and learn build sequence and final functional checks.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use mock records for three assemblies with the same offset on one fixture and no results from a second fixture using the same batch. Spend ten minutes writing the assembly handoff, ten listing evidence the tool maker needs and ten identifying separate acceptance records for the fixture and trial build. Use records only.
