# Choreographers vs. Producers and Directors: When a Dance Moves Out of Frame

## What each role delivers

Choreographers develop and revise movement and guide rehearsals. Producers and directors may manage production resources or creative decisions. This example includes the director’s shot plan and the producer’s schedule.

| Output | Revised movement and rehearsal notes | Shot plan and production decisions |
| Basis | Movement, counts and formations | Story priorities, framing, space and shoot time |

## How the work meets

A fixed-camera test for a fictional short film loses a dancer during a transition. The choreographer marks the endpoint that must stay and offers a shorter route. The director compares that route with reframing. Production checks whether moving the camera fits the schedule before the team rehearses the selected option.

## How to enter

BLS lists high school completion or equivalent, five or more years of related experience and long-term on-the-job training for choreographers. It lists a bachelor’s degree and less than five years of related experience as typical for producers and directors. Review whether a posting focuses on directing scenes or managing production. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Choreography can follow rehearsal and shoot calls. Directors coordinate framing and performance, and producers may coordinate several crews. Ask who confirms the shot and time available that day.

## Moving between roles

Choreographers can bring movement staging to directing and add camera and set collaboration skills, or build scheduling and resource skills for producing. Producers and directors can bring production planning and develop movement and rehearsal skills.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock records: the fixed frame loses a dancer at count ten and the venue has 30 minutes left. Spend ten minutes each describing a movement option, a shot option and who must confirm each before another take.
