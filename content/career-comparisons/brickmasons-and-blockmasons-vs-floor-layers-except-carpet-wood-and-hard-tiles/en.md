# Brickmasons and Blockmasons vs. Floor Layers: A Wall Base in the Way

## What each role delivers

Brickmasons and blockmasons lay out and build masonry. In this example, floor layers fit sheet covering at walls and obstructions and check joints.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Brickmasons and Blockmasons | Floor Layers, Except Carpet, Wood, and Hard Tiles |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Wall base and masonry handoff | Sheet fitting and joint check |
| Basis | Masonry boundaries and drawings | Substrate, obstructions and sheet joints |

## How the work meets

A fictional clinic is ready for sheet flooring except at one projecting masonry base. The mason records the location and drawing. The floor layer identifies the affected sheet joint and routes the boundary issue to the site lead.

## How to enter

BLS lists high school or equivalent and apprenticeship for brickmasons and blockmasons. For this floor-laying occupation, it lists no formal educational credential and moderate-term on-the-job training. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Masonry may be checked as each wall section is completed. Floor covering can depend on approved boundaries and substrate readiness. Ask who accepts the substrate and protects finished work.

## Moving between roles

Moving to flooring, bring layout skills and learn substrate checks and joints. Moving to masonry, bring measurement and obstruction checks and learn wall layout and masonry work.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock drawing allows a 3 mm projection. Point A is 2 mm and B is 7 mm. A sheet joint is planned at B. Spend ten minutes each documenting masonry, marking the flooring issue and describing what must be confirmed before release.
