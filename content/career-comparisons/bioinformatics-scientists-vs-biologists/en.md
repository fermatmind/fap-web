# Bioinformatics Scientist vs. Biologist

## Reproducible analysis and biological interpretation

A bioinformatics scientist can turn a biological research question into a computational analysis that colleagues can repeat. A biologist may define observations, design comparisons, and explain what the results support. Both can contribute to research design and data analysis.

## Compare the work products

| Work | Bioinformatics scientist | Biologist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First check | Field definitions, data versions, and transformation rules | Observation definitions and which conditions are comparable |
| Deliverable | Reproducible data table and processing notes | Interpretation and limits on what the observations support |

## A zero means two different things

A fictional plant study combines flowering counts from two teams. One uses zero for an observed plant with no flowers. The other uses zero for an observation that did not take place. The bioinformatics scientist preserves the source values and documents a rule that distinguishes zeros from missing observations. The biologist checks what the teams actually observed and explains which records can inform a comparison of flowering. They record those limits alongside the analysis.

## U.S. education and early experience

O*NET places both occupations in Job Zone Five, where graduate preparation is common. For bioinformatics, try a supervised research or coursework project that documents how a data table was transformed and how someone else can reproduce it. For biology, seek a supervised project where you help design observations and present conclusions with their limits. Read the posting for the degree and research experience it asks for. ## Moving between the jobs

A biologist can bring study design and measurement definitions to bioinformatics, then build skills in data processing, version control, and reproducibility. A bioinformatics scientist can bring data checks and analysis skills to biology, then develop experience defining observations and interpreting results in their study context.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use two fictional rows. On day three, Table A lists zero flowers and defines zero as observed with no flowers. Table B also lists zero but defines it as no observation. Spend ten minutes writing two conversion rules, ten deciding which record can inform a comparison of day-three flowering, and ten making a review table with the original value, its meaning, and the source note.
