# Middle-School CTE Teachers vs. Postsecondary Family and Consumer Sciences Teachers

## What each role delivers

Middle-school CTE teachers use demonstrations and practice to teach technical skills. Postsecondary family and consumer sciences teachers develop college coursework and assess projects in fields such as nutrition or child development.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Career/Technical Education Teachers, Middle School | Family and Consumer Sciences Teachers, Postsecondary |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Classroom activities and skill feedback | College coursework and project evaluation |
| Evidence | Student demonstration | Coursework and research sources |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $65,030 | $75,870 |

## How the work meets

Two fictional lessons use the same sample food label. The middle-school teacher checks whether students distinguish a serving from the whole package. The college instructor assigns two research summaries and asks students to explain what the evidence allows them to conclude.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree and less than five years of related experience as typical for middle-school CTE teachers. For family and consumer sciences teachers, postsecondary, its occupation row lists a doctoral or professional degree as typical. California’s Designated Subjects CTE credential is one example of authorization for named vocational subjects through grade 12. Check the state and school assignment. [California CTC CTE authorization](https://www.ctc.ca.gov/credentials/leaflets/cl-888/) [BLS occupation rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Middle-school work follows class periods and classroom setup. College work includes grading longer analyses and may include supervising projects or laboratories. Ask which kinds of courses the position covers.

## Moving between roles

Moving to college, bring tested teaching activities and build subject research and evidence-based grading skills. Moving to middle school, adapt complex material and learn classroom routines and applicable authorization.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use two fictional labels and a short source summary for each. Spend ten minutes writing a middle-school reading task, ten writing a college evidence comparison, and ten making different scoring criteria. Use paper only.
