# Self-Enrichment Teachers vs. Tutors: Group Activity or Targeted Practice?

## What each role delivers

Self-enrichment teachers organize interest-based activities. Tutors review specific material with learners and assess progress during focused practice.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Self-Enrichment Teachers | Tutors |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Theme-based lesson and learner work | Focused practice and progress note |
| Basis | Group goals and participation | Specific learning gap |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $46,800 | $43,350 |

## How the work meets

A fictional adult community class creates photo stories. The instructor plans a group exercise with a sequence as the output. A tutor separately helps an adult practice explaining the sequence and checks whether they can do it independently.

## How to enter

BLS lists high school or equivalent and less than five years of related experience as typical for self-enrichment teachers. It lists some college without a degree for tutors. Check the employer’s subject and learner requirements. [BLS occupation rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Enrichment work often follows group schedules. Tutoring follows appointments and individual goals. Ask how planning, feedback and cancellations are handled.

## Moving between roles

Moving to tutoring, build a focused practice and follow-up method from your activity plans. Moving to enrichment, build group activities and a session sequence from your feedback skills.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use six fictional photo titles. A group needs to create a story; a separate learner can order the titles but cannot explain why. Spend ten minutes on each plan and ten on separate evidence of progress.
