# Occupational Therapists vs. Speech-Language Pathology Assistants: Who Does What in a Library Practice Session?

## Who owns each plan

An occupational therapist evaluates how a person completes daily activities and decides what activity or environment changes to consider. A speech-language pathology assistant carries out delegated communication practice and documents performance for the supervising speech-language pathologist.

## Compare the work

| Compare | Occupational therapist | Speech-language pathology assistant |
|---|---|---|
| Focus here | Handling a book and card in the checkout sequence | Practicing an assigned way to ask for help and recording prompts |
| Deliverable | Activity assessment and adjustment suggestions | Practice data and a report to the supervisor |

[O*NET 29-1122.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-1122.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 31-9099.01](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/31-9099.01#Tasks)

## The same library goal

Imagine a client practicing how to borrow a book. The occupational therapist watches the client retrieve their library card while holding the book, compares what happens when the card is set out first, and shares the activity findings with the supervising speech-language pathologist. Following that pathologist's protocol, the assistant guides the client through asking a librarian for help, records which attempts needed a prompt, and reports the results to the supervisor.

## Getting started in the U.S.

Occupational therapists typically need a master's degree in occupational therapy and a state license. California is one example for speech-language pathology assistants. California requires an eligible education pathway. For the bachelor's route, the Board may consider 36 weeks of qualifying full-time work in place of the 100-hour fieldwork or clinical experience requirement. Check the Board's out-of-state work criteria if relying on that option. The supervising speech-language pathologist is responsible for services delivered by the assistant. ## Working conditions

The occupational therapist may need time to arrange a practice task and document performance. The assistant may deliver several planned sessions and log results. Ask when the supervisor reviews the records and how to raise concerns that fall outside the written protocol.

## Moving between roles

An assistant can bring observation and documentation experience to occupational therapy study, then build clinical skills in activity and environment assessment. An occupational therapist considering assistant work should verify the destination state's education and registration requirements and clarify who supervises delegated work.

## Try both in 30 minutes

Use three fictional notes. The client cannot find the library card in their bag while holding a book. They complete checkout when the card is set out first. They need a verbal prompt on two of three chances to ask for help. Spend 10 minutes writing the activity note, 10 recording practice results, and 10 drafting a handoff to the supervisor.
