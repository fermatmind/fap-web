# Data Warehousing Specialists vs. Database Architects on a Sales Reporting Project

## Where the work differs

Data warehousing specialists map source records, build loads and check what arrives in the warehouse. Database architects design and document structures that meet business requirements. Both can model data, but each brings a different kind of evidence to the same project.

## Compare the handoffs

| Work product | Data Warehousing Specialists | Database Architects |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Source mapping, load design and reconciliation | Model, relationships and reasons for design choices |

## A shared situation

A store records sales by payment, while an online shop records them by line item. The architect works with business owners to define what one reporting record represents and how refunds relate to orders. The warehouse specialist applies that definition to both sources, compares totals against source records and documents cases that cannot be reconciled. The team resolves those cases before treating the totals as comparable.

## How to prepare

O*NET classifies data warehousing specialists as work that generally requires considerable preparation. [O*NET Job Zone](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-1243.01#JobZone) BLS describes a bachelor’s degree in computing or a related field as typical for database architects. [BLS occupational preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/database-administrators.htm#tab-4) Job Zones describe a broad level of preparation. Ask the employer which education, training and experience count for the specific job.

For warehousing, use a small teaching dataset to show where records came from and how you checked the load. For architecture, make two models for the same business question and explain your choice. Ask employers which teams own reporting and design review.

## What to ask an employer

Architecture work can revolve around designing changes for applications. Warehouse work needs to make scheduled loads adapt to those changes and keep track of source problems. Ask warehouse teams who responds to late loads and which reports have priority. Ask architecture teams who signs off on definitions and which application teams review new models.

## Moving between roles

Move toward architecture by tracing a data quality issue to a design assumption. Move toward warehousing by adding source mappings and repeatable quality checks to a model already in use.

## Try it in 30 minutes

A fictional store reports payment S12 for 100 credits. Online details for S12 are item A at 60 and item B at 40, with a later full refund on B. The shared ID denotes the same transaction. Spend ten minutes defining the reporting record, ten mapping both sources, and ten writing an example check that keeps the summary and detail from being counted as separate sales.
