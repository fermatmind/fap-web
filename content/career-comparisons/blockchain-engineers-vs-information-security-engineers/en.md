# Blockchain Engineers vs Information Security Engineers

## Two engineering responsibilities in one system

Blockchain engineers design shared records and build the applications or integrations that write and verify them. Information security engineers design, implement, and verify controls that protect systems and data. Both can build safeguards. Ask which part of the system the role owns and what evidence it hands over.

## A shared receipt and a restricted attachment

A fictional equipment-rental platform lets Partners A and B verify return receipts. Partner A submits a receipt and an attachment containing a name. Both partners can verify the receipt ID and return status. Before approved deletion, only authorized Partner A staff can view the attachment. An authorized platform custodian handles deletion against a recorded approval. The blockchain engineer hands the security engineer the shared fields, the attachment reference, and the requirement that return status remain verifiable after deletion. The security engineer implements access controls and deletion logging, then hands back the access check results for both partners and the deletion record. The ledger engineer checks the remaining return status, and the data owner signs off on both sets of evidence.

## What to show when applying

For a U.S. blockchain engineering role, ask whether you would own the ledger, an application, or partner integrations. A fictional receipt project can show how you model shared data and verify it after a change. For a security engineering role, ask which controls you would implement. A sample access matrix and verification record can show how you turn a requirement into an enforceable rule and check the outcome.

## What makes the work busy

Ask the blockchain team how partner releases are coordinated and who handles late interface changes. Ask the security team how access changes and recovery drills are scheduled and who takes over failed changes or urgent findings.

## Switching between the roles

A blockchain engineer can bring a redacted data-flow diagram and role verification record when moving into security engineering. Build experience with controls beyond the ledger and with recovery verification. A security engineer can bring evidence of designing and validating access controls, then learn how partners integrate with a ledger and verify shared records.

## Try a 30-minute paper exercise

Use Partner A's fictional receipt R-12, marked returned, and attachment F-12, which contains a made-up name and is present at the start. Spend ten minutes placing fields in the shared record or restricted store. Spend ten minutes specifying the pre-deletion results: authorized Partner A staff can view F-12 and Partner B staff cannot. Use the final ten minutes for the post-deletion checks: staff at both partners can no longer view F-12, both can still verify the returned status of R-12, and the deletion record points to its approval.
