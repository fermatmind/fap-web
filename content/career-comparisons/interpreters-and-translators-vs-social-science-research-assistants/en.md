# Interpreters and Translators vs Social Science Research Assistants

## Carry the meaning across languages or trace how answers were collected

Interpreters keep a live exchange moving across languages. Translators can revise a written question before it reaches participants. Social science research assistants check the resulting records against the study plan. Choose which task you would rather troubleshoot: what a question communicates or how an answer was collected and recorded.

## Compare the work products

| Work | Interpreters and translators | Social science research assistants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Materials | Speech, signing, written questions, or a glossary | Study protocol, instrument versions, and response records |
| Delivery | Live interpretation or translated instrument with queries | Pilot log, data dictionary, or quality checks |
| Review | Flag wording that may change what is asked | Flag records whose collection conditions differ |

## The school survey uses two different time windows

A U.S. Census Bureau methods paper describes committee review for translations of survey instruments, involving both language specialists and survey methodologists. [Census Bureau methods paper](https://www.census.gov/library/working-papers/2009/adrm/ssm2009-09.html)

Imagine a school-district survey pilot. The English item asks about absences in the past 30 days, but its draft Spanish version asks about the previous calendar month. The translator flags the difference and revises the item once the survey lead approves the time window. If a bilingual feedback session uses an interpreter, the interpreter conveys the student's remarks and the reviewer's questions. The research assistant links each pilot response to its instrument version, preserves the original values, and records which cases need a decision before tabulation.

## Check the assignment before choosing training

BLS lists a bachelor's degree as typical entry education for both occupations. Interpreting and translation also require proficiency in at least two languages and the relevant mode of work. For example, California's standard path to Spanish spoken-language court interpreter certification involves a written exam, a bilingual interpreting exam, and enrollment with the Judicial Council. [California court interpreter certification](https://languageaccess.courts.ca.gov/court-interpreters-resources/become-court-interpreter/certified-spoken-language-interpreter) For research support, a survey practicum can give you a work sample: a short pilot log, a data dictionary, and a documented data check. Ask the lab or survey team what methods and software the opening requires. [BLS entry education for both roles](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) · [BLS language and credential requirements](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/interpreters-and-translators.htm)

## Check who sets your schedule and reviews your work

Interpretation assignments may involve irregular hours, and written translation can have tight turnaround. Ask whether preparation is included and how later revisions are handled. O*NET survey responses indicate that extended sitting is common for research assistants. Ask how much field collection is involved and who reviews the data changes. U.S. median annual employee wages in May 2025 were $60,170 for interpreters and translators and $61,990 for social science research assistants. The wage figures exclude self-employed earnings. ## Build evidence for the role you want

From translation, carry over your query log and add an instrument-version table that lets the research team reproduce a check. From research support, use your understanding of survey design, then build a bilingual sample that explains how you preserved the intended time window and response choices.

## Try a 30-minute survey handoff

In a fictional pilot, Q8 asks “How many days of school did you miss in the past 30 days?” Spanish v1 says “el mes pasado.” The study lead confirms a rolling 30 days. Case P01 used v1 and answered 2. Case P02 used the corrected v2 and answered 1. The lead directs you to keep both originals and hold P01 out of the count. Spend 12 minutes revising Q8 and noting the change, 13 minutes building a two-row log of version, original value, and inclusion, and five minutes verifying that P01 still shows 2 but is excluded.
