# Paralegals and legal assistants vs Private detectives and investigators

## What the client receives

A paralegal turns case materials into an organized file, draft or research note for a lawyer to review. A private investigator checks a defined factual question and reports what the available information supports. Both may read the same receipt, but the paralegal prepares it for legal review and the investigator records how it supports or fails to support a fact.

## Tasks, workplaces and entry routes

| | Paralegals and legal assistants | Private detectives and investigators |
|---|---|---|
| Main output | Indexed case file and draft for attorney review | Findings report with sources and open questions |
| Example workplace | Law firm or legal department | Investigation firm or employer’s investigation unit |
| Entry focus | Legal studies and supervised legal support | Related experience and the state rules for employees or business operators |

[BLS paralegals](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/legal/paralegals-and-legal-assistants.htm) · [BLS private investigators](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/protective-service/private-detectives-and-investigators.htm)

## A disputed delivery date

Imagine a California law firm handling a commercial delivery dispute. Its paralegal indexes the contract, receipts and version history and lists missing records for the supervising attorney. The firm’s licensed investigator receives a limited assignment to compare dates on documents the client is authorized to share. The investigator writes a source-linked chronology and flags one date as unresolved. The lawyer decides how to assess and use the material in the matter.

## Training and California requirements

Many U.S. paralegals enter with an associate degree or paralegal studies certificate. California additionally defines qualification routes and requires attorney direction and supervision. One route is a certificate from an ABA-approved paralegal program. The paralegal does not provide legal advice. For the qualified manager of a California licensed private investigation business, BSIS lists age 18 or older, generally three years and at least 6,000 hours of qualifying compensated experience, a written examination and criminal history review. Certain degrees can count toward part of the experience. An employee may work under a licensed investigator’s control without an individual license, but cannot independently take clients, issue reports or bill. Check your target state’s requirements for the duties you intend to perform. ## What to ask employers

Ask the legal team who reviews drafts and how deadlines are assigned when several matters move at once. Ask the investigation employer how a written scope is approved, who reviews findings, and how a report records unresolved questions.

## Experience that transfers

A paralegal can bring file discipline and careful citations to an investigation support role, then build qualifying experience under a licensed investigator and verify requirements before managing a business. An investigator can bring source records and concise chronologies to legal support, then meet the relevant qualifications and learn legal research and drafting under attorney supervision.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use four fictional records. L1 is an unsigned contract draft, L2 is its signed counterpart, F1 has conflicting delivery dates, and F2 has matching dates in the submitted records. Under the exercise rules, label L1 as a draft and send the indexed L2 to the attorney. Mark F1 unresolved until a confirmed replacement record explains the discrepancy and report F2 as consistent within the supplied records. Spend ten minutes sorting, ten writing an exhibit index and a findings note, and ten checking source, scope and decisions reserved for the lawyer.
