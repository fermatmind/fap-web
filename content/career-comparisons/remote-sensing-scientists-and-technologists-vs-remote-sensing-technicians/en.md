# Remote Sensing Scientists and Technologists vs. Remote Sensing Technicians: A Change Along an Image Seam

## What each role delivers

Remote sensing scientists and technologists select analysis methods and test the reliability of results. Remote sensing technicians process sensor data, check accuracy and document the inputs. Both may use analytical software.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Remote Sensing Scientists and Technologists | Remote Sensing Technicians |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Change analysis, validation and caveats | Checked image batch and quality log |
| Basis | Method, reference data and comparability | Tile IDs, dates and alignment checks |

## How the work meets

A fictional team checks whether a construction site expanded. The technician notices a suspected boundary that coincides with a seam and records tile IDs and alignment checks. The scientist tests the result with imagery from a single date and documents which area can be confirmed.

## Work conditions

Analysis may involve repeated method trials and review. Production may follow batches and issue reports. Ask who validates a new method and who reproduces an issue.

## Moving between roles

Moving from technical work into analysis adds method selection and validation design. Moving into production adds repeatable batch processing, version tracking and quality documentation.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock records: an apparent change follows the seam between tiles A and B, taken three days apart. A same-date replacement covers only the north. Spend ten minutes each documenting the issue, designing a verification check and explaining what to report for each segment.
