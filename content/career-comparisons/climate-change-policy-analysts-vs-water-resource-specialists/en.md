# Climate Change Policy Analyst vs. Water Resource Specialist: Planning for a Dry Season

## What each role delivers

A climate change policy analyst compares measures and their public impacts. A water resource specialist assesses supply strategies using inflows, storage, demand and system constraints.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Climate change policy analysts | Water resource specialists |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Adaptation options and impact memo | Supply scenarios, operational constraints and recommendations |
| Basis | Policy goals, public impact and decision authority | Inflows, demand, storage and data coverage |

## How the work meets

A fictional city plans for a dry season. The water resource specialist estimates the shortfall under two inflow scenarios and flags missing recent records from one intake. The climate analyst uses the supported range to compare conservation schedules for public buildings with a household conservation incentive and reports costs, coverage and conditions for starting each.

## How to enter

Policy candidates can show a comparison of scenarios and measures. Water resource candidates can show a supply-and-demand analysis and explain data limitations. Ask whether the role has any duties that require professional sign-off.

## Work conditions

Policy follows consultation and budget dates. Water resource studies also follow seasonal monitoring and changes in operations. Ask who revisits response triggers when the data change.

## Moving between roles

Policy analysts bring evaluation into water resources and can add hydrology, supply models and operating rules. Water resource staff moving into policy can add impact assessment, budgets and institutional authority.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock scenarios: expected inflow meets demand, low inflow does not, and one intake lacks recent records. Spend ten minutes each on the technical brief, two policy options with start conditions, and a summary of what the city can decide now.
