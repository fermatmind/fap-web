# Search Marketing Strategists vs. Video Game Designers: Who Investigates Demo Drop-Off?

## What the roles own

A search marketing strategist studies how players look for games, briefs the landing page, and measures whether it brings people into the demo. A video game designer develops rules and levels, then uses prototypes and playtests to refine the experience.

## Compare the work

| Compare | Search marketing strategist | Video game designer |
|---|---|---|
| Inputs | Queries, campaign page visits, and demo starts | Play records, player feedback, and level tests |
| Outputs | Promotion brief and search performance review | Rules or level specs, prototypes, and playtest notes |

## The same puzzle demo

Suppose a landing page promises players that they can try the main puzzle mechanic in five minutes. The strategist separates visits from demo starts and checks the promise against the build. The designer watches people who start, sees them struggle to rotate a tile, and tries a clearer cue in a paper prototype. The playtest records whether players complete their first rotation unaided. Both roles bring their findings to the production team to prioritize changes.

## Getting started in the U.S.

O*NET classifies both roles in Job Zone Four, which describes occupations that generally require considerable preparation. For strategy, show a short campaign brief and define a demo-start measure. For game design, show a paper level, playtest notes, and a revision based on what players did. ## Working conditions

Strategy work follows campaign dates and reporting cycles. Game designers need to coordinate changes with art, engineering, and testing, and may have to wait for a playable build. Ask who schedules playtests and how quickly the design team receives feedback.

## Changing roles

Moving from marketing into game design, bring your audience research and practice creating rules and observing play. Moving into marketing, use your understanding of player language and learn query research and campaign measurement.

## Try both in 30 minutes

Use a paper puzzle with two steps: turn the tile clockwise to point its arrow at an exit, then drag it into the exit square. In the fictional session, four of eight landing-page visitors start the demo. Three of the four try dragging first, and two ask how to rotate. Spend 10 minutes writing a headline and calculating the demo-start rate, 10 sketching a rotation cue, and 10 defining how to count unaided first rotations out of all demo starters.
