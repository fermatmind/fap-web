# Dental Laboratory Technicians vs Prosthodontists

## The fabrication record and the treatment decision

Dental laboratory technicians make, alter, or repair devices such as crowns and dentures using work instructions and supporting records. Prosthodontists evaluate patients, plan and fit restorations, and follow clinical results. They can also design or make a restoration, or oversee the lab work.

## Outputs, U.S. preparation, and pay

| Item | Dental laboratory technicians | Prosthodontists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical output | Fabricated device or sample and build record | Restoration plan, fit decision, and follow-up record |
| Work setting | Fabrication lab or production area, coordinating with dental offices | Dental practice, working with patients and a clinical team |
| Typical U.S. entry education | High school diploma or equivalent | Doctoral or professional degree |
| Typical training | Moderate-term on-the-job training | Internship or residency classification |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $49,610 | $311,180 |

These are U.S. occupation medians for wage and salary workers, excluding the self-employed. ## When a sample meets the order and the target changes

A fictional dental practice sends work order C-09 for shade samples before fabrication of a front crown. The lab delivers two samples that match the original target, A, and includes its build record. At the appointment, the patient asks to switch to previously discussed target B. The prosthodontist reviews the request and sends C-09 v2, which calls for a new B sample first. The technician documents the original delivery, returns the new sample with its build record, and leaves final fabrication on hold. The prosthodontist reviews the new sample with the patient and tells the lab whether to proceed.

## Entry and licensing

For lab jobs, ask whether the employer trains new staff on crowns, removable appliances, or digital production, and when a trainee can submit work for sign-off. The prosthodontist path includes dental school and specialty training. Verify dental licensure and specialty practice requirements with the state dental board where you intend to work. [BLS dentist entry guide](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/dentists.htm#tab-4)

## What changes the day's workload

Ask a lab how it prioritizes new orders and remakes when a target changes after sample delivery. Ask a dental practice how it schedules a return visit, communicates changes, and records the final clinical decision.

## Evidence to bring when changing roles

A technician applying to a clinical training program can show how they documented material or fabrication decisions. Clinical practice requires the relevant dental education, training, and licensure. A prosthodontist moving into fabrication work can bring experience specifying device requirements and learn the employer's material workflows, equipment, and quality checks.

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use three fictional records: C-09 v1 requests A samples only, the lab log shows two A samples delivered, and C-09 v2 requests a B sample with final fabrication pending confirmation. Spend ten minutes assigning a status to each record, ten minutes writing the lab's next handoff, and ten minutes drafting the clinical instruction that would clear final fabrication and identifying what remains on hold until it arrives. A and B are labels for this exercise.
