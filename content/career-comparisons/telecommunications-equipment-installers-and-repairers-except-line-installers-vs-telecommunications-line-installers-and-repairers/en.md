# Telecommunications Equipment Installers and Repairers, Except Line Installers vs Telecommunications Line Installers and Repairers

## Find where your work order stops

Equipment installers work on units inside central offices or at customer premises. Line installers work on the cable routes that carry signals between locations. The two crews may meet at the same connection and still own different parts of the repair.

## Compare the site and the work record

| Work | Equipment installer or repairer | Telecommunications line installer or repairer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What you work on | Equipment and its interfaces | Cable or fiber and its route |
| Where you work | Central offices and customer sites | Aerial, underground or in-building routes |
| What you leave behind | Unit ID, work completed and functional test record | Span ID, route or connection changes and line test record |

[Equipment tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/49-2022.00) · [Line tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/49-9052.00)

## Follow a handoff where the unit test is complete

Picture a fictional telecommunications field work order. The equipment installer records a completed functional check for unit D7 and notes that the span's status is still open. The line installer checks the assignment against the route record and returns the result for span F2, noting an identifier mismatch on the ticket. A designated lead has enough information to reconcile the records and decide whether the assignment can close. [OSHA field work scope](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.268)

## Compare the two training routes

BLS lists a postsecondary nondegree award and moderate-term on-the-job training as typical for equipment installers. For line installers, it lists a high school diploma or equivalent and long-term on-the-job training. For covered telecommunications center and field work, OSHA requires employer training. Ask whether the assignment is construction and which credentials, training and supervision apply. [BLS Table 1.2](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) · [OSHA scope and training](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.268)

## Put pay next to the working conditions

U.S. median annual pay in May 2025 was $63,890 for equipment installers and repairers and $74,330 for telecommunications line installers and repairers. Ask how much work takes place at customer sites or outdoors, and how the employer handles emergency callouts. Labeling and test documentation carry across both roles. Moving into line work adds route and crew skills, while moving into equipment work adds unit-specific functional checks. [BLS pay and work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/installation-maintenance-and-repair/telecommunications-equipment-installers-and-repairers-except-line-installers.htm)

## Try 30 minutes with two incomplete records

Spend ten minutes reading mock D7 (unit check complete) and F2 (span result pending). Spend 12 minutes writing a handoff that names the missing record, the crew responsible and the open status. Use eight minutes to check whether someone on the next shift could tell where the remaining work belongs. Use only the mock records.

## Ask which side of the connection you would own

Ask the employer to describe an ordinary work order and where your responsibility begins and ends. Compare site travel, crew time and the tests you would need to document before choosing between the two routes.
