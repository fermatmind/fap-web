# Paralegals and Legal Assistants vs Title Examiners, Abstractors, and Searchers

## Research a legal issue or trace property records

Paralegals gather facts, research law and prepare drafts for attorney review. Title examiners, abstractors, and searchers trace records that show ownership, liens, or restrictions. Choose between organizing a whole legal matter and tracing the records for a specific property.

## Compare the handoffs

| Work | Paralegals and legal assistants | Title examiners, abstractors, and searchers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inputs | Client file, deadlines, legal sources | Deeds, recorded interests, property descriptions |
| Delivery | Chronology, research memo, or document draft | Record references, title abstract, and exceptions for review |
| Reviewer | Supervising attorney | Authorized title or legal reviewer |

## A recorded release does not match the file

In a fictional Texas sale, a file checklist says a lien was released, but the copy supplied by the seller carries a different instrument number. The paralegal assembles the chronology and correspondence for the supervising attorney. The title examiner compares the relevant recorded instruments and routes the discrepancy to the authorized reviewer. The title company determines whether it can issue a commitment and what conditions apply. [State Bar of Texas supervision](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?ContentID=54788&Section=articles&Template=%2FCM%2FHTMLDisplay.cfm) · [Texas Department of Insurance title FAQ](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/title/titlefaqs.html)

## Check training and Texas role scope

BLS lists an associate degree as typical for paralegals and a high school diploma or equivalent plus moderate-term on-the-job training for title examiners, abstractors, and searchers. Ask a law office how it supervises delegated research. Ask a title employer which duties are limited to searching or summarizing and whether the position also covers escrow activities. Texas has separate escrow officer license and appointment requirements for covered duties. [BLS education and training rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) · [State Bar definition](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?ContentID=54788&Section=articles&Template=%2FCM%2FHTMLDisplay.cfm) · [TDI escrow officer rules, L-2](https://tdi.texas.gov/title/titlem6a.html)

## Compare review queues and employee pay

Legal support may involve managing several deadlines at once. Ask who checks draft citations and how urgent edits are assigned. Title support involves repeated checks across records. Ask about file volume, review turnaround, and how conflicts are sent for a decision. U.S. May 2025 median annual employee wages were $62,890 for paralegals and legal assistants and $58,650 for title examiners, abstractors, and searchers. OEWS excludes self-employed earnings. From legal support, reuse your file index and add a mock title abstract with recording references and a clear unresolved issue. From title work, reuse your source notes and add a chronology and a short research memo for attorney review.

## Try a 30-minute file discrepancy exercise

Use a fictional July 3 checklist citing release R-114 and a July 5 copy labeled R-141. Spend 12 minutes building a lawyer-facing chronology and question. Spend 13 minutes making a two-row title source log with dates, identifiers, and the assigned reviewer. Use five minutes to confirm both records keep the discrepancy open and neither reports the lien as released.
