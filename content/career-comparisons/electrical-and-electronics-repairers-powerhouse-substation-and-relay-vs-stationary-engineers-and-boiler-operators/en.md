# Substation and Relay Repairer vs. Stationary Engineer or Boiler Operator

## Electrical maintenance and stationary equipment operation

Powerhouse, substation and relay repairers inspect electrical equipment and document maintenance. Stationary engineers and boiler operators run assigned boilers and auxiliary equipment and log operating conditions. Both roles may report equipment concerns.

## Compare the two records

| Item | Powerhouse, substation and relay repairer | Stationary engineer or boiler operator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Check | Equipment identity, findings and maintenance scope | Operating history, service status and shift records |
| Document | What was verified and what remains open | Operating implications and next-shift follow-up |
| Hand off | To the designated electrical maintenance lead | To the designated operating lead |

[O*NET 49-2095.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/49-2095.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 51-8021.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/51-8021.00#Tasks)

## The heating log and electrical work order disagree

At a fictional facility the heating shift log says an inspection is complete, while an electrical work order shows only that the record was received. The boiler operator confirms which equipment and shift the log describes, then flags the service-status question for the operating lead. The repairer verifies what the electrical record covers and reports the gap. Authorized staff confirm the next steps under facility procedures.

## U.S. education, training and licenses

BLS assigns the repair occupation a postsecondary nondegree award, less than five years of related work experience and moderate-term on-the-job training. It assigns stationary engineers and boiler operators high school or equivalent, no related work experience and long-term on-the-job training. Some state and local governments require a boiler or stationary engineering license, and employers may require a competency exam. Check the required license class and assigned equipment. [BLS detailed training table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/education-and-training-by-occupation.htm) · [BLS stationary engineering entry guidance](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/production/stationary-engineers-and-boiler-operators.htm#tab-4)

## Ask how the shift is organized

Repair applicants can ask when maintenance and record reviews occur. Stationary engineering applicants can ask about rotations, how much equipment the shift covers and who takes unresolved issues from the handoff.

## Moving between roles

Electrical repairers can bring equipment documentation and fault reporting to stationary equipment training, then check boiler-specific credentials. Stationary engineers and boiler operators can bring observation and handoff skills to electrical repair, then build equipment-specific knowledge and verify the employer's training requirements.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a fictional heating handoff, electrical work order and equipment index. The log says an inspection is complete. The work order acknowledges receipt of the record. Spend ten minutes writing the operating handoff, ten drafting an electrical record check and ten naming the recipients and evidence needed to close each item.
