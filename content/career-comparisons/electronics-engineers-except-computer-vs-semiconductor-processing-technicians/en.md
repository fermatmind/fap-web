# Electronics Engineers vs. Semiconductor Processing Technicians: The Outlier Lot

## What each role delivers

Electronics engineers design and verify electronics. Semiconductor processing technicians follow work orders and document processing and inspection results.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Electronics Engineers, Except Computer | Semiconductor Processing Technicians |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Circuit performance and retest evidence | Process, lot and inspection records |
| Basis | Design requirements and sample test data | Work orders, sequence and inspection limits |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $130,220 | $51,430 |

## How the work meets

One of two fictional device lots falls outside expectations in electrical tests. The technician retains the work order revision, lot ID and surface inspection result. The engineer links them to electrical tests for the same design across both lots and proposes a retest scope.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for electronics engineers and a high school diploma or equivalent with moderate-term on-the-job training for semiconductor processing technicians. Ask about shifts and training. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Engineering follows prototype and verification milestones. Processing may follow shifts and lot schedules. Ask who confirms record completeness and who decides when to hold a lot.

## Moving between roles

Moving to processing, bring testing habits and learn process controls and site procedures. Moving to electronics, bring inspection and traceability experience and add circuit design and system verification.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Lot L1 uses work order A, has no surface exception and passes the electrical range check. L2 uses order B, has one recorded surface exception and reads high. Spend ten minutes each writing a process exception, listing engineering retests and identifying the fields that connect the records.
