# Oil and Gas Rotary Drill Operator vs. Wellhead Pumper

## Identify the well and the stage of work

Rotary drill operators run assigned drilling equipment, direct crew activities and record drilling progress. Wellhead pumpers operate and monitor pumping equipment and check production at assigned wells. The job records describe different equipment and stages of work. Assigned maintenance may include work on meters and gauges. The job and site determine who is authorized to do it.

## Compare records by purpose

| Record | Rotary drill operator | Wellhead pumper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work logged | Footage, tools and activities at the rig | Well production, pump condition, flow-line observations and assigned maintenance |
| Verification | Identify which well and shift the drilling entry covers | Match the check to the production well and time on the route |
| Handoff | Outstanding drilling records and site issues | Production observations that still need review |

## A production check shows up under the wrong well

In a fictional field, a crew is drilling at Well A while a pumper checks producing Well B. A shared daily summary puts the pumper's observation under Well A. The operator can verify the drilling log and shift. The pumper can verify the production check against the original route record. The daily report owner can correct the assignment through the project record process and preserve the original evidence.

## U.S. education and job training

BLS assigns no formal educational credential to rotary drill operators and a high school diploma or equivalent to wellhead pumpers. It assigns moderate-term on-the-job training to both. Ask the employer about the experience, training and authorization required for the particular equipment or route. [BLS education and training table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/education-and-training-by-occupation.htm) BLS assigns less than five years of related experience to rotary drill operators. It assigns the same related-experience category to the two occupations.

## Ask about crew rotation and route coverage

For drilling jobs, ask about crew rotations and time set aside for handoff. For a pumper job, ask how many wells a route covers, how open issues are shared and who follows up when a reading is missing.

## Transferring between the roles

A drill operator can bring experience observing equipment and documenting a shift, then learn the employer's production checks and route records. A wellhead pumper can bring familiarity with equipment observations and reliable well identification, then complete rig-specific training and learn the crew's operating and reporting duties.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use three fictional records: a rig log for Well A, a route sheet for Well B, and a daily report that assigns the route check to Well A. Spend ten minutes listing the record identifiers and sources. Spend ten on separate verification notes for the rig operator and the pumper. Spend the last ten writing the correction request, the responsible reviewer and the original records to retain.
