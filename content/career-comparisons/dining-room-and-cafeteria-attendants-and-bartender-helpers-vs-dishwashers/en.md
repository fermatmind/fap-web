# Dining Room Attendant vs. Dishwasher: Who Keeps the Trays Moving at Lunch?

## What each role delivers

Dining room and cafeteria attendants clear and reset service areas and restock tableware. Dishwashers clean and organize dishes and utensils so the next service can use them. Duties can overlap in a small establishment.

## How the work meets

A fictional cafeteria is short of trays during lunch. The attendant brings used trays back and tells the dish station how many the service area needs. The dishwasher confirms which rack has finished the site's checks, sets those trays at the designated pickup point and reports the quantity. The attendant restocks from that rack and tells the shift lead what is still short.

## Work conditions

Dining room work follows guest arrivals and table turnover. Dish stations can be wet and busy for long stretches. Ask how workers flag a shortage and how the station marks items ready for pickup.

## Moving between roles

Attendants can bring their knowledge of peak demand and learn the dish station's checks and storage practices. Dishwashers can bring tableware organization skills and learn table resets, service-area restocking and guest-facing expectations.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use three mock entries: five trays remain at the counter, one rack is ready and another is still under review. Spend ten minutes writing the service area's request, ten preparing the dish station's reply and ten assigning the inventory update and open check. Use the records alone.
