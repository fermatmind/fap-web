# Electronic Equipment Installers and Repairers, Motor Vehicles vs Recreational Vehicle Service Technicians

## A device category and a vehicle specialty

Motor vehicle electronics installers and repairers work on devices such as communications, sound, security, and navigation equipment. RV service technicians diagnose and repair systems on RVs and travel trailers. Some specialize in electrical work, plumbing, hydraulics, or chassis equipment. The assignments can overlap on installed electronics.

## Outputs, U.S. training, and pay

| Item | Motor vehicle electronics workers | RV service technicians |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | Device installation and functional check | Vehicle service record and system verification |
| Setting | Automotive installation or service shop | RV service shop or dealership |
| Typical entry education | High school diploma or equivalent | High school diploma or equivalent |
| Typical on-the-job training | Moderate-term | Long-term |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $48,420 | $52,000 |

The national wage and salary medians exclude self-employment. ## One vehicle comes in with two complaints

At a fictional shop, a customer reports silent navigation prompts and a problem with an RV water fixture. The electronics installer links the unit ID to E-65 and records the navigation audio check. The RV technician documents the fixture inspection and verification in R-65, then collects the electronics result for the vehicle order. Under the shop's rules, the service lead reviews both reports and decides whether to hand over the vehicle.

## What to check before applying

Ask electronics shops which devices and vehicles they cover, how they teach fitment, and who reviews completed installations. Ask RV employers which vehicle systems they teach during on-the-job training and how they assess independent work. Verify any assignment-specific credentials.

## How jobs move through the shop

Ask electronics teams how they schedule appointments that need vehicle-specific information. Ask RV teams how many workstations a vehicle may visit and who updates the customer when parts or another check are pending.

## A supervised starting point in either direction

Moving into RV service, bring a fictional device verification record and start by helping track vehicle electrical orders and checks under supervision. Learn the living systems and vehicle handoff. Moving into electronics, bring a fictional cross-system diagnostic record and start with supervised fitment and functional checks. Learn vehicle documentation and the assigned device functions.

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use a different fictional vehicle at the same shop. E-66 says the display shows an image and navigation audio remains unverified. R-66 has a signed water-fixture check. U-66 requires both checks for handover. Spend ten minutes identifying what each record covers, ten minutes writing an electronics follow-up and a service status note, and ten minutes recommending release or hold to the service lead. Use only the records.
