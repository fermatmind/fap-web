# Critical Care Nurse vs. LPN or LVN

## Critical care assessment and assigned nursing care

Critical care nurses use assessment findings to prioritize care for critically ill patients. Licensed practical nurses and licensed vocational nurses provide assigned basic care, monitor patients and report changes. State rules and facility assignments govern supervision and scope.

## Compare what each worker hands off

| Item | Critical care nurse | LPN or LVN |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Check | Findings, trends and care priorities in a critically ill patient | Observed changes, patient concerns and assigned care |
| Record | Assessment findings and issues for the critical care team | Observations, assigned care and whom the nurse notified |
| Coordinate | Review priorities with the receiving critical care team | Escalate findings to the designated supervising clinician |

## A patient is transferred to critical care

In a fictional hospital, an LVN assigned to the sending ward documents a new patient concern and tells the designated receiving clinician when it was reported. A critical care RN in the receiving unit reviews that handoff along with their own assessment and identifies which nursing concerns need follow-up first. Actual assignments and decisions depend on state scope and hospital policy.

## U.S. training and credentials

An LPN or LVN typically completes a state-approved education program, passes an exam and obtains a license. For a critical care RN job, start with the approved RN education and state licensure route, then check the unit's orientation and specialty expectations. AACN's adult direct-care CCRN is a specialty certification with an eligible RN or APRN license and practice requirements. Ask the employer whether it requires the credential. [BLS practical nursing training](https://www.bls.gov/OOH/healthcare/licensed-practical-and-licensed-vocational-nurses.htm#tab-4) · [BLS registered nurse entry guidance](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/registered-nurses.htm#tab-4) · [AACN adult direct-care CCRN](https://www.aacn.org/certification/get-certified/ccrn-adult)

## Ask about the shift

Critical care applicants can ask how the unit organizes assessment, team rounds and transfers. LPN or LVN applicants can ask which patient care tasks are assigned, which clinician supervises the work and where changes are documented.

## Moving between jobs

LPNs or LVNs can bring observation, documentation and handoff experience to an RN bridge program that qualifies them for licensure, then pursue unit training. Critical care RNs considering a more routine care setting can bring assessment and escalation skills and should check which license, supervision and responsibilities the position calls for.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a fictional transfer schedule, one reported change and two unverified details. Spend ten minutes writing the sending ward handoff, ten drafting the receiving unit's follow-up list, and ten marking known facts, open items and who receives each record. Use paper records only.
