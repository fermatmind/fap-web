# Postsecondary Chemistry Teachers vs. Materials Scientists: Reading a Performance Chart

## What each role delivers

Postsecondary chemistry teachers use data and observations to teach and assess chemistry. Materials scientists study structure and properties, test against specifications and identify the evidence needed for development.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Chemistry Teachers, Postsecondary | Materials Scientists |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Course materials and assessment | Material performance evaluation |
| Basis | Learning objectives and reasoning | Structure, properties and specifications |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $93,250 | $117,790 |

## How the work meets

A fictional film chart has one aging observation. An instructor asks students to distinguish observations from explanations. The materials scientist notes that the record cannot establish consistency across batches and asks the research lead for more evidence.

## How to enter

BLS lists a doctoral or professional degree as typical for postsecondary chemistry teachers and a bachelor’s degree as typical for materials scientists. Check the research specialization and the degree requirements of the specific position. [BLS occupation rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Faculty work often follows courses and student projects. Materials research follows testing schedules and development decisions. Ask who uses test reports and how teaching and research are evaluated.

## Moving between roles

To move toward materials research, bring data explanation skills and add specification and project documentation experience. To move into teaching, turn research cases into lessons, feedback and assessable goals.
