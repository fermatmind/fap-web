# Occupational Therapists vs. Occupational Therapy Assistants: Who Revises the Plan?

## Who updates the plan

An occupational therapist evaluates what limits a client's daily activities, develops the treatment plan, and reviews it as the client progresses. An occupational therapy assistant carries out treatment under the applicable supervision and reports how the client performed.

## One meal-prep goal, two contributions

Suppose an outpatient client wants to prepare a simple meal independently. The therapist evaluates the task and asks the team to observe whether the client can gather supplies in sequence. The assistant guides the planned activity and records that the client found a plate and cup on both attempts, then paused. The therapist reviews those observations alongside the previous evaluation, decides whether to change the activity sequence, and sends any change back to the assistant.

## Getting started in the U.S.

Therapists typically need a master's degree in occupational therapy and a state license. Assistants typically need an associate degree from an accredited occupational therapy assistant program and usually need a state-issued license. BLS describes fieldwork and an exam as part of the licensure pathway. Check the state board for the specific licensing and supervision requirements where you plan to work. [BLS therapist entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/occupational-therapists.htm#tab-4) · [BLS assistant entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/occupational-therapy-assistants-and-aides.htm#tab-4)

## Working conditions

Therapists need time for evaluations, meetings, and plan reviews. Assistants may deliver a series of treatment sessions and document progress between them. Ask when the team reviews notes and how assistants escalate observations that may require a plan change.

## Moving between roles

An assistant can bring treatment and documentation experience into therapist training. The therapist role still requires its own education and licensure. A therapist considering an assistant position should verify the state's requirements for that role and clarify supervision and assigned duties with the employer.

## Try both in 30 minutes

Use two fictional notes. The plan lists a plate, a cup, and utensils in that order. The session note says the client retrieved the plate and cup twice, then paused, asking what came next on one attempt. Spend 10 minutes writing the assistant's observation, 10 listing what the therapist needs to check before revising the plan, and 10 drafting a two-way handoff.
