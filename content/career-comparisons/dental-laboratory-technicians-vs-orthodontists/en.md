# Dental Laboratory Technicians vs Orthodontist

## A fabricated device and a clinical decision

Dental laboratory technicians make or modify oral devices from a work order and supporting model. Orthodontists examine patients, plan treatment, and follow clinical progress. The appliance can pass a lab check and still require an adjustment after the orthodontist assesses fit.

## Work setting, entry, and U.S. pay

| Item | Dental laboratory technicians | Orthodontists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main output | Device made to order and associated quality records | Clinical evaluation, treatment plan, and follow-up decisions |
| Work setting | Lab or fabrication area, often coordinating with a dental office | Dental practice, with patient appointments and clinical staff |
| Typical U.S. entry education | High school diploma or equivalent | Doctoral or professional degree |
| Typical U.S. training | Moderate-term on-the-job training | Internship or residency classification |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $49,610 | $289,140 |

The BLS figures are national medians for wage and salary workers and exclude the self-employed. ## The work order and model disagree

At a fictional dental practice, work order W-17 v2 specifies model M-17 v2 for a removable appliance. The lab receives M-17 v1. The technician puts the job on hold and sends the practice the order ID, the required revision, and the revision received. The orthodontist checks the treatment record, confirms which model applies, and has the practice send the matching files. The technician resumes once they match and returns the device with its revision record. At the visit, the orthodontist checks that record and assesses fit before deciding whether to use the device.

## Training and licensing

For U.S. lab jobs, ask which tasks the employer teaches on the job and whether the role focuses on orthodontic devices, restorations, or digital production. The orthodontist route includes dental school and specialty training. Verify dental licensure and specialty practice requirements with the board in the state where you intend to practice. [BLS dentist entry guide](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/dentists.htm#tab-4)

## What to ask about the workday

Ask the lab how it schedules orders, decides when to stop a job for missing information, and handles remakes. Ask the orthodontic practice how the schedule splits between new patients and follow-ups and who sends change orders back to the lab.

## What transfers between the roles

A technician can bring fabrication quality records and experience communicating with dental offices. Clinical practice requires the corresponding education, training, and licensure. An orthodontist can bring experience writing orders and discussing device requirements. Lab work also calls for learning the materials, equipment, and production checks used by the employer.

## Try a 30-minute paperwork exercise

Use three fictional records. W-17 v2 calls for M-17 v2, the lab has M-17 v1, and a response from the practice lists a replacement model labeled M-17 v2. Spend ten minutes marking the reason for the initial hold, ten minutes writing the request and checking what lets the lab resume, and ten minutes specifying what revision record goes back with the device and who decides whether the patient can use it.
