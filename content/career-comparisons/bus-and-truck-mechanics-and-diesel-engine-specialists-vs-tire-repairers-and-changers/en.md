# Bus and Truck Mechanic and Diesel Engine Specialist vs. Tire Repairer and Changer

## Vehicle diagnosis and specialist tire service

Bus and truck mechanics and diesel engine specialists inspect and repair diesel vehicles and their systems. Tire repairers and changers examine, repair, and replace tires. A fleet may assign different parts of one work order to each role.

## Compare the work products

| Work | Diesel vehicle mechanic | Tire repairer and changer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First check | Vehicle symptoms and service records | Tire condition and documented wheel position |
| Deliverable | Diagnostic findings and repair items | Tire inspection and service record |

May 2025 U.S. median annual wages were $61,770 for diesel service technicians and mechanics and $37,710 for tire repairers and changers. These OEWS estimates cover wage and salary jobs and exclude self-employment. [BLS diesel pay](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/installation-maintenance-and-repair/diesel-service-technicians-and-mechanics.htm#tab-5) · [O*NET tire wages](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/49-3093.00) · [OEWS methods](https://www.bls.gov/oes/methods_25.pdf)

## Two concerns on the same vehicle

A fictional fleet driver reports weak acceleration. A separate inspection notes wear on the right rear tire. The diesel technician reviews the power complaint, and the tire technician verifies and records the location and condition of the tire. The diesel technician submits the verification result for the power complaint. The tire technician submits the condition follow-up tied to the right rear position. In this fleet, the authorized shift lead reviews both completed items before deciding whether to return the vehicle to service.

## U.S. preparation and first jobs

BLS describes high school and on-the-job training as a typical diesel route, with some employers preferring postsecondary diesel training. O*NET places tire repairers and changers in Job Zone 1–2, a category for occupations that call for little to some preparation. Look for supervised tire service work and ask what training applies to the equipment and vehicles you would handle. [BLS diesel preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/installation-maintenance-and-repair/diesel-service-technicians-and-mechanics.htm#tab-4) · [O*NET tire preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/49-3093.00#JobZone) Ask whether the job includes test-driving large vehicles and whether those assignments require a commercial driver’s license. For rim wheel service covered by OSHA’s large-vehicle standard, employers must train workers for the type of wheel they service and evaluate their ability to do the work safely. [OSHA rim wheel training standard](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.177)

## Compare workplaces and schedules

Diesel mechanics typically work in repair shops and may sometimes work in the field. Tire repair can involve physical tasks and indoor work without climate control. Ask how the shop assigns vehicles and service bays, and how it handles on-call work and handoffs. [BLS work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/installation-maintenance-and-repair/diesel-service-technicians-and-mechanics.htm#tab-3) · [O*NET tire work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/49-3093.00#WorkContext)

## Moving between the trades

A tire technician can bring inspection and work-order experience into diesel training, then learn vehicle systems and diagnostics. A diesel mechanic can bring vehicle knowledge to tire service, then learn the shop’s equipment and how it tracks condition and wheel position.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use three fictional records: Vehicle 28 has weak acceleration; the current inspection notes right rear tire wear; the old work order records a left front tire replacement. Spend ten minutes sorting the concerns, ten writing two clarification questions, and ten routing each concern to a service bay with a short handoff. Mark both items as open for the shift lead’s review before any release decision.
