# Loss prevention managers vs. retail loss prevention specialists

## One case and the program behind it

Loss prevention managers oversee loss-control programs, review discrepancies and train teams. Retail loss prevention specialists identify and investigate losses, keep records and may implement or monitor prevention processes. Both may work on individual cases. The main distinction in a given job is who also owns program follow-up and resources.

## Compare the roles

| Item | Loss prevention manager | Retail loss prevention specialist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main deliverable | A program review, corrective plan and follow-up | A documented discrepancy or case and handoff |
| Setting to ask about | A store, distribution site or several locations | Store-based reviews, records and shift handoffs |
| U.S. preparation evidence | O*NET Job Zone Four, considerable preparation | O*NET Job Zone 1–2, very little to some preparation |

## Two stores report transfer discrepancies

At a fictional retailer, a store counts one fewer item than its transfer record shows. The specialist checks the paperwork and count, records the difference and hands it to the inventory owner and loss prevention manager. The manager compares a similar unresolved case at another store and assigns follow-up and training. The inventory owner decides whether to adjust the record under the retailer's policy. Each role records its own work.

## Getting started in the U.S.

O*NET's zones describe broad preparation ranges. Most Zone Four occupations require a four-year bachelor’s degree, with exceptions. Zone 1–2 usually calls for a high school diploma or GED, though requirements vary. Inventory or store operations can build experience for a specialist opening. Experience reviewing controls, coordinating several sites or training teams can help prepare for a manager opening. Ask about role-specific hiring requirements and delegated duties. ## Ask when the work peaks

In the example, the specialist responds to a transfer discrepancy during a shift. The manager schedules reviews and training across locations. Ask how quickly a case must be handed over, whether travel is involved and who approves corrective work.

## Moving between the roles

A specialist can bring a redacted discrepancy report and handoff, then develop trend review, coordination and training skills. A manager can bring a redacted corrective action and training log, then build practice verifying individual stock records and handing off exceptions within a shift.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use mock transfer records. Store A expected twelve items and counted eleven. Store B has an earlier unresolved discrepancy. Neither cause is known. The retailer retains original records until verification and approval. Spend ten minutes recording the Store A discrepancy, ten scheduling follow-up and naming owners, and ten writing the handoff. Identify what the specialist verifies, what the manager coordinates and who can approve an adjustment.
