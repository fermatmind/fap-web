# Brickmasons and Blockmasons vs. Refractory materials repairers (except brickmasons): The Wall and the Lining

## What each role delivers

Brickmasons and blockmasons build and check masonry. Refractory materials repairers in this occupation repair or replace industrial linings, such as the linings of ladles and pouring spouts.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Brickmasons and Blockmasons | Refractory materials repairers (except brickmasons) |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Masonry dimensions and construction handoff | Industrial lining repair record |
| Basis | Building drawings, wall line and wall condition | Asset ID, lining condition and service order |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $62,120 | $61,290 |

National medians; local and starting pay vary. [BLS Table 1.2, rows 47-2021 and 49-9045](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## How the work meets

During a fictional shutdown, a maintenance coordinator sends a chipped corridor partition to the masonry crew and a worn lining in an out-of-service ladle to equipment maintenance. The mason returns the wall location and condition. The lining repairer returns the asset ID, repair record and inspection status for the authorized return-to-service decision.

## How to enter

BLS lists high school or equivalent for both, with an apprenticeship for brickmasons and blockmasons and moderate-term on-the-job training for refractory materials repairers in this category. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Masonry can follow building progress, while lining repair can follow industrial maintenance windows. Ask about training, equipment handoff, checks and release responsibility.

## Moving between roles

Moving to lining work, build on drawings and defect records and add refractory and equipment service training. Moving to masonry, bring repair records and learn building drawings and wall layout.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use two mock tickets: A identifies a chip in corridor partition W4 and includes a building drawing. B identifies a worn lining in out-of-service ladle L7 and has no return-to-service signature. Spend ten minutes each identifying the scope, writing handoff notes and listing missing release evidence. Route ticket A to masonry and ticket B to equipment maintenance. Without the release signature, record L7 as awaiting return-to-service approval.
