# Business Continuity Planner vs. Compliance Manager

## Keep essential work moving and review the temporary process

A business continuity planner identifies which operations a disruption threatens, prepares workarounds, and tests the plan. A compliance manager checks how those procedures account for applicable approval, recordkeeping, or reporting requirements and follows up on gaps. The two can review the workaround together before the exercise.

## Compare what each role produces

| Work | Business continuity planner | Compliance manager |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preparation | Identify critical services, dependencies, and criteria for switching to a workaround | Check which approvals and records the workaround needs |
| Exercise output | Recovery steps, bottlenecks, and results | Control gaps, owners, and follow-up checks |
| Collaboration | Service owners, technical staff, and frontline teams | Process owners and staff responsible for approval and review |

## The intake system goes down during a drill

At a fictional service organization, the planner asks staff to simulate taking high-priority requests on paper and entering them in the system after recovery. The compliance manager checks whether the paper records show who reviewed each request and whether the handoff accounts for every form. The process owner gets both sets of findings and can assign a retest for recovery steps and a separate check of the records.

## Preparing for U.S. openings

For continuity planning, prepare an example of mapping a critical operation's dependencies or designing a tabletop exercise. For compliance management, prepare an example of documenting a procedure failure and verifying a corrective action. Ask what authority, industry experience, and training the employer expects for the specific opening.

## Ask how exercises and incidents affect the schedule

Ask the continuity team when exercises run and who can activate the plan during an outage. Ask compliance staff which exceptions need approval in the moment and what gets reviewed after recovery. For either job, ask who covers interruptions outside normal hours.

## Moving between the jobs

A continuity planner can show how exercise findings turn into assigned actions, then build experience reviewing controls and sampling records. A compliance manager can bring process maps and corrective-action records, then practice setting recovery priorities, mapping dependencies, and running an exercise.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Sketch a fictional service intake process and assume a two-hour outage. Spend ten minutes choosing requests that still need same-day attention. Spend ten writing a paper intake and recovery sequence. Use the last ten to add review and handoff fields and describe how you would check that one request made it back into the system correctly.
