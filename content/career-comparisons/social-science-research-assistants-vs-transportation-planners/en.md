# Social Science Research Assistants vs. Transportation Planners: How a Bus Survey Shapes a Proposal

## What each role delivers

Social science research assistants clean data, summarize findings and document methods. Transportation planners weigh feedback alongside ridership and operating constraints. They may also design surveys.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Social Science Research Assistants | Transportation Planners |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Clean survey data and sample notes | Transport options and recommendation |
| Basis | Response source, duplicates and coverage | Ridership, travel needs and constraints |

## How the work meets

A fictional bus route receives requests for more early service. The research assistant finds that most weekday responses came from one stop, tabulates the responses by source and notes coverage. The planner combines the feedback with ridership by time and vehicle availability and compares an earlier first trip with added service in an existing window.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for social science research assistants. For transportation planners, 55% of U.S. O*NET education responses cited a bachelor’s degree and 45% cited a master’s degree. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) [O*NET education data 19-3099.01](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-3099.01#Education)

## Work conditions

Research work can follow collection, cleaning and reporting milestones. Planning can involve analysis, public meetings and proposal review. Ask who checks the survey tables and who presents the recommendation.

## Moving between roles

Moving into planning, bring survey and statistical skills and learn network and service constraints. Moving into research support, bring problem definition and public engagement and add coding, cleaning and documentation.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock records show 72 of 90 responses came from one stop. Two peak trips are full, and the first trip has room. Spend ten minutes each describing sample coverage, comparing two service changes and listing two kinds of data you still need.
