# Interior Designers vs Set and Exhibit Designers

## Design the room or design the experience it houses

Interior designers establish how indoor space works, including circulation, finishes and lighting. Set and exhibit designers use a script or content brief to develop scenery, displays, and what an audience sees. They coordinate fabrication or installation so the design can work in the available space.

## An exhibit handoff inside a museum gallery

The American Museum of Natural History’s Gilder Center has the Macaulay Family Foundation Collections Gallery on the first floor of its Collections Core. In a work example based on that setting, an interior designer could hand over a plan with room circulation and lighting constraints. An exhibit designer could place display cases and viewing stops, then return a marked plan where a stop might narrow the route. The museum project lead would coordinate a review with design, curatorial, and fabrication staff before installation. [Museum gallery description](https://www.amnh.org/exhibitions/permanent/gilder-center/collections-core)

## Prepare for the role and verify title rules

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for both. Interior design study can include drawing, CAD, and space planning. Set and exhibit designers can study theater design, exhibit design, or a related field and build models that show how a design can be installed. Interior design rules vary by state and duty. New Jersey protects the Certified Interior Designer title through certification, including the NCIDQ exam and qualifying education and experience. Check the actual job's duties and any title, permit or signing requirements. [BLS interior entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/arts-and-design/interior-designers.htm#tab-4) · [BLS set and exhibit entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/arts-and-design/set-and-exhibit-designers.htm#tab-4) · [New Jersey title law](https://www.njconsumeraffairs.gov/Statutes/interior-design-law.pdf) · [New Jersey certification](https://www.njconsumeraffairs.gov/int/Pages/FAQ.aspx)

## Ask how revisions and installation are scheduled

BLS describes interior designers splitting work between offices and project sites. Set and exhibit designers can travel and may work evenings or weekends around productions or displays. Ask who reviews drawings on site, who handles changes near opening, and whether installation or teardown is part of the assignment. [BLS interior work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/arts-and-design/interior-designers.htm#tab-3) · [BLS set and exhibit work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/arts-and-design/set-and-exhibit-designers.htm#tab-3)

## Move across the handoff with a work sample

An interior designer moving into exhibits can start with a circulation plan and learn to sequence content and label drawings for fabrication. A set or exhibit designer moving toward interiors can show coordination on a display detail, then learn programming for long-term use, finish and lighting schedules, and applicable rules.

## Try a 30-minute gallery exercise

Use fictional records. Plan S marks a door and a main route to keep clear. Case list C contains two display cases. Story card T puts case A first, but its proposed viewing stop overlaps the route. Spend eight minutes marking the source of each constraint, twelve drafting a room circulation plan or an exhibit placement sketch, and ten checking the handoff. Identify the overlap, the condition you kept, and what needs confirmation from the other designer.
