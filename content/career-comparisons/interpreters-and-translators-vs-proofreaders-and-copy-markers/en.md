# Interpreters and Translators vs Proofreaders and Copy Markers

## Carry meaning across languages or catch errors in the proof

Try interpreting if you like following live questions and conveying them in another spoken or signed language. Try translation if you want time to research terms and revise written copy. Proofreading suits work you can check line by line, mark precisely, and verify after the corrections come back.

## Compare the handoff

| Work | Interpreters and translators | Proofreaders and copy markers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Input | Live remarks, signed conversation, or written source | Approved copy, typeset pages, and a style sheet |
| Delivery | Interpretation or a target-language draft with queries | Marked pages and a check of corrections |
| When to ask | When the meaning, term, or source fact is unclear | When a difference may reflect an editorial choice |

[O*NET language work](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/27-3091.00) · [O*NET proofreading work](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-9081.00)

## A map label and a live visitor question

The U.S. National Park Service's Harpers Ferry Center Spanish style guide advises keeping map place names in their original form so readers can match them with signs. [NPS Spanish Style Guide, §§6.13 and 7.45](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/hfc/upload/Spanish-HFC-Style-Guide-2015-1.pdf)

In a fictional visitor-map project using that guide, the translator renders the route description into Spanish and keeps the signed trailhead name. Two source files give different distances, so the translator sends both locations to the editor for confirmation. After layout, the proofreader sees that a digit in the approved distance changed and a place name was split across lines. They mark both spots and check the revised PDF. At a live orientation, an interpreter conveys a visitor's question about the route and interprets the ranger's reply.

## Check U.S. entry routes by assignment

BLS lists a bachelor's degree as typical entry education for both occupations. For interpreting or translation, also verify the language pair, subject-area experience, and any setting-specific certification requirements. BLS says certification is generally optional, but court candidates should check the requirements of the court system where they plan to work. Try a recorded interpreting sample for live work or a paired source-and-translation sample for written work. For proofreading, prepare before-and-after proofs with page references and ask whether a style-guide exercise is part of hiring. [BLS occupation rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) · [BLS interpreting and translation entry path](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/interpreters-and-translators.htm)

## Ask about deadlines, availability, and pay

BLS reports that part-time work is common for interpreters and translators and that schedules can vary. Ask how cancellation, preparation time, or rush work is handled. O*NET describes frequent deadlines and extensive sitting for proofreaders. Ask about pages per shift and who checks final corrections. BLS lists May 2025 U.S. median annual wages of $60,170 for interpreters and translators and $51,120 for proofreaders and copy markers. These figures describe employee wages and exclude self-employed earnings. [BLS work schedules and pay coverage](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/interpreters-and-translators.htm) · [O*NET proofreading work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-9081.00) · [BLS comparable wage rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Show the skill your next role needs

Moving from translation to proofreading? Reuse your terminology list and query log, then add a marked proof with page references and show which fixes you verified. Moving the other way? Bring your eye for inconsistencies, then submit a paired source and translation with a note showing how you resolved one phrase that could change the meaning.

## Try a 30-minute visitor map exercise

Use this fictional brief: the English copy reads “From Willow Trailhead, walk 0.8 mi to the overlook.” A route sheet lists 0.6 mi. Spend 12 minutes translating the sentence and writing a query, then use the mock editor reply “0.8 mi is correct” to lock the approved copy. Over the next 13 minutes, copy your approved Spanish translation, split Willow Trailhead across two lines, and delete “mi” to make a mock proof. Mark both errors and make a corrected copy. Use the last five minutes to compare the corrected proof with the approved translation. Keep the query and response with the translation, and the marked proof with the verified revision.
