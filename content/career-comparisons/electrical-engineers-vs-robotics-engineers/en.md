# Electrical Engineers vs. Robotics Engineers: Reading a Cell Stop Record

## What each role delivers

Electrical engineers may own equipment power and installation. Robotics engineers integrate and test sensor data, software and machine behavior.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Electrical Engineers | Robotics Engineers |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Power and installation specification | Robot task and system verification |
| Basis | Supply conditions and site constraints | Sensor state, task order and test results |

O*NET reports the Engineers, All Other wage series for one occupation in this comparison. These data cannot establish a separate median for that role. [O*NET wage mapping](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/17-2199.08)

## How the work meets

A fictional material-handling cell stops during a trial. The electrical engineer documents power status and time. The robotics engineer identifies the waiting-for-pickup state and submits the difference between the log timestamps for review.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for electrical engineers. In robotics, show the integration, simulation or site acceptance work relevant to the job. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Electrical work may follow equipment and installation windows. Robotics may involve simulation, integration and acceptance testing. Ask about equipment and record access.

## Moving between roles

Moving to robotics, bring equipment interface skills and add task logic and integration. Moving to electrical engineering, bring system testing and add load calculations and installation requirements.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use fictional summaries: power reads normal at 10:03, the robot stops while waiting for pickup at 10:04, and the clocks are not synchronized. Spend ten minutes each on the power record, task record and shared checks. Use paper only.
