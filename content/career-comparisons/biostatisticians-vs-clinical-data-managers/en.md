# Biostatistician vs. Clinical Data Manager

## Study data checks and statistical analysis

Clinical data managers coordinate data checks and follow up on gaps with study teams. Biostatisticians plan the statistical comparison and interpret results. Both may write checks or code, but each produces different records for the study.

## Compare the handoff

| Work | Biostatistician | Clinical data manager |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First check | The comparison, data available, and effect of missing information | Field definitions, discrepancies, and query responses |
| Deliverable | Analysis plan and results with limitations | Reviewed data, open issues, and version notes |

## Two follow-up fields are blank

In a fictional study, a site confirms that one follow-up visit did not happen. A separate blank field still has an unanswered query. The clinical data manager records each status and flags the pending item in the handoff. The biostatistician uses those distinctions to identify what is ready for analysis and how unresolved records limit the comparison.

## U.S. education and early experience

O*NET places biostatisticians in Job Zone Five, where graduate study is common. A supervised study can build experience writing an analysis plan and explaining results. Clinical data managers are in Job Zone Four, where bachelor’s-level and related preparation are common. A research data assistant position or coursework project can build experience tracking queries and delivering versioned tables. Check the specific opening. [Biostatistics preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-2041.01#JobZone) · [Clinical data management preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-2051.02#JobZone)

## Compare how the work is scheduled

Both roles use computers and coordinate with study teams. Data management may follow query and dataset delivery deadlines. Biostatistics may follow study planning, review, and analysis milestones. Ask who owns open queries and when the statistician joins study planning. [Biostatistics work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-2041.01#WorkContext) · [Clinical data management work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-2051.02#WorkContext)

## Moving between the jobs

A data manager can bring knowledge of fields and record provenance into biostatistics, then build skills in inference and missing-data analysis. A biostatistician can bring study design and coding experience into data management, then learn query tracking and dataset handoffs.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use three fictional notes. Participant A has a blank second visit field, and the site confirms the visit did not happen. Participant B has the same blank with no query response. The analysis plan classifies confirmed statuses only. Spend ten minutes recording the two statuses, ten drafting the data handoff, and ten listing what still needs confirmation before analysis.
