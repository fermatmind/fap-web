# Water Resource Specialist vs. Water/Wastewater Engineer: From a Supply Scenario to Design

## What each role delivers

A water resource specialist analyzes supply, demand and watershed or operating tradeoffs. A water and wastewater engineer develops calculations, equipment requirements and engineering documents from confirmed design conditions.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Water resource specialists | Water/Wastewater Engineers |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Supply scenarios, sensitivities and system recommendations | Design calculations, drawings, specifications and review record |
| Basis | Demand forecasts, inflows and system constraints | Confirmed design flows, site, connections and applicable requirements |

## How the work meets

A fictional water project compares phased and all-at-once expansion. The water resource specialist supplies normal and high demand scenarios and flags uncertainty in the long-term forecast. The engineer develops facility options for the confirmed near-term conditions, identifies equipment and connections that may need space, and labels draft drawings while long-term inputs await a decision.

## How to enter

Water resource candidates can show a watershed or supply assessment. Engineering candidates can show calculations and drawings and explain how they were reviewed. Ask who is responsible for engineering documents. In California, §6735 requires covered civil engineering documents to be prepared by or under the responsible charge of a licensed civil engineer. Permitted or construction-release plans and specifications, and final civil engineering calculations and reports, require the licensee’s signature, seal or stamp, and date. [California Professional Engineers Act §6735](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/laws/pe_act.pdf)

## Work conditions

Water resource work follows data and planning reviews. Engineering also follows design stages, technical review and field feedback. Ask who accepts the design basis and releases drawings.

## Moving between roles

Water resource specialists can bring hydrology to engineering and add engineering calculations, drawings and credential requirements. Engineers can bring facility knowledge to water resources and add watershed scenarios, demand studies and policy communication.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock brief: two demand scenarios are modeled, but the long-term design basis lacks approval. The near-term site boundary is confirmed, and the external connection needs verification. Spend ten minutes each on the water resource handoff, the preliminary design scope and the remaining approvals and inputs before release.
