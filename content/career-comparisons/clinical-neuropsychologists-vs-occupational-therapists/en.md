# Clinical Neuropsychologists vs. Occupational Therapists: Who Investigates a Missed Step When Rescheduling?

## What each assessment answers

A clinical neuropsychologist integrates interviews, testing, and observations to explain cognitive performance, including attention and memory. An occupational therapist evaluates how a person carries out a daily task and what conditions affect completion.

## Compare the work

| Compare | Clinical neuropsychologist | Occupational therapist |
|---|---|---|
| Focus here | Attention, learning, and memory findings | Finding the new date, recording it, and confirming the change |
| Deliverable | Neuropsychological assessment report | Activity observations and support needs |

## One appointment, two assessments

Imagine a client wants to reschedule follow-up appointments at home. The neuropsychologist uses an interview and assessment to describe how the client performs when holding several pieces of information briefly. The occupational therapist watches the client find the new date in a mock appointment screen but miss the confirmation step, then observes performance with a checklist. The team shares both records and decides what needs further assessment.

## Getting started in the U.S.

State psychology licensure and specialty board certification have separate requirements. California, for example, requires a qualifying doctorate, supervised professional experience and exams for psychologist licensure. For ABPP clinical neuropsychology specialty certification, candidates who completed doctoral training or respecialization on or after January 1, 2005 generally need a residency equivalent to two full years under its criteria. Occupational therapists typically need a master's degree in occupational therapy and state licensure. [California Board of Psychology](https://psychology.ca.gov/licensees/faq.shtml) · [ABPP specialty requirements](https://abpp.org/application-information/learn-about-specialty-boards/clinical-neuropsychology/specialty-specific/) · [BLS occupational therapy entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/occupational-therapists.htm#tab-4)

## Working conditions

Clinical neuropsychology involves time for interviews, assessment, and report writing. Occupational therapy may involve setting up a practice environment and documenting performance under different conditions. Ask how the reports reach the follow-up team.

## Moving between roles

Interviewing and interdisciplinary communication carry over. Moving into neuropsychology calls for specialized assessment and report training. Moving into occupational therapy calls for training in activity and environment assessment. Check the destination role's education and licensure requirements.

## Try both in 30 minutes

Use three fictional notes: the client often asks for repetition when given two pieces of information at once, missed confirmation on a mock rescheduling screen, and completed all three steps with a checklist. Spend 10 minutes writing an assessment question and an activity observation, 10 deciding what each record supports, and 10 drafting a joint handoff.
