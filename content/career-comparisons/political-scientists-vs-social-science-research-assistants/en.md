# Political Scientists vs Social Science Research Assistants

## Set the research question and assemble the evidence

Political scientists study public decisions, policies, and how institutions work. Social science research assistants help a research team collect, check, and analyze its materials. In one project, the assistant may uncover a gap in the records and the political scientist may adjust the research question because of it.

## Compare the deliverables and U.S. pay

| Item | Political scientists | Social science research assistants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Example setting | Policy research organization or university | University or survey project team |
| Typical deliverable | Policy comparison and a statement of its limitations | Source log, checked data, or draft tables |
| U.S. median annual wage, 2025 | $142,080 | $61,990 |

These are national occupational medians. Compare hours, appointment length, and pay for the specific opening. [O*NET political scientists](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-3094.00) · [O*NET research assistants](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-4061.00) · [BLS Table 1.2](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## A pause and a start belong on separate rows

New York’s Metropolitan Transportation Authority documented the June 2024 pause of Manhattan congestion pricing and recorded January 5, 2025 as the start of operations. Here is how a research team could use those public records. An assistant would give the pause announcement, board action, and operational start separate entries with sources. A political scientist would use the timeline to define a comparison period and list the additional traffic counts and context needed. The project lead would review the period and evidence before drawing any conclusions. [MTA pause record](https://www.mta.info/document/146321) · [MTA operating start](https://www.mta.info/document/180056)

## Prepare for the actual position

BLS lists a master's degree as typical entry education for political scientists and a bachelor's degree for social science research assistants. A political science candidate can show a policy comparison with a specific question, sources, and limits. A research assistant candidate can show an evidence log or a checked table with documented changes. Ask the hiring team which methods, degree, and data access training apply to the position. [BLS entry education](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Ask what happens when definitions change

For a political science position, ask how much time goes to reading policy changes, drafting reports, and defending comparison choices. For an assistant position, ask which records you maintain, how often you work in the field, and who reviews changes to the dataset. Ask what happens when a definition changes after data collection.

## Turn one work sample into another

An assistant moving toward political science can use a documented source log to build an implementation timeline and learn comparative research design. A political scientist moving toward research support can turn a past analysis into a source inventory and verification checklist, then learn the team's access and version conventions. Use public or fictional material in either sample.

## Try a 30-minute rollout exercise

Use three fictional records. Notice P lists June 1 as the announcement date for both districts. Service sheet S shows District A began accepting bookings June 1 and District B on June 8. Activity report T only covers June 1–7. Spend eight minutes labeling each source, twelve drafting a date log or comparison memo, and ten checking it. The log should preserve announcement and start dates with source IDs. The memo should specify that District B’s first operating week, June 8–14, is missing.
