# Electronics Engineers vs. Materials Scientists: The Contact Resistance Question

## What each role delivers

Electronics engineers design components and evaluate circuits or device performance. Materials scientists study composition, structure and properties to explain how samples differ.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Electronics Engineers, Except Computer | Materials Scientists |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Circuit performance and verification | Material properties and sample findings |
| Basis | Circuit specifications and device behavior | Sample composition and test conditions |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $130,220 | $117,790 |

## How the work meets

A fictional monitor shows a change in contact resistance after environmental testing. The electronics engineer repeats the checks and identifies the affected lot. The materials scientist compares records for contact samples tested under the same conditions. The team chooses which hypothesis to check first.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for both. Electronics candidates can show a circuit design and retest record. Materials candidates can show a sample study with clear test conditions. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Electronics work may follow prototype and device test cycles. Materials work may follow sample preparation, testing and analysis. Ask how the team links circuit lots to material samples.

## Moving between roles

Moving into materials research, bring failure documentation and add characterization and sample controls. Moving into electronics, bring property analysis and add circuit design and device verification.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use four simulated readings from one circuit design. Two units used different contacts and show higher resistance. Spend ten minutes each on a circuit retest table, a sample comparison and the evidence that would distinguish a lot effect from a material effect.
