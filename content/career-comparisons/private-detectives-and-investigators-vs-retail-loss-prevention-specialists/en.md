# Private detectives and investigators vs Retail Loss Prevention Specialists

## Two different kinds of findings

A private investigator answers a question defined in a client’s assignment and reports what the evidence supports. A retail loss prevention specialist reviews stock discrepancies and helps the store improve its own processes. The investigator delivers a scoped report. The specialist routes ongoing follow-up to the people who own receiving and inventory.

## Tasks, workplaces and entry

| | Private investigators | Retail loss prevention specialists |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Factual findings and limitations for the client | Discrepancy record and follow-up for retail management |
| Example workplace | Licensed investigation business | Retailer’s operations team |
| Entry focus | Qualifying experience and the state’s trade rules | Inventory, incident documentation and any credential tied to assigned duties |

## A disputed credit for returned stock

Imagine a retailer disputing a supplier credit. The loss prevention specialist reviews the approved returns record and puts two unmatched items on the inventory manager’s review list. A licensed investigation business is separately assigned to verify one claim about when the supplier acknowledged receipt. Its investigator reads the authorized documents and returns a dated finding with a limitation. The retailer decides the inventory adjustment and any action on the credit.

## California entry and work scope

The California Bureau of Security and Investigative Services distinguishes work under a licensed investigator from operating a licensed investigation business. A qualifying manager generally needs at least three years and 6,000 hours of qualifying experience, with education credits where eligible, and must meet the written exam and other eligibility requirements. An unlicensed employee may work under a licensed PI’s control and cannot independently take clients, issue reports or bill. For retail loss prevention, ask whether you will review inventory or perform regulated security duties. For the California proprietary private security officer category, check whether the job is unarmed, the employee works exclusively for one employer, the primary duty is providing security for that employer, and the services are not contracted out. The position must also require a distinctive uniform that identifies the officer and involve likely contact with the public. If the definition applies and no statutory exemption covers the job, obtain BSIS registration before working in that capacity. Check the specific jurisdiction for each job. [BSIS investigator FAQ](https://www.bsis.ca.gov/consumers/faqs/pi) · [BSIS proprietary security officer](https://www.bsis.ca.gov/forms_pubs/ppso_fact)

## Questions about workload

Ask the investigation firm who approves a change in assignment and reviews the final report. Ask the retailer how often reviews coincide with inventory counts and who follows up with operations when a discrepancy remains open.

## Moving between the roles

An investigator can bring clear source notes to retail loss prevention and learn returns, counting and inventory controls. A loss prevention specialist can bring experience explaining a discrepancy to an investigation support role, then learn scope management and verify which experience counts toward the applicable license.

## A 30-minute desk exercise

Use four fictional cards. R1 is a return with mismatched unit counts and R2 is a matched count. F1 has two conflicting dates for the supplier’s acknowledgment and F2 has two matching dates. Route R1 to the inventory manager for a documented recount and retain R2 in routine records. Flag F1 as unresolved until an authorized dated acknowledgment is available. Report F2 as consistent within the supplied records. Use ten minutes to sort, ten to draft a store referral and a findings note, and ten to confirm each owner and missing evidence.
