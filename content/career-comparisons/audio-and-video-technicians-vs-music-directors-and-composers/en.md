# Audio and Video Technicians vs. Music Directors and Composers at a Choir Recording

## Which decisions would you rather make?

Audio and video technicians coordinate media equipment and production signals. Music directors guide how a group performs a piece, while composers write or arrange music. At a recording, technical and musical choices affect each other, so the crews need signals they can share.

## Compare the work products

| Handoff | Audio and Video Technicians | Music Directors and Composers |
|---|---|---|
| Choir recording | Cue sheet, recording note, and media files | Performance cues from the director, score or arrangement from the composer |

U.S. occupational task references · [Audio and video technicians](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/27-4011.00#Tasks) · [Music directors and composers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/27-2041.00#Tasks)

## The choir holds the last note

Imagine the music director wants the final phrase to slow down and end on a visible cue. The director rehearses that choice with the choir and explains the signal to the crew. The A/V technician updates the cue sheet so the camera holds through the ending and labels the take. If the producer wants a new melody or arrangement, that becomes a separate conversation with whoever is responsible for writing it.

## Choose preparation based on the work

BLS describes A/V education as varying by position from a high school diploma to a college degree. It describes a bachelor’s degree as typical for music directors and composers, with variation by job and related experience common. For A/V, show how you documented a supervised event setup. For direction, show a rehearsal and explain a performance choice. For composition, provide a score or demo and explain a revision. [BLS media technicians](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/broadcast-and-sound-engineering-technicians.htm#tab-4) · [BLS music directors and composers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/entertainment-and-sports/music-directors-and-composers.htm#tab-4)

## Ask when the work gets reviewed

Venue A/V work follows the setup and recording window. A music director works around rehearsals and performances, while a composer may have a separate writing and review schedule. A/V applicants can ask how much setup and file delivery time is scheduled. Music directors can ask how many rehearsals they get and who chooses the repertoire. Composers can ask whether review happens on a written score or a demo and who gives the final feedback.

## Take one project across roles

A technician interested in music can use rehearsal experience to try leading a short passage while building score and listening skills. A musician interested in production can turn a performance plan into a crew cue sheet and learn how the venue manages signals and recorded materials.

## Try a 30-minute choir cue exercise

A fictional choir cue sheet ends at measure 24. The music director asks for four extra beats and will signal the release. Camera A holds a wide shot. Camera B was set to cut to the audience at the original ending. Spend ten minutes writing the musical cue, ten updating the camera cut and recording stop, and ten checking that both teams use the same signal. Flag who should review a separate request to change the melody.
