# Loss Prevention Managers vs. Security Managers: Who Reviews an Inventory Shortage?

## What each role reviews

A loss prevention manager looks for loss risks in inventory and business processes and decides which exceptions need follow-up. A security manager coordinates site protection, incident response, and documentation. Employers assign the specific investigation responsibilities.

## Compare the work

| Compare | Loss prevention manager | Security manager |
|---|---|---|
| Question here | Do the count, transfers, and returns agree? | What relevant incident did the shift record? |
| Output | Reconciliation, open items, and process follow-up | Verified incident summary and handoff |

## The same inventory count

Imagine a store counts six fewer items than its records show. The loss prevention manager finds a transfer entry for four units and asks for receiving confirmation. The security manager checks the shift reports for a related incident and shares an authorized summary with the time and review scope. Loss prevention separates any reconciled inventory from open items and assigns the next follow-up.

## Working conditions

Loss prevention work may follow stock counts and depend on records from multiple stores or teams. Security managers may cover shifts and respond to new incident reports. Ask who maintains records and approves access to them.

## Moving between roles

Loss prevention managers can use their review and training experience in security and develop shift coordination. Security managers can use their handoff and documentation experience in loss prevention and learn stock records, transfers, and loss analysis.

## Try both in 30 minutes

Use three fictional records. The ledger shows 30 units and the physical count shows 24. A transfer lists four units with receipt still unconfirmed. The shift report lists no related incident in that period. Spend 10 minutes drafting a reconciliation checklist, 10 writing a shift summary with its scope, and 10 drafting a handoff that identifies what remains unexplained.
