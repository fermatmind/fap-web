# Physical Therapists vs. Sports Medicine Physicians: Who Reviews Readiness to Return to Competition?

## What each role reviews

A physical therapist assesses movement and physical function and reassesses treatment progress. A sports medicine physician examines athletes, coordinates referrals, and advises on whether returning could cause further injury.

## Compare the work

| Compare | Physical therapist | Sports medicine physician |
|---|---|---|
| Focus here | Practice performance and conditions | Follow-up findings and medical considerations |
| Output | Functional reassessment | Medical recommendation and follow-up plan |

## The same return-to-competition question

Imagine a school athlete wants to return to competition next week. The physical therapist documents which clinic activities the athlete finished, where they stopped early, and which competition demands have yet to be assessed. The sports medicine physician reviews that report alongside the history and follow-up examination and provides a medical recommendation. The team documents who receives the recommendation and when open items will be reviewed.

## Getting started in the U.S.

Physical therapists entering the field need a Doctor of Physical Therapy degree and state licensure. The U.S. physician pathway involves medical school and residency, and all states require physician licensure. After that, one sports medicine credential route is the American Board of Family Medicine's Certificate of Added Qualification. It requires maintaining family medicine certification, completing at least 12 months of full-time training in an ACGME-accredited sports medicine fellowship, completing the application, and passing the sports medicine exam. [BLS physical therapist entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/physical-therapists.htm#tab-4) · [BLS physician entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/physicians-and-surgeons.htm#tab-4) · [ABFM sports medicine credential](https://www.theabfm.org/added-qualifications/sports-medicine/)

## Working conditions

Physical therapists need time for practice sessions and reassessment. Physicians may coordinate follow-up with training or competition schedules. Ask who receives the medical recommendation and how new findings are communicated.

## Moving between roles

Movement observation and clinical communication carry over. Physician practice requires its own medical education and licensure. Moving into physical therapy requires its own professional education and licensing pathway.

## Try both in 30 minutes

Use fictional notes. The athlete completes the first task, stops midway through the second, and has not been assessed in competition conditions. The physician reviews the case Thursday. Spend 10 minutes writing the functional note, 10 identifying what the physician should review, and 10 drafting a handoff that leaves unresolved items open.
