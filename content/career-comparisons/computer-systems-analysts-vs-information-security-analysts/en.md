# Computer Systems Analysts vs. Information Security Analysts: The Release Review

## What each role delivers

Systems analysts translate business requirements into workflows and acceptance criteria. Information security analysts assess risks and recommend safeguards.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Computer Systems Analysts | Information Security Analysts |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Workflow and acceptance criteria | Risk assessment and control recommendations |
| Basis | Business requirements | Authorized evidence and risk priorities |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $105,850 | $129,180 |

## How the work meets

A fictional tracking tool displays submission status. The systems analyst documents which views employees and managers need. The security analyst evaluates the proposed disclosure scope and records any conditions. Both submit their notes to the designated release owner.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for both roles and less than five years of related experience for information security analysts. Ask the employer which systems, policies and review duties apply. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Systems analysts work around business interviews and change reviews. Security analysts follow risk evidence and remediation follow-up. Ask who accepts findings or approves a release.

## Moving between roles

Moving into security, use impact analysis and learn control assessment. Moving into systems analysis, use risk communication and learn process mapping and feature acceptance.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use a fictional matrix: employees see their own status, managers see department totals, and export access is undecided. Spend ten minutes each on acceptance, risk notes and approval ownership. Use records only.
