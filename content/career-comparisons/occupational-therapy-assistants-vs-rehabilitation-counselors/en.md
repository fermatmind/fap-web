# Occupational Therapy Assistants vs. Rehabilitation Counselors: Who Follows Up After a Practice Task?

## What each role delivers

An occupational therapy assistant implements a treatment plan under supervision and records performance. A rehabilitation counselor works with the client on employment goals and coordinates training and other supports.

## The same job training goal

Imagine a client preparing for a placement in a community records office. The assistant guides a label-sorting activity from the treatment plan and reports where the client pauses without the illustrated cue. With permission, the supervising therapist shares relevant observations with the rehabilitation counselor. The counselor asks the training provider whether it can supply a comparable cue and agrees on first-week support and follow-up with the client.

## Getting started in the U.S.

BLS says occupational therapy assistants typically need an associate degree from an accredited program and usually need a state-issued license. Rehabilitation counselors typically need a master's degree in rehabilitation counseling or a related field. Some jobs accept a bachelor's degree, and some require a license or certification. Check the applicable state and employer requirements. ## Working conditions

Assistants may record performance between treatment sessions. Counselors often coordinate schedules with clients and providers. Ask when therapy notes are reviewed and who follows up with the training site.

## Moving between roles

Assistants can bring observation and communication into counseling and learn referral and employment planning. Counselors can bring interviewing and documentation into assistant training and develop skills in supervised treatment. Verify destination-role requirements.

## Try both in 30 minutes

Use three fictional notes. The client sorts the labels with a three-step visual cue and pauses at step two without it. The training site can print materials but has yet to confirm a format. The client agrees to share only the request for a visual cue with the provider. Spend 10 minutes on the assistant's observation, 10 on the counselor's questions for the provider, and 10 on a handoff that specifies what may be shared and who reviews it.
