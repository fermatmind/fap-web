# Postal Service Mail Carriers vs Postmasters and Mail Superintendents

## One route's outcomes and staffing across routes

Mail carriers prepare and deliver mail along assigned routes and record exceptions. Postmasters and mail superintendents coordinate staffing and operations, including the way unresolved delivery work is handed off. The difference is visible when a route is interrupted: the carrier reports what happened while the manager assigns the next action.

## A road closure interrupts one route

In a fictional postal unit, a road closure prevents the carrier from reaching three stops. The carrier records the affected stops and brings the undelivered items and exception record back to the unit. The manager designates who will check access before the next delivery attempt and arranges coverage from another route if access is confirmed. The carrier follows the confirmed assignment. The manager checks at shift handoff that every open stop has an owner.

## Check entry data and the specific USPS process

BLS lists no formal educational credential and short-term on-the-job training as typical for carriers. For postmasters and mail superintendents it lists high school or equivalent, less than five years of related experience, and moderate-term on-the-job training as typical. USPS lists VEA 474 for specified carrier jobs. For driving duties, check the license and driving record requirements and the carrier road test before driving on the job. For a posted postmaster position under EL-312, check the area of consideration and required qualifications, including whether external applicants are eligible. ## Ask what changes on an interrupted shift

For delivery, ask what portion of the assignment is outdoors, whether you cover multiple routes, and where you return undelivered items. For management, ask how many routes or sites you oversee, who approves staff coverage, and how service complaints and unresolved work get assigned at shift change.

## Switch by turning a handoff into a work sample

A carrier can turn a fictional interruption record into a staffing and follow-up plan, then seek supervised shift coordination and check eligibility for the vacancy. A manager moving to delivery can show how to turn an operations plan into clear route instructions, then practice route sequencing and accurate delivery records. Check the carrier assessment and applicable driving requirements. [USPS exams](https://about.usps.com/careers/how-to-apply/exams.htm) · [USPS selection](https://about.usps.com/handbooks/el312/el312c7_020.htm)

## Try a 30-minute paper exercise

Use a fictional log. Route A has three unfinished stops because a road is closed. Route B is complete and has one employee available for coverage. Access has yet to be verified. Spend eight minutes identifying the known facts, twelve drafting either a carrier return note or a manager's coverage plan, and ten checking it. Keep the three stops open, name who checks access and when, and for the manager's plan state when coverage can begin.
