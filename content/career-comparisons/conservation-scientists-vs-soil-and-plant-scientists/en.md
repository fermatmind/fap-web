# Conservation Scientist vs. Soil and Plant Scientist: Applying a Ground-Cover Trial

## What each role delivers

A conservation scientist combines land, water and soil information to plan conservation. A soil and plant scientist studies responses to management practices and may use those findings to advise land users.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Conservation Scientist | Soil and Plant Scientists |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Parcel-specific measures and monitoring plan | Soil and plant comparisons and limits of the evidence |
| Basis | Slope, erosion, access and maintenance | Soil, vegetation, treatment and observation conditions |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $73,010 | $78,850 |

## How the work meets

A fictional county compares ground cover on trial plots. The soil and plant scientist reports better vegetation cover on loam in the treatment than in the comparison plot and notes that another soil type had no comparison plot. The conservation scientist combines the result with slope, access and maintenance capacity. The plan prioritizes supported locations and proposes additional trials elsewhere.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for both roles. Conservation candidates can show a site plan and monitoring criteria; soil and plant science candidates can show field study design and analysis. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Soil and plant research follows seasons and observation schedules. Conservation projects also need land-user coordination and implementation windows. Ask which parcels are scheduled for further verification.

## Moving between roles

Conservation scientists can bring field context to soil and plant research and add study design and statistics. Soil and plant scientists can bring land and crop knowledge into conservation and add spatial planning, implementation and maintenance.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock evidence: site A has comparable records for two ground-cover treatments. Site B is steeper and has different soil with no trial results. Spend ten minutes each on what the trial supports, checks needed for site B and a plan by parcel.
