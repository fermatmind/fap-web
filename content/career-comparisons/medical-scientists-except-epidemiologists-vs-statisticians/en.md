# Medical Scientist vs. Statistician: What Can a Follow-Up Study Establish?

## What each role delivers

Medical scientists, except epidemiologists, design and interpret medical research. Statisticians develop or verify comparisons and report estimates and their limits. Both may contribute to the study design.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Medical Scientists, Except Epidemiologists | Statisticians |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Study question, measurement rationale and medical interpretation | Analysis rules, estimate and uncertainty |
| Basis | Study purpose, measurement timing and population | Design, unknown records, target measure and error |

## How the work meets

A fictional study compares two follow-up reminder schedules. The medical scientist explains that the study tracks attendance rather than health improvement. The statistician counts completed and unknown visits by group, produces estimates under the agreed rules and shows how unknown records affect the comparison. The team reports both limits.

## How to enter

BLS lists a doctoral or professional degree as typical for medical scientists and a master’s for statisticians. Ask about research duties and check licensing requirements if the position includes patient care. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Medical research follows protocols, follow-up and reporting. Statistics also follows data versions, freezes and reviews. Ask what domain collaboration the job requires.

## Moving between roles

Medical scientists bring study design into statistics and can add inference and method validation. Statisticians can bring analysis to medical research and add domain knowledge, research ethics and measurement.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock file: 14 of 20 in group A have confirmed visits, with two unknown. In group B, 12 of 20 have confirmed visits, with four unknown. Spend ten minutes each on the research question, bounds under different outcomes for unknown visits, and a statement of what cannot be concluded.
