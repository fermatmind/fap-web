# Postal Service Clerks vs Postmasters and Mail Superintendents

## Service at the counter and responsibility for the operation

A Postal Service clerk helps customers select services, accepts and rates mail, and records the transaction. Postmasters and mail superintendents organize staff and operations for a facility or group of facilities. A clerk can take responsibility for getting a transaction right. A manager has to keep the entire shift covered and resolve escalated problems.

## Outputs and U.S. pay

| Item | Postal Service clerks | Postmasters and mail superintendents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Example setting | Retail counter | Post office or postal operations unit |
| Typical output | Acceptance record, receipt, and handoff | Staff schedule, operating decisions, and escalation follow-up |
| U.S. median annual wage, 2025 | $62,130 | $96,660 |

These are national occupational medians. Compare the actual appointment, grade, and schedule when evaluating an opening. ## A handoff record goes missing just before dispatch

At a fictional postal facility, a clerk finds receipts for two accepted pieces while the outgoing list contains only one. The clerk records the discrepancy and routes the two records to the supervisor. The supervisor puts an authorized employee on the reconciliation, assigns another employee to the growing customer line, and names who will follow up. Under this facility's procedure the clerk updates the handoff when the evidence is confirmed, and the supervisor verifies the follow-up is assigned.

## Check the actual USPS announcement

BLS lists no formal educational credential and short-term on-the-job training as typical for postal clerks. For postmasters and mail superintendents it lists high school or equivalent, less than five years of related experience, and moderate-term on-the-job training as typical. USPS lists VEA 477 for specified retail roles. For a posted postmaster role under the nonbargaining selection process, check the area of consideration and the qualifications, including whether the vacancy is open to external applicants. ## Ask who covers the rush and what happens after it

At the counter, ask when queues peak, who checks the closing record, and which questions go to a supervisor. For management, ask how many staff or sites you would cover, who can adjust staffing, and how unresolved cases move into the next shift.

## Show the next role's work in a sample

Moving from the counter into management, turn a fictional discrepancy note into an incident follow-up with staff coverage, owners, and a deadline. Seek opportunities to practice supervised shift coordination, then check the vacancy's eligibility. Moving to the counter, use a fictional complaint response to demonstrate clear communication and build practice with retail service decisions, receipts, and end-of-shift reconciliation. Check the assessment for the target title. [USPS selection process](https://about.usps.com/handbooks/el312/el312c7_020.htm) · [USPS exams](https://about.usps.com/careers/how-to-apply/exams.htm)

## Try a 30-minute dispatch exercise

Use a fictional dispatch due in twenty minutes, a line of six customers, and receipts for A and B. Only A is on the outgoing list. One other employee can help. Spend eight minutes marking what is known, twelve writing either a counter exception note or a supervisor's staffing and follow-up plan, and ten checking it. The note should leave B unresolved and attach its source record. The plan should name a reviewer, assign queue coverage, and set a time to check back.
