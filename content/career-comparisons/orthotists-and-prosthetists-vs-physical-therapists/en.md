# Orthotists and Prosthetists vs. Physical Therapists: What Does Each Check After a Prosthesis Fitting?

## What each role checks

Orthotists and prosthetists assess device needs, fit, and comfort. Physical therapists assess movement and physical function and review treatment plans.

## The same fitting follow-up

Imagine a client with a lower-limb prosthesis reports no discomfort while seated but describes a loose feeling after turning. The physical therapist documents the activity and assistance at the time and shares that observation with the prosthetist. The prosthetist checks device fit within the prescription and care plan, reports what the check showed or what remains open, and the physical therapist uses that information to plan the next mobility assessment.

## Getting started in the U.S.

Orthotists and prosthetists typically complete a master's program and residency, and some states require licensure. Physical therapists entering the occupation need a Doctor of Physical Therapy degree, and all states require licensure. Verify the specific state's requirements. ## Working conditions

Device work can involve measuring, fittings, and coordinating fabrication. Physical therapy includes time for assessment and documentation. Ask how the team schedules joint follow-up and who communicates the next step.

## Moving between roles

Both roles use interviewing and clinical handoffs. Moving into device work takes training in measurement, design, and fitting. Moving into physical therapy takes clinical training in movement and physical function. Verify qualifications for the destination role.

## Try both in 30 minutes

Use three fictional notes. The seated fitting produced no reported discomfort. The client reported looseness after turning in the hallway with a staff member guarding nearby. A device follow-up has not been completed. Spend 10 minutes writing the mobility note, 10 deciding what the prosthetist should verify, and 10 drafting the two-way handoff.
