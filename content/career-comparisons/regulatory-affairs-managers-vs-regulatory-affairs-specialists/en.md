# Regulatory affairs managers vs. regulatory affairs specialists

## Who sets the plan and who gets the package ready?

Regulatory affairs managers develop strategies and set review priorities, budgets and staffing across projects. Regulatory affairs specialists can develop strategies for specific products and prepare or review submission materials. Both may work on strategy and documents. Ask who owns the product plan, who allocates resources across projects, and who has sign-off authority.

## Compare the roles

| Item | Regulatory affairs manager | Regulatory affairs specialist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main deliverable | A strategy, review plan and record of priorities | A product strategy or checked package, issue log and version record |
| Work setting to ask about | Meetings across products, reviewers and project teams | Document review and follow-up with subject-matter teams |
| U.S. preparation evidence | O*NET Job Zone Four, considerable preparation | O*NET Job Zone Four, considerable preparation |

O*NET wage displays use the broader Managers, All Other and Compliance Officers categories, respectively. [O*NET manager tasks and wage scope](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-9199.01) · [O*NET specialist tasks and wage scope](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/13-1041.07)

## Two packages need one reviewer

At a fictional company, only one review slot remains today. Package A is complete and has an internal deadline today. Package B has a missing confirmation and is due tomorrow. The specialist reports each package's status and deadline. The manager allocates today's slot to A and arranges follow-up for B. The specialist updates the tracker and collects the missing item. The designated signatory makes the submission decision.

## Getting started in the U.S.

O*NET places both roles in Job Zone Four. Most occupations in this zone require a four-year bachelor's degree, with exceptions. For a specialist path, document or project support can build experience checking versions and tracking requirements. For a manager path, build on review experience with project prioritization, coordinating reviewers and escalating decisions. Ask employers which product experience they expect and how authority is delegated. [O*NET manager preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-9199.01#JobZone) · [O*NET specialist preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/13-1041.07#JobZone)

## What changes near a deadline?

In the example, the specialist follows individual document gaps while the manager balances review time across projects. Ask how many packages or projects you would cover, who resolves missing input, and when a late change needs escalation.

## Moving between the roles

A specialist can bring a redacted checklist and missed-deadline review, then develop resource planning and cross-team decision skills. A manager can bring a redacted strategy decision and review handoff, then build detailed practice assembling packages, checking versions and following up on individual questions.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use fictional project cards. There is one review slot today and another tomorrow morning. A is complete and due for internal review by close today. B is due by close tomorrow and has a missing confirmation expected tomorrow morning. B may use tomorrow’s slot only if the confirmation arrives before review starts. Otherwise, the manager must escalate or reschedule. External submission requires a separate sign-off. Spend ten minutes checking the cards, ten allocating review slots and follow-up, and ten writing the specialist’s tracker update and manager’s decision note. Name who schedules review, who follows up and who gives final authorization.
