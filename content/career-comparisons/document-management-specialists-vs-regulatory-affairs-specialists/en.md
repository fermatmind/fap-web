# Document management specialists vs. regulatory affairs specialists

## File status and submission requirements

Document management specialists keep records findable and changes traceable. Regulatory affairs specialists identify what applies to a submission, coordinate documents and may develop product strategies. Both check documents. The questions are whether the record is controlled and whether it fits the submission.

## Compare the work

| Item | Document management specialist | Regulatory affairs specialist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deliverable | A verified version, owner and filing trail | A submission checklist, gap record or product strategy |
| Work setting to ask about | Document systems supporting multiple teams | Regulatory team coordinating content owners and reviewers |
| U.S. preparation evidence | O*NET Job Zone Four, considerable preparation | O*NET Job Zone Four, considerable preparation |

## The checklist calls for one more attachment

At a fictional company, two approved attachments are in the controlled folder. A declaration is required under the project checklist, but only its draft is available. The document specialist checks and reports the status. The regulatory specialist confirms the checklist still applies and asks the owner for the approved declaration. The document specialist records the approved file once received. The regulatory specialist verifies the package and sends it to the designated signatory.

## Getting started in the U.S.

O*NET puts both in Job Zone Four, where most occupations require a four-year bachelor's degree, with exceptions. Document support work can build experience in file classification, workflows and revision records. Regulatory project support can build experience finding requirements, tracking documents and explaining gaps. Ask which systems, products and responsibilities the employer expects. [O*NET document preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-1299.03#JobZone) · [O*NET regulatory preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/13-1041.07#JobZone)

## Ask who handles exceptions

In the example, the document team records revisions as they are approved, while regulatory staff follow up before review deadlines. Ask how filing batches are checked, who decides whether a checklist item applies and who communicates a delay.

## Moving between the roles

A document specialist can bring a redacted version register and handoff, then learn requirement research and submission review. A regulatory specialist can bring a redacted checklist and revision record, then practice document system workflows, metadata and retention checks.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a mock checklist. Attachments 1 and 2 are approved; the declaration exists only as a draft. The project requires approved copies of all three before sign-off. Take ten minutes marking file status, ten drafting the document specialist's version verification and the regulatory specialist's request, and ten writing the readiness note. State who supplies the approved file, who verifies the checklist and who signs the package.
