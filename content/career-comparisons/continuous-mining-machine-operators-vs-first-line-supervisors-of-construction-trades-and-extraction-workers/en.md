# Continuous Mining Machine Operators vs. First-Line Extraction Supervisors

## What each role delivers

Continuous mining machine operators carry out assigned machine work and report problems. In an extraction crew, first-line supervisors coordinate assignments, check progress and route issues beyond the operator’s authority.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Continuous Mining Machine Operators | First-Line Supervisors of Construction Trades and Extraction Workers |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Machine assignment and exception report | Crew plan and unresolved shift items |
| Scope | Assigned equipment and work area | Several assignments and their dependencies |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $61,810 | $79,920 |

## How the work meets

In a fictional shift handoff, the operator reports that maintenance has taken the M2 ticket but has supplied no update. The supervisor sees two crews assigned to the same area. The operator carries forward the ticket status, while the supervisor resolves the schedule conflict and leaves M2 work pending confirmation.

## How to enter

BLS lists no formal educational credential and moderate-term on-the-job training as typical for continuous mining machine operators. It lists high school or equivalent and five or more years of related work experience as typical for first-line supervisors. At mines covered by MSHA’s underground training rules, §48.7 sets training requirements for new task assignments, subject to its stated conditions. [BLS rows 47-5041 and 47-1011](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) [30 CFR 48.7](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-30/chapter-I/subchapter-H/part-48/subpart-A/section-48.7)

## Work conditions

Operators work around equipment and the assigned underground area. Supervisors divide attention between crews, conditions and open issues. Ask how each shift confirms ownership of pending checks.

## Moving between roles

To move into supervision, build from equipment and exception reports to crew plans and documented follow-through. To move into operation, complete the mine’s training for the assigned equipment and task.
