# Agricultural Inspector vs. Precision Agriculture Technician

## Inspection evidence and field data for production

Agricultural inspectors check commodities, facilities, or operations against applicable requirements. Precision agriculture technicians use positioning data and maps to support farm work. An inspector may use a map alongside records that identify a particular lot.

## Compare what each role delivers

| Work | Agricultural inspector | Precision agriculture technician |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First check | Inspection requirements, lot records, and evidence | Field identifiers, map versions, and location data |
| Deliverable | Inspection findings and requests for evidence | A documented map and an identifier cross-reference |

O*NET reports wages for the broader Agricultural Technicians category, so its table cannot isolate precision agriculture technician pay. [O*NET wage coverage](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-4012.01#WagesEmployment)

## A lot record and a map show different field IDs

At a fictional cooperative, a receiving record lists Field A7, but the current map shows A17. The technician checks map versions and sends a cross-reference noting that the relationship is still unconfirmed. The inspector checks the harvest and receiving records and asks the record owner for documentation that identifies the lot’s origin.

## U.S. preparation

O*NET places agricultural inspectors in Job Zone 1–2. Seek supervised work that develops inspection and recordkeeping skills and check the hiring agency or employer’s specific qualifications. Precision agriculture technicians are in Job Zone Three. A GIS course or supervised agricultural technology project can build experience working with field maps and location data. [Inspection preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/45-2011.00#JobZone) · [Precision agriculture preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-4012.01#JobZone)

## Compare worksites and schedules

Inspection can involve visits to production or handling facilities and time for reports. Precision agriculture work may involve checking location data in the field and processing maps indoors. Ask how harvest schedules affect the workday and who reviews records before they are used. [Inspection work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/45-2011.00#WorkContext) · [Precision agriculture work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-4012.01#WorkContext)

## Moving between the roles

An inspector can bring field documentation and review skills, then learn GIS and spatial data quality. A technician can bring mapping and traceability experience, then build knowledge of inspection requirements and reporting procedures.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use three fictional records. The receiving form says A7. The old map includes A7. The new map shows A17 only and has no cross-reference. Spend ten minutes listing the known IDs and sources, ten writing a question for the mapping team, and ten noting what evidence you would still need to establish the lot’s origin.
