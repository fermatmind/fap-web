# Chemical Technician vs. Chemist: What Happens When a Reference Check Fails?

## What each role delivers

A chemical technician runs tests and records sample and instrument conditions. A chemist studies substances, interprets results and may develop or improve the methods used to measure them. Both can work at the bench and take part in method validation.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Chemical Technicians | Chemists |
|---|---|---|
| First checks | Sample IDs, method revision and instrument and reference records | Method scope, prior validation and explanations for the result |
| Output | Traceable test records and exception reports | Interpretation, method review or a validation plan |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $60,390 | $91,240 |

## How the work meets

A fictional paper-products lab measures color. The technician notices that the reference sample also reads outside the lab's acceptance range. They retain the raw data and give the chemist the method revision, instrument log and list of affected samples. The chemist reviews what the method was validated to measure and requests checks of the reference sample and instrument under the lab's procedure before deciding whether the method itself needs review. The affected results remain open.

## How to enter

BLS lists an associate's degree with moderate-term on-the-job training for chemical technicians and a bachelor's degree as typical entry education for chemists. Technician applicants can show a sample log or an exception report. Chemistry applicants can show a method comparison and explain its validation scope. [BLS Table 1.2](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) Some chemistry and materials research jobs may require a master’s degree or Ph.D. [BLS research education](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/life-physical-and-social-science/chemists-and-materials-scientists.htm)

## Work conditions

Technician schedules may follow incoming samples and instrument availability. Chemists may also spend time planning validation, analyzing results and writing reports. Ask how the lab preserves original results and who receives an exception.

## Moving between roles

Technicians can bring instrument and sample traceability skills to chemistry and build experiment design and interpretation skills. Chemists can bring method knowledge to technician work and build experience in routine documentation, throughput and exception handling.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use a mock test run with ten samples, a failed reference check and a missing instrument log entry. Take ten minutes to mark the affected evidence, ten to write the technician's handoff and ten to list the chemist's review questions before assigning a cause. Use paper records only.
