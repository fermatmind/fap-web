# Social Science Research Assistant vs. Statistician: Recalculating After a Data Correction

## What each role delivers

Social science research assistants verify and organize data and may produce analytic tables. Statisticians plan and validate estimates and explain how revisions affect the results.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Social Science Research Assistants | Statisticians |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Dataset version, correction log and QA table | Estimate, rerun and explanation of changes |
| Basis | Source forms, coding rules and exceptions | Target measure, unit of analysis and error |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $61,990 | $105,650 |

## How the work meets

A fictional after-school study finds the same local ID at two centers. The assistant checks source forms, combines the center and local IDs, and flags rows that still need verification. The statistician confirms the unit of analysis, reruns the participation estimate and checks whether the previous table needs replacement.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for social science research assistants and a master’s for statisticians. Show a QA record or a reproducible estimate, depending on the role. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Assistants work around data delivery and QA cycles. Statisticians also follow freeze and publication schedules. Ask how a correction reaches a published table.

## Moving between roles

Assistants can build on data QA and add sampling, estimation and uncertainty analysis. Statisticians can bring analytical skills and learn collection records and version management.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock file: two records labeled 07 belong to different centers. A third 07 is from the same center as one of them but has a different timestamp. Spend ten minutes each on flags, verification and version notes, and when to rerun the estimate.
