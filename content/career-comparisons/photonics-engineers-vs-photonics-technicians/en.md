# Photonics Engineers vs Photonics Technicians

## Design the test or make the result reproducible

Photonics engineers turn performance requirements into prototypes and verification plans. Photonics technicians set up, check, and document measurements. Decide whether you would rather choose what the experiment should answer or make sure another person can reproduce what the bench showed.

## Compare the handoffs

| Work | Photonics engineers | Photonics technicians |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inputs | Requirements, prototype, and previous results | Work instruction, sample, fixture, and instruments |
| Delivery | Test plan, analysis, and proposed design changes | Raw readings, setup notes, and exception log |
| Main check | Does the result support the design decision? | Can the next person repeat the setup and trace the result? |

[O*NET engineers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/17-2199.07) · [O*NET technicians](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/17-3029.08)

## A reference check changes after a meter swap

NIST describes measuring LED lamp output and evaluating manufacturers’ in-house meters. In a fictional lighting lab, the reference check shifts after an instrument swap. The technician saves the readings, meter IDs, and reference lamp ID before alerting the project engineer. The engineer requests an overlap check with both instruments on the same reference before interpreting any change in the product. [NIST lighting measurement work](https://www.nist.gov/pml/about-pml/pml-working-you/it-really-bright)

## Compare two U.S. training routes

The University of Rochester offers a BS in optical engineering with a team design capstone. Monroe Community College offers an Optical Systems Technology AAS with hands-on fabrication and testing. O*NET puts photonics engineers in Job Zone Four (considerable preparation) and photonics technicians in Job Zone Three (medium preparation). Zone Four commonly involves a bachelor’s degree and substantial preparation. Zone Three includes routes such as vocational training, related on-the-job experience, or an associate degree. Ask engineering employers what design and analysis they expect and technician employers what equipment training and sign-off the job requires. [Rochester optical engineering program](https://www.hajim.rochester.edu/optics/undergraduate/index.html) · [MCC associate degree route](https://www.monroecc.edu/academics/programs/optical-systems-technology/index.html) · [O*NET engineering preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/17-2199.07) · [O*NET technician preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/17-3029.08)

## Ask about lab routines and pay coverage

Engineers may divide their time between design review and prototype tests. Technicians may spend much of a shift at a bench or in a controlled work area, depending on the assignment. Ask about shift coverage, cleanroom work and who clears failed runs. O*NET displays wage figures for two broader occupational groups. Compare actual postings for role-specific pay, overtime and shift terms. ## Build the missing piece for a move

From technician work, bring a test log and add a requirement, a predicted result, and a rationale for the next experiment. From engineering, bring your system knowledge and demonstrate a repeatable procedure with setup, instrument status, and raw readings.

## Try a 30-minute paper handoff

Use a fictional lighting lab log. Reference lamp M07 reads 98 normalized units on meter A and 91 on meter B. The entries were taken on different days, and both meters are in calibration. Spend 12 minutes writing a technician log showing the reference ID, meter and reading. Spend 13 minutes writing an engineer’s plan to compare both meters on M07 in the same session. Use five minutes to check that both records preserve the original readings and leave the cause open. No equipment is needed.
