# Mining Roof Bolter vs. Oil and Gas Rotary Drill Operator

## Drilling serves different purposes

Mining roof bolters drill holes and install bolts as part of underground ground support and check bolting equipment. Rotary drill operators carry out oil and gas drilling work, log progress and coordinate assigned crew tasks.

## Compare what goes into the shift handoff

| Item | Mining roof bolter | Rotary drill operator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work area | An underground area assigned for roof support | An assigned well and drilling interval |
| Status to report | Support work status and area references | Drilling activity, footage and tools used |
| Follow-up | Resolve which area and plan version the record covers | Identify the well interval and verify the shift entry |

[O*NET 47-5043.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/47-5043.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 47-5012.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/47-5012.00#Tasks)

## One training lead reviews two transfers

A fictional contractor has two workers with drilling experience requesting transfers. One wants an underground support job, where a roof bolter assigned to mentor them checks whether they can identify the support area and explain its status. The other wants a rig crew job, where a drill operator assigned to mentor them checks how they match a borehole assignment to a drilling log. The training lead uses the two observations to assign different training and evaluators. The record check informs training; approval for work remains with the designated mine or rig personnel.

## U.S. education and on-the-job training

BLS lists high school or equivalent as typical for mining roof bolters, and no formal educational credential for oil and gas rotary drill operators. It assigns moderate-term on-the-job training to both. Ask the mine or rig employer about training that applies to the setting and the equipment before independent work. [BLS education and training table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/education-and-training-by-occupation.htm) MSHA Part 48 covers underground mines. Ask the mine about its required new miner and task training. [MSHA training coverage](https://arlweb.msha.gov/S%26HINFO/minersrights/OnlineTraining/health_safety_training_part_46_or_part_48.html) BLS assigns less than five years of related experience to rotary drill operators. For the comparison occupation, the table assigns no prior related experience.

## Ask how the shift is organized

Ask a mine employer where underground crews meet for handoff and how much shift time is spent getting to and from the work area. Ask a drilling employer about rotations, time assigned to logs and who receives unresolved issues.

## Skills that can transfer

Roof bolters can use their experience with equipment checks and location references when training for drill work. They need rig-specific operating and crew skills. Rotary drill operators can bring crew handoffs and recordkeeping to mine work, then learn ground-support equipment, area plans and mine-specific work requirements.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use fictional worker skill cards, an underground support handoff and a drilling log. Spend ten minutes drafting a record-check task for a roof bolter trainee, ten minutes doing the same for a rig trainee, and ten minutes identifying who evaluates each gap. Use the exercise only to plan training.
