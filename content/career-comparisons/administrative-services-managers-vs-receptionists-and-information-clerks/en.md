# Administrative Services Managers vs. Receptionists and Information Clerks During a Rush

## Which work do you want to own?

Receptionists and information clerks handle today's visitors, calls, and appointments. Administrative services managers can decide how the desk is staffed and organized. Both care about wait times. The receptionist works through the current queue, while the manager investigates why it forms.

## Compare the outputs

| Work product | Administrative Services Managers | Receptionists and Information Clerks |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Desk coverage and an updated procedure | Visitor directions, messages, and appointment records |

U.S. occupational task references · [O*NET 11-3012.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-3012.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 43-4171.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-4171.00#Tasks)

## How the work intersects

Two groups arrive early for different meetings. The receptionist checks their appointments, calls the hosts, and directs each group according to the office's visitor procedure. The manager reviews when the delays recur, tests extra coverage for overlapping meetings, and checks whether clearer appointment notices reduce confusion.

## Build entry experience

BLS describes a bachelor’s degree and related experience as typical for administrative services managers. [BLS occupational preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/administrative-services-managers.htm#tab-4) O*NET classifies receptionists and information clerks as work that generally requires very little to some preparation. [O*NET Job Zone](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-4171.00#JobZone) Job Zones describe a broad level of preparation. Ask the employer which education, training and experience count for the specific job.

To explore reception, practice routing calls and maintaining a fictional appointment calendar. To explore administrative services, build a coverage plan around measured peak times. Ask the employer whether a job handles an individual desk, a service team, or both.

## What to ask at an interview

At the front desk, visitors and calls can require attention at once. An administrative manager can look across shifts and use recurring delays to change staffing or procedures. Ask reception employers what happens when calls and visitors arrive together and who helps with ambiguous requests. Ask the administrative team who can change desk coverage and how service quality is reviewed.

## Moving between the roles

Build toward administration by turning recurring front desk issues into a proposal for a procedure or staffing trial. If moving toward reception, practice uncovering what each person needs and writing a useful handoff.

## Try the work in 30 minutes

Three fictional visitors arrive at 9. A's host is available. B's host cannot respond until 9:15, and C has no booking. The desk checks each booking first, gives a wait estimate, and admits visitors only after confirming the visit. Spend ten minutes writing the desk response, fifteen proposing a change for next week, and five specifying what to measure.
