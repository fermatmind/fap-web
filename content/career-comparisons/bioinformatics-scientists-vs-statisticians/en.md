# Bioinformatics Scientist vs. Statistician: Repeated Records in a Plant Study

## What each role delivers

Bioinformatics scientists develop and verify computational workflows for biological records. Statisticians define estimates and comparisons, evaluate assumptions and report uncertainty.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Bioinformatics Scientists | Statisticians |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Harmonized data, field definitions and QA record | Analysis target, method and uncertainty |
| Basis | Plant identifiers, times, batches and sources | Design, repeated observations and missingness |

## How the work meets

A fictional greenhouse study compares two light conditions. The bioinformatics scientist identifies local IDs reused in two batches, preserves batch and plant labels and distinguishes records from repeated observations of one plant. The statistician checks how conditions were assigned and counts actual plants per group before defining the estimate and treatment of repeats.

## Work conditions

Computational workflows depend on data and field changes. Statistics also follows analysis plans, freezes and reporting. Ask what must be updated if identifiers change.

## Moving between roles

Bioinformatics scientists can bring workflow skills into statistics and add sampling, estimation and uncertainty analysis. Statisticians can bring inference and computing into bioinformatics and add biological data interpretation, integration and workflow testing.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock file: ID 07 appears twice in batch A at different times, and batch B also uses 07 in a different greenhouse area. Spend ten minutes each on linking the records, defining the analysis unit and assignment details to check, and drafting what can be reported now.
