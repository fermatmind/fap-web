# Art Therapist vs. Recreational Therapist

## The material can be the same while the goals differ

Art therapists may use a creative process for expression and reflection, recording how the client engages and describes the work. Recreational therapists can use art alongside other activities to support goals such as joining, choosing or sustaining participation.

## Compare what goes in the record

| Decision | Art therapist | Recreational therapist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plan | Select media to support a goal for expression or reflection | Adapt the activity to an assessed participation goal |
| Observe | How the client uses the medium and describes the experience | What the client does independently and what support is needed |
| Follow up | Revisit the response to the creative process | Review participation and adjust the activity plan |

## One collage group, two observations

A fictional rehabilitation group uses collage. One participant chooses colors but waits for staff to hand over materials. An art therapist can ask how the color choices relate to the theme and record the participant's account. A recreational therapist can observe whether the participant starts choosing materials without prompting. With authorization, the two can coordinate where supplies are placed so that the next observation has a clear starting point.

## U.S. training and credential checks

In New York, Creative Arts Therapy practice and title use generally require a license unless a statutory exemption applies. NYSED requires a qualifying master’s or doctoral program in creative arts therapy registered by NYSED or accredited by an accepted agency, or a program NYSED finds substantially equivalent. The education includes supervised internship or practicum. After the qualifying degree, applicants must complete at least 1,500 contact hours of supervised experience and pass an accepted exam. Check the relevant state if applying elsewhere. For recreational therapy, BLS assigns a bachelor's degree and lists no related work experience or on-the-job training in its typical entry table. Employers may prefer or require certification, and state rules may apply. The BLS occupation handbook says recreational therapy degree programs usually include an internship. One certification route pairs a bachelor’s degree in recreational therapy with a supervised internship and a passing exam result. A small number of states require licensure or certification. ## What the schedule needs to accommodate

Ask an art therapy employer how it allocates time for setup, documentation and storage or return of artwork. Ask a recreational therapy employer how it balances sessions, equipment access and treatment team review.

## Moving between the roles

Art therapists can bring activity design and observation experience to recreational therapy training, then develop recreation-based assessment and participation planning skills. Recreational therapists can use their goal-setting and group work experience as they build training in art media and documentation of the creative process. Check which credentials apply to the target job.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use fictional notes about three color cards and a participant who chooses a color but waits for two prompts before collecting supplies. Spend ten minutes writing a creative-process observation and a question for the participant. In the next ten, draft a participation goal and a way to record independence or prompting. In the final ten, write one supply arrangement both professionals could coordinate with authorization.
