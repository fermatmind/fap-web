# Postsecondary Education Teachers vs. Philosophy and Religion Teachers: The Classroom Rule Case

## What each role delivers

Postsecondary education teachers teach methods, assessment and education research. Postsecondary philosophy and religion teachers teach subjects such as ethics, logic or religious studies and assess conceptual and written work.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Education Teachers, Postsecondary | Philosophy and Religion Teachers, Postsecondary |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Methods coursework and feedback | Discussion and argument assessment |
| Basis | Learning evidence | Claims, premises and counterexamples |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $75,350 | $80,260 |

## How the work meets

Two fictional seminars use a rule that gives each participant one turn. The education instructor asks students to design an observation of participation. The philosophy instructor asks them to defend competing accounts of fairness and respond to a counterexample.

## How to enter

BLS lists a doctoral or professional degree as typical for both occupations. The education teacher row also lists less than five years of related experience. Ask what courses and research areas the department assigns. [BLS occupation rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Both roles may involve seminars, written feedback and research. Education work may include field observation; philosophy and religion courses may involve sustained reading and argument feedback. Ask about teaching and supervision load.

## Moving between roles

To move toward philosophy or religion teaching, bring discussion design and develop disciplinary scholarship and argument assessment. To move toward education, use ethics cases and build observation and evaluation skills.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use a fictional turn-taking rule and two observation notes. Spend ten minutes writing an education evidence question, ten writing an ethical counterexample question, and ten distinguishing the scoring criteria.
