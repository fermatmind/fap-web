# Postsecondary Family and Consumer Sciences Teachers vs. Farm and Home Management Educators

## What each role delivers

Postsecondary family and consumer sciences teachers develop coursework in areas such as nutrition and consumer decisions. Farm and home management educators teach and advise producers, households and other community participants.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Family and Consumer Sciences Teachers, Postsecondary | Farm and Home Management Educators |
|---|---|---|
| Output | College course and project feedback | Community education and follow-up |
| Basis | Course goals and student work | Producer or household needs |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $75,870 | $60,220 |

## How the work meets

A fictional program receives requests about household budgeting and farm recordkeeping. The college teacher writes a case assignment with an assessment rubric. The community educator verifies local needs, organizes separate learning activities and plans follow-up.

## How to enter

BLS lists a doctoral or professional degree as typical for the postsecondary occupation and a master’s degree for farm and home management educators. Check the institution and its program responsibilities. [BLS occupation rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

College work follows courses and assessment cycles. Outreach follows events, partners and participants’ availability. Ask about travel and how the employer checks whether participants applied the training.

## Moving between roles

Moving to outreach, add needs assessment and follow-up to your teaching skills. Moving to college, use field examples and build disciplinary coursework and grading.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use fictional requests from a farmer who wants clearer records and a household that wants to compare expenses. Spend ten minutes each on a college prompt, a community session plan and one completion measure for each.
