# Marriage and Family Therapist vs. Mental Health Counselor

## Start with the focus of treatment

Marriage and family therapists assess how recurring interactions affect the people involved. Mental health counselors can focus treatment on a client's distress, coping strategies and ability to manage daily activities. Both may hold individual sessions and document treatment progress.

## Compare what gets documented

| Stage | Marriage and family therapist | Mental health counselor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Define the goal | Identify the exchanges each person wants to change | Identify the concerns and the daily activities affected |
| Agree on an action | Try a shared way to make or confirm a request | Try a coping strategy the client can practice and track |
| Review progress | Ask how each person responded and what happened next | Review changes in distress and daily functioning |

## When a caregiving schedule keeps breaking down

Two adult siblings need to arrange rides to a parent's appointments. One keeps asking for an immediate answer and the other stops responding. A family therapist could ask both to describe what they hear at that point and help them agree on a written confirmation step. If one sibling is also losing sleep over caregiving, a mental health counselor can explore how the stress carries into the evening and agree on what to review next time. For parallel services, clarify who participates in each service and what each provider is authorized to share.

## Training and state licensure in the U.S.

Both tracks generally require graduate training, but check each license separately. BLS reports that marriage and family therapists typically enter with a master's degree and that all states require licensure, including supervised postdegree clinical experience and a state-recognized exam. For mental health counselors, BLS describes a typical master's degree and internship and says states require licensure, including supervised clinical work and an exam. Ask the relevant state board which program, supervisors, placements and hours count for the credential you want.

## What to ask about the workweek

Family appointments can require coordinating several schedules, so ask how often appointments extend into evenings or weekends. In a counseling practice, check expected sessions per day and time allowed for progress notes and supervision. Ask both how canceled sessions are handled.

## Switching tracks

Family therapists can bring goal setting and progress monitoring to mental health counseling, then check whether their training and experience meet the counseling board's rules and where further individual treatment training is needed. Counselors moving into family therapy can reuse interviewing and documentation skills and seek supervised experience with multiple participants and family interaction assessment.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use two fictional siblings. One is free Thursday and needs confirmation by Tuesday. The other is on a Tuesday night shift and has never received the schedule. Their last two conversations ended early. Take ten minutes to map the exchange and propose a confirmation step both could use. Assume the second sibling reports lost sleep after the arguments and spend ten minutes drafting two individual-session questions and one follow-up item. Use the final ten minutes to draft separate case notes and mark information whose sharing would require authorization.
