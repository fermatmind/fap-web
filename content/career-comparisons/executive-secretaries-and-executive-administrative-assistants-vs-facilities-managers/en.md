# Executive secretaries and executive administrative assistants vs. facilities managers

## Keep the executive schedule or keep the site running

Executive administrative staff coordinate calendars, meetings and documents. Facilities managers oversee repairs, room availability and related work. When a conference room closes, the two roles exchange information and complete different parts of the recovery.

## Compare the work and preparation

| Item | Executive administrative support | Facilities manager |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deliverable | An updated schedule and a complete meeting packet | A maintenance work order and a verified room status |
| Work setting to ask about | Executive office or support desk, including offsite meeting and travel coordination | Facility office plus site visits and rounds |
| Typical U.S. entry education and related work | High school diploma or equivalent, less than five years of related experience | Bachelor's degree, less than five years of related experience |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $76,590 | $106,660 |

Wages cover nonfarm wage and salary workers and exclude self-employment. ## A conference room is taken out of service

At a fictional office, water dripping from the ceiling closes a conference room. The facilities manager opens a work order, keeps the room out of use and confirms the capacity and availability of an alternate space. The executive assistant uses that information to reschedule the meeting within existing authority, sends the invite and updates the meeting packet. Facilities retains responsibility for verifying readiness before reopening the original room.

## How to build U.S. experience

For executive support, use a sample schedule and a meeting packet to demonstrate prior coordination work. Ask what the executive delegates and what requires approval. For facilities, build experience with work orders, vendor coordination and site checks. Ask whether the role includes management only or hands-on work that has separate trade requirements. [BLS preparation](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Ask where the pressure comes from

For executive support, ask how travel changes and last-minute meetings affect availability. For facilities, ask whether maintenance happens after hours, how many sites are covered and who responds to a call when a room closes.

## Moving between the roles

From executive support, bring a redacted event checklist and vendor correspondence log. Build experience tracking assets, prioritizing service requests and documenting completion. From facilities, bring a room status handoff and an incident timeline. Practice resolving calendar conflicts and handling executive materials with limited access.

## A 30-minute tabletop exercise

Eight people need a room at 10. The closed room has no reopening confirmation. Available rooms seat ten and six. Spend ten minutes preparing the capacity and readiness handoff, ten updating the invitation and ten writing a brief record of what remains open. Facilities signs off on room status and executive support sends the meeting update.
