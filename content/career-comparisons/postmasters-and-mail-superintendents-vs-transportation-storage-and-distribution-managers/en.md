# Postmasters and Mail Superintendents vs. Transportation, Storage and Distribution Managers on a Mail Handoff

## Start with the work

Postmasters and mail superintendents supervise mail processing, service and staffing at a postal operation. Distribution managers allocate vehicles, space and people across shipments and receiving work.

## Compare the handoffs

| Work product | Postmasters and Mail Superintendents | Transportation, Storage and Distribution Managers |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Verified bag count and staffing at the postal site | Vehicle capacity, receiving coverage and delay plan |

## One operating scenario

In a fictional evening handoff, a postal supervisor confirms which bags are sorted and ready and which still need review. The distribution manager checks available vehicle space and the next site's deadline. Each team records its commitments and shares a controlled update if the plan changes.

## How to prepare

The O*NET tables use Zone 1–2 for postmasters and mail superintendents and Zone 4 for distribution managers. These are broad categories. Check the postal employer's actual supervisor hiring route. Mail processing and customer service can build site experience. Receiving and shift planning can prepare you for distribution. [Postmasters and Mail Superintendents preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-9131.00#JobZone) · [Transportation, Storage and Distribution Managers preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-3071.00#JobZone)

## What shapes the workday

Postal work can follow counter peaks and mail processing schedules. Distribution also depends on trucks, dock space and arrival windows. Ask who stays responsible for customer updates after a handoff.

## Moving between roles

Toward distribution, add cross-site and vehicle planning to mail operations. Toward postal supervision, learn site service and processing responsibilities and check the employer's appointment route.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Fictional records show 18 bags due at 4. Fourteen are verified and four finish at 4:25. A truck at 4:10 has room for 14, and a truck at 5:10 has room for six. Spend ten minutes recording site status, fifteen planning handoff and follow-up, and five stating who needs the update.
