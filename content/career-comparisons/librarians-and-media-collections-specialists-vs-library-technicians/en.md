# Librarians and media collections specialists vs. library technicians

## One collection, different responsibilities

Librarians and media collections specialists help shape collections and services. Library technicians process materials, maintain records and support services under local procedures. Both may catalog and help patrons. What distinguishes a particular opening is which decisions and records the employer assigns to it.

## Compare the roles

| Item | Librarian or media collections specialist | Library technician |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deliverable | A documented collection or service decision | Checked catalog records and prepared items |
| Setting to ask about | A library service desk, collections office or teaching space | Processing workroom, stacks or service desk |
| Typical U.S. entry education | Master's degree | Postsecondary nondegree award |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $68,270 | $44,580 |

Wages cover nonfarm wage and salary workers and exclude self-employment. ## Adding community oral histories

At a fictional public library, a librarian reviews two recordings and their access permissions. The librarian approves public access for the first and asks for additional documentation for the second. The technician checks identifiers and metadata for both, makes the approved item accessible and records the other as internal only. The librarian decides whether later documentation permits public access. The technician updates the catalog after receiving that decision.

## Getting started in the U.S.

BLS identifies a master's in library science or a similar program as typical for librarians. School librarian or media specialist requirements can differ by state; check the relevant state education authority for school openings. For library technicians, BLS describes a postsecondary certificate in library technology. Look for coursework in cataloging, circulation, and library systems and ask which practical skills the employer assesses. [BLS librarian preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/education-training-and-library/librarians.htm#tab-4) · [BLS technician preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/education-training-and-library/library-technicians-and-assistants.htm#tab-4)

## Ask about the shift

For librarian work, ask how collection review, reference desk shifts and programs share the week. For technician work, ask who checks records in a batch, which desk shifts you cover and how you report an exception.

## Moving between the roles

A technician can bring a redacted cataloging sample and discrepancy log, then develop collection evaluation and service planning skills and check degree requirements. A librarian can bring a redacted collection decision and intake handoff, then practice batch processing, checking records in the local system and tracking corrections.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a mock policy and two oral-history records. A has public access permission and a matching file ID. B has permission for internal retention only and a matching ID. The library requires separate permission for public access. Spend ten minutes deciding each status, ten drafting the technician's catalog checklist and ten writing the librarian's access decision and permission request. Identify who approves a status change and which record can be public today.
