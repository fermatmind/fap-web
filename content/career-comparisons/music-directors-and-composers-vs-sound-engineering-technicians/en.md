# Music Directors and Composers vs. Sound Engineering Technicians: A Quiet Passage Gets Lost

## What each role delivers

Music directors and composers may write, interpret and rehearse music. Sound engineering technicians test equipment, record and adjust sound quality. Both can listen to a test take.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Music Directors and Composers | Sound Engineering Technicians |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Performance interpretation and recording notes | Recorded tracks and quality log |
| Basis | Phrasing, dynamics and clarity | Levels, noise, channels and takes |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $73,710 | $73,130 |

## How the work meets

At a fictional string session, the music director wants to preserve a quiet ending. The technician hears the ending in the room but finds it close to the background noise in the recording. The technician checks the setup and records a test. The music director reviews the dynamics, and the technician logs the accepted take.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree and less than five years of related experience for music directors and composers. It lists a postsecondary nondegree award and short-term on-the-job training for sound engineering technicians. Check whether the sound opening involves studio recording, live setup or mixing. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Music work may span rehearsals, score revisions and recording. Technical work may include setup, testing and mixing. Ask who signs off on the test take before recording.

## Moving between roles

Moving to sound, use listening and rehearsal skills and add equipment, signal flow and file delivery. Moving to music, use recording judgment and build score reading, arranging and rehearsal direction.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock takes: A has the desired quiet ending but audible background noise. B is clear but the players flatten the dynamics. Spend ten minutes each on the music requirement, recording checks and shared listening criteria.
