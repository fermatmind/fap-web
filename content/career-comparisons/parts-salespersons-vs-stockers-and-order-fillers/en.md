# Parts Salesperson vs. Stockers and Order Fillers on a Back-Ordered Part

## Start with what you deliver

Parts salespersons identify the requested part and explain availability or ordering options. Stockers and order fillers check the location, label and physical quantity. Both can pull stock, but a count alone cannot establish suitability.

## Compare the handoffs

| Work product | Parts Salesperson | Stockers and Order Fillers |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Part identification, availability options, and a customer update | Verified item, location, quantity, and exception |

## Follow one order

A fictional repair shop requests P18. The system shows stock, but the main bin holds only P18-B. The stocker checks the markings and reports the discrepancy. The salesperson asks the customer to confirm the model and uses approved product information to evaluate other options.

## How to prepare

O*NET places both roles in Job Zone 1–2, a broad preparation category. For parts sales, learn catalog lookup and how to collect the details needed to confirm a part. For inventory work, learn location, picking and count checks through receiving or stock support. ## Work conditions

Parts sales can alternate between calls, catalogs and customer discussions. Picking work follows physical locations and order deadlines. Ask who checks similar item numbers and whether the counter and warehouse share staff.

## Move between the roles

To move into sales, build catalog and customer skills on top of inventory accuracy. To move into picking, add scanning, location checks and shortage reporting.

## Try it in 30 minutes

A fictional customer needs two P18 parts. The primary bin has two P18-B parts. A second bin contains one P18. The catalog gives no substitution rule. Spend ten minutes writing the count, fifteen preparing customer options, and five listing what needs confirmation.
