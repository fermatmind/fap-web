# Mathematician vs. Statistician: From a Queue Model to Observed Waits

## What each role delivers

Mathematicians study relationships and structures and derive results under stated assumptions. Statisticians use data to estimate quantities, check models and describe uncertainty. Applied work may use mathematical models and numerical methods in both roles.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Mathematician | Statisticians |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Assumptions, derivation and numerical examples | Sample analysis, estimates and validation record |
| Basis | Logic, boundary conditions and computability | Coverage, errors, missing data and fit |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $126,710 | $105,650 |

## How the work meets

A fictional library wants to estimate lunchtime waits. The mathematician derives a simplified model under steady arrivals and two open desks. The statistician reviews arrival and service logs, finds a period with one desk open and analyzes it separately, noting when the sample is too small. They document the situations in which the model can be used.

## How to enter

BLS lists a master’s degree as typical for both occupations. A mathematics portfolio can show derivation and numerical evaluation; a statistics portfolio can show data checks, estimation and validation. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Mathematical work can involve extended derivation and review. Statistical work also follows data delivery and reporting dates. Ask which outputs need revision when assumptions change.

## Moving between roles

Mathematicians bring modeling into statistics and can add sampling, estimation and empirical validation. Statisticians can bring numerical and modeling experience into mathematics and develop proof and theory.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock file: the model assumes two open desks. On Monday only one was open from noon to 12:30. On Tuesday both were open but ten minutes of arrival counts are missing. Spend ten minutes each on assumptions, usable comparison windows and a conclusion with follow-up records.
