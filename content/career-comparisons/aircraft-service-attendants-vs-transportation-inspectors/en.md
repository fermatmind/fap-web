# Aircraft Service Attendants vs Transportation Inspectors

## What each role delivers

Aircraft service attendants carry out assigned ground services such as fueling, replenishing water, or cleaning aircraft exteriors. They document their work and report issues they find during service. Transportation inspectors examine equipment or cargo within a defined scope, record findings, and follow up. The inspector occupation spans transportation modes. Check which mode a posting covers.

[O*NET aircraft service tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-6032.00#Tasks) · [O*NET transportation inspection tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-6051.00#Tasks)

## One ticket closes while a finding stays open

At a fictional airport ground-service operation, the 2 p.m. handoff includes one aircraft service ticket and one airport cargo equipment finding.

- S-12 (crew service log): Aircraft A-18, drinking-water replenishment, completed at 1:40 p.m.
- I-07 (cargo trailer inspection): The inspection sheet identifies trailer T-24. The attached photo is labeled T-42. The open question is which trailer the photo documents.

The attendant reports the completion time for S-12. The inspector keeps I-07 open and asks the shift records custodian for the original photo and the corresponding inspection sheet. In this example, the designated reviewer confirms which trailer the record describes and resolves the ID discrepancy. The duty operations lead handles subsequent work assignments. Closing the discrepancy confirms only which equipment the record covers.

## How to check entry requirements

Ask the ground-service employer what tasks new hires are assigned, which airport access they need, and when they can work independently. For inspection, check the transport mode, applicable standards, required experience or credentials, and who can sign each record. Use the posting and the responsible employer or authority to verify the requirements for the assignment.

## Where the pressure falls

Ask service teams how many orders they handle per shift and who reprioritizes orders when arrival times move. Ask inspection teams how much fieldwork is involved, who reviews conflicting evidence, and how open findings are handed over between shifts.

## Evidence to bring when switching roles

A ground-service applicant moving toward inspection can show a redacted service handoff, then use artificial records to demonstrate how they check evidence and document a finding. An inspector moving toward ground service can show examples of coordinating around open items and confirm the training required for the assigned service work.

## Try a 30-minute paperwork exercise

Use the fictional S-12 and I-07 records. Spend ten minutes writing two separate status updates, ten minutes identifying the disputed field and the evidence owner, and ten minutes drafting the handoff to the duty operations lead. Check that your work records completion of S-12, keeps I-07 open for review, and identifies who handles the next decision.
