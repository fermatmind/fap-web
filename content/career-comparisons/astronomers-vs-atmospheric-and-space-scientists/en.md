# Astronomer vs. Atmospheric and Space Scientist

## Celestial observations and atmospheric conditions

Astronomers investigate celestial objects and phenomena. The U.S. occupational profile for atmospheric and space scientists focuses on atmospheric, weather, and climate work. This comparison uses an atmospheric assignment that supports an observing site.

## Compare what each role delivers

| Work | Astronomer | Atmospheric scientist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First check | Whether the images are usable for studying the target | Where and when atmospheric records were collected |
| Deliverable | An image quality note for the target | A cloud summary with its coverage gaps |

May 2025 U.S. annual wage medians were $128,820 for astronomers and $99,070 for the BLS category Atmospheric Scientists, Including Meteorologists. OEWS covers wage and salary employment and excludes self-employment. ## Cloud records can help review a blurred image

A fictional observing site records blurred images from 10:10 to 10:25 p.m. The atmospheric scientist summarizes the cloud record and flags a missing entry at 10:20. The astronomer aligns the weather summary with the images and flags which exposures need review. The team carries forward the gap when deciding what can be ruled out.

## U.S. education and early experience

BLS describes a doctorate as typical for astronomy research and academic positions. For atmospheric science, a bachelor’s degree in meteorology or a related field is typical for entry, and research positions typically require a master’s or doctorate. An astronomy data project can build observation analysis skills, while a weather internship or research project can build practice reading atmospheric records. [BLS astronomy preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/life-physical-and-social-science/physicists-and-astronomers.htm#tab-4) · [BLS atmospheric preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/life-physical-and-social-science/atmospheric-scientists-including-meteorologists.htm#tab-4)

## Compare schedules and worksites

Astronomers may work in offices or at observing facilities and may need to observe at night. Many U.S. federal forecasters work at weather stations, and some forecasting jobs involve shifts. Atmospheric researchers often work in offices and labs, with field data collection for some projects. Ask which facilities the role uses and what triggers evening or overnight work. [BLS astronomy setting](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/life-physical-and-social-science/physicists-and-astronomers.htm#tab-3) · [BLS atmospheric setting](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/life-physical-and-social-science/atmospheric-scientists-including-meteorologists.htm#tab-3)

## Moving between the roles

An astronomer can bring observation and data quality experience into atmospheric work, then study atmospheric processes and weather datasets. An atmospheric scientist can bring experience organizing observations across time and location, then build astronomy knowledge and experience with telescope data.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use fictional notes: images are blurred from 10:10 to 10:25 p.m. The site records clouds at 10:10, no entry at 10:20, and clear conditions at 10:30. Spend ten minutes lining up the records, ten marking confirmed facts and gaps, and ten drafting one follow-up request for each team.
