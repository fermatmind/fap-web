# Blockchain engineers vs Penetration Testers

## What each role delivers

A blockchain engineer turns a product requirement into a change and shows how it meets the agreed behavior. A penetration tester performs an authorized security assessment and reports findings and limitations. On a shared release, the engineer explains how the product meets its requirement. The tester explains what the assessment evidence supports.

## Tasks and work settings

| | Blockchain engineer | Penetration tester |
|---|---|---|
| Main handoff | Design and implementation record | Assessment report and remediation recommendations |
| Example team | Product engineering | Internal assessment or consulting team |
| Useful preparation | Distributed systems and software delivery | Systems knowledge, authorization and evidence reporting |

## A release review has two sign-offs

Consider a fictional shared inventory ledger. The blockchain engineer submits release notes and evidence that a revised record format meets the approved product requirement. The tester submits a completed assessment summary and marks a finding unresolved because sign-off evidence has not arrived. The engineering lead checks the feature against the requirement. The release owner reviews the assessment status before deciding whether the release may proceed.

## Preparing for each role

O*NET places both occupations in Job Zone Four. A four-year degree is common in that group, and substantial related experience or training is typical. For engineering, build software design and delivery experience and ask what distributed-system responsibilities the employer assigns. For assessment, build systems knowledge and practice writing clear findings and scope statements from provided fictional material. Ask how the employer supervises new assessors and what qualifications it requires. [O*NET engineering preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/15-1299.07) · [O*NET assessment preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/15-1299.04)

## Ask how deadlines are set

Ask engineers how design review and release acceptance are scheduled. Ask testers whether assignments are project-based, who approves the scope and who resolves disagreement about the report.

## Moving between the roles

An engineer can bring knowledge of system behavior and change records to assessment work, then build skill in independent evidence review and communicating limitations. A tester can bring experience writing actionable findings to engineering, then build software implementation, requirements review and release ownership experience.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use four fictional records. B1 is an engineering acceptance record missing the lead’s signature; B2 has signed product acceptance. A1 is a supplied finding summary without remediation sign-off; A2 has a documented sign-off. First sort the four cards, then draft one note for each team. In the last ten minutes, check your answers against the mock rules. Hold B1 for the engineering lead’s sign-off, file B2, refer A1 to the remediation owner for a documented decision and retain A2 with the assessment record.
