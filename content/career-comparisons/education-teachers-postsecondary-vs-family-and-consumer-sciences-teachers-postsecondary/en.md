# Postsecondary Education Teachers vs. Family and Consumer Sciences Teachers: The Household Case

## What each role delivers

Postsecondary education teachers teach and assess instructional methods. Postsecondary family and consumer sciences teachers teach subjects such as nutrition or consumer decisions and assess disciplinary work.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Education Teachers, Postsecondary | Family and Consumer Sciences Teachers, Postsecondary |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Instructional design and feedback | Family and consumer sciences coursework |
| Basis | Alignment of goals, activities and assessment | Subject evidence, constraints and options |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $75,350 | $75,870 |

## How the work meets

A fictional case presents two households with different budgets and care schedules. The education teacher asks teacher candidates to design a lesson and say what would demonstrate learning. The family and consumer sciences teacher asks students to compare constraints and explain the limits of any recommendation.

## How to enter

BLS lists a doctoral or professional degree as typical for both occupations. The education teacher row also lists less than five years of related experience. Check the department, subject and assigned courses. [BLS occupation rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Education work may include methods coursework and feedback on fieldwork. Family and consumer sciences work may include projects or disciplinary labs. Ask how these activities are counted.

## Moving between roles

Moving toward family and consumer sciences, carry over course design and add disciplinary preparation. Moving to education, turn subject examples into teacher training and build assessment design and educational inquiry.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use fictional household budget and care schedule cards. Spend ten minutes writing a teacher candidate’s learning goal, ten writing a subject analysis question and ten making separate criteria for observable evidence.
