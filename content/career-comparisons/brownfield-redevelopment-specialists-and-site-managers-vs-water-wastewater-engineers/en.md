# Brownfield Redevelopment Specialist and Site Manager vs. Water/Wastewater Engineer

## Site constraints and engineering design

Brownfield redevelopment specialists and site managers may coordinate assessment, cleanup, and reuse planning. Water and wastewater engineers may design water, drainage, or wastewater systems. Both can contribute to a design discussion from their own areas of responsibility.

## Compare the deliverables

| Work | Brownfield redevelopment specialist and site manager | Water/wastewater engineer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First check | Which areas were assessed and which remain open | Drainage requirements, routing constraints, and connections |
| Deliverable | Site constraints and supporting assessment information | Design options with assumptions and review conditions |

O*NET links the two pay profiles to Managers, All Other and Civil Engineers. Those categories do not isolate the two specialties. ## A proposed drainage route crosses an area still under assessment

In a fictional redevelopment project, the environmental professional reports that the east area needs further assessment. The site manager sends its mapped extent and information gaps to the engineer. The engineer compares the proposed route with an option along the west side and lists the missing site and connection information each needs. The project lead schedules a review when the records are available.

## U.S. preparation and authorization

O*NET places the brownfield role in Job Zone Four, where a bachelor’s degree and related preparation are common. Supervised environmental project work can build experience documenting assessment scope. BLS describes a related engineering bachelor’s degree as typical for civil engineers. An internship can build experience reviewing system drawings and project constraints. Check the applicable state engineering board and employer for which duties require licensure or approval authority. [O*NET site preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-9199.11#JobZone) · [BLS engineering preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/civil-engineers.htm#tab-4)

## Compare workplaces and schedules

Site managers can coordinate in offices and visit sites to check progress. BLS describes civil engineering as involving time in offices and at construction sites. Ask how often you would visit the site and how the team handles design reviews when assessment records arrive late. [O*NET site work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-9199.11#WorkContext) · [BLS civil engineering work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/civil-engineers.htm#tab-3)

## Moving between the roles

Site managers can bring project scope and coordination experience into engineering preparation, then build design, calculation, and review skills. Engineers can bring drawing and interface coordination skills to site management, then learn how to interpret assessment reports and coordinate site work.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use fictional notes: the east assessment is incomplete, the proposed route crosses the east area, and the west alternative has an unconfirmed connection point. Spend ten minutes listing constraints, ten writing one missing information request for each route, and ten defining when the team should review the options.
