# Dietitians and Nutritionists vs. Occupational Therapists: Who Helps With Breakfast at Home?

## What each role assesses

A dietitian or nutritionist assesses nutrition needs, restrictions, and preferences to develop a meal plan. An occupational therapist looks at how someone prepares a meal and whether setup, packaging, or the task sequence affects completion.

## The same breakfast plan

Imagine a client preparing breakfast at home after discharge. The dietitian reviews documented restrictions, preferences, and available foods to outline several choices. In a practice kitchen, the occupational therapist sees that the client can get the utensils but cannot open one of the packages. The therapist shares the specific barrier. The dietitian checks which alternatives still fit the plan, and the occupational therapist observes the task again with an agreed option.

## Getting started in the U.S.

BLS describes bachelor's or master's education and supervised training for the combined dietitian and nutritionist occupation. For the U.S. Registered Dietitian Nutritionist (RDN) credential, CDR has required a graduate degree for new registration exam eligibility since January 1, 2024. Review state licensure separately. Occupational therapists typically need a master's degree in occupational therapy and must be licensed in every state. ## Working conditions

Nutrition work may involve interviews, reviewing food records, and coordinating meals. Occupational therapy may involve setting up a practice kitchen and arranging equipment. Ask who follows up on how the plan works at home.

## Moving between roles

Both roles use interviewing and communication about home routines. A move into dietetics calls for training in nutrition assessment and meal planning. A move into occupational therapy calls for training in activity analysis and environmental assessment. Verify the education and credentials for the new role.

## Try both in 30 minutes

Use a fictional plan. The care team's breakfast rule for this exercise is to include a protein item and avoid peanuts. The client prefers A, a peanut-free yogurt in a sealed cup they cannot open. B is an easy-open fruit cup with no protein item. A peanut-free protein option is listed as available but its packaging is unknown. Spend 10 minutes marking which choice meets the written requirements and what the nutrition team must verify, 10 documenting the packaging barrier and what to observe next, and 10 writing a joint handoff.
