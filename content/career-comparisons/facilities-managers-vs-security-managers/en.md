# Facilities Managers vs. Security Managers: Who Reopens a Lobby After a Leak?

## Which part each role owns

A facilities manager oversees building condition, maintenance, and repair. A security manager coordinates protection of people and property, including temporary access arrangements and incident response.

## Compare the work

| Compare | Facilities manager | Security manager |
|---|---|---|
| Daily focus | Equipment, contractors, and maintenance windows | Access, security coverage, and incident response |
| Output in this example | Repair and floor inspection record | Temporary access plan and handoff |

[O*NET 11-3013.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-3013.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 11-3013.01](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-3013.01#Tasks)

## The same lobby incident

Imagine a leak in an office lobby just before a group of visitors arrives. The facilities manager calls the maintenance team and asks for the source, repair time, and floor inspection result. The security manager directs visitors to the site's approved alternate entrance, tells reception, and logs the temporary arrangement. When maintenance finishes, both roles provide their checks to the site's reopening lead, who confirms that normal access can resume and updates reception.

## Working conditions

Facilities managers work around scheduled maintenance, contractors, and urgent repairs. Security managers may need to cover shifts and respond to incidents. Ask who takes alerts after hours, who may close an area, and who signs off on reopening.

## Moving between roles

Facilities managers can bring knowledge of buildings and incident coordination into security and learn shift coverage, response procedures, and reporting. Security managers can bring inspection and handoff experience into facilities and learn maintenance planning, vendor coordination, and repair verification.

At 9:20 the leak has been repaired but the floor inspection is unsigned. At 9:35 maintenance sends the inspection result. Spend 10 minutes writing a repair handoff, 10 writing a temporary reception and notification plan, and 10 naming two reopening checks and their owners.
