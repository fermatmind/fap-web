# Executive Administrative Assistant vs. Event Planner: A Keynote Schedule Change

## What each role delivers

Executive secretaries and executive administrative assistants manage schedules, travel and meeting materials. Meeting, convention, and event planners align speakers, venues and vendors around the event program.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Executive Secretaries and Executive Administrative Assistants | Meeting, Convention, and Event Planners |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Confirmed executive itinerary, materials and calendar update | Approved program and production instructions |
| Basis | Executive priorities, travel constraints and confidentiality | Room, speakers, production and attendee needs |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $76,590 | $61,160 |

National medians; local and starting pay vary. [BLS Table 1.2 rows 43-6011 and 13-1121](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## How the work meets

At a fictional forum, a delayed train pushes a keynote speaker’s arrival to 10:30 at the earliest. The assistant checks arrival, remote availability and alternate times, then shares the details the planner may use. The planner checks audiovisual support, the host, other speakers and breaks, and circulates the schedule once confirmed. The assistant updates the executive calendar and briefing.

## How to enter

BLS lists a high school diploma or equivalent and less than five years of related experience as typical for executive secretaries and executive administrative assistants. It lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for event planners. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Executive assistants work around leadership calendars and meeting deadlines. Planners coordinate multiple production and event windows. Ask how schedule changes reach each team.

## Moving between roles

Calendar and document skills transfer to planning; add production, venue requirements and vendor coordination. Planners moving into executive support can build on scheduling by learning executive priorities, calendar rules and document access.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock program: the event starts at 10 a.m. The speaker arrives after 10:30 and is available remotely from 10 to 10:20, but the remote connection has not been tested. There is an opening at 10:40, and the host leaves at 11. Spend ten minutes each on the executive update, planning options and the order of approval and notifications.
