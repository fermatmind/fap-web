# LPN or LVN vs. Phlebotomist

## Patient care and specimen collection

Licensed practical and licensed vocational nurses provide assigned nursing care and monitor and report changes in condition. Phlebotomists collect blood specimens and verify that the patient's identity and laboratory paperwork match.

## Compare the handoffs

| Item | LPN or LVN | Phlebotomist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verify | Assigned care and relevant observations | Patient identity and the laboratory request |
| Document | Patient response and whom the nurse notified | Collection status and any specimen or request discrepancy |
| Report to | The designated nursing or clinical lead | The assigned collection or laboratory lead |

## The appointment and lab order show different names

In a fictional outpatient clinic, a phlebotomist notices inconsistent identifiers on the appointment and requisition and reports the mismatch for verification. The patient also says they feel unwell. The LPN or LVN can document the reported concern and alert the appropriate clinician under the site's procedure. Each worker records a different issue and who is responsible for following up.

## U.S. education and credentials

To become an LPN or LVN, BLS describes a state-approved program leading to a certificate or diploma, a licensing exam and a state license. Scope and supervision depend on the state and setting. Phlebotomists typically earn a postsecondary certificate, although some enter with a high school diploma and employer training. State rules and employer certification requirements vary. Check the relevant regulator and employer. In its national table, BLS assigns both occupations a postsecondary nondegree award and no related work experience or on-the-job training. Individual entry routes, degree programs and authorization still vary. [BLS practical nursing entry guidance](https://www.bls.gov/OOH/healthcare/licensed-practical-and-licensed-vocational-nurses.htm#tab-4) · [BLS phlebotomy entry guidance](https://www.bls.gov/OOH/healthcare/phlebotomists.htm#tab-4) · [BLS detailed training table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/education-and-training-by-occupation.htm)

## Ask how the shift is organized

Ask a nursing employer how it assigns patient care, recordkeeping and escalation. For phlebotomy, ask about peak collection times, rotation through collection sites and supervisor coverage for record discrepancies.

## Moving between roles

LPNs and LVNs can bring patient communication and documentation skills into phlebotomy and should verify the required collection training and credentials. Phlebotomists can bring identity verification and escalation skills to nursing education, then complete approved training and qualify for nursing licensure.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a fictional appointment, lab requisition and patient note. Spend ten minutes marking the mismatch and who can verify it, ten writing a nursing handoff from the patient note, and ten naming the owner and verification evidence for each issue. Work from the fictional records only.
