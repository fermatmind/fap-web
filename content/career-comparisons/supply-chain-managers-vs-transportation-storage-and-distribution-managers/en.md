# Supply Chain Managers vs Transportation, Storage, and Distribution Managers

## Set supply priorities or run the operation that fulfills them

Supply chain managers coordinate purchasing, inventory, and logistics across teams. Transportation, storage, and distribution managers may supervise a warehouse, transportation operation, or distribution team and turn demand into daily capacity and staffing decisions. One role can cover both, so check which decisions the employer assigns.

## Compare work products

| Item | Supply chain managers | Transportation, storage, and distribution managers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Example setting | Supply planning team at a retailer or manufacturer | Distribution center or transportation operations team |
| Main work product | Replenishment and inventory priorities | Receiving schedule, staffing and dispatch plan |
| Decision to test | Which shipment affects customer commitments first? | Which shipment can the site receive and process? |

O*NET uses the broader 11-3071 wage series for Supply Chain Managers. That series provides no independent wage comparison between these assignments. [O*NET supply chain](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-3071.04) · [BLS transportation, storage, and distribution managers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/transportation-storage-and-distribution-managers.htm)

## Take a port appointment through to receiving

The Port of Los Angeles describes cargo tracking and truck appointment tools. In a work example based on that setting, a supply chain manager checks which orders are exposed when pickup timing changes and brings a replenishment priority to the purchasing and sales teams. A distribution manager checks the appointment against dock space and staffing, proposes a receiving window, and tells the supply team which shipments are still unconfirmed. The carrier and receiving team confirm the executable handoff. [Port Optimizer](https://portoflosangeles.org/business/supply-chain/port-optimizer%E2%84%A2) · [Port cargo operations](https://portoflosangeles.org/business/operations)

## Build experience suited to the U.S. assignment

O*NET puts supply chain managers in Job Zone Four, a broad preparation category in which a bachelor’s degree is common. Build experience in purchasing, inventory or planning and show how you coordinated a decision with another team. BLS describes related work experience as necessary for the broader transportation and warehouse management occupation. A high school diploma is typical there, and some employers prefer or require a bachelor’s. Build experience in transportation or warehouse operations and check the actual role for supervisory and any task-specific requirements. [O*NET Job Zone](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-3071.04#JobZone) · [BLS entry guidance](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/transportation-storage-and-distribution-managers.htm#tab-4)

## Ask who takes an exception after the schedule is set

Ask how often the supply chain team resets inventory priorities and who contacts vendors. Transportation and distribution managers may work at warehouses and offices or visit other sites. Ask how site managers cover shifts, dock delays, and carrier issues outside normal hours. [BLS work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/transportation-storage-and-distribution-managers.htm#tab-3)

## Show experience across the handoff

Moving from distribution to supply chain management, reuse a fictional dock plan as evidence of capacity constraints, then learn inventory exposure, supplier options, and order priorities. Moving toward distribution, turn a replenishment plan into a receiving schedule and learn crew coverage, space constraints, and shift-level verification.

## Try a 30-minute receiving exercise

Use fictional records: inventory sheet I shows A must be stocked by noon today to avoid a stockout and B has two days of stock. Appointment list P shows both at 9 a.m. Dock card D shows one slot at 9 that can be stocked by noon, and one slot at 2 p.m. Spend eight minutes reconciling the records, twelve drafting a priority note or receiving plan, and ten checking it. A goes first. Mark B’s change as subject to carrier confirmation and assign someone to confirm it.
