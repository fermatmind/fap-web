# Investment Fund Managers vs Treasurers and controllers

## Choose the portfolio decision or the financial control

An investment fund manager evaluates portfolio exposures and decisions within a fund’s mandate. A treasurer works on funding and cash availability. A controller focuses on accounting, reporting and controls. If you prefer researching risk and documenting portfolio decisions, explore fund management. If you prefer reconciling financial evidence or managing cash commitments, explore treasury or controllership.

## Two ways to use the cash forecast

| Compare | Investment fund managers | Treasurers and controllers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Key inputs | Fund objectives, positions and market information | Bank records, accounting data and expected cash flows |
| Setting | Investment team and risk reviews | Corporate or fund finance, cash or reporting cycles |
| Deliverable | Decision and risk rationale for a portfolio | Cash forecast, reconciliations or control documentation |

## A redemption arrives before a receipt is confirmed

The SEC’s guide describes liquidity programs for covered open-end funds. In an illustrative fund team, an expected receipt is missing from confirmed cash on a redemption date. A controller assigned to fund-level accounting reconciles confirmed balances, payment deadlines and expected settlement dates, then sends the unresolved difference to the portfolio team. A corporate treasurer’s forecast tracks separate cash belonging to the adviser. The fund manager documents how the two cash scenarios affect the portfolio’s liquidity assessment and routes the issue to the designated program personnel. Fund cash and the adviser’s corporate cash are tracked separately for this example. [SEC Rule 22e-4 guide](https://www.sec.gov/files/secg-liquidity.htm)

## Build one portfolio sample and one finance sample

BLS reports that financial managers typically have a bachelor’s degree and five or more years of related experience. That benchmark covers the broader occupation. For fund management, look for investment research or risk work and build a memo that makes assumptions reviewable. For treasury or controllership, build experience in cash analysis, accounting or audit, with a work sample showing how a discrepancy was resolved. Ask which registrations, credentials or delegated authorities apply to the employer and activities. [BLS financial manager profile](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/financial-managers.htm)

## Ask what sets the deadline

Fund roles can have work tied to market hours, investment reviews and unexpected events. Finance roles can have recurring cash deadlines and quarter-end or month-end reporting pressure. Ask who covers absences and how the team handles deadlines outside ordinary hours. Compare compensation at similar seniority and in the same market, including time and variable pay.

## Switch through a shared cash or risk project

A fund analyst can bring settlement tracking or cash scenario work into treasury and add banking workflows and controls. A controller or treasury analyst can bring reconciliation and cash forecasting into fund operations or investment risk support and add portfolio analytics and mandate review. Use the target team’s actual responsibilities to decide what to learn next.

## Try two notes in thirty minutes

Use fictional figures only. Confirmed cash is $600,000, scheduled redemptions are $750,000, and an expected $250,000 receipt has no confirmed settlement date. Spend 12 minutes writing a cash reconciliation note. Spend 13 minutes writing a portfolio risk note with the cash position under both outcomes. Take five minutes to check that the handoff names who confirms the receipt and who reviews the implications, without proposing trades.
