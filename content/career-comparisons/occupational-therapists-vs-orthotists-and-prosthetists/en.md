# Occupational Therapists vs. Orthotists and Prosthetists: Who Checks How a Prosthesis Works on the Job?

## What each role checks

An occupational therapist evaluates how someone carries out daily or work activities. Orthotists and prosthetists evaluate device needs, design and fit orthoses or prostheses, and follow up on fit and function.

## Compare the work

| Compare | Occupational therapist | Orthotist or prosthetist |
|---|---|---|
| Main record | Task performance and conditions that affect it | Measurements, device fit, and follow-up findings |

## The same return-to-work goal

Imagine a bookstore employee using an upper-limb prosthesis to stamp and bag purchases. At a practice counter, the occupational therapist records which step is interrupted and how the counter layout affects the task. The prosthetist checks fit and device control within the prescription and care plan, then records whether follow-up adjustments are needed. The occupational therapist revisits the same tasks and reports what changed and what still needs checking.

## Getting started in the U.S.

Occupational therapists typically need a master's degree in occupational therapy, and all states require a license. Orthotists and prosthetists typically complete a master's program and residency. Some states require their own license for that occupation. Check the relevant state authority before choosing a training route. [BLS occupational therapy entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/occupational-therapists.htm#tab-4) · [BLS orthotics and prosthetics entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/orthotists-and-prosthetists.htm#tab-4)

## Working conditions

Occupational therapists may observe people in a clinic or a simulated work setting. Orthotists and prosthetists split time among measurements, fitting visits, and fabrication or coordination. Ask who arranges follow-up when the device fits in the clinic but problems arise on the job.

## Moving between roles

The roles share client communication and observation skills. Moving into occupational therapy requires training in activity assessment and care planning. Moving into orthotics and prosthetics requires training in device design, measurement, fitting, and follow-up. Check the education and credential requirements for the destination role.

## Try both in 30 minutes

Use three fictional notes. The client stamps two items at a low counter, pauses halfway through opening a paper bag, and reports a loose feeling after 10 minutes in the prosthesis. Spend 10 minutes documenting task performance, 10 listing what the prosthetist needs to check during follow-up, and 10 writing a two-way handoff. Work from the notes on paper.
