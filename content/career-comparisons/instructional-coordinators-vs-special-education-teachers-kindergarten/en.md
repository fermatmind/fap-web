# Instructional Coordinators vs. Kindergarten Special Education Teachers: Revising Activity Cards

## What each role delivers

Instructional coordinators review how teachers use materials and organize training. Kindergarten special education teachers adapt activities for children’s learning goals and support needs.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Instructional Coordinators | Special Education Teachers, Kindergarten |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Shared resource revision proposal | Adapted instruction and observations |
| Basis | Patterns across classrooms | Child’s learning goals and support needs |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $77,440 | $65,120 |

## How the work meets

Two fictional classrooms struggle to read a shared transition card. The coordinator collects feedback and proposes a common revision. A special education teacher changes presentation under one child’s existing support plan and records participation for the team.

## How to enter

BLS lists a master’s degree and five or more years of related experience for instructional coordinators. Its combined kindergarten and elementary special education row lists a bachelor’s degree. Verify school and state requirements. California Education Specialist authorizations vary by specialty and grade range. [California CTC](https://www.ctc.ca.gov/credentials/leaflets/cl-808ca/) [BLS occupation rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Coordinators follow review and training schedules. Teachers follow classroom routines, observation and team meetings. Ask which observations can be shared beyond the student’s team.

## Moving between roles

Moving into special education, add kindergarten instruction and applicable teacher preparation. Moving into coordination, bring adaptation examples and develop cross-class review and staff training.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use two fictional notes: two classes struggle with card sequence, and one child already uses individual picture prompts. Spend ten minutes on a common revision note, ten on an observation plan, and ten marking who receives either record.
