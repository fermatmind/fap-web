# Compliance Manager vs. Document Management Specialist

## Compliance decisions and records people can verify

Compliance managers identify issues that need follow-up, document how they are handled and coordinate reporting when applicable. Document management specialists organize records and help manage how users find and access them.

## Compare the work products

| Item | Compliance manager | Document management specialist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verify | The requirement, event and evidence still needed | Document versions, ownership and access |
| Produce | An issue assessment and follow-up recommendations | A retrievable index with version and access notes |
| Coordinate | The business owner and authorized decision-makers | Record owners and system administrators |

## An internal review finds two approval versions

A fictional company's supplier review finds two files that appear final. The document specialist checks version history and ownership and flags a missing effective date. The compliance manager maps the record to the applicable approval rule and asks the business owner to verify what happened. A designated decision-maker handles any exception, corrective action or reporting.

## U.S. preparation

O*NET assigns both occupations to Job Zone Four, where many jobs need a four-year degree and relevant preparation. Employers set the specific field and experience. For compliance management, ask about the industry, reporting obligations and prior case experience. For document management, ask which record systems, classification and access responsibilities the role covers. [O*NET compliance preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-9199.02#JobZone) · [O*NET document management preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-1299.03#JobZone)

## Ask how work is scheduled

Ask the compliance team how it handles routine reviews, investigations and remediation deadlines. Ask a document team how much time goes to access requests, migration and version maintenance.

## Moving between the roles

Compliance managers can bring requirements analysis and evidence review, then build skills in classification, metadata and records workflows. Document specialists can bring version and access controls, then learn regulatory interpretation, investigation writing and risk communication.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a fictional internal rule and two approval records. One has a signature but no effective date and the other has a date but no approval signature. Spend ten minutes indexing the evidence, ten drafting questions for the compliance review, and ten naming who can verify the operative version and who owns follow-up.
