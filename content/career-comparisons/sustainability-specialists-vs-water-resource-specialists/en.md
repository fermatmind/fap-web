# Sustainability Specialist vs. Water Resource Specialist: Interpreting a Campus Water-Savings Pilot

## What each role delivers

A sustainability specialist coordinates organizational savings measures and indicators. A water resource specialist assesses supply, demand and watershed constraints and compares management options.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Sustainability specialists | Water resource specialists |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Project actions, water-use ledger and status | Supply-demand assessment, constraints and alternatives |
| Basis | Meters, billing dates, boundaries and project timing | Inflows, storage, demand and seasonal conditions |

## How the work meets

A fictional campus runs a water-saving pilot. The sustainability specialist checks two buildings and finds different billing periods and occupied days, which the report records. The water resource specialist checks the campus share of system use and whether the pilot covers the peak dry period, then defines a window for the next evaluation.

## How to enter

Sustainability candidates can show project indicators and data corrections. Water resource candidates can show hydrology or supply-demand analyses with limitations. Ask whether the work covers facilities or utility and watershed planning.

## Work conditions

Internal reports follow billing cycles. Water resource work also follows seasonal monitoring. Ask who records a change in the reporting boundary.

## Moving between roles

Sustainability specialists can bring usage records to water resources and develop hydrology, models and demand forecasting. Water resource specialists moving into sustainability can apply systems analysis and add internal project delivery and reporting.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock records: both buildings used less water than last month, but one was closed for five days. The city expects peak dry-period demand next month. Spend ten minutes each on campus data checks, system data needs and two status notes.
