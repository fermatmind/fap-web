# Occupational Therapists vs. Physical Therapists: Who Checks Bathroom Independence Before Discharge?

## What each assessment answers

An occupational therapist looks at how someone completes a daily activity. A physical therapist looks at movement and physical function. A discharge goal can require both assessments, with each therapist documenting the conditions they actually observed.

## The same discharge goal

Imagine a patient who wants to use the bathroom independently after discharge. The physical therapist records what assistance the patient needs to stand from a chair, walk a short hallway, and turn. In a simulated bathroom, the occupational therapist observes the sequence of entering, managing clothing, and using the toilet, then compares the room with the layout the family describes. The therapists tell the discharge team which conditions they observed and which still need verification.

## Getting started in the U.S.

Occupational therapists typically need a master's degree in occupational therapy. Physical therapists entering the occupation need a Doctor of Physical Therapy degree. All states require licensure for both occupations. Check the relevant state board when comparing programs and practice requirements. ## Working conditions

Both roles may involve time on your feet alongside documentation and team discussion. Ask how often discharge assessments happen at bedside, who confirms home conditions, and how the team coordinates tasks that need two staff members.

## Moving between roles

Documentation, patient communication, and interdisciplinary handoffs carry over. A physical therapist moving toward occupational therapy needs training in activity and environment assessment. An occupational therapist moving toward physical therapy needs training in movement and physical function. Each route requires meeting the education and licensing requirements for the destination role.

## Try both in 30 minutes

Use three fictional notes: the patient walked eight meters with one staff member guarding nearby, paused at clothing management in the simulated bathroom, and has an unverified doorway width at home. Spend 10 minutes assigning which findings each role should address, 10 listing what still needs checking, and 10 drafting a joint handoff.
