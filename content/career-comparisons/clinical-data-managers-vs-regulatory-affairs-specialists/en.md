# Clinical data managers vs. regulatory affairs specialists

## A verified dataset and a reviewable submission package

Clinical data managers check study data, record issues and verify versions. Regulatory affairs specialists coordinate documents, requirements and submissions. Both may review the same table. The handoff works when the table's data provenance and its place in the package are both clear.

## Compare the work

| Item | Clinical data manager | Regulatory affairs specialist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deliverable | Query log, verification record and confirmed data version | Document checklist, revision record and submission package |
| Work setting to ask about | Data team working with study staff | Regulatory team working with data, writing and review staff |
| U.S. preparation evidence | O*NET Job Zone Four, considerable preparation | O*NET Job Zone Four, considerable preparation |

O*NET displays wages for the broader Data Scientists and Compliance Officers groups. Those figures represent their respective groups. [O*NET data role](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-2051.02) · [O*NET regulatory role](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/13-1041.07)

## A draft cites the wrong table version

At a fictional organization, a draft cites table V1 while the data team has verified V2. The data manager checks the confirmation record and change note and gives both to regulatory affairs. The regulatory specialist pauses the document handoff, updates the checklist and reference, and asks the data team to verify the update. The project’s designated approver decides whether the package is ready to submit.

## Getting started in the U.S.

A data coordinator role can build experience checking records and tracking queries. For regulatory work, document support can build experience finding requirements and checking package versions. O*NET places both occupations in Job Zone Four, which describes considerable preparation. O*NET says most occupations in this zone require a four-year bachelor’s degree, with exceptions. The zone describes preparation across occupations. Ask employers about education, product-area experience and supervision. Use fictional records in work samples. [O*NET data preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-2051.02#JobZone) · [O*NET regulatory preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/13-1041.07#JobZone)

## Ask what happens before the deadline

O*NET responses show recurring communication and time pressure in both occupations. In the example, the data team works through repeated queries and version checks. The regulatory team coordinates document reviews as submissions approach. Ask the data team who covers open queries during a review, and ask the regulatory team who resolves late changes and approves delivery. [O*NET data work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-2051.02#WorkContext) · [O*NET regulatory work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/13-1041.07#WorkContext)

## Moving between the roles

A data manager can bring a redacted issue log and version handoff, then learn how to find submission requirements and organize a package. A regulatory specialist can bring a redacted document checklist and change record, then learn data validation, query tracking and version verification.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use fictional records only. V2 is the confirmed table, the package still cites V1 and the package sign-off is blank. Spend ten minutes listing the mismatch and the confirming evidence, ten drafting the data handoff and regulatory checklist, and ten writing a status note to the approver. Explain whether the package is ready and who verifies the data, updates the reference and authorizes the next step.
