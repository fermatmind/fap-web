# Broadcast Technicians vs. Sound Engineering Technicians at a Live Concert

## Two ways of listening

Broadcast technicians check what viewers receive as a program goes out. Sound engineering technicians shape and record the audio that the production needs. They may listen to the same concert, but one is tracking the outgoing program and the other is working on the sound itself.

## Compare the handoffs

| Work product | Broadcast Technicians | Sound Engineering Technicians |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Live program monitor log and incident handoff | Program mix, recorded tracks, and audio revision notes |

U.S. occupational task sources · [O*NET 27-4012.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/27-4012.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 27-4014.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/27-4014.00#Tasks)

## The host is hard to hear

Imagine viewers say the host is hard to hear while the venue feed sounds clear. The broadcast technician compares the incoming program with the outgoing monitor and notes where the levels begin to differ. The sound engineering technician reviews the mix delivered to production and says whether raising the host would affect the music or crowd sound. If the difference appears only after transmission, the broadcast team continues tracing it under its assigned procedure while the audio team holds the mix steady.

## How to prepare

BLS says broadcast technicians typically need an associate degree, while education for sound engineering technicians can range from high school to a college degree depending on the position. To explore broadcast work, practice reading a rundown and logging an interruption. To explore sound, build a short mix from recordings you have permission to use and explain your choices. [BLS education guidance](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/broadcast-and-sound-engineering-technicians.htm#tab-4)

## Ask about the workday

Broadcast work follows airtime and shift handoffs. Sound engineers can make judgments during a show and may also return to the tracks for editing and listening afterward. Ask broadcasters which programs need live monitoring, when shifts start, and who can switch to a backup. Ask sound teams how many rehearsals and mix reviews are typical and who approves the final audio.

## Skills that transfer

Move toward sound by adding editing and listening examples to your monitoring experience. Move toward broadcast by adding show timing, incident records, and escalation practice to your audio projects.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use a fictional concert log at 8:17 p.m. The program arriving at broadcast has clear speech, the live remote monitor has quiet speech, and the music level appears unchanged. Spend ten minutes drafting an on-air incident note, ten writing whether to hold or change the mix and why, and ten choosing which evidence to share with the on-duty lead.
