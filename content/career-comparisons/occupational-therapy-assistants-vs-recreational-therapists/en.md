# Occupational Therapy Assistant vs. Recreational Therapist

## Which plan is each role responsible for?

Occupational therapy assistants implement assigned parts of occupational therapy plans under occupational therapist direction, observe task performance and report progress. Recreational therapists plan activities around assessed treatment goals and monitor how patients participate.

## Compare what goes into the record

| Stage | Occupational therapy assistant | Recreational therapist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plan | Review the assigned occupational therapy task | Set a recreation-based goal from assessed needs |
| Observe | Record task performance and assistance | Track choices, initiation and participation |
| Follow up | Report observations to the occupational therapist | Review activity and progress with the care team |

[O*NET 31-2011.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/31-2011.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 29-1125.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-1125.00#Tasks)

## Two observations from one gardening group

A fictional rehabilitation facility runs a tabletop gardening group. The occupational therapy assistant follows an assigned plan by observing whether a patient can retrieve and put away tools, then reports the assistance needed to the occupational therapist. The recreational therapist observes whether the participant selects a task and volunteers at their turn. The providers can share authorized details about the activity conditions for their separate reviews.

## U.S. education, training and credentials

BLS assigns an associate degree to occupational therapy assistants and a bachelor's degree to recreational therapists, with no related work experience or on-the-job training assigned in its national table for either. The BLS handbook separately explains that occupational therapy assistants usually need a state license. It describes an accredited assistant program, fieldwork and a passing exam result as the typical licensure route. The national table’s on-the-job training category concerns training needed in the occupation and does not replace program fieldwork. Recreational therapy employers may prefer or require certification; check the applicable state and employer rules. The BLS occupation handbook says recreational therapy degree programs usually include an internship. One certification route pairs a bachelor’s degree in recreational therapy with a supervised internship and a passing exam result. A small number of states require licensure or certification. [BLS detailed training table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/education-and-training-by-occupation.htm) · [BLS occupational therapy assistant entry guidance](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/occupational-therapy-assistants-and-aides.htm#tab-4) · [BLS recreational therapy entry guidance](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/recreational-therapists.htm#tab-4)

## What to ask about the schedule

For an occupational therapy assistant job, ask how treatment assignments and check-ins with the supervising therapist are scheduled. For recreational therapy, ask how individual sessions, groups, materials and team review fit into a week.

## Switching roles

Assistants can bring experience tracking task performance and using agreed supports, then develop recreation-based assessment and activity-planning skills. Recreational therapists can bring progress documentation and team coordination to occupational therapy assistant training, then verify program, clinical placement and licensure requirements.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a fictional note: a participant selected a planting card, needed one prompt to retrieve a tool and volunteered at their turn. Take ten minutes to write an occupational task observation, ten to document progress toward a participation goal, and ten to identify both recipients and one activity detail that can be shared with authorization.
