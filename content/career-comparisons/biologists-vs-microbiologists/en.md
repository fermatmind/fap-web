# Biologist vs. Microbiologist

## Compare the scope of the research

Biologists can study organisms and their relationships with the environment. Microbiologists focus on microorganisms and their interactions with environments or other organisms. Both may analyze evidence and prepare research reports.

## Compare the work products

| Decision | Biologist | Microbiologist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verify | Species, habitat and the timing of observations | What each microbial result represents and whether records are comparable |
| Document | Patterns in organisms and their surroundings | Microbial findings and the limits of the evidence |
| Coordinate | Check site and date with the project team | Confirm that the result refers to the same site and period |

[O*NET 19-1029.04](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-1029.04#Tasks) · [O*NET 19-1022.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-1022.00#Tasks)

## Two wetland reports appear to show a change

A fictional wetland project records less plant cover on one section of shoreline. A microbial monitoring summary changes around the same time. The biologist verifies the vegetation map and observation dates. The microbiologist checks whether the existing summary is for that section of shore and the same period. The team can then note which findings can be compared and which links still need evidence.

## U.S. education and training

BLS places biologists in its broader Biological Scientists, All Other group for this comparison. The national table assigns that group and microbiologists a bachelor's degree, no related work experience and no on-the-job training as typical entry categories. The BLS microbiologist handbook notes that some employers prefer a master's degree or Ph.D. Check the particular project, methods and graduate preparation required. At the individual occupation level, O*NET places biologists in Job Zone Five, where most occupations require graduate education. It places microbiologists in Job Zone Four. The BLS parent group and O*NET preparation levels describe different populations, so check the research position’s degree expectations. [BLS detailed training table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/education-and-training-by-occupation.htm) · [BLS microbiologist entry guidance](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/life-physical-and-social-science/microbiologists.htm#tab-4) · [O*NET biologists preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-1029.04#JobZone) · [O*NET microbiologist preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-1022.00#JobZone)

## What to ask about the workweek

Ask a biology employer how time is divided between field observations, data analysis and reporting. Ask a microbiology employer about time allocated to laboratory work, record review and joint interpretation.

## Moving between roles

Biologists can bring study design and scientific reporting into microbiology and build training in microbial science and the relevant workplace methods. Microbiologists can bring data quality and reporting skills into broader biology work, then develop experience in field observations, species records and ecological context.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a fictional vegetation map for the north bank in June and an existing microbial summary dated June with no shoreline identifier. Spend ten minutes summarizing the plant observations, ten listing the missing fields in the microbial record, and ten drafting a joint note that separates facts, open questions and what would resolve them.
