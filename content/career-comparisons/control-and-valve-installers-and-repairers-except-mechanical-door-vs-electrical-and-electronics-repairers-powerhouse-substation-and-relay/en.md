# Control and Valve Repairer vs. Substation and Relay Repairer

## Valve and control work versus utility electrical repair

Control and valve installers and repairers work on assigned valves and control devices and document maintenance. Powerhouse, substation and relay repairers check utility electrical equipment and record findings. Work can overlap around instruments, so verify the assigned equipment and authority.

## Compare the service records

| Item | Control and valve installer or repairer | Powerhouse, substation and relay repairer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verify | The valve or control component and its maintenance record | The utility electrical device and its inspection record |
| Document | Component identity, service history and unresolved questions | Equipment identity, findings and items for review |
| Coordinate | Confirm the work-order scope with the assigned owner | Confirm the utility equipment scope with the maintenance lead |

## The work order says only 'control device issue'

At a fictional facility, a work order fails to identify whether its reference is to a valve assembly or utility electrical equipment. The valve repairer checks the component list and maintenance record. The electrical repairer checks the equipment identifier and existing inspection record. The designated supervisor uses the two notes to assign the follow-up.

## U.S. education and training

For control and valve installers and repairers other than mechanical door workers, BLS assigns high school or equivalent, no related work experience and moderate-term on-the-job training. For powerhouse, substation and relay repairers it assigns a postsecondary nondegree award, less than five years of related experience and moderate-term on-the-job training. Check the employer's specific equipment training and any applicable authorization or credential requirements. [BLS detailed education and training table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/education-and-training-by-occupation.htm)

## Ask how work is scheduled

For a valve role, ask which equipment the crew covers and how it divides shop and field time. For electrical repair, ask when maintenance windows occur and who reviews unfinished findings across shifts.

## Moving between roles

Valve repairers can bring device identification and maintenance records to electrical repair training, then learn the utility equipment and its requirements. Electrical repairers can bring documentation and discrepancy reporting to valve work, then learn the mechanical controls and applicable qualification requirements.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a fictional work order, valve maintenance note and electrical equipment index. Spend ten minutes marking the missing identifier, ten stating which record each worker needs to verify, and ten writing a note for the work-order owner with the proposed assignment and the missing evidence. Use records only.
