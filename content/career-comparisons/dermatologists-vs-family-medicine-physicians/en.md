# Dermatologists vs Family Medicine Physicians

## Specialty depth and continuing care

Dermatologists diagnose and treat conditions of the skin, hair, and nails. Some also perform dermatologic surgery. Family medicine physicians diagnose and treat patients across age groups, provide preventive care, and coordinate ongoing care and referrals. Both may see patients with skin concerns, with different assessment and follow-up responsibilities.

## The consultation arrived while a result is pending

At a fictional U.S. clinic, a family physician sees a patient with recurrent skin symptoms. Referral F-24 summarizes the timeline, prior care, and the patient's concern, and notes that routine follow-up is already booked. After the specialty visit, the dermatologist returns note D-24 with a specialty assessment and flags one outstanding result for the dermatology team to track. Under this clinic's rules, dermatology updates the result and notifies the referring practice. Family medicine keeps the regular visit and incorporates the specialty note into the ongoing record. Each physician signs their own assessment.

## Education, residency, and licensing

Both routes typically involve medical school (MD or DO), residency, and state physician licensure. Compare dermatology and family medicine residency tracks separately, then verify licensing requirements with the medical board in the state where you intend to practice. Ask the employer about specialty certification and privileges for the role. [BLS physician entry guide](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/physicians-and-surgeons.htm#tab-4)

## What to ask about the workweek

Ask dermatology practices how they schedule consultations, return visits, and procedures and who tracks results. Ask family medicine practices how visits, ongoing care, and referral follow-up share the schedule, and how specialist notes return to the team.

## What carries over between specialties

Clear notes and dependable follow-up transfer between settings. Moving toward dermatology calls for specialty training and the corresponding privileges. Moving toward family medicine calls for broad clinical training, continuity practice, and coordination across services. Check the actual training and credentialing path for the intended role.

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use fictional F-24, D-24, and update U-24: the specialty note is filed, the result has not been issued, and the routine visit remains booked. Spend ten minutes separating completed and open items, ten minutes drafting the specialty result-tracking note, and ten minutes writing the family practice handoff. Check that you named the result owner and retained the existing follow-up appointment.
