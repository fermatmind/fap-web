# Jewelers and precious stone and metal workers vs. patternmakers, metal and plastic

## Both may make models

Jewelers and precious stone and metal workers make or repair jewelry. Their work can include making casting models and inspecting finished pieces. Metal and plastic patternmakers may make patterns for molding and check them against engineering drawings. Ask which object you deliver and how the next team uses it.

## Compare the roles

| Item | Jeweler or precious stone and metal worker | Metal and plastic patternmaker |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work product | A model, jewelry item or repair and inspection record | A verified industrial pattern and dimensional record |
| Setting to ask about | Jewelry bench, production shop or repair counter | Pattern shop and coordination with molding staff |
| Typical U.S. entry education | High school diploma or equivalent | High school diploma or equivalent |
| Typical on-the-job training | Long-term | Moderate-term |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $52,540 | $58,000 |

Wages cover nonfarm wage and salary workers and exclude self-employment. ## Two objects for one exhibition

A fictional exhibition needs a display pendant and cast display bases. The jewelry worker checks the pendant against the order, spots a surface scratch and records the needed rework before final inspection. The patternmaker checks the pattern for forming the base molds, logs the reference width and submits it to the molding lead for review. The exhibition lead tracks jewelry readiness and pattern handoff separately.

## Getting started in the U.S.

Jewelry workers typically develop skills on the job. BLS also describes trade-school courses or an apprenticeship that combines paid training and instruction. Ask whether the employer trains new workers in modelmaking, finishing or repair. For industrial patternmaking, BLS lists moderate-term on-the-job training to develop competency. Ask how new workers progress from reading drawings to checking patterns for molding. [BLS jewelry training](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/production/jewelers-and-precious-stone-and-metal-workers.htm#tab-4) · [BLS typical preparation](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Ask what drives the bench work

For jewelry, ask how much time goes to repeat orders or individual repairs and who confirms the finish. For patternmaking, ask how often drawings change and who receives feedback from molding.

## Moving between the roles

A jewelry worker can bring a redacted model revision and finish checklist, then learn engineering drawings, tolerances and molding handoffs. A patternmaker can bring a redacted dimensional report and revision record, then build experience with jewelry materials, finish checks and the customer's specifications.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use two mock inspection cards. The pendant meets its dimensions but has a visible scratch, which the order disallows. The base pattern measures 90 mm, within 89–91 mm, but it is marked with obsolete revision A. Spend ten minutes identifying both issues, ten drafting the rework and version requests, and ten writing the handoffs. State who checks the finish, who confirms the current pattern revision and dimensions, and what each needs before the item moves on.
