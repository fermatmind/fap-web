# Acute Care Nurses vs Critical Care Nurses

## A transfer changes the nursing assignment

Acute care nurses provide advanced nursing care for acute conditions, which can include care before or after surgery. Critical care nurses focus on seriously ill patients who require close observation and intensive support. The roles can overlap in an ICU. Ask which unit and patient population the job serves.

## Work, setting, and U.S. entry

| Item | Acute care nurses | Critical care nurses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | Care record, task status, and transfer handoff | Receiving assessment, monitoring record, and care handoff |
| Setting examples | Hospital units caring for acute conditions | ICUs and other intensive care settings |
| U.S. entry | Approved nursing education and RN licensure, plus unit orientation | Approved nursing education and RN licensure, plus critical care preparation specified by the employer |

The BLS wage series covers registered nurses as a broader group. ## A pending report needs a named owner

At a fictional hospital, the medical team has arranged a transfer from an acute care unit to the ICU. Transfer note T-07 lists a pending report with no confirmed recipient. The sending nurse documents the outstanding item and handoff status. The ICU nurse records the receiving assessment on arrival and checks the pending item with the sending nurse. Under this hospital's example rules, the receiving nurse confirms ownership of the report for the shift, and both record acknowledgment. If ownership remains open, the receiving charge nurse coordinates it.

## Education, licensure, and local orientation

In the U.S., approved routes toward RN licensure can include a nursing bachelor's degree, associate degree, or diploma from an approved program. BLS specifies graduation from an approved program and passing a qualifying exam for state licensure. Check your state's requirements and the employer's education minimum. Ask acute care teams about the units and patient populations included in orientation. For critical care, ask about supervised ICU orientation and the checks before taking an independent assignment.

## What makes a shift busy

Ask an acute care unit how often it admits and transfers patients and who tracks open items. Ask a critical care unit how it reallocates shift duties when an ICU transfer arrives and who supports the receiving nurse during clusters of intensive needs.

## What transfers between units

A simulation or approved handoff exercise can show how you document outstanding items and close the loop. An acute care nurse moving into critical care needs training for the receiving unit's monitoring and assessment requirements. A critical care nurse moving to an acute unit should learn its patient mix, transfer workflow, and workload across multiple patients.

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use fictional T-07 (report pending and ownership unconfirmed) and I-07 (patient has arrived and the receiving assessment was documented). Spend ten minutes drafting the sending handoff, ten minutes drafting the acknowledgment, and ten minutes naming whom to contact if ownership is still open and which facts each person should record. Keep the exercise to documentation and handoff responsibilities.
