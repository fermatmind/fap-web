# Blockchain Engineers vs. Computer Systems Analysts: The Shared Record Decision

## What each role delivers

Blockchain engineers design and evaluate blockchain systems. Systems analysts turn the needs of the participating organizations into workflow, integration and acceptance requirements.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Blockchain Engineers | Computer Systems Analysts |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Shared-record prototype and evaluation | Workflow, integration and acceptance criteria |
| Basis | Consistency across participants | Business rules and handoffs |

## How the work meets

Three fictional organizations need to track material transfers. The analyst identifies a requirement to retain the old status when a record is corrected. The engineer uses mock entries to evaluate whether the proposed design exposes that history and reports the result and limitations.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for computer systems analysts. For blockchain roles, check the employer’s software and distributed systems expectations and show a small project with test documentation. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Engineers spend more time on technical design and verification. Analysts alternate between stakeholder discussions, rules and acceptance review. Ask who confirms the workflow and who reviews technical evidence.

## Moving between roles

Engineers moving to analysis can build on system boundaries and test evidence and add stakeholder discovery. Analysts moving to engineering can bring requirements and add programming and distributed systems practice.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use fictional records for three organizations: one transfer is accepted on time and one gets a correction that must retain its history. Spend ten minutes each on acceptance criteria, a paper prototype view and the handoff with unverified items.
