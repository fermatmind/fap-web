# Electrical Engineers vs. Electronics Engineers: Power and Signal at the Same Interface

## What each role delivers

Electrical engineers may design equipment power and installation requirements. Electronics engineers may design circuits and verify signal behavior. They can share responsibility for defining the interface.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Electrical Engineers | Electronics Engineers, Except Computer |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Power design and installation constraints | Circuit design and verification |
| Basis | Loads, supply conditions and equipment interfaces | Input limits, signals and circuit behavior |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $120,630 | $130,220 |

## How the work meets

A fictional plant adds a temperature recorder. The electrical engineer checks supply and installation constraints. The electronics engineer checks the recorder board’s input limits and signal requirements. They flag a mismatch in the proposed equipment before scheduling verification.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical entry education for both. Compare power and installation work with circuit and verification work. Check actual duties and any applicable licensing or signing requirements with the employer and state board. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Electrical roles may coordinate with installers and vendors. Electronics roles may spend more time on design reviews and bench evaluation. Check the assignment and where the work happens.

## Moving between roles

Moving into electronics, build on power and interface knowledge and add circuit and signal verification. Moving into electrical engineering, build on device interfaces and add load calculations and installation requirements.
