# Blockchain Engineers vs Information Security Analyst

## Two outputs from the same access requirement

Blockchain engineers design and test distributed ledgers and the applications that use them. They deliver working features and verification results. Information security analysts evaluate the safeguards around systems and data, document risks, and coordinate follow-up. Both roles can assess security. Ask who builds a control, who reviews the evidence, and who can approve the change.

## Work setting, output, and U.S. entry

| Item | Blockchain engineering | Information security analysis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | Ledger integration, feature, or validation results | Control findings, evidence, and follow-up recommendations |
| Working partners | Application and platform teams, partner engineers | System owners, operations staff, and security teams |
| Typical preparation | O*NET Job Zone Four. A four-year degree is common in this group, with substantial relevant skill, knowledge, or experience. | BLS lists a bachelor's degree and related work experience as typical for analysts |

The U.S. median annual wage for information security analysts was $129,180 in May 2025. O*NET classifies blockchain engineers within a broader group for its published wage data. ## A carrier can do more than the agreement allows

A fictional logistics company is preparing to add two carriers to a shared delivery-status ledger. The business owner allows both carriers to record delivery times on their own shipments and reserves corrections for internal customer service. The exercise log shows Carrier A can record a delivery on its own shipment. Carrier B can also correct its own delivery time. The engineer documents the required change and verifies both actions against the approved role assignments. The analyst records the excess permission, recommends keeping Carrier B off the ledger, and asks for verification evidence in the change record. Under this company's rules, a designated release owner decides when onboarding can proceed.

## What to show when applying

For a U.S. blockchain engineering role, ask whether the job owns node operations, application code, or integration. A small portfolio project can show how records enter a ledger, who may submit them, and how the expected behavior was verified. For information security analysis, ask which related IT experience counts, whether a certification is required or preferred, and how the team records findings and corrective actions.

## Ask how work reaches your desk

Ask the engineering team who coordinates changes when a partner's integration window shifts. Ask the analyst team whether the work comes through a monitoring rotation, a project review queue, or both, and who takes ownership when a finding is escalated.

## How to make a move between roles

Engineers moving toward analysis can bring a redacted record of a permissions review and practice tying each finding to an approved requirement and an owner. Analysts moving toward engineering can bring a clear control requirement with criteria for acceptance and demonstrate that they can implement and verify it in a portfolio project.

## Try a 30-minute paper review

Use the fictional approval and exercise log above. Spend ten minutes marking which recorded action fits the agreement and which exceeds it. Spend ten minutes writing an engineer's verification note with an expected result for recording and for correcting. Spend ten minutes drafting the analyst's handoff with Carrier B's onboarding status, the missing evidence, and the person who makes the release decision.
