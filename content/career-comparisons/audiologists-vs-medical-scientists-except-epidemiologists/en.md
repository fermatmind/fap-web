# Audiologists vs Medical Scientists, Except Epidemiologists

## An individual follow-up and a study question

An audiologist assesses hearing and supports follow-up care for individuals. A medical scientist designs studies to answer health questions and reports findings with their limitations. Some audiologists conduct research. Some medical scientists also carry out clinical duties when qualified to do so.

## Work, U.S. preparation, and pay

| Item | Audiologists | Medical scientists, except epidemiologists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | Hearing evaluation, recommendations, and follow-up record | Study protocol, analysis, and findings with limitations |
| Work setting | Hearing clinics and other health care or education settings | Research institutions, hospitals, or R&D organizations |
| Typical U.S. preparation | Doctor of Audiology (Au.D.) and state licensure | Ph.D., often in a life science, or a medical degree |

## One missed visit produces two records

A fictional hearing center and research team want to study whether a change in appointment reminders affects follow-up completion. Service record R-14 shows that a patient did not attend and the reason field is blank. The audiologist sends the scheduling coordinator the missed-visit status and a request to arrange follow-up. The medical scientist has defined completed, incomplete, and unknown status before analysis. Under this project's approved data rules, the audiologist shares a coded record with the visit marked incomplete and the reason unknown. The scientist records the reason as missing and explains its effect on the interim summary. The care team arranges the individual follow-up, and the study lead reviews the research summary.

## Training and authorization

For U.S. audiology, check Au.D. programs and the licensing requirements of the state where you intend to work. For medical science, ask whether the position expects doctoral research, a medical degree, or a master's degree plus relevant experience. Check any separate clinical licensing requirement if a research role also includes patient care.

## What to ask about the workday

Ask an audiology employer when follow-up slots, reports, and referrals are handled and who owns missed visits. Ask a research team about data review and reporting deadlines and who resolves incomplete fields before analysis.

## Moving between the roles

An audiologist can bring an approved, de-identified example of consistent documentation and add study design, analysis, and research ethics training. A medical scientist can bring measurement and data quality experience and pursue the professional training, supervised practice, and state authorization required for audiology.

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use fictional R-14 (missed, blank reason) and R-15 (completed as scheduled). Spend ten minutes assigning care statuses and next actions. Spend ten minutes drafting the research handoff using the three completion categories and flagging missing fields. Spend ten minutes writing a short summary that names who follows up on R-14, who reviews the research record, and why two entries cannot establish an effect of the reminders.
