# Clinical Neuropsychologists vs Speech-Language Pathologists

## Two reports answer different questions

Clinical neuropsychologists integrate cognitive tests, interviews, and behavior observations into a report with limitations and recommendations. Speech-language pathologists evaluate communication function and set intervention goals. Both may assess attention and memory when these affect the question they are addressing.

## Outputs, settings, and U.S. preparation

| Item | Clinical neuropsychologists | Speech-language pathologists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | Integrated cognitive and behavioral assessment report | Functional communication assessment, goals, and progress notes |
| Setting examples | Assessment, rehabilitation, or multidisciplinary clinics | Schools, hospitals, or rehabilitation services |
| Typical preparation | Doctoral psychology training and supervised clinical neuropsychology specialization | At least a master's in speech-language pathology, with state licensure requirements |

## One appointment, two observation conditions

A fictional U.S. rehabilitation program wants to observe whether a client can relay the details of a follow-up visit. In note N-21, the clinical neuropsychologist records that the client needed the date repeated to relay the details after spoken instructions in a quiet room. The report limits this observation to the setting and task and goes to the speech-language pathologist. In note C-21, the SLP records that the client relayed the date and location using a written appointment card during a simulated front-desk exchange. The SLP sends the communication result and proposed next task back to the team. Each clinician signs their own evaluation under the program's rules, and a team coordinator assembles the follow-up schedule.

## Training, licensure, and specialty credentials

For clinical neuropsychology, check state psychology licensure and employer requirements for supervised specialty training. ABPP lists postdoctoral training criteria for specialty board certification. For speech-language pathology, check graduate programs, supervised clinical experience, and requirements from the state board. For school positions, ask about any additional education credential.

## What to ask about workload

Ask an assessment clinic how much time is set aside to score, interpret, and write reports. Ask an SLP team how often sessions repeat and how it records whether communication skills carry into everyday routines.

## What transfers between the roles

Both paths use careful observation, documentation, and handoffs. Someone moving toward clinical neuropsychology needs training in cognitive assessment and integrating clinical results. Someone moving toward speech-language pathology needs training in functional communication assessment and intervention. Verify the education and practice requirements for your intended role.

## Try a 30-minute desk exercise

Use notes N-21 and C-21 above. Spend ten minutes listing the setting, how information was presented, and what was observed. Spend ten minutes writing two handoff excerpts, one on what the cognitive observation covers and one proposing the next communication task. Spend ten minutes identifying what is still missing for a comparison and labeling who signs each note and who assembles the follow-up schedule.
