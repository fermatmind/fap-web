# Postsecondary Education Teachers vs. Instructional Coordinators: How a Rubric Changes

## What each role delivers

Postsecondary education teachers teach methods and assess lesson plans or fieldwork. Instructional coordinators study implementation, review materials and organize staff development.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Education Teachers, Postsecondary | Instructional Coordinators |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Methods course and candidate feedback | Materials review and staff development |
| Basis | Learning goals and fieldwork | Cross-class implementation |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $75,350 | $77,440 |

## How the work meets

A fictional district pilots a reading rubric. A college instructor uses a mock sample to assess how candidates justify scores. The coordinator finds that two classrooms read a criterion differently, convenes a calibration session and submits a revision recommendation.

## How to enter

BLS lists a doctoral or professional degree and less than five years of related experience as typical for postsecondary education teachers, and a master’s degree with five or more years of related experience for instructional coordinators. Check state and district requirements for public-school coordination roles. [BLS preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/education-training-and-library/instructional-coordinators.htm#tab-4) [BLS occupation rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Faculty work often follows academic terms and field placements. Coordination follows observations, material review and training. Ask who publishes approved changes and how teachers receive them.

## Moving between roles

Teachers can bring course evidence into coordination and build cross-class review and staff training skills. Coordinators can bring implementation cases to college teaching and add disciplinary research and methods assessment.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use three fictional rubric samples. Two classes score sample B two levels apart. Spend ten minutes drafting an assignment for teacher candidates, ten planning staff calibration, and ten defining evidence to judge each result.
