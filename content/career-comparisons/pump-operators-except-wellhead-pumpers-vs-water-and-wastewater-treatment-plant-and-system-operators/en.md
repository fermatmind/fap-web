# Pump Operators, Except Wellhead Pumpers vs Water and wastewater treatment plant and system operators

## Move the flow or track the treatment process

Pump operators in this comparison focus on equipment and transfer records. Treatment plant and system operators monitor how a water or wastewater process is performing and pass that information to the next crew. Duties can overlap at one facility. Ask whether the opening covers individual equipment, treatment processes, or both.

## Compare the two shift records

| Compare | Pump operators | Treatment plant and system operators |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main focus | Pump equipment and material transfer | Treatment process performance and operating records |
| Work setting | Pump stations and other transfer facilities | Treatment facilities, field rounds or control rooms |
| Handoff | Transfer timing, equipment status and unresolved issues | Plant trend and monitoring summary for the next crew |

[O*NET pump operators](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-7072.00) · [O*NET treatment operators](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/51-8031.00)

## Follow the record from lift station to plant

The City of Dunedin, Florida, describes wastewater moving through its lift stations and onward to the treatment plant. In an illustrative handoff on a similar system, the pump operator sends the station log with one interval marked missing. The plant operator checks the plant’s own record and notes that the meter includes more than that station’s flow. The next shift receives both records and the names of the people who need to confirm the gap and the meter coverage. The station operator verifies the transfer log, and the plant operator verifies the plant record. Any operational change goes to the authorized supervisor for the affected facility. [Dunedin city project description](https://engage.dunedin.gov/citywide-lift-station-control-panel-replacement)

## Check what the employer trains and what the state licenses

O*NET lists pump operators in Job Zone 1–2, where a high school diploma or equivalent is usual and training varies. Ask employers about equipment training and any credentials that apply to their facility. BLS reports a high school diploma or equivalent and long-term on-the-job training for treatment operators. A state license is typically required, and trainees may work toward it during on-the-job training. Florida DEP requires a license for covered drinking water plant, domestic wastewater plant and water distribution system operation. Ask which license class and supervised experience the opening requires. [O*NET preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-7072.00) · [BLS treatment operator profile](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/production/water-and-wastewater-treatment-plant-and-system-operators.htm) · [Florida DEP certification program](https://floridadep.gov/water/certification-restoration/content/water-and-domestic-wastewater-operator-certification)

## Ask which sites and shifts you would cover

A pump operator should ask whether the job involves a single facility, travel among stations or callouts. For treatment work, check rotating shifts, weekend coverage and the time spent on rounds versus records. Compare wages, shift premiums and travel across local openings with similar responsibilities. [BLS treatment work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/production/water-and-wastewater-treatment-plant-and-system-operators.htm)

## Use a pump log as a bridge to treatment work

Pump operators can bring experience documenting equipment status and changes. To move into treatment, add process fundamentals, monitoring records and the applicable operator licensing pathway. Treatment operators moving into pump-focused work can bring shift discipline and build experience with station records and equipment-specific training.

## Try a thirty-minute paper handoff

Use fictional records. The station logged 20,000 gallons from 7 to 8 a.m. and has no record from 8 to 9 a.m. The plant recorded 52,000 gallons for both hours together, including a second inflow with no separate meter. Spend 12 minutes writing the pump handoff and 13 minutes writing the plant note. Use five minutes to identify who checks the missing hour and who confirms meter coverage. State what each record measures before comparing totals.
