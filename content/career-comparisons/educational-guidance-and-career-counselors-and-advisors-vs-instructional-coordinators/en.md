# School and Career Counselor vs. Instructional Coordinator

## Student choices and curriculum design

Educational, guidance, and career counselors and advisors help students navigate courses and education or career options. Instructional coordinators work with teachers and administrators to review materials, training and classroom practice.

## Compare the two work products

| Decision | School or career counselor and advisor | Instructional coordinator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verify | Student plans, course constraints and options | How the curriculum and materials are being used |
| Document | Advising options and a follow-up date | Proposed lesson or material changes and teacher support |
| Share with | The student and authorized support staff | Teachers and the curriculum team |

[O*NET 21-1012.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/21-1012.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 25-9031.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/25-9031.00#Tasks)

## A course prerequisite is hard to understand

In a fictional high school, several students want to take a design course but interpret the prerequisite differently. An advisor can verify each student's record and discuss alternatives while the requirement is clarified. With authorization, the advisor passes on the recurring question. The coordinator works with the teacher to compare the catalog entry with course materials and draft clearer text. After approval through school procedures, advisors use the updated wording.

## U.S. education and credentials

BLS says school counselors typically need a master's degree and a state-issued credential. Career and academic advising requirements vary by employer and state; some states require career counselor licensure. BLS assigns instructional coordinators a master's degree, five or more years of related work experience and no on-the-job training in its national table. Its handbook notes that public school coordinators may need a state-issued license. Check the state and target employer for both tracks. [BLS counselor and advisor entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/community-and-social-service/school-and-career-counselors.htm#tab-4) · [BLS instructional coordinator entry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/education-training-and-library/instructional-coordinators.htm#tab-4) · [BLS national training table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/education-and-training-by-occupation.htm)

## How the school calendar affects the work

Ask an advising employer how it handles appointment spikes near registration or graduation. Ask a curriculum employer how it schedules classroom visits, materials reviews and teacher training across schools or grades.

## Moving between the roles

Advisors can bring student feedback and knowledge of course sequences, then develop curriculum analysis, teacher training and observation skills. Coordinators can bring knowledge of curriculum and prerequisites, then build advising interviews, career research and student-record skills.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use fictional records: a student's completed courses, an ambiguous prerequisite and a prior catalog entry. Spend ten minutes listing two advising options with facts to confirm. Spend ten drafting a catalog clarification and a question for the teacher. In the final ten, identify the approval owner and a date for the advisor to follow up.
