# Light truck drivers vs. postal service mail carriers

## Moving a load and serving a mail route

Light truck drivers verify loads and record deliveries or pickups. Postal service mail carriers sort and deliver individual items along assigned routes and document signatures or attempted deliveries when required. Both may carry parcels. The distinction is what they must record when the handoff changes.

## Compare the roles

| Item | Light truck driver | Postal service mail carrier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deliverable | Verified load, proof of delivery and exceptions | Mail delivered or tracked as attempted, with a receipt where required |
| Setting to ask about | Vehicle, warehouses, receiving docks and customer sites | Route on foot or by vehicle, including outdoor stops |
| Typical U.S. entry education | High school diploma or equivalent | No formal educational credential in the BLS occupation classification |
| Typical on-the-job training | Short-term | Short-term |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $44,860 | $60,550 |

Wages cover nonfarm wage and salary workers and exclude self-employment. ## Two items at the same building

A fictional office building expects a supplier carton and a mail item that requires the addressee’s signature under the example policy. The recipient is absent. The light truck driver verifies the carton and uses the authorized receiving desk. The carrier records the unsuccessful attempt, leaves pickup information and returns the mail item to the station. The driver reports any route issue to dispatch; the carrier hands the item and record back to the station.

## Getting started in the U.S.

For light truck work, BLS identifies a driver’s license and clean driving record. Verify what applies to the vehicle and job. For carrier work, check the specific posting and USPS hiring requirements, including a safe driving record where applicable. The BLS education category describes the occupation; the employer applies its own eligibility rules. [BLS delivery-driver entry guidance](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/transportation-and-material-moving/delivery-truck-drivers-and-driver-sales-workers.htm#tab-4) · [USPS hiring requirements](https://about.usps.com/careers/how-to-apply/welcome.htm)

## Ask about the route and end of shift

On the truck side, ask how stops are scheduled and how long loading and receiving can take. On the mail side, ask how sorting, walking distance and the return of undelivered items fit into the shift.

## Moving between the roles

For a move into mail delivery, bring a redacted route and delivery exception log, then learn address sequencing, mail categories and attempted-delivery procedures. To move into light truck work, bring a redacted route handoff, then learn to reconcile shipping papers and coordinate loading and dispatch.

## A 30-minute tabletop exercise

Use a fictional shipment with one carton and one mail item. The recipient is away. The shipment permits an authorized receiving desk; the mail item must be signed for by the addressee under the exercise rules. Spend ten minutes deciding what goes to the desk or returns, ten drafting the receipt and pickup notice, and ten writing separate handoffs. Record the authority, status and next responsible party for each item.
