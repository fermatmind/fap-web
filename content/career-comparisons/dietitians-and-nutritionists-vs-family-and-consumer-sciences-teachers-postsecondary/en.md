# Dietitians and Nutritionists vs. Postsecondary Family and Consumer Sciences Teachers

## What each role delivers

Dietitians and nutritionists assess nutrition needs and provide services within their role. Postsecondary family and consumer sciences teachers may teach nutrition and assess coursework or projects.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Dietitians and Nutritionists | Family and Consumer Sciences Teachers, Postsecondary |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Nutrition-service record and follow-up plan | Coursework and project feedback |
| Basis | Client needs and service scope | Course objectives and student work |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $76,400 | $75,870 |

## How to enter

BLS Table 1.2 lists a bachelor’s degree and internship or residency as typical for the broad dietitians and nutritionists occupation, and a doctoral or professional degree as typical for postsecondary family and consumer sciences teachers. Check the state and credential path separately. Florida’s examination route has education and supervised experience requirements and uses CDR’s dietitian registration exam. CDR requires a graduate degree for applicants first establishing exam eligibility since January 1, 2024, along with its other eligibility conditions. ## Work conditions

Nutrition work follows service schedules, clients and documentation. Higher education follows coursework, grading and projects. Ask how employers divide service, scholarly work and teaching.

## Moving between roles

Moving to college teaching, turn anonymized examples into learning goals and rubrics. Moving to nutrition service, check credential rules and build supervised practice for the specific setting.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use a fictional menu with dates and meal names but no needs summary. Spend ten minutes on a service information request, ten on an evidence question for students and ten defining the deliverable for each role. Use paper only.
