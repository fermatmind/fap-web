# Audiologists vs Speech-Language Pathologists

## Different assessments for the same student

Audiologists assess hearing and related concerns and document what the person can hear under the conditions evaluated. Speech-language pathologists assess communication and plan intervention. Some SLP roles also work with swallowing. Both can contribute to communication support when hearing affects access to speech.

## Outputs, U.S. entry, and pay

| Item | Audiologists | Speech-language pathologists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | Hearing evaluation and follow-up recommendations | Communication assessment, goals, and progress records |
| Work setting | Health care settings and some school roles | Schools, health care, and other service settings |
| Typical U.S. entry education | Doctor of Audiology (Au.D.) | At least a master's in speech-language pathology |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $95,780 | $97,870 |

The medians cover U.S. wage and salary workers and exclude self-employment. ## What gets handed over after a screening

In a fictional U.S. school partnership, the audiologist selects the screening protocol and oversees training and quality checks. A trained SLP can help carry out screening under that protocol and follow its referral criteria. Hearing screen H-06 says “refer for evaluation.” Classroom note L-06 says the student asked for directions to be repeated during group work and completed the activity when pictures were provided. The SLP sends both records to the audiologist with the classroom context. The audiologist shares formal evaluation findings, test conditions, and follow-up recommendations. The SLP uses that information to interpret the communication assessment and works with the school team on what to observe next. Each professional signs their own evaluation. [ASHA childhood hearing screening](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/professional-issues/childhood-hearing-screening/)

## Training and authorization

Audiologists typically enter with an Au.D. SLPs typically need at least a master's in speech-language pathology and supervised clinical experience as part of the licensing pathway. BLS reports that all states license both professions. Check the state board where you intend to work and ask the school employer about any additional education credential.

## How a workday is divided

Ask audiology employers how much time goes to evaluations, follow-up appointments, and report writing. Ask SLP employers how much time goes to evaluation versus repeated sessions and how often the role coordinates with families, teachers, or other clinicians.

## What transfers between the roles

A redacted referral and clear progress notes can show skills both teams use. Moving into audiology requires training in hearing evaluation. Moving into speech-language pathology requires training in communication assessment and intervention. Either move also means completing the applicable education, supervised practice, and licensing requirements.
