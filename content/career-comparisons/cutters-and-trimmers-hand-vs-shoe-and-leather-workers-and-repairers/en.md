# Cutters and trimmers, hand vs. shoe and leather workers and repairers

## Cut the part or complete the item

Hand cutters and trimmers use work orders and patterns to cut or trim material. Shoe and leather workers and repairers make or repair finished items. Cutting may be part of that work, along with deciding which part to replace and checking the completed item.

## Compare the roles

| Item | Hand cutter or trimmer | Shoe and leather worker or repairer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deliverable | Parts or trimmed items checked against specifications | An assembled or repaired item and a completion check |
| Work setting to ask about | A cutting table or production workbench | A production station or a repair bench |
| Typical U.S. entry education | No formal educational credential | High school diploma or equivalent |
| Typical on-the-job training | Short-term | Moderate-term |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $38,020 | $37,800 |

Wages cover nonfarm wage and salary workers and exclude self-employment. ## A pair of shoes needs new lining

At a fictional repair shop, the repairer inspects wear and specifies replacement lining for the left and right shoes. The cutter checks the templates and order, spots the missing count for the right piece, and asks the repairer to confirm it. The cutter supplies one of each and a check record after clarification. The repairer fits the parts, checks the finished pair and decides whether the job is ready for pickup.

## Getting started in the U.S.

For hand cutting, practice interpreting a mock work order and checking size and orientation against a paper pattern. For shoe or leather work, ask whether training covers making new items or handling repair orders, and what assembly or inspection skills are assessed. [BLS preparation](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Ask how work moves through the shop

A cutter can ask how often patterns change, who checks a first piece and what makes a batch unacceptable. A shoe or leather worker can ask who approves repair scope, how tasks are divided across stations and what counts as a finished job.

## Moving between the roles

A cutter can bring a redacted pattern checklist and count record, then learn material selection, assembly and final inspection. A repairer can bring a redacted part specification and repair debrief, then practice template layout, repeated measurement and batch counts.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use only mock cards and a paper work order for a matching left and right lining. Card A has only a left pattern. Card B has both, but the right-hand orientation mark conflicts with the order. Spend ten minutes comparing the cards, ten drafting a request for the corrected pattern, and ten writing a check sheet for the two pieces after clarification. Record what the cutter can resolve and what the repairer must confirm before the finished item can be checked.
