# Executive Secretaries and Executive Administrative Assistants vs. Purchasing Managers at a Supplier Review

## Responsibilities

Executive secretaries and executive administrative assistants prepare the schedule, briefing materials, and follow-up so a leader can make a decision. Purchasing managers develop the supplier comparison and interpret contract and delivery terms.

## Compare the handoffs

| Deliverable | Executive Secretaries and Executive Administrative Assistants | Purchasing Managers |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Decision agenda, briefing package, and updated attendee plan | Bid comparison, supplier questions, and a recommendation |

U.S. occupational task sources · [O*NET 43-6011.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-6011.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 11-3061.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-3061.00#Tasks)

## One work example

A fictional supplier adds a condition shortly before a review that has been shortened. The executive assistant revises the agenda and confirms who can attend. The purchasing manager checks whether the new term changes the recommendation and tells the assistant which pages need replacing. Approval follows the purchasing process.

## Entry preparation

BLS describes several years of related work experience as typical for executive secretaries and executive administrative assistants. [Preparation source](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/secretaries-and-administrative-assistants.htm#tab-4) BLS describes a bachelor’s degree and several years of related experience as typical for purchasing managers. [Preparation source](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/purchasing-managers-buyers-and-purchasing-agents.htm#tab-4)

Look for administrative support involving calendars and meeting packets if you want executive work. For purchasing, look for supplier or contract support that includes comparison of delivery terms.

## Work conditions

Executive support follows leadership availability and can shift quickly. Purchasing can depend on supplier follow-up and formal reviews. Ask who controls the document version and who can accept a term.

## Moving between roles

Toward purchasing, add supplier evaluation to your briefing skills. Toward executive support, show how you turn a detailed recommendation into a clear decision agenda.

## Try it in 30 minutes

A fictional review drops from 30 to 20 minutes. A has a confirmed delivery date and B's date is open. The leader only needs to decide whether to continue reviewing today. There is no budget approval on the agenda. Spend ten minutes revising the meeting, fifteen preparing the comparison, and five noting what the team cannot promise.
