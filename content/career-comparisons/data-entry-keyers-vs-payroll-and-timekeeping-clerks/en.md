# Data entry keyers vs. payroll and timekeeping clerks

## Two kinds of verification

Data entry keyers verify source documents and record the correct fields. Payroll and timekeeping clerks verify hours and adjustments in the context of a pay period. A good handoff lets both teams trace how a number reached the payroll record.

## Compare the work

| Item | Data entry keyer | Payroll and timekeeping clerk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work product | Verified fields and an exception log | Reconciled time records and documented payroll adjustments |
| Work setting | Computer work with source documents | Computer work with time sheets, pay records and payroll contacts |
| Typical U.S. preparation | High school diploma or equivalent, short-term on-the-job training | High school diploma or equivalent, moderate-term on-the-job training |
| 2025 U.S. median annual wage | $41,340 | $58,260 |
The wage estimates cover nonfarm wage and salary workers and exclude self-employed workers. ## A shift change arrives before cutoff

At a fictional chain, the approved time sheet shows 40 hours. A shift-change form adds four hours and is still missing a manager's approval. The data entry keyer logs the approved 40-hour baseline and the four reported hours separately. The keyer flags the reported time for verification and retains both sources. The store manager verifies the time and approves the change. The payroll clerk checks the pay period and prepares the adjustment. A payroll reviewer authorizes the adjustment for processing.

## First steps in the U.S.

Try source review and field validation for data entry. Ask how the employer teaches its forms and correction process. For payroll, practice reconciling mock time sheets and ask which timekeeping software you will learn, how many pay cycles training covers and who reviews adjustments. [BLS typical preparation](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## What to ask about the schedule

Ask the data entry team how records are assigned and when a supervisor must review a correction. Ask the payroll team which days are governed by cutoff, how late time changes reach the reviewer, and who responds to employee questions.

## Moving between the jobs

Take a redacted exception log and a sample source reconciliation from data entry into payroll. Build experience tracing approvals and applying pay-period rules. For the reverse move, bring a redacted time reconciliation and correction log. Practice mapping unfamiliar fields and checking the accuracy of batches.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use a fictional approved 40-hour time sheet and a separate report of four additional hours with no supervisor signature. Spend ten minutes making a source-linked record that preserves the 40-hour baseline and the four reported hours, ten drafting a request for the store manager to verify actual time worked, and ten handing the discrepancy to the payroll reviewer. Record the four hours as an open item. A missing signature triggers follow-up. The reviewer decides how to reconcile verified time and authorizes any adjustment.
