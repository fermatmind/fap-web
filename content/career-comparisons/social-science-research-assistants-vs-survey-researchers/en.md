# Social Science Research Assistant vs. Survey Researcher: A Skip-Logic Error in the Field

## What each role delivers

A social science research assistant supports data delivery, quality checks and reporting. A survey researcher designs instruments, pretests them and oversees collection. Both may help monitor quality.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Social Science Research Assistants | Survey researchers |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Field log, affected records and QA notes | Instrument version, collection protocol and response to the issue |
| Basis | Submissions, version labels and QA rules | Question routing, eligibility, mode and comparability |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $61,990 | $69,460 |

## How the work meets

A fictional community survey skips an item on phones. The assistant identifies affected records by version and device and distinguishes valid skips from unintended blanks. The researcher reproduces the issue, decides how to mark revised submissions and asks the project lead to assess whether follow-up is warranted.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for research assistants and a master’s for survey researchers. Ask who approves questionnaire revisions during collection. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Assistants may follow daily deliveries and checks. Survey researchers coordinate pretests, interviewers and collection dates. Ask who consolidates issue reports.

## Moving between roles

Research assistants can bring QA skills to survey work and add measurement, sampling and field coordination. Survey researchers can bring collection knowledge and develop versioning and data management skills.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock file: item 5 should display only to eligible respondents, but a phone version hides it for everyone. Version and device are recorded, along with the earlier answer that determines eligibility. Spend ten minutes each classifying blanks, planning a correction check, and deciding what is needed before combining versions.
