# Petroleum engineers vs Roustabouts, Oil and Gas

## Choose the engineering question or the field assignment

A petroleum engineer assesses production or design options and prepares recommendations. A roustabout takes on assigned assembly, maintenance or materials work at the site. Choose engineering if you prefer analyzing what should change and explaining why. Choose field crew work if you prefer hands-on assignments and practical coordination.

## Compare the two work products

| Compare | Petroleum engineers | Oil and gas roustabouts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work inputs | Well records, constraints and design requirements | Work order, equipment and materials list |
| Setting | Office analysis and site visits | Field assignments with a crew |
| Handoff | Technical rationale, review needs and approved changes | Work status, discrepancies and remaining tasks |

[O*NET engineers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/17-2171.00) · [O*NET roustabouts](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/47-5071.00)

## A part arrives with a different ID

SLB’s published Permian Basin case describes both application engineering and field service in a pump installation. Imagine a surface work order on a similar project. A roustabout finds a part ID that differs from the materials list and reports it to the crew lead with the work order still open. The engineer checks the project records and documents whether the discrepancy requires a design review. If a change is needed, it goes to the authorized approver before the crew receives a revised assignment. These job assignments are illustrative. [SLB case study](https://www.slb.com/resource-library/case-study-with-navigation/al/esp-solution-exceeds-expectations-in-rare-permian-completion-scheme)

## Compare entry routes and authority

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for petroleum engineers. It lists no formal educational credential or related work experience as typical for roustabouts, and moderate-term on-the-job training. Ask the crew employer which assignments require additional training. BLS says licensure is unnecessary for entry-level petroleum engineers. For work in Texas, check the assignment against the engineering rules, applicable exemptions and signing or sealing requirements. [BLS preparation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) · [BLS engineering profile](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/petroleum-engineers.htm) · [Texas engineering rules](https://pels.texas.gov/downloads/lawrules.pdf)

## Compare pay alongside time in the field

BLS reports 2025 national median annual wages of $144,910 for petroleum engineers and $46,960 for oil and gas roustabouts, using OEWS. Ask how often engineers travel and whether crew assignments involve overnight or extended shifts. Compare local offers by similar seniority, hours and travel commitments. [BLS wage table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Build a bridge through documented field work

A roustabout can turn experience with work-order discrepancies into a sample for technical support and build the education and analysis skills needed for engineering. An engineer moving into field support can bring clear documentation and learn crew procedures, materials tracking and site-specific training.

## Try two notes in thirty minutes

Use fictional work order H5. It calls for part E7, while the delivery receipt shows E8 and the installation signoff is blank. Spend 12 minutes writing a crew note that cites both records. Spend 13 minutes writing a technical review note with the drawing revision and authorization you would need to verify. Spend five minutes assigning each open question and checking that delivery has not been treated as authorization to install.
