# Conservation Scientist vs. Forest and Conservation Worker: A Replanting Crew Finds a Shortfall

## What each role delivers

A conservation scientist plans land management measures using soil, water and monitoring evidence. Forest and conservation workers carry out field assignments, which may include checking equipment, selecting and planting seedlings and recording work.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Conservation Scientist | Forest and Conservation Workers |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Replanting areas, priorities and follow-up criteria | Completed fieldwork and exceptions by area |
| Basis | Site conditions, management goals and monitoring | Approved map, field markers, seedlings and equipment |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $73,010 | $43,680 |

## How the work meets

In a fictional replanting project, a conservation scientist uses survival counts and site conditions to prioritize two areas. The crew finds too few usable seedlings in one area and a boundary marker that does not match the map in the other. The workers record the differences for the supervisor, who confirms where work may proceed. The scientist checks whether deferring work changes the conservation objective.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for conservation scientists. It lists high school completion or equivalent and moderate-term on-the-job training for forest and conservation workers. Ask which duties require employer training or specific credentials. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Conservation science combines site review, planning and follow-up. Field crews may adjust schedules around weather, equipment and terrain. Ask where to report an inconsistent boundary or shortage.

## Moving between roles

Field workers can use observation and records in conservation science and add soil, resource management and evaluation skills. Scientists moving into field roles can use map and plan knowledge and add equipment training and crew practices.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Mock assignment: Area A needs 20 seedlings and has 16 usable ones. Area B has all ten seedlings but a boundary marker conflicts with the map. Spend ten minutes each on work that can proceed or needs approval, verification and supply questions, and a handoff to the supervisor and scientist.
