# Clergy Member vs. School and Career Counselor

## Two kinds of guidance

Clergy lead religious services and rites and may offer guidance on spiritual or personal matters. Educational, guidance, and career counselors and advisors help students explore education and work options and navigate academic requirements.

## Compare the decisions each role can support

| Decision | Clergy member | School or career counselor and advisor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First question | What observance or support matters to the person? | What options and deadlines apply to the student's plan? |
| Deliverable | Guidance or an activity arrangement within the faith community | Advising options and an academic follow-up |
| Approval | Confirm arrangements within the religious organization | Identify who can approve a school change |

[O*NET 21-2011.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/21-2011.00#Tasks) · [O*NET 21-1012.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/21-1012.00#Tasks)

## A religious observance conflicts with an advising appointment

In a fictional college, a student wants to attend a faith community observance that overlaps with a college planning appointment. At the student's request, a clergy member can explain whether the faith community offers another time. An advisor can verify the school's rescheduling options and deadline. The student decides what to pursue, and each organization confirms its own arrangement. Any coordination follows the student's authorization and applicable procedures.

## U.S. education and appointment

BLS assigns clergy a bachelor's degree, no related work experience and moderate-term on-the-job training as typical national categories. Ask the faith organization about its training and appointment or ordination process. BLS says school counselors typically need a master's degree and state-issued credential. Requirements for career and academic advising vary, and some states license career counselors. [BLS national education and training table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/education-and-training-by-occupation.htm) · [BLS counselor and advisor entry guidance](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/community-and-social-service/school-and-career-counselors.htm#tab-4)

## What the workweek looks like

Ask the faith organization about services, observances and availability for pastoral appointments. Ask the advising employer how it covers registration deadlines and appointments during graduation or admissions season.

## Moving between the roles

Clergy can bring communication and referral experience to advising, then learn education rules, career resources and any credential requirements. Counselors can bring interview and follow-up skills to a faith-based role, then verify the organization's preparation and appointment requirements and develop its tradition-specific knowledge.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use two fictional service times and a school appointment policy. Spend ten minutes drafting a question for the religious organization and ten listing options the advisor can verify. Spend the last ten marking the student's choice, which office confirms each arrangement and what details could be shared with authorization.
