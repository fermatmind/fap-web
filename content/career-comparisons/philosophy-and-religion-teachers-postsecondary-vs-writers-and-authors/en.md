# Postsecondary Philosophy and Religion Teachers vs. Writers and Authors: Revising an Ethics Story

## What each role delivers

Postsecondary philosophy and religion teachers organize subject discussions and assess reasoning. Writers and authors create and revise manuscripts for an audience or publication.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Philosophy and Religion Teachers, Postsecondary | Writers and Authors |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Argument feedback and assessment | Draft and editorial revision |
| Basis | Course reading and academic criteria | Audience, genre and editorial brief |
| U.S. median annual wage (2025) | $80,260 | $76,910 |

## How the work meets

A fictional story describes neighbors sharing a common space. The instructor asks students to identify the rule’s premises and test them with an objection. The writer responds to an editor’s request to change perspective and shorten the draft without losing the character’s motive.

## How to enter

BLS lists a doctoral or professional degree as typical for postsecondary philosophy and religion teachers. Its writers and authors row lists a bachelor’s degree and long-term on-the-job training as typical. Check the academic position or publishing assignment. [BLS occupation rows](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Teaching follows seminars, terms and grading. Writing follows drafting and editorial deadlines. Ask who reviews the output and how revisions are tracked.

## Moving between roles

Teachers can bring argument and research skills into writing and build audience-focused revision. Writers moving into college teaching need disciplinary study and a way to assess learning.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Read a fictional three-sentence story. The editor asks for a cut and the course asks for a fairness argument. Take ten minutes on each assignment and ten listing separate criteria for success.
