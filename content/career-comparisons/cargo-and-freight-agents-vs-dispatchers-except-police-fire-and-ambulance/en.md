# Cargo and Freight Agents vs. Dispatchers (Except Police, Fire and Ambulance) When Receiving Closes Early

## Where responsibilities differ

Cargo and freight agents check what transport is arranged and what the customer or carrier has agreed to. Dispatchers turn current crew and vehicle availability into an assignment. A changed receiving window forces the teams to compare their plans.

## Compare the handoffs

| Handoff | Cargo and Freight Agents | Dispatchers (Except Police, Fire and Ambulance) |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Carrier window, shipment details, and customer confirmation | Updated run schedule, assignments, and acknowledgments |

## Follow one delivery

A fictional customer moves its receiving cutoff from 5 to 3. The freight agent checks the transport terms and asks whether the customer accepts a split. The dispatcher compares available capacity and arrival times and sends feasible assignments back to the freight and shift leads. The customer and drivers receive one confirmed version.

## How to prepare

O*NET places cargo and freight agents in Job Zone 1–2, a broad preparation category. Look for booking or shipment support that lets you practice carrier communication and checking shipment records. [O*NET Job Zone](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-5011.00#JobZone)

O*NET places dispatchers (except police, fire and ambulance) in Job Zone 1–2, a broad preparation category. Look for dispatch support, scheduling, or fleet service work that teaches you to record availability and relay changes. [O*NET Job Zone](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-5032.00#JobZone)

## What shapes the workday

Freight work may follow booking deadlines and responses from outside the company. Dispatch is paced by work already under way and incoming requests. Ask how the employer handles competing changes and who sends the final update.

## Moving between roles

Move from freight into dispatch by adding live assignment and crew coordination to your transport knowledge. Move toward freight by learning carrier terms, supporting documents, and customer confirmations.

## Try it in 30 minutes

A fictional site closes at 3 and expects eight cartons. Vehicle A can arrive at 2:40 with room for six. Vehicle B can take all eight but arrives at 3:20. The customer has not agreed to split. Spend ten minutes on customer options, ten on the run changes and limits, and ten identifying what cannot be promised yet.
