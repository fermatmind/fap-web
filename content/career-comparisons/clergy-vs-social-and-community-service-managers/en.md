# Clergy vs. Social and Community Service Manager

## Spiritual care and managing a community program

Clergy may lead religious services and provide spiritual guidance. Social and community service managers coordinate programs and the people who deliver them. Both may serve the same community and handle different parts of a request.

## Compare what each role delivers

| Work | Clergy | Social and community service manager |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First check | The spiritual support the resident wants | Eligibility, capacity, staffing, and available times |
| Deliverable | A spiritual care arrangement or referral contact | A service arrangement and follow-up record |

May 2025 U.S. median annual wages were $60,810 for clergy and $80,390 for social and community service managers. These OEWS figures cover wage and salary jobs and exclude self-employment. [Clergy wage table](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/21-2011.00) · [BLS manager pay](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/social-and-community-service-managers.htm#tab-5) · [OEWS methods](https://www.bls.gov/oes/methods_25.pdf)

## A resident needs a conversation and a ride

At a fictional community center, a resident asks to speak to a clergy member and wants to know when a paused ride service will resume. The clergy member arranges a conversation and, with consent, sends the transportation request to the program team. The manager checks vehicle and staff availability, then tells the resident what the program can offer. The handoff contains only the information needed to arrange the ride.

## U.S. preparation and entry routes

O*NET places clergy in Job Zone Five, which describes extensive preparation. Ask the religious body about training, supervised service, and appointment or ordination. BLS describes at least a bachelor’s degree and related work experience as typical for social and community service managers. A supervised program role can build experience coordinating services and tracking outcomes. [O*NET clergy preparation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/21-2011.00#JobZone) · [BLS manager preparation](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/social-and-community-service-managers.htm#tab-4)

## Compare work settings

Clergy work may involve face-to-face conversations and religious activities. BLS describes community service management in settings including offices, clinics, and shelters. Ask about hours for community events, time spent with service teams, and who responds to changes in program capacity. [O*NET clergy work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/21-2011.00#WorkContext) · [BLS management work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/social-and-community-service-managers.htm#tab-3)

## Moving between the roles

Clergy can bring a de-identified referral workflow and a short outreach review, then build experience managing capacity, staffing, and program outcomes. A program manager can bring a de-identified ride schedule and a program review that explains how a service gap was handled. If seeking a clergy role, follow the religious body’s training and appointment or ordination process.

## A 30-minute paper exercise

Use fictional notes. A resident wants to talk by Wednesday and needs a Thursday ride. Thursday morning has no vehicle, but there is room in the afternoon. The resident consents to sending the ride request to the program. Spend ten minutes separating the requests, ten drafting a scheduling note, and ten writing a response that marks the time still to confirm.
