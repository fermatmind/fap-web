# Coil Winder vs. Machine Assembler: When the Label and Build List Disagree

## What each role delivers

A coil winder supplies coils and their production records. A machine assembler positions components, verifies the build against its requirements and checks the finished equipment. The example below concerns a fictional machine containing a coil assembly.

## Compare the work

|---|---|---|
| Input | Winding work order and materials | Build list, parts and assembly specifications |
| Output | Finished coil and lot record | Assembled machine and inspection results |

## How the work meets

At a fictional factory, winding supplies coils used in a drivetrain's electromagnetic brake assembly. The machine assembler sees that one container label shows a different part revision from the build list, so sets that container aside. The winding operator traces the lot and packing records and sends the verified findings to quality staff. The assembler follows the confirmed build list and records the completed unit's inspection separately.

## How to enter

BLS lists high school completion or equivalent and moderate-term on-the-job training as typical for both. Ask to see how a work-order change is communicated between stations and when an operator can resume a held job. BLS lists no related work experience as typical for either occupation. [BLS Table 1.2](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Winding can be organized in production runs. Assembly may move at the pace of component deliveries and scheduled inspections. Ask what happens to the rest of a job when one item fails an identity check.

## Moving between roles

Moving into assembly, bring work-order and lot skills and add configuration and final checks. Moving into winding, bring experience resolving part mismatches and learn winding specifications and production records.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Use a mock build list requiring revision B, a box marked A and a passing revision-B record for a different box. Spend ten minutes flagging what does not match, ten drafting a hold and handoff, and ten stating the evidence needed to resume assembly.
