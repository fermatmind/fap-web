# Computer Systems Analysts vs. Document Management Specialists: Finding the Approved File

## What each role delivers

Systems analysts study processes and integration and define acceptance criteria. Document management specialists define classifications and help make electronic records searchable and usable.

## Compare the work

| Work item | Computer Systems Analysts | Document Management Specialists |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Workflow and acceptance criteria | Classification, version and retrieval rules |
| Basis | Cross-team process | Record types and lifecycle |

## How the work meets

A fictional purchasing team replaces its approval system. The document specialist distinguishes drafts, approved versions and corrections and proposes metadata rules. The analyst turns those rules into workflow and retrieval checks for the business owner to review.

## How to enter

BLS lists a bachelor’s degree as typical for systems analysts. For document management roles, show examples of classification and version rules, and check the employer’s records and access responsibilities. [BLS occupation table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## Work conditions

Analysts divide time between interviews and acceptance reviews. Document specialists work with record owners, search and classification. Ask who approves changes or exceptions.

## Moving between roles

Moving into document management, add taxonomy and record lifecycle practice to requirements work. Moving into analysis, use governance experience and add workflow and integration design.

## Try it in 30 minutes

Draft D1 is visible to purchasing only, approved A1 is visible to finance, and correction C1 is pending and must retain A1. Spend ten minutes each writing classification rules, workflow acceptance checks and the names of the roles that review them.
